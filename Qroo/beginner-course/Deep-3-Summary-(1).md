## **General Comparison: Preterite vs. Imperfect**

- **Preterite**: Used for actions that are completed in the past. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Fui a la tienda.* (I went to the store.) <sup>o</sup> Example: *María se mudó a México hace cinco años.* (Maria moved to Mexico five years ago.)
- **Imperfect**: Used for actions that were ongoing or habitual in the past, with no clear endpoint. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Juan estaba en el ejército cuando se casó con María.* (Juan was in the Army when he married Maria.) <sup>o</sup> Example: *Mi papá fumaba todos los días.* (My father used to smoke every day.)

**Verbs That Change Meaning Depending on the Tense** Some verbs take on different meanings depending on whether they are in the preterite or the imperfect tense:

- **Conocer**: <sup>o</sup> Preterite: *to meet* – *Conocí a Juan en 2010.* (I met Juan in 2010.) <sup>o</sup> Imperfect: *to know* – *María me dijo que conocía a Juan.* (Maria told me that she knew Juan.)

- **Saber**: <sup>o</sup> Preterite: *to find out* – *¿Cuándo supiste que Juan te mentiste?* (When did you find out that Juan lied to you?) <sup>o</sup> Imperfect: *to know* – *Yo no sabía que querías ir al concierto.* (I didn't know that you wanted to go to the concert.)
- **Querer**: <sup>o</sup> Preterite: *to want to do something* – *Al escuchar la noticia quise gritar.* (Upon hearing the news, I wanted to scream.) <sup>o</sup> Preterite (Negative): *to refuse* – *No quise decirle mi nombre.* (I refused to tell him/her my name.) <sup>o</sup> Imperfect: *to want* (ongoing) or *to love* – *Nunca me dijiste que querías comprar una casa.* (You never told me that you wanted to buy a house.)
- **Poder**: <sup>o</sup> Preterite: *was able to (and did)* – *Pude levantar la caja yo mismo.* (I could lift the box by myself.) <sup>o</sup> Imperfect: *was capable of* – *Juan me dijo que podía trabajar el sábado.* (Juan told me that he could work on Saturday.)
- **Tener**: <sup>o</sup> Preterite: *got, received* – *Tuve la impresión de que Juan no quería venir a mi fiesta.* (I got the impression that Juan did not want to come to my party.) <sup>o</sup> Imperfect: *had (ongoing)* – *Yo tenía la impresión de que Juan no quería venir a mi fiesta.* (I had the impression that Juan did not want to come to my party.)

## **Using "Deber" in the Past**

- **Preterite**: To express something that should or should not have been done. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Debí decirle la verdad.* (I should have told her the truth.) <sup>o</sup> Example with "haber": *Debí haberle dicho la verdad.* (I should have told her the truth.)
- **Imperfect**: To suggest an action that should have been done but was not attempted.

<sup>o</sup> Example: *Le dije a Juan que debía vender su casa.* (I told Juan he should sell his house.) <sup>o</sup> Alternative with the conditional tense: *Le dije a Juan que debería vender su casa.* (I told Juan he should sell his house.)

**Setting the Scene with the Imperfect** The imperfect tense is often used to set the background or describe ongoing conditions in the past:

- Example: *Hacía frío y el cielo parecía que iba a nevar en cualquier momento.* (It was cold and the sky looked like it was going to snow at any moment.)

**Continuous Form: Preterite vs. Imperfect** The continuous form adds another layer of meaning when discussing past actions:

- **Preterite Continuous**: Emphasizes that an action was completed within a specific period of time. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Estuve leyendo dos horas.* (I was reading for two hours.)
- **Imperfect Continuous**: Describes an action that was in progress when another event occurred. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Yo estaba leyendo cuando me llamó María.* (I was reading when Maria called me.)

**Key Takeaways** Understanding when to use the preterite and imperfect is crucial for mastering Spanish at an intermediate level. These tenses work together to help you narrate stories, describe past actions, and convey the right nuances. The more you practice, the better you'll get at choosing the right tense for each situation.
