In this lesson, we explore the versatile Spanish verb *poner*, which usually means "to put" but appears in many other useful constructions. The video touches on various tenses and moods, including the **preterite**, **imperative**, **subjunctive**, and **pronominal form** (*ponerse*), with practical examples throughout.

#### **1. Poner = to put (physically place something somewhere)**

Most commonly, *poner* means to place something. Example:

- ¿Dónde pusiste las llaves? = Where did you put the keys?
- Puse las flores en la cocina. = I put the flowers in the kitchen.

We often use *poner* in the **preterite** tense for completed actions:

#### **Preterite conjugation of PONER (irregular):**

#### **Yo Tú Él / Ella / Usted Nosotros Vosotros Ellos / Ustedes**

puse pusiste puso pusimos pusisteis pusieron

Examples:

- ¿Dónde pusiste los platos? = Where did you put the plates?
- Los puse en la mesa. = I put them on the table.

Note: Direct object pronouns like *los*, *las*, *lo*, *la* are placed before the conjugated verb.

## **2. Imperative Mood (Commands)**

Used for telling someone to put something somewhere.

## **Tú form (informal):**

- Affirmative: *pon* <sup>o</sup> Pon las llaves en la mesa. = Put the keys on the table.

- Negative: *no pongas* <sup>o</sup> No pongas las llaves en la mesa. = Don't put the keys on the table.

#### **Usted form (formal):**

- Both affirmative and negative: *ponga* <sup>o</sup> Ponga las llaves en la mesa. = (You formal) Put the keys on the table. <sup>o</sup> No ponga las llaves en la mesa. = Don't put the keys on the table.

Note: Affirmative commands attach pronouns (e.g., *ponlas*), and negative commands keep them in front (*no las pongas*).

#### **3. Poner with Subjunctive Trigger**

A common subjunctive trigger is *¿Quieres que…?* = Do you want me to…?

Since *poner* is irregular in the present subjunctive, here's the conjugation:

**Yo Tú Él / Ella / Usted Nosotros Vosotros Ellos / Ustedes** ponga pongas ponga pongamos pongáis pongan

Examples:

- ¿Quieres que ponga las maletas en el coche? = Do you want me to put the suitcases in the car?
- ¿Quieres que ponga la mesa? = Do you want me to set the table?

## **4. Indirect Object + Poner (used with food and drinks)**

Phrases like *¿Qué le pusiste a…?* = What did you put in/on it?

- ¿Qué le pusiste a la sopa? = What did you put in the soup?
- Le puse sal. = I put salt in it.

Here, *le* refers to the soup (indirect object), and *sal* is the direct object.

# **5. Poner + Adjective (emotional states)**

Use *poner* to express emotional impact: "to make [someone] feel…"

- Las noticias me pusieron triste. = The news made me sad.
- Las mentiras me ponen furioso. = Lies make me furious.

*Poner* must match the subject, and the adjective must agree with the person affected.

#### **6. Ponerse = to become / to put on clothing (pronominal form)**

Use *ponerse* to talk about emotional or physical states you enter into, or when putting on clothes.

- Me puse feliz al verte. = I became happy when I saw you.
- Nos pusimos nerviosos. = We got nervous.
- Ella se puso un vestido rojo. = She put on a red dress.

**Commands with reflexive pronouns:**

- Ponte la camisa blanca. = Put on the white shirt.
- No te pongas la camisa roja. = Don't put on the red shirt.

#### **7. Common Expressions with PONER**

- *Poner a prueba* = to put to the test <sup>o</sup> Vamos a poner tu español a prueba. = We're going to put your Spanish to the test.
- *Poner la mesa* = to set the table <sup>o</sup> Los niños siempre ayudan a poner la mesa.
- *Poner en marcha* = to start up / set in motion <sup>o</sup> Vamos a poner en marcha el nuevo proyecto.
- *Poner huevos* = to lay eggs <sup>o</sup> La gallina puso dos huevos. = The hen laid two eggs.
