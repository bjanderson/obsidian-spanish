# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 13 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In this lesson, we focus on **SER**, the other main verb for "to be" in Spanish. You've already learned **ESTAR**, which is used for temporary states and locations. Now you're learning **SER**, which is used to describe more permanent traits like identity, origin, occupation, and relationships.

#### **Present Tense Conjugation of SER (Latin American focus)**

yo soy tú eres él / ella es nosotros somos ustedes son (vosotros sois – only used in Spain)

## **When to Use SER**

SER is used for:

- Describing identity: *Soy Paul.*
- Asking or stating who someone is: *¿Quién es? Es María.*
- Occupation or role: *Es profesora.*
- Describing relationships: *Es mi amigo.*
- Origin: *¿De dónde eres? Soy de Estados Unidos.*
- Nationality: *Es canadiense. Es inglesa.*
- Physical or personality traits: *Es alto. Es calvo.*

# **Articles with Occupations and Identity**

When using SER to state someone's job or identity, you normally omit *un/una*:

*Es profesora. Es carpintero. Es hombre.*

But if you add a descriptive adjective, the article returns:

*Es una buena profesora.*

*Es un buen carpintero.*

Note: Adjectives like *bueno* change form and placement depending on what they describe (e.g. *un buen amigo* vs. *un amigo bueno*).

## **Adjective Placement**

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun:

*La casa roja.*

*El profesor calvo.*

However, subjective or opinion-based adjectives may come before the noun:

*Un excelente cantante.*

*Una vieja amiga.* (meaning a long-time friend)

Some adjectives change meaning based on position:

*Una vieja amiga* = a long-time friend

*Una amiga vieja* = a friend who is old

## **Nationality and Origin**

SER is used to express where someone is from and what their nationality is:

*Soy de Estados Unidos.*

*Es canadiense.*

*Es inglesa.*

Some nationalities don't change for gender, only number:

*Los hombres son canadienses.*

*Las mujeres son estadounidenses.*

*El libro es aburrido.* – The book is boring (in general) *Estoy aburrido.* – I'm bored (right now)

*La banana es verde.* – The banana is green (color)

*La banana está verde.* – The banana is unripe

#### **Alive and Dead Always Use ESTAR**

Even though they seem permanent, the states of being alive or dead use ESTAR:

*Está viva.* – She is alive

*Está muerto.* – He is dead

*Los peces están muertos.* – The fish are dead

#### **Note on Time and Dates**

SER is also used to tell time, days, and dates (e.g. *Es martes*, *Son las dos*), but that will be covered in a later lesson.
