**"You have to wash the car before your dad arrives."**

The verb "arrives" is in the subjunctive: *llegue.*

But wait—why? Is there doubt he's arriving? Is he missing? Are we hoping he *might* show up someday? It doesn't seem to fit that big definition.

Or how about this one:

**"I usually get up before the sun comes out."**

That sounds like a routine. And we all know the sun's going to come out. So why is it *salga* and not *sale*?

That's why I don't teach the subjunctive by focusing on vague definitions. Instead, I teach **patterns**. Because here's the truth: if you just learn which phrases trigger the subjunctive, you'll be able to use it correctly every time.

One of those triggers—and the one I focus on in this video—is:

## **"antes de que" + subjunctive**

It means *before something else happens*, and it always requires the subjunctive when there's a **subject change**.

Here are a few examples:

- **Antes de que empiece la película, tengo que comprar palomitas.** (Before the movie starts, I have to buy popcorn.)
- **Antes de que cante el gallo, debemos levantarnos.** (Before the rooster crows, we should get up.)
- **Antes de que lleguen los invitados, debemos preparar la cena.** (Before the guests arrive, we should prepare dinner.)

Now, we can flip the sentence around and put *antes de que* in the middle:

- **Debo comprar palomitas antes de que empiece la película.**

Same rule applies—if there's a subject change, you use *antes de que* plus the subjunctive.

But if there's **no subject change**, you don't need *que* or the subjunctive. Just use *antes de* followed by an infinitive.

Examples:

- **Suelo leer antes de acostarme.** (I usually read before going to bed.)
- **Quiero hacer una lista antes de irme de vacaciones.** (I want to make a list before going on vacation.)

So here's the rule I want you to take away today:

- **Subject change?** → *antes de que* + subjunctive
- **No subject change?** → *antes de* + infinitive

That's it. If you focus on learning these triggers, you'll use the subjunctive correctly without stressing over abstract definitions.
