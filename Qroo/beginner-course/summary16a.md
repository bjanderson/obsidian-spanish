# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 16 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In this lesson, we tackle a common challenge for English speakers: understanding how the verb *gustar* works in Spanish. Unlike English, where the subject likes something, in Spanish the thing being liked is actually the subject of the sentence.

For example:

- *Me gusta el café* literally means "The coffee is pleasing to me."
- The coffee (*el café*) is the subject, and *me* is the indirect object: "to me."

## **Key Concept: Indirect Object Pronouns**

You'll need to learn these to use *gustar* correctly:

- me (to me)
- te (to you)
- le (to him/her/you formal)
- nos (to us)
- os (you informal, vosotros Spain only)
- les (to them/you all)

*Note:* Although sometimes redundant, Spanish includes both the object pronoun and the optional clarifier like *a Juan* or *a María*. For example:

- *A Juan le gusta el pan.*
- *A María le gustan las fresas.*

# **Verb Forms:**

- Use *gusta* for singular nouns and verbs in the infinitive. Example: *Me gusta bailar.*
- Use *gustan* for plural nouns. Example: *Me gustan los libros.*

# **Definite Articles Required:**

When using *gustar* with nouns, always include the definite article (*el, la, los, las*):

- *Me gusta el pan.*
- *Nos gustan las películas.*

#### **Making Questions:**

You can make a question by simply changing your intonation:

- *¿Te gusta el chocolate?*
- *¿Les gustan los animales?*

### **Short Answers:**

- *¿Te gusta el pan?* → *Sí, me gusta.*
- *¿Le gusta el chocolate a Juan?* → *Sí, le gusta.*
- *¿Le gustan las fresas a María?* → *Sí, le gustan.*

### **When Talking About Activities:**

Even if you mention more than one activity, always use *gusta*:

- *Me gusta correr, nadar y esquiar.* (Infinitives are always treated as singular.)

## **Practice Section:**

The lesson ends with a guided practice session where you translate sentences like:

- I like music.
- Do you like to dance?
- Does Juan like chocolate?
- We like animals.
