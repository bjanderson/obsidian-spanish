# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 19 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In this lesson, we take a much-needed breather after the challenge of combining direct and indirect object pronouns. Today's focus is on **possessive adjectives** (my, your, his...) and **possessive pronouns** (mine, yours, his...), how to use them naturally in Spanish, and when *not* to use them.

### **1. Asking "Whose is it?"**

Spanish uses the pattern:

- **¿De quién es + singular noun?**
- **¿De quién son + plural noun?**

Examples:

¿De quién es el coche? – Whose car is it?

¿De quién son las llaves? – Whose keys are they?

To clarify the subject, you can also say:

**¿De quién es el coche? ¿De él? ¿De ella? ¿De María?**

## **2. Possessive Adjectives**

Used **before** a noun. Agree in **number** and sometimes **gender** with the noun they modify.

| Meaning         | Singular                     | Plural                           |
|-----------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| My              | mi perro                     | mis perros                       |
| Your (tú)       | tu casa                      | tus casas                        |
| (usted)/Their   | su amigo                     | sus amigos                       |
| Our             | nuestro coche / nuestra      |                                  |
| Your (vosotros) | vuestro libro / vuestra casa | vuestros libros / vuestras casas |

#### **3. Possessive Pronouns**

These replace a noun and show ownership. Used with **definite articles** when standing alone and must agree in **number and gender** with the noun they replace.

| Meaning          | Singular   |            |              |              |
|------------------|------------|------------|--------------|--------------|
| Mine             | el mío     | la mía     | los míos     | las mías     |
| Yours (tú)       | el tuyo    | la tuya    | los tuyos    | las tuyas    |
| (usted)/Theirs   | el suyo    | la suya    | los suyos    | las suyas    |
| Ours             | el nuestro | la nuestra | los nuestros | las nuestras |
| Yours (vosotros) | el vuestro | la vuestra | los vuestros | las vuestras |

### Examples:

—¿Es tu abrigo?

—No, **el mío** está en la cocina.

(Is it your coat? No, **mine** is in the kitchen.)

—¿Son tus llaves?

—No, **las mías** están en la mochila.

(Are they your keys? No, **mine** are in the backpack.)

## **4. Clarifying Ownership with "De"**

Because "su" and "suyo" are ambiguous, Spanish often clarifies with:

- **Es su casa. → Es la casa de él / de ella / de María.**

Examples:

¿De quién es el carro?

—**Es suyo.**

—¿De ella?

—**Sí, es el carro de ella.**

#### **5. When NOT to Use Possessives**

In Spanish, possessives are **not normally used with body parts or clothing** when the owner is obvious. Use **definite articles** instead.

Examples:

—**Me duele el codo.** (My elbow hurts.)

—**Ponte la camisa.** (Put your shirt on.)

Use possessives **only to emphasize** ownership:

—**¡Es mi abrigo!** (That's my coat!)

### **6. Practice Tips**

Most mistakes happen when choosing the wrong **gender/number agreement** or forgetting the **article** before possessive pronouns (el, la, los, las). Just follow the patterns:

- Use **mi/mis**, **tu/tus**, etc. before nouns.
- Use **el mío**, **la tuya**, **los suyos**, etc. when replacing nouns.
- Add **de + name/pronoun** to clarify ambiguous ownership with "su" and "suyo."
