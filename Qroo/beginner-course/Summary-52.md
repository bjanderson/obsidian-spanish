In this exclusive Qroo Spanish Crew lesson, Paul explains how the meaning of the verb **deber**—which generally translates as *should* or *ought to*—changes depending on the tense. Understanding this distinction helps learners express advice, obligation, or personal reflection more naturally in Spanish.

#### **1. Deber in the Present Tense – Stronger Obligation**

When conjugated in the **present tense**, *deber* often implies **obligation** or **necessity**, similar to saying "must" in English.

### **Examples:**

- *Debes estudiar.* You must study. / You should study. (Depending on tone and context, it can sound forceful.)

Use this form cautiously when giving advice, as it can come across as an order or strict expectation.

## **2. Deber in the Conditional Tense – Softer Suggestion or Advice**

When conjugated in the **conditional tense**, *deber* sounds **softer and more polite**, closer to making a **suggestion** or giving advice.

## **Conjugations (conditional):**

- yo **debería** I should
- tú **deberías** you should
- él / ella / usted **debería** he/she/you (formal) should
- nosotros **deberíamos** we should
- vosotros **deberíais** you all (Spain) should
- ellos / ellas / ustedes **deberían** they / you all (formal) should

# **Examples:**

- *Deberías estudiar español todos los días.* You should study Spanish every day.
- *Yo debería ordenar mi oficina.* I should tidy up my office.
- *Deberíamos comer más sano.* We should eat healthier.

This is the version Paul uses most often—especially for self-talk, gentle reminders, or recommendations.

## **3. Why This Matters in Conversation**

In English, "should" is ambiguous—it can mean advice or a command depending on context. But Spanish gives you tools to **adjust tone more precisely** through tense.

- Present: *Debes ir ahora.* You must go now. (more direct, may feel bossy)
- Conditional: *Deberías ir ahora.* You should go now. (softer, more respectful)

Using the conditional form is a great way to **sound more natural and less confrontational**, especially when speaking to others.

# **4. Pattern to Practice: "Debería + Infinitive"**

This is a **plug-and-play structure**. Once you know how to say *debería*, *deberías*, etc., you can add any infinitive (unconjugated verb) after it.

### **Examples:**

- *Debería levantarme.* I should get up.
- *Deberías comer algo.* You should eat something.
- *Deberíamos estudiar más.* We should study more.
- *Deberían reciclar.* They should recycle.

Start your day with inner narration:

- *Debería levantarme.* I should get up.
- *Debería hacer café.* I should make coffee.
- *Debería sacar la basura.* I should take out the trash.

This builds fluency with natural sentence patterns and increases your verb flexibility.

#### **5. When to Include "Yo" or Other Subjects**

Normally in Spanish, you can drop the subject because the verb ending reveals who you're talking about. But in the **conditional tense**, *yo debería* and *él/ella/usted debería* are **identical**, so adding the subject can help avoid confusion.

#### **Example:**

- *Yo debería limpiar la casa.* I should clean the house. (Otherwise, someone might think you're saying they should.)

#### **Conclusion:**

If you want to sound more **considerate, polite, and natural** when giving suggestions or reflecting on your own responsibilities, use **deber in the conditional tense**. It's one of the most powerful and practical tools in your Spanish speaking toolbox.
