# **2. Moods and Tenses in Spanish**

# **Moods**

The Real Academia Española (RAE) recognizes three primary moods in Spanish, each expressing the speaker's attitude toward the action:

- **Indicative (Indicativo):** Used for stating facts, describing reality, or expressing certainty. *Example:* "She writes" – "Ella escribe"
- **Subjunctive (Subjuntivo):** Used to express subjectivity, doubt, desire, emotion, or uncertainty. In Spanish, the subjunctive is widely used in subordinate clauses after expressions of hope, emotion, or opinion. It indicates that the event is viewed as non-factual or as a possibility rather than an established fact. *Example:* "I hope she writes" – "Espero que ella escriba"
- **Imperative (Imperativo):** Used for commands, requests, or direct invitations. *Example:* "Write!" – "¡Escribe!"

*Note: While English sometimes discusses "conditional" as a mood, in Spanish it is treated as a tense.*
