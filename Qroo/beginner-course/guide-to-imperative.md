# **Guide to Spanish Imperative Conjugation**

#### **Introduction**

The imperative mood in Spanish is used to **give commands, make requests, offer advice, or give instructions**. Unlike other verb tenses, the imperative does not have a **first-person singular (yo) form**, as one does not command oneself. However, it does include **tú, usted, ustedes, vosotros, and nosotros**, each of which follows a specific conjugation pattern.

In this guide, we will cover each form in detail, providing clear explanations, charts, irregular verb lists, and examples.

# **1. Tú (Informal Singular Command)**

The **tú** form is used when giving commands to someone you address informally. It has two versions:

- **Affirmative Commands:** Use the **third-person singular (él, ella) form** of the **present indicative**.
- **Negative Commands:** Use the **second-person singular of the present subjunctive**.

# **Regular Verbs**

| Infinitive          | Affirmative Tú | Negative Tú |
|---------------------|----------------|-------------|
| Hablar (to speak)   | Habla          | No hables   |
| Comer (to eat)      | Come           | No comas    |
| Escribir (to write) | Escribe        | No escribas |

# **Irregular Affirmative Tú Commands**

Some verbs have **irregular affirmative** commands that do not follow the usual pattern.

| Hacer (to do)    | Haz | No hagas  |
|------------------|-----|-----------|
| Ir (to go)       | Ve  | No vayas  |
| Poner (to put)   | Pon | No pongas |
| Salir (to leave) | Sal | No salgas |
| Ser (to be)      | Sé  | No seas   |
| Tener (to have)  | Ten | No tengas |
| Venir (to come)  | Ven | No vengas |

# **Pronoun Placement in Tú Commands**

- **Affirmative:** Attach pronouns to the end (e.g., *Cómelo* Eat it)
- **Negative:** Place pronouns before the verb (e.g., *No lo comas* Don't eat it)

### **2. Usted (Formal Singular Command)**

The **usted** command is used when speaking formally or showing respect. It is formed using the **third-person singular of the present subjunctive**.

# **Regular Verbs**

| Infinitive | Affirmative Usted | Negative Usted |
|------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Hablar     | Hable             | No hable       |
| Comer      | Coma              | No coma        |
| Escribir   | Escriba           | No escriba     |

# **Irregular Usted Commands**

These verbs use their **irregular subjunctive** forms:

| Estar | Esté | No esté |
|-------|------|---------|
| Ir    | Vaya | No vaya |
| Saber | Sepa | No sepa |
| Ser   | Sea  | No sea  |

#### **Pronoun Placement in Usted Commands**

- **Affirmative:** *Tráigalo, por favor* (Bring it, please)
- **Negative:** *No lo traiga* (Don't bring it)

# **3. Ustedes (Formal & Plural Command)**

The **ustedes** form is used for addressing a group politely or formally. It is formed using the **third-person plural of the present subjunctive**.

# **Regular Verbs**

| Infinitive | Affirmative Ustedes | Negative Ustedes |
|------------|---------------------|------------------|
| Hablar     | Hablen              | No hablen        |
| Comer      | Coman               | No coman         |
| Escribir   | Escriban            | No escriban      |

# **Irregular Ustedes Commands**

| Verb  | Affirmative Ustedes | Negative Ustedes |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| Dar   | Den                 | No den           |
| Estar | Estén               | No estén         |
| Ir    | Vayan               | No vayan         |
| Saber | Sepan               | No sepan         |
| Ser   | Sean                | No sean          |

#### **Pronoun Placement in Ustedes Commands**

- **Affirmative:** *Díganmelo* (Tell it to me)
- **Negative:** *No me lo digan* (Don't tell it to me)

### **4. Vosotros (Informal Plural Command - Spain Only)**

The **vosotros** form is used when speaking to a group informally in Spain. It differs from other commands in that the **affirmative** form is derived from the **infinitive**, while the **negative** form follows the **present subjunctive**.

# **Regular Verbs**

| Infinitive | Affirmative Vosotros | Negative Vosotros |
|------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Hablar     | Hablad               | No habléis        |
| Comer      | Comed                | No comáis         |
| Escribir   | Escribid             | No escribáis      |

### **Irregular Vosotros Commands**

| Verb  | Affirmative Vosotros | Negative Vosotros |
|-------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Dar   | Dad                  | No deis           |
| Estar | Estad                | No estéis         |
| Ir    | Id                   | No vayáis         |
| Saber | Sabed                | No sepáis         |
| Ser   | Sed                  | No seáis          |

#### **Reflexive Verbs in Vosotros**

For **affirmative** commands, remove the final **-d** and add **-os**.

#### **5. Nosotros (Let's Commands)**

The **nosotros** command ("let's...") is formed using the **first-person plural of the present subjunctive**.

# **Regular Verbs**

| Infinitive | Affirmative Nosotros | Negative Nosotros |
|------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Hablar     | Hablemos             | No hablemos       |
| Comer      | Comamos              | No comamos        |
| Escribir   | Escribamos           | No escribamos     |

# **Reflexive Verbs in Nosotros Commands**

For **affirmative nosotros** commands, drop the final **-s** before adding the reflexive pronoun **nos**.

| Infinitive                | Affirmative Nosotros | Negative Nosotros |
|---------------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Levantarse (to get up)    | Levantémonos         | No nos levantemos |
| Sentarse (to sit down)    | Sentémonos           | No nos sentemos   |
| Vestirse (to get dressed) | Vistámonos           | No nos vistamos   |
| Acostarse (to go to bed)  | Acuestémonos         | No nos acostemos  |

# **Pronoun Placement in Nosotros Commands**

- **Affirmative:** Pronouns are attached, and an accent is added for pronunciation (e.g., *Vámonos* - Let's go).
- **Negative:** Pronouns go before the verb (e.g., *No nos vayamos* Let's not go).

This guide provides a thorough understanding of imperative conjugations in Spanish, with patterns, irregularities, and examples to master commands effectively.
