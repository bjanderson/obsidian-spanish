## **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 17 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In the previous lesson, we focused on *gustar* and how to say you like things using indirect object pronouns. In this lesson, we go further and learn how to express deeper feelings like **loving things or people** and how to avoid misunderstandings when talking about liking **people or pets**.

## **Liking and Loving Things:**

You already know how to say you like something using *gustar*: *Me gusta el café.*

To express stronger feelings, you now add:

- **Encantar** to love or be delighted by something Example: *Me encantan las fresas.* (I love strawberries)
- **Fascinar** to be fascinated by or deeply love something Example: *A María le fascina la cultura japonesa.* (María loves Japanese culture)

These verbs follow the exact same pattern as *gustar* and use *me, te, le, nos, les* with either the third person singular or plural form (*encanta/encantan, fascina/fascinan*).

## **Liking or Disliking People (Non-Romantic):**

Using *gustar* to talk about liking people can imply romantic interest, even if you don't mean it that way. Instead, use the expression:

- **Caer bien / caer mal** to get along with someone or not Example: *María me cae bien.* (I like María) Example: *Juan me cae mal.* (I don't like Juan)

These expressions are used everywhere in the Spanish-speaking world and are safe for non-romantic contexts. They also follow the same structure as *gustar*, where the person being liked is the subject of the verb.

## **Romantic Liking and Loving People:**

If you do mean romantic interest or love, then you can use:

- **Gustar** to like someone romantically Example: *Me gusta María.* (I like María – romantically)
- **Amar** to love (strongest, most direct) Example: *Yo amo a mi familia.* (I love my family) *Amar* is straightforward and uses direct object pronouns when needed.

- **Querer** to want or love (commonly used for people and pets) Example: *Te quiero mucho.* (I love you a lot) Example: *Queremos a nuestros hijos.* (We love our children) This verb uses the personal "a" with people and pets.
