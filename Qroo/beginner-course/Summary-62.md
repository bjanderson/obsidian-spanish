#### **Lesson Summary: Que yo sepa & Que yo recuerde**

## **1.** *Que yo sepa* **– As far as I know**

- This phrase uses the verb *saber* in the **present subjunctive** form (*sepa*).
- It is a **set expression** used to introduce a statement where the speaker admits limited knowledge. <sup>o</sup> Example: *¿Dónde vive Juan?* – *Que yo sepa, vive en Guadalajara.* <sup>o</sup> Translation: *As far as I know, he lives in Guadalajara.*

**Key Note:** The subjunctive is used here not because of a specific grammatical trigger, but because it has become fixed that way in Spanish usage. Just accept the form as it is.

## **2.** *Que yo recuerde* **– As far as I remember**

- This expression uses the verb *recordar* in the **present subjunctive** form (*recuerde*).
- Like *que yo sepa*, it serves as a soft, flexible way to share something based on your memory. <sup>o</sup> Example: *¿Dónde trabaja María?* – *Que yo recuerde, trabaja en el supermercado.* <sup>o</sup> Translation: *As far as I remember, she works at the supermarket.*

**Both phrases function well as sentence starters**, giving learners a natural way to slow the conversation slightly, think of the next part, and still sound fluent.

#### **Conclusion:**

*Que yo sepa* and *que yo recuerde* are great additions to your Spanish toolbox. These fixed expressions not only make you sound more fluent, but they also give you a moment to gather your thoughts when responding to questions. Because they always use the subjunctive, they're also an easy way to get used to common subjunctive forms in everyday conversation. Memorize them as complete chunks and start using them today to boost your confidence and fluency.
