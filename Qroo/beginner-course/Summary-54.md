# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 54 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In this lesson, Paul introduces *cada vez* as a flexible and underappreciated phrase in Spanish that means "each time" or "every time." The lesson explores its use in temporal constructions, progressive comparisons, and frequency expressions.

# **1. Cada vez que – Each Time That / Every Time That**

The most basic and common use of *cada vez que* is to express "each time that" or "every time that" something happens.

Examples:

- *Cada vez que te veo, me siento feliz.* Every time I see you, I feel happy.
- *Cada vez que Juan viene a mi casa, termina quejándose de algo.* Every time Juan comes to my house, he ends up complaining about something.

This can be paired with expressions like *terminar + gerundio* or *acabar + gerundio* to express "to end up doing something."

# **2. Cada vez más – More and More**

This expression is used to show gradual increase or intensification.

Examples:

- *La ciudad está cada vez más peligrosa.* The city is getting more and more dangerous.
- *El tráfico es cada vez más pesado.* Traffic is getting heavier and heavier.
- *Ella está cada vez más nerviosa antes de los exámenes.* She gets more and more nervous before exams.

Common expressions to practice:

- *Cada vez más gente* More and more people
- *Cada vez más seguido / más a menudo* More and more often

# **3. Cada vez menos – Less and Less**

This is used to express a gradual decrease.

Examples:

- *Tengo cada vez menos tiempo libre.* I have less and less free time.

### **4. Cada vez mejor / Cada vez peor – Better and Better / Worse and Worse**

These forms express improvement or deterioration.

Examples:

- *Mi español está cada vez mejor.* My Spanish is getting better and better.
- *Mi español está cada vez peor.* My Spanish is getting worse and worse.

### **5. Combining Uses**

A great way to practice *cada vez* is by combining its uses into a single sentence:

Example:

- *Cada vez que estudio, entiendo cada vez más.* Every time I study, I understand more and more.

### **Conclusion**

*Cada vez* constructions are highly useful and appear frequently in both spoken and written Spanish. They allow speakers to express repetition, intensification, frequency, and progression in a natural way. Learners are encouraged to add these formulas to their word lists and try combining them for fluency and flexibility in communication.
