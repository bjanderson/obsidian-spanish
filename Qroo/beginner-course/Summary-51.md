In this lesson, Paul introduces another essential trigger for the Spanish subjunctive: when one action **depends on another occurring first**. This commonly occurs in **time clauses** introduced by **cuando**, as well as **en cuanto** ("as soon as") and **una vez que** ("once").

## **Key Rule**

If a sentence has **two clauses** and the action in one **must occur before** the other can happen, the **first action (the one that must happen first)** is expressed using the **subjunctive**.

## **Trigger pattern:**

[Main clause – imperative or future tense] + **cuando / en cuanto / una vez que** + [verb in the subjunctive]

## **Examples (Present/Future Contexts)**

- *Avísame cuando tengas la información.* (Let me know when you have the information.) → "Tengas" (subjunctive) is used because the action of obtaining the information must occur before the speaker is notified.
- *Cenaremos cuando Juan llegue.* (We'll eat dinner when Juan arrives.) → Future action ("we'll eat") depends on another future event happening first ("Juan arrives").
- *Pensamos ir a la playa cuando deje de llover.* (We plan to go to the beach when it stops raining.) → Subjunctive: *deje de llover* (stops raining) must happen before they go.

## **Imperative Constructions**

The subjunctive often follows **cuando** in requests or instructions:

- *Cuando tengas tiempo, ayúdame.* (When you have time, help me.)
- *Cuando recibas el mensaje, mándamelo.* (When you receive the message, send it to me.)

The **imperative mood** (e.g., *avísame*, *ayúdame*, *mándamelo*) is commonly used in the main clause, and the verb after *cuando* is in the **present subjunctive**.

### **Habitual Actions: Indicative, Not Subjunctive**

If the sentence describes something that happens regularly or habitually, use the **indicative**, not the subjunctive.

- *Siempre nos levantamos cuando el gallo empieza a cantar.* (We always get up when the rooster starts to crow.) → Habitual action = indicative mood.

Compare with:

- *Nos levantaremos cuando el gallo empiece a cantar.* (We'll get up when the rooster starts to crow.) → Future-dependent action = subjunctive mood.

Look for words like *siempre* (always) or *soler* (to usually do something) to recognize habitual action.

## **Verb Spotlight:** *Soler* **– "To Usually Do Something"**

Paul introduces *soler* as a useful verb for expressing routine actions. It's followed by an infinitive and conjugated in the present indicative.

Examples:

- *Solemos levantarnos cuando el gallo canta.* We usually get up when the rooster crows.
- *Sueles cenar a las seis.* You usually eat dinner at six.
- *María suele estudiar todos los días.* María usually studies every day.

#### **More Time Expressions that Trigger the Subjunctive**

In addition to *cuando*, these expressions trigger the subjunctive when used with future or imperative contexts:

- **en cuanto** as soon as *En cuanto tenga la información, te aviso.* (As soon as I have the information, I'll let you know.)
- **una vez que** once *Una vez que termine, saldré.* (Once I finish, I'll leave.)

In casual speech, *cuando* can often substitute these expressions.

## **Time Expression That** *Always* **Triggers the Subjunctive:** *Antes de que*

Paul clarifies that *antes de que* ("before") is **not** governed by the same rule. It **always** takes the subjunctive—even with habitual actions.

### Examples:

- *Limpio la casa antes de que Juan llegue.* (I clean the house before Juan arrives.)
- *Siempre limpio la casa antes de que Juan llegue.* (I always clean the house before Juan arrives.) → Still subjunctive.

## **Past Tense Usage: Imperfect Subjunctive**

When telling a story or referring to completed past actions, the same logic applies—but with the **imperfect subjunctive**.

Example:

- *Le dije que me avisara cuando llegara.* (I told him to let me know when he arrived.)

Here, two subjunctive triggers are present:

- 1. *Le dije que me avisara* Imperfect subjunctive from indirect command.

2. *cuando llegara* – Imperfect subjunctive from time clause.

Use this structure to maintain tense agreement in past contexts.

#### **Summary Chart**

| Context                  | Time Expression                  | Mood                  |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Future-dependent         | cuando / en cuanto / una vez que | Subjunctive           |
| Habitual actions         | cuando / en cuanto / una vez que | Indicative            |
| Always takes subjunctive | antes de que                     | Subjunctive           |
| Past-time dependence     | cuando (in past-tense narration) | Imperfect Subjunctive |

## **Conclusion:**

This lesson emphasizes that when one action **must happen first** and you're describing the timing with *cuando* or similar phrases, the first action is placed in the **subjunctive**. By watching for subject changes and considering whether an action is habitual or dependent, you can accurately choose between the **indicative** and **subjunctive** moods in time clauses.
