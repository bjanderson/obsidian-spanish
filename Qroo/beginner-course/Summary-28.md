# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 28 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

This lesson goes beyond just memorizing colors. You'll learn how to use color words correctly as adjectives in Spanish—including agreement in gender and number, tricky exceptions like *naranja* and *café*, and how to describe light and dark shades. These subtle rules are often overlooked but make a big difference in speaking naturally and correctly.

#### **1. Colors Are Adjectives**

Colors in Spanish function as adjectives. That means:

- They usually come **after** the noun (unlike English).
- They **must agree** with the noun in **gender** and **number**, unless they're invariable.

#### **Examples:**

- *La casa roja* = The red house
- *Las casas rojas* = The red houses
- *El coche azul* = The blue car
- *Los coches azules* = The blue cars

## **2. Types of Color Adjectives**

#### **Type 1 – Fully Variable**

These change for **gender** and **number**.

- Example: *rojo, blanca, amarillos, moradas*

#### **Type 2 – Only Number Agreement**

These adjectives change **only for number**, not gender.

- Example: *azul, verde, gris*

These adjectives **don't change** at all. They're often borrowed from nouns or are compound colors.

- Example: *naranja, púrpura, café, color ladrillo*

#### **3. Using "Ser" vs "Estar" with Colors**

- Use **ser** for permanent traits like the color of an object. <sup>o</sup> *La camisa es roja.*
- Use **estar** for location or something temporary. <sup>o</sup> *La planta verde está en la cocina.*

#### **4. Light and Dark Colors**

Use:

- **claro** for "light"
- **oscuro** for "dark"

These follow the color they describe and are **invariable**—they don't change to match the noun.

## **Examples:**

- *verde claro* = light green
- *azul oscuro* = dark blue
- *Los zapatos verde oscuro*
- *La casa es azul oscuro*

## **5. Using "color + [color]"**

To skip gender and number agreement altogether, use the **"color" construction**. It's always safe and grammatically correct:

- *zapatos color café* = brown shoes
- *camisa color salmón* = salmon-colored shirt
- *casa color ladrillo* = brick-colored house

#### **6. A Note About Brown**

The word **brown** is one of the trickiest to use in Spanish because there's **no single word that works in every context**. Usage depends on **region** and **context**:

- **Marrón** common in Spain and Argentina, often used for shoes, eyes, objects
- **Café** widely used in Latin America for most things that are brown
- **Castaño** used for hair or sometimes eyes
- **Pardo** more limited usage, often in set expressions like *oso pardo* (brown bear)

When in doubt, **"color café"** is a safe construction in most Latin American countries.
