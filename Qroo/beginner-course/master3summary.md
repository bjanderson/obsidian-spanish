# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 3 Summary**

In the last video, you learned how to make your own sentences using the TION-CION cognate trick and quiero + infinitive (I want to...). This comes from the verb querer – to want.

### Key Points:

- 1. Expanding on "Querer": <sup>o</sup> We introduced tú quieres – you want. <sup>o</sup> Tú is the informal "you," used with friends, family, and peers. <sup>o</sup> Usted is the formal "you," used with strangers, elders, or superiors.
- 2. Making Questions: <sup>o</sup> To ask questions, simply change the intonation: ▪ ¿Quieres confirmar la información? – Do you want to confirm the information?
- 3. Using "Reservación" and "Reserva": <sup>o</sup> Both words mean reservation, but reservación is more common in Latin America, while reserva is used in Spain.
- 4. Practicing with "Querer": <sup>o</sup> Examples: ▪ Yo quiero confirmar la información. – I want to confirm the information. ▪ ¿Tú quieres confirmar la reservación? – Do you want to confirm the reservation?
- 5. Introduction to Spanish Verbs: <sup>o</sup> There are three types of Spanish verbs: -AR, -ER, and -IR. <sup>o</sup> Examples: ▪ AR Verbs: hablar (to speak), trabajar (to work), estudiar (to study) ▪ER Verbs: querer (to want), comer (to eat), leer (to read)

▪IR Verbs: compartir (to share), conseguir (to get), dormir (to sleep)

#### 6. Verb Conjugations:

<sup>o</sup> Conjugations are always presented in a specific order: ▪ yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros/nosotras, vosotros/vosotras, ellos/ellas/ustedes <sup>o</sup> For querer: ▪ yo quiero ▪tú quieres

## 7. Using Verbs in Sentences:

<sup>o</sup> Examples: ▪ Yo quiero comer. – I want to eat. ▪¿Tú quieres comer? – Do you want to eat?

# 8. Tenses and Moods Overview:

<sup>o</sup> Tenses indicate when an action occurs (past, present and future). Here is a more detailed breakdown: ▪ Present Tense: Actions happening now or regularly. ▪ Yo hablo (I speak). ▪ Present Perfect: Actions that started in the past but are relevant to the present. ▪ Yo he hablado (I have spoken). ▪ Preterite: Completed past actions. ▪ Yo hablé (I spoke). ▪ Imperfect: Ongoing or habitual past actions. ▪ Yo hablaba (I used to speak). ▪ Past Perfect (Pluperfect): Actions completed before another past action. ▪Yo había hablado (I had spoken).

▪ Future: Actions that will happen. ▪ Yo hablaré (I will speak). ▪ Future Perfect: Actions that will be completed by a future point. ▪ Yo habré hablado (I will have spoken). <sup>o</sup> Moods indicate the speaker's attitude toward the action: ▪ Indicative: States facts and certainty. ▪ Yo hablo (I speak). ▪ Subjunctive: Expresses doubt, wishes, or hypotheticals. ▪ Yo hable (that I speak). ▪ Imperative: Gives commands or requests. ▪Habla (speak - tú), No hables (don't speak - tú).
