# **Qroo Paul's Spanish Master Course Lesson 14 Summary Skool.com/qroo**

In this lesson, Qroo Paul explains the two main verbs in Spanish that both translate as "to know": *saber* and *conocer*. While they may seem interchangeable in English, they have different meanings and uses in Spanish. The lesson also revisits important patterns like *tener que*, *querer*, and how to use infinitives after certain verbs.

**SABER** – to know facts, information, or how to do something

Use *saber* when talking about things that can be learned, remembered, or explained:

### Examples:

¿Sabes dónde está el hotel? – Do you know where the hotel is? Sí, yo sé dónde está el hotel. – Yes, I know where the hotel is.

¿Sabes si Juan tiene que trabajar el viernes? – Do you know if Juan has to work on Friday?

### **Conjugation of SABER (Present Tense)**

yo sé

tú sabes

él / ella sabe

nosotros sabemos

ustedes saben

(*vosotros sabéis* is used only in Spain)

**Saber + infinitive** = to know how to do something

¿Sabes nadar? – Do you know how to swim?

Sí, sé nadar. – Yes, I know how to swim.

¿Sabe cocinar María? – Does María know how to cook?

You can also say:

No sé nadar, pero quiero aprender. – I don't know how to swim, but I want to learn.

\*\*\*\* Saber cómo + infinitive – is used "to know how to do something" when referring to a process or the specific steps of doing something: ¿Sabes cómo encontrar el nombre del vecino en la base de datos? (Do you know how to find the neighbor's name in the database?)

## **Word Order Tips:**

If a sentence begins with a question word like *qué* or *quién*, the subject follows the verb:

¿Qué sabe Juan? – What does Juan know? But without a question word, the subject can appear before or after: ¿Sabe cocinar María? or ¿María sabe cocinar?

**CONOCER** – to know or be familiar with people, places, or things

Use *conocer* when referring to being acquainted with a person, having visited a place, or being familiar with a song, book, etc.

Examples:

¿Conoces a Juan? – Do you know Juan? Sí, conozco a Juan. – Yes, I know Juan. ¿Quieres conocer a Juan? – Do you want to meet Juan?

### **Conjugation of CONOCER (Present Tense)**

yo conozco tú conoces él / ella conoce nosotros conocemos ustedes conocen

(*vosotros conocéis* is used only in Spain)

### **Important Note: The personal "a"**

When *conocer* is used with a person or pet, you must use the personal *a*: ¿Conoces a María? – Do you know María? ¿Tienes que conocer a Juan? – Do you have to meet Juan?

### **Places and Things:**

When *conocer* is used with places, it typically means you've been there or are familiar with it:

¿Conoces México? – Have you been to / Are you familiar with Mexico?

When referring to being aware that something exists (not necessarily being familiar with it), a better phrase might be:

¿Has oído hablar del museo? – Have you heard of the museum?

When talking about *facts* related to things (e.g., lyrics of a song), use *saber*:

¿Sabes la letra de la canción? – Do you know the lyrics? But for general familiarity: ¿Conoces la canción? – Are you familiar with the song?

## **Mixing SABER and CONOCER:**

¿Sabes si Juan conoce a María? – Do you know if Juan knows María?
