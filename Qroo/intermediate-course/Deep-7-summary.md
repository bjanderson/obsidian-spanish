### **Understanding the Imperfect Subjunctive**

- **Example:** <sup>o</sup> *Juan me dijo que fuera a la tienda.* (Juan told me to go to the store.) <sup>o</sup> Here, "fuera" is in the imperfect subjunctive. <sup>o</sup> *Juan me dijo que fuese a la tienda.* is also correct, showing that the imperfect subjunctive has two possible conjugations: the -ra and -se endings.
- **Recommendation:** Focus on the -ra endings, as they are more commonly used in spoken Spanish. For this lesson, we'll only use the -ra form.

### **Conjugation Overview**

- **-AR Verbs:** <sup>o</sup> Yo: -ara <sup>o</sup> Tú: -aras <sup>o</sup> Él/Ella/Usted: -ara <sup>o</sup> Nosotros/Nosotras: -áramos <sup>o</sup> Vosotros/Vosotras: -arais <sup>o</sup> Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes: -aran
- **-ER and -IR Verbs:** <sup>o</sup> Yo: -iera <sup>o</sup> Tú: -ieras <sup>o</sup> Él/Ella/Usted: -iera

<sup>o</sup> Nosotros/Nosotras: -iéramos <sup>o</sup> Vosotros/Vosotras: -ierais <sup>o</sup> Ellos/Ellas/Ustedes: -ieran

#### **How to Conjugate:**

- Start with the preterite conjugation, drop the endings, and add the imperfect subjunctive endings. <sup>o</sup> **Example with regular verbs:** ▪ Hablar: *Yo hablara, tú hablaras, él/ella/usted hablara, nosotros habláramos, vosotros hablarais, ellos/ellas/ustedes hablaran.* ▪ Beber: *Yo bebiera, tú bebieras, él/ella/usted bebiera, nosotros bebiéramos, vosotros bebierais, ellos/ellas/ustedes bebieran.*
- **Irregular Verbs:** <sup>o</sup> For verbs like *querer*, *haber*, *estar*, and *poder*, use the preterite stem. <sup>o</sup> Ir and ser are truly irregular but share the same conjugation: *Yo fuera, tú fueras, él/ella/usted fuera, nosotros fuéramos, vosotros fuerais, ellos/ellas/ustedes fueran.*

### **When to Use the Imperfect Subjunctive**

- **Subjunctive Triggers:** The triggers remain the same as those you've learned for the present subjunctive.
- **Examples:** <sup>o</sup> *María me dijo que quería que fuera a la tienda antes de que lloviera.* (Maria told me that she wanted me to go to the store before it rained.) <sup>o</sup> *Ella esperaba que los estudiantes tuvieran suficiente tiempo para terminar el proyecto.* (She was hoping that the students had enough time to finish the project.)

# **Optional Subjunctive Triggers:**

- Words like *tal vez*, *quizá*, and *quizás* can trigger the subjunctive, but not always.
- **Examples:** <sup>o</sup> *Quizá Juan venga mañana.* (Perhaps Juan will come tomorrow.)

- <sup>o</sup> *Quizá Juan fue el que dejó la puerta abierta.* (Perhaps Juan was the one who left the door open.)
- **Guideline:** <sup>o</sup> If the event is in the present or past, the subjunctive is optional. <sup>o</sup> If the event is in the future, use the subjunctive, future indicative, or conditional.

### **Conditional/Imperfect Subjunctive Combo:**

- This combination is used to express hypothetical situations.
- **Examples:** <sup>o</sup> *Si fuera rico, me compraría un yate.* (If I were rich, I would buy a yacht.) <sup>o</sup> *Si fuera posible, me mudaría a México.* (If it were possible, I would move to Mexico.)

#### **Pluperfect Subjunctive:**

- **Example:** <sup>o</sup> *Si hubiera sabido que te comportarías así, no te habría invitado.* (If I had known you would behave like this, I wouldn't have invited you.)
- This is the subjunctive of *haber* followed by a past participle.

## **Using the Imperfect Subjunctive in Storytelling:**

- **Example:** <sup>o</sup> *Juan me dijo que le llamara en cuanto tuviera la información.* (Juan told me to call him as soon as I had the information.)
- Use the imperfect subjunctive after subjunctive triggers when telling stories or recounting past events.
