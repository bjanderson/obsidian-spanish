**Introduction:** Hey everybody, Qroo Paul here! In today's lesson, we're diving deep into the fascinating world of **adjectives in Spanish** and their **word order**. You might remember that when you first learned about adjectives in Spanish, you were taught two main things:

- 1. Adjectives agree in **number and gender** with the noun they modify.
- 2. Adjectives usually follow the noun.

However, as you become more comfortable with Spanish, you'll start to notice that sometimes, adjectives appear **before the noun**, and that can change the meaning! In this lesson, we explore when and why that happens.

## **Restrictive vs. Non-restrictive Adjectives:**

- 1. **Restrictive Adjectives**: These are adjectives that describe **objective qualities** of a noun, helping to differentiate it from others. They **always** follow the noun. <sup>o</sup> Example: *Una casa grande* (A big house) <sup>o</sup> Example: *Pan integral* (Wheat bread, literally "integral bread")
- 2. **Non-restrictive Adjectives**: These adjectives add **extra information** or express a **subjective opinion** about the noun. They can be placed **before** or **after** the noun, but when placed before, they emphasize importance or a personal view. <sup>o</sup> Example: *La hermosa ciudad de San Miguel de Allende* (The beautiful city of San Miguel de Allende) <sup>o</sup> Example: *Una deliciosa torta* (A delicious dessert)

**Changing Meaning Based on Position:** Some adjectives in Spanish change their meaning depending on whether they are placed before or after the noun. Here are a few examples:

- **Antiguo**: <sup>o</sup> *Un antiguo amigo* (An old friend, from the past) <sup>o</sup> *Una casa antigua* (An ancient house)
- **Pobre**: <sup>o</sup> *El pobre hombre* (The unfortunate man)

- <sup>o</sup> *Un hombre pobre* (A poor man, without money)
- **Nuevo**: <sup>o</sup> *Un nuevo coche* (A new car, new to you, possibly used) <sup>o</sup> *Un coche nuevo* (A brand-new car)
- **Grande**: <sup>o</sup> *Un gran libro* (A great book) <sup>o</sup> *Un libro grande* (A large book)
- **Mismo**: <sup>o</sup> *La misma historia* (The same story) <sup>o</sup> *La historia misma* (The story itself)
- **Simple**: <sup>o</sup> *Un simple adorno* (A mere decoration) <sup>o</sup> *Un adorno simple* (A simple decoration)
- **Único**: <sup>o</sup> *Mi único problema* (My only problem) <sup>o</sup> *Un problema único* (A unique problem)
- **Viejo**: <sup>o</sup> *Un viejo amigo* (An old friend, a longtime friend) <sup>o</sup> *Un amigo viejo* (An old friend, in age)
- **Diferente**: <sup>o</sup> *Diferentes ideas* (Various ideas) <sup>o</sup> *Ideas diferentes* (Different ideas)

## **Special Cases: Bueno and Malo**

- **Bueno**: <sup>o</sup> *Es un buen poeta* (He's a good poet, talented) <sup>o</sup> *Es un poeta bueno* (He's a good poet, morally good)

- **Malo**: <sup>o</sup> *Es un mal doctor* (He's a bad doctor, not skilled) <sup>o</sup> *Es un doctor malo* (He's a bad doctor, unethical)

**Adjectives That Always Come Before the Noun:** Some adjectives always precede the noun, such as:

- **Mucho** (a lot of) <sup>o</sup> *Mucho trabajo* (A lot of work)
- **Poco** (little, few) <sup>o</sup> *Poco tiempo* (Little time)
- **Ambos** (both) <sup>o</sup> *Ambos padres* (Both parents)

**Ordinal Numbers:** Ordinal numbers like *primero* (first), *segundo* (second), *tercero* (third), etc., generally come before the noun they modify, but sometimes can follow the noun, especially in titles.

- Example: *El primer capítulo* (The first chapter)
- Example: *Carlos Quinto* (Charles the Fifth)
