| Why is *la mano* feminine even though it ends in *-o*? |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| It's simply a common exception to the *-o* = masculine pattern — its gender must be memorized.<br><br><!--ANKI_NOTE_ID:1786407688221--> |

| Fill in the article: ___ foto |
| ------------------------------------ |
| la foto<br><br><!--ANKI_NOTE_ID:1786407688223--> |

| Why does *la foto* stay feminine even though it ends in *-o*? |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| It's short for *la fotografía*, and the shortened form keeps the original feminine gender.<br><br><!--ANKI_NOTE_ID:1786407688225--> |

| Fill in the article: ___ agua fría |
| ------------------------------------------ |
| el agua fría<br><br><!--ANKI_NOTE_ID:1786407688228--> |

| Why does *agua* take *el* even though it's a feminine noun? |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| To avoid two *a* sounds colliding — it's a pronunciation fix, not a gender change. The adjective still agrees as feminine, e.g. *el agua fría*.<br><br><!--ANKI_NOTE_ID:1786407688230--> |
