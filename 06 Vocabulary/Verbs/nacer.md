# nacer

## Meaning

*Nacer* means "to be born": *Nací en Bolivia* (I was born in Bolivia). It also
extends figuratively to things coming into being: *La idea nació de una conversación*
(The idea was born from a conversation). *Nacer* belongs to the common *-zco* verb
family: any *-er/-ir* verb whose stem ends in a vowel plus *c* (here, *na-c-er*)
inserts a *-zc-* in the *yo* form of the present (*nazco*, not *naco*), and that same
*-zc-* stem carries through the entire present subjunctive and the commands built on
it. Everywhere else — preterite, imperfect, future, and so on — *nacer* conjugates
as a fully regular *-er* verb. Other common *-zco* verbs include *conocer*, *parecer*,
*aparecer*, *reconocer*, and *ofrecer*.

## Present Indicative

| Subject                 | Conjugation |
| ------------------------ | ------------ |
| yo                       | nazco      |
| tú                       | naces      |
| usted / él / ella        | nace       |
| nosotros / nosotras      | nacemos    |
| ustedes / ellos / ellas  | nacen      |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                                     | English                                | Notes                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Nazco en una familia grande, con seis hermanos.     | I was born into a big family, with six siblings. | *yo*, irregular *nazco*    |
| ¿Naces en esta ciudad o llegaste después?           | Were you born in this city or did you arrive later? | *tú*, question           |
| Cada año nace un nuevo modelo de ese carro.         | A new model of that car is born every year.  | *ella/it*, figurative          |
| En esta región nacemos hablando dos idiomas.        | In this region we're born speaking two languages. | *nosotros*, figurative     |
| Muchos terneros nacen en esta época del año.        | Many calves are born at this time of year.   | *ellos*                        |

## Commands (Imperative)

*Nacer* describes something outside anyone's control, so it has no natural direct
command form in everyday use — you can't order someone "be born." The forms exist
grammatically but appear almost only in poetic or rhetorical contexts.

| Form      | Affirmative | Negative    |
| --------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| tú        | nace       | no nazcas   |
| usted     | nazca      | no nazca    |
| nosotros  | nazcamos   | no nazcamos |
| ustedes   | nazcan     | no nazcan   |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                             | English                          | Notes                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| ¡Nace de nuevo cada día, decía el poema!   | Be born anew each day, said the poem!   | *tú*, affirmative, poetic/rhetorical use |
| No nazcamos con miedo al cambio.           | Let's not be born with a fear of change. | *nosotros*, negative, figurative/rhetorical |
