# hay

## Meaning

*Hay* is the impersonal present-tense form of the verb [[haber]], used to state that
something exists — "there is"/"there are." Unlike most verbs, *hay* is invariant: it
doesn't change for singular or plural. *Hay un perro* ("There's a dog") and *Hay dos
perros* ("There are two dogs") both use *hay*. See [[haber]] for the full conjugation
paradigm across every tense.

Learners often confuse *hay* with forms of *estar* ("to be" for location). Use *hay*
to introduce something for the first time (existence); use *está*/*están* once the
thing is already known and you're saying where it is: *Hay un libro en la mesa*
("There's a book on the table") vs. *El libro está en la mesa* ("The book is on the
table").

*Hay que* + infinitive is a common, impersonal way to express obligation: *Hay que
llegar temprano* ("One has to arrive early"/"You need to arrive early").

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Hay mucha gente en la calle hoy.            | There are a lot of people on the street today. | existence, plural noun       |
| ¿Hay un baño cerca de aquí?                 | Is there a bathroom near here?           | existence, question               |
| No hay leche en el refrigerador.            | There's no milk in the fridge.           | existence, negation                |
| Hay que estudiar para el examen.            | You need to study for the exam.          | *hay que* + infinitive, obligation |
| Hay tres parques en este barrio.            | There are three parks in this neighborhood. | existence, plural noun          |
