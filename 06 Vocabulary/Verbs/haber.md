# haber

## Meaning

*Haber* is almost always an auxiliary verb — it combines with a past
participle to build the compound ("perfect") tenses: *he comido* ("I have
eaten"), *habíamos llegado* ("we had arrived"). It does **not** mean "to have"
in the sense of owning something — that's [[tener]]. Don't confuse *haber*
with the everyday possession verb.

*Haber* also has an impersonal existential use — "there is/are/was/were" — but
in that role it freezes into a single invariant form per tense regardless of
number: [[hay]] (present), *había* (imperfect), *hubo* (preterite — never
*hubieron*, a common learner mistake), *habrá* (future). *Hay que* +
infinitive expresses impersonal obligation, like "one has to."

## Present Indicative

| Subject                 | Conjugation |
| ------------------------ | ------------- |
| yo                       | he          |
| tú                       | has         |
| usted / él / ella        | ha          |
| nosotros / nosotras      | hemos       |
| ustedes / ellos / ellas  | han         |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| He terminado la tarea.                     | I have finished the homework.         | *yo*, auxiliary                |
| ¿Has visto mi teléfono?                    | Have you seen my phone?               | *tú*, auxiliary, question       |
| Ella ha viajado mucho.                     | She has traveled a lot.               | *ella*, auxiliary               |
| Hemos comido ya.                           | We've already eaten.                  | *nosotros*, auxiliary            |
| No han llamado todavía.                    | They haven't called yet.              | *ellos*, auxiliary, negation      |

## Commands (Imperative)

*Haber* has no true personal commands — as an auxiliary and impersonal verb,
it doesn't get ordered to happen the way an action verb does. The only
imperative-like use is the archaic/literary *hete aquí* ("here you are"),
which isn't part of everyday speech and isn't covered here.

## Gerund and Past Participle

| Form             | Spanish  | English |
| ---------------- | -------- | ------- |
| Gerund (-ing)    | habiendo | having  |
| Past Participle  | habido   | had     |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                                     | English                              | Notes                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Habiendo terminado, se fue a casa.               | Having finished, he went home.           | gerund, adverbial use        |
| Ha habido muchos cambios este año.               | There have been many changes this year.  | impersonal compound form      |
