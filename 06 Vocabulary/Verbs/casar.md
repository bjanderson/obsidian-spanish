# casar

## Meaning

*Casar* means "to marry" in the sense of joining two people in marriage — the role
of a priest, judge, or official (*El juez los casó*, "The judge married them"). In
everyday speech, when talking about a couple getting married themselves, Spanish
almost always uses the reflexive form *casarse (con)* ("to get married (to)"): *Se
casó con su novio de toda la vida* ("She married her lifelong boyfriend"). It's a
fully regular *-ar* verb; the reflexive form simply adds *me/te/se/nos/se* before
the same conjugated verb.

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                        |

## Commands (Imperative)

*Casar* is rarely commanded directly — you don't usually order someone to marry
someone. The forms exist mostly for the reflexive, in formal or ceremonial contexts.

| Form      | Affirmative | Negative      |
| --------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| tú        | casa        | no cases      |
| usted     | case        | no case       |
| nosotros  | casemos     | no casemos    |
| ustedes   | casen       | no casen      |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                          | English                        | Notes                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Cásate cuando estés listo, no antes.    | Get married when you're ready, not before. | *tú*, affirmative, reflexive          |
| No te cases sin pensarlo bien.          | Don't get married without thinking it through. | *tú*, negative, reflexive          |
