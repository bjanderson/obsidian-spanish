# cuidar

## Meaning

*Cuidar* means "to take care of" or "to look after" — watching over someone or
something's well-being: *Cuido a mi abuela* (I take care of my grandmother). When
the object is a specific person, it takes the personal *a*: *cuidar a los niños*
(to look after the children). Used reflexively, *cuidarse* means "to take care of
oneself": *Cuídate* (Take care of yourself). It's a completely regular *-ar* verb
with no stem changes or spelling shifts in any tense.

## Present Indicative

| Subject                 | Conjugation |
| ------------------------ | ------------- |
| yo                       | cuido       |
| tú                       | cuidas      |
| usted / él / ella        | cuida       |
| nosotros / nosotras      | cuidamos    |
| ustedes / ellos / ellas  | cuidan      |

**Examples:**

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Cuido a mi sobrino los fines de semana.   | I take care of my nephew on weekends. | *yo*, personal *a*            |
| ¿Cuidas tú a tus padres o tu hermana?     | Do you take care of your parents, or does your sister? | *tú*, question    |
| Ella cuida el jardín con mucho cariño.    | She takes care of the garden with a lot of love. | *ella*                |
| Cuidamos a los perros de los vecinos.     | We take care of the neighbors' dogs.  | *nosotros*, personal *a*      |
| Mis primos no cuidan bien sus cosas.      | My cousins don't take care of their things well. | *ellos*, negation      |
