# su / sus

## Meaning

*Su* and *sus* are short-form possessive adjectives placed before the noun — *su*
before a singular noun, *sus* before a plural one. Unlike English
"his/her/its/their," Spanish doesn't distinguish those meanings in the word
itself — *su*/*sus* covers all of them, and context (or an added clarifying
phrase) tells you which one is meant: *su casa* can mean "his house," "her
house," "your (formal) house," or "their house."

*Su*/*sus* agree only in **number** with the noun that follows, not gender: *su
libro* (his/her book) and *su casa* (his/her house) both use *su*.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Ana perdió su teléfono.                     | Ana lost her phone.                      | "her"                              |
| ¿Dónde está su oficina, señor Pérez?        | Where's your office, Mr. Pérez?          | "your" (formal), question         |
| El perro busca su comida.                   | The dog looks for its food.              | "its"                              |
| Mis abuelos venden sus muebles antiguos.    | My grandparents are selling their antique furniture. | "their," plural           |
| Marta invitó a sus amigas a la fiesta.       | Marta invited her friends to the party.  | "her" + personal *a*, plural       |
| Los niños guardaron sus juguetes.            | The kids put away their toys.            | "their," plural                    |
