# afuera

## Meaning

*Afuera* means "outside" — the everyday, standalone way to say a location
is outdoors (*Vamos afuera*, "Let's go outside"; *Los niños están afuera*,
"The kids are outside"). It's extremely common in colloquial Latin
American speech, more so than [[fuera|fuera]] for this exact use — see
that entry for the fuller usage split: *afuera* is the natural pick for a
plain locative ("outside," as a place), while *fuera* is the safer choice
in the fixed *fuera de* + noun construction and set idioms (*fuera de
casa*, *fuera de sí*), where *afuera de* sounds off to most speakers.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                                | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Los perros duermen afuera.                     | The dogs sleep outside.                       | standalone location            |
| ¿Por qué no jugamos afuera?                    | Why don't we play outside?                    | question                      |
| Hace frío afuera hoy.                          | It's cold outside today.                      | statement                     |
| No dejes la bicicleta afuera.                  | Don't leave the bike outside.                 | negative command               |
| Desde afuera, la casa se ve pequeña.           | From outside, the house looks small.          | with preposition *desde*       |
