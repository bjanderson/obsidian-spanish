# once

## Meaning

*Once* is the number "eleven," invariable: *once años* ("eleven years"). Common in time
expressions (*las once* = "eleven o'clock") and everyday counting. In several Latin
American countries — notably Chile and Bolivia — *once* (or *la once*/*las onces*) is also
a light afternoon meal or coffee/tea break: *tomar once(s)* means "to have afternoon
tea." This regional usage is worth flagging since it doesn't map to any English sense of
the number.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                                          | Notes                                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Llegamos a las once de la noche.            | We arrived at eleven at night.                       | telling time                               |
| Tiene once años y está en quinto grado.     | She's eleven years old and in fifth grade.           | age                                        |
| Compramos once sillas para el salón.        | We bought eleven chairs for the room.                | counting objects                           |
| Faltan once días para las vacaciones.       | There are eleven days left until vacation.           | countdown                                  |
| Vamos a tomar once(s) esta tarde.           | We're going to have afternoon tea/coffee this afternoon. | Chilean/Bolivian dialect note        |
