# el / la / los / las

## Meaning

*El*, *la*, *los*, and *las* are the four forms of the Spanish definite article
"the." Spanish articles agree in gender and number with the noun they point to:
masculine/feminine, singular/plural.

Don't confuse *el* (no accent, article, "the") with *él* (accent, pronoun, "he").
They sound the same but the accent mark is what tells them apart in writing.

*La*, *los*, and *las* also each have a second job as a direct object pronoun
("her/it," "them" masculine, "them" feminine): *La vi ayer* ("I saw her
yesterday"), *Los vi en el parque* ("I saw them in the park"), *Las compré ayer*
("I bought them yesterday"). That's a different grammatical role from the article,
even though the words are spelled the same — context makes clear which one is in
play.

## Full Paradigm

| Gender / Number   | Form  |
| -------------------- | ------- |
| Masculine singular      | el      |
| Feminine singular        | la      |
| Masculine plural           | los     |
| Feminine plural              | las     |

**Special rule:** a feminine singular noun that starts with a stressed *a-* or *ha-*
sound takes *el* instead of *la*, purely to avoid the awkward double-a sound — the
noun is still grammatically feminine. This reverts to *las* in the plural.

| Spanish        | English      | Why           |
| ---------------- | -------------- | --------------- |
| el agua fría       | the cold water  | *agua* is feminine, but stressed *a-* |
| las aguas frías    | the cold waters | plural reverts to *las*                |
| el hacha           | the axe         | stressed *ha-*                          |

*Los* also covers a mixed group of masculine and feminine nouns together, the same
way *ellos* covers a mixed group of people: *los padres* can mean "the fathers" or
"the parents" (mother and father together).

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                          | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| El perro duerme en la sala.                 | The dog is sleeping in the living room. | masculine singular, subject        |
| ¿Dónde está el baño?                        | Where's the bathroom?                  | masculine singular, question       |
| Cerré el libro y me fui.                    | I closed the book and left.            | masculine singular, direct object   |
| La casa es muy grande.                      | The house is very big.                 | feminine singular, subject         |
| ¿Tienes la llave?                            | Do you have the key?                   | feminine singular, question         |
| Cerré la puerta antes de salir.              | I closed the door before leaving.      | feminine singular, direct object    |
| Los libros están en la estantería.          | The books are on the shelf.            | masculine plural, subject          |
| ¿Dónde dejaste los boletos?                 | Where did you leave the tickets?       | masculine plural, question         |
| Vi a los niños en el parque.                | I saw the children in the park.        | masculine plural + personal *a*     |
| Las llaves están en la mesa.                | The keys are on the table.             | feminine plural, subject           |
| ¿Compraste las entradas?                    | Did you buy the tickets?               | feminine plural, question          |
| Las tiendas cierran a las nueve.            | The stores close at nine.              | feminine plural                    |
