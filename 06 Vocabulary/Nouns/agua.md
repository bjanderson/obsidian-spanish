# agua

## Meaning

*Agua* means "water." It's grammatically feminine — but in the singular it
takes the masculine-looking article *el* (*el agua*, not *la agua*). This is a
well-known exception: feminine nouns that start with a stressed *a-* or *ha-*
sound take *el* in the singular purely to avoid two stressed "a" sounds
clashing back to back (*la agua* is awkward to say; *el agua* flows). The noun
is still feminine — any adjective modifying it agrees in the feminine, e.g.
*el agua fría* ("the cold water"), not *el agua frío*. The plural reverts to
the normal feminine article: *las aguas*. This same pattern applies to other
words like *el hacha* ("the axe") and *el alma* ("the soul").

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                          | Notes                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| El agua está muy fría.                    | The water is very cold.             | singular, subject, feminine adjective *fría* |
| Necesito un vaso de agua.                 | I need a glass of water.            | singular, after a preposition, "de"    |
| ¿Bebiste suficiente agua hoy?             | Did you drink enough water today?   | question                                 |
| No tengo agua fría en la nevera.          | I don't have cold water in the fridge. | negation                              |
| Las aguas del lago son cristalinas.       | The lake's waters are crystal clear. | plural, subject, "del"                  |
