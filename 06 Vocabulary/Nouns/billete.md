# billete

## Meaning

*Billete* is a paper banknote (*un billete de cien pesos*). It's a masculine
noun. In Spain, *billete* also means "ticket" (for travel, the lottery, etc.),
but in neutral Latin American Spanish that sense is normally *boleto* instead
— *billete* alone is understood almost exclusively as a banknote. The examples
below show the banknote sense plus a couple of *boleto* sentences for
contrast, since English "bill/ticket" can point to either word depending on
dialect.

## Singular / Plural

| Number   | Form           |
| -------- | -------------- |
| Singular | el billete     |

## Examples

| Spanish                                            | English                                | Notes            |
| -------------------------------------------------------| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Pagué con un billete de cien pesos.                      | I paid with a hundred-peso bill.             | banknote                |
| ¿Dónde compro el boleto de avión?                        | Where do I buy the plane ticket?             | question, boleto (ticket, LatAm) |
| No tengo billetes pequeños para el cambio.                | I don't have small bills for change.         | negation, banknote      |
| Compró el boleto de tren con anticipación.               | They bought the train ticket in advance.     | boleto (ticket, LatAm)  |
| Guardó el boleto de la lotería.                          | They kept the lottery ticket.                | boleto (ticket, LatAm)  |
