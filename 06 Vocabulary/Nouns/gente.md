# gente

## Meaning

*Gente* means "people." It's feminine: *la gente*. The important irregularity: in
Spanish, *gente* is a **singular collective noun**, unlike English "people," which is
grammatically plural. That means it takes **singular** verb agreement: *la gente es
amable* ("people are kind"), never *la gente son amable*. Think of it like "the crowd"
or "the group" in English — a singular word standing in for many individuals.

## Singular / Plural

| Number   | Form        |
| -------- | ----------- |
| Singular | la gente    |
| Plural   | las gentes* |

*The plural *gentes* technically exists (used for distinct groups of people, e.g. "the
peoples of Latin America" — *las gentes de Latinoamérica*), but it sounds archaic or
overly literary in everyday speech. In normal conversation, use *la gente* for both
"person" in bulk and "people" in general, and rely on quantifiers (*mucha gente*, *poca
gente*, *toda la gente*) instead of pluralizing it.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                                       | Notes                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| La gente es muy amable aquí.                 | People are very kind here.                     | singular verb agreement (the key point)   |
| Vi a mucha gente en el concierto.            | I saw a lot of people at the concert.          | object, personal a                        |
| ¿Conoces a toda esta gente?                  | Do you know all these people?                  | question, personal a                      |
| No es gente de aquí.                         | They aren't people from around here.           | negation                                  |
| Depende de la gente con la que trabajas.     | It depends on the people you work with.        | after a preposition                       |
