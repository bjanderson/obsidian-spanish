# pero

## Meaning

*Pero* is invariant and joins two contrasting ideas — the everyday word for "but."
It contrasts with *sino* ("but rather"), a separate, less common word used only
after a negative to replace one thing with another (*No es rojo, sino azul*, "It's
not red, but blue") — *pero* is the general-purpose contrast word used everywhere
else.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Quiero ir, pero no tengo tiempo.            | I want to go, but I don't have time.     | contrast between clauses           |
| Es caro, pero vale la pena.                 | It's expensive, but it's worth it.       | contrast between clauses           |
| Hablamos, pero no llegamos a un acuerdo.    | We talked, but we didn't reach an agreement. | contrast between clauses       |
| Me gusta el plan, pero tengo dudas.         | I like the plan, but I have doubts.      | contrast between clauses           |
| Es pequeño, pero muy cómodo.                | It's small, but very comfortable.        | contrast between adjectives        |
