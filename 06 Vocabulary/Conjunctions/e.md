# e

## Meaning

*E* is just [[y]] ("and") with a small spelling change: right before a word
that starts with an *i-* or *hi-* sound, *y* becomes *e* to avoid two *i* sounds
running together. *padre e hijo* ("father and son"), not *padre y hijo*.

The swap doesn't happen before *hie-* (a diphthong, different sound): *agua y
hielo* ("water and ice") keeps *y*, because *hielo* starts with a *hie-* sound,
not a plain *i-*. It also doesn't apply when the second word merely starts with
the *letter* y or the sound changes for other reasons — the rule is about the
sound, not the spelling.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                          | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Compré papel e impresora nueva.             | I bought paper and a new printer.      | before *i-*                        |
| Habla español e inglés.                     | She speaks Spanish and English.        | before *i-*                        |
| Ana e Isabel son primas.                    | Ana and Isabel are cousins.             | before *i-*, names                 |
| Necesitamos madera e instrumentos.          | We need wood and tools.                | before *i-*                        |
| Trajo agua y hielo para la fiesta.          | He brought water and ice for the party. | *hie-*, keeps *y*                  |
