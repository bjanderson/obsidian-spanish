# sino

## Meaning

*Sino* means "but rather" — a corrective conjunction used only after a negative
statement, to replace the rejected thing with the correct one: *No es rojo, sino azul*
("It's not red, but blue"). When the second part has its own conjugated verb, use *sino
que*: *No dijo que vendría, sino que llamaría* ("She didn't say she'd come, but rather
that she'd call"). Don't confuse *sino* with *pero* (the general, everyday "but," used
for ordinary contrast rather than correction) or with *si no* (two words, "if not"):
*Avísame si no puedes venir* ("Let me know if you can't come"). Introduced alongside
[[mientras]] and [[aunque]] in this batch.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                                          | Notes                              |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| No quiero café, sino té.                    | I don't want coffee, but rather tea.                       | correction after negative                 |
| No fue un error, sino una decisión.         | It wasn't a mistake, but rather a decision.                | correction after negative                 |
| No se fue, sino que se quedó callado.       | She didn't leave, but rather stayed quiet.                 | *sino que* + conjugated verb              |
| No lo hizo por dinero, sino por amor.       | He didn't do it for money, but rather for love.            | correction after negative                 |
| Avísame si no puedes venir.                 | Let me know if you can't come.                             | *si no*, "if not" — not to be confused with *sino* |
