# aunque

## Meaning

*Aunque* means "although," "even though," or "even if" — it introduces a concession,
admitting one fact while asserting something that goes against the expectation it
creates. With the indicative, it presents the concession as a known fact: *Aunque
llueve, vamos a salir* ("Although it's raining, we're going out" — it is, in fact,
raining). With the subjunctive, it presents the concession as hypothetical, downplayed,
or not confirmed: *Aunque llueva, vamos a salir* ("Even if it rains, we're going out" —
whether it actually rains is still open). This indicative/subjunctive contrast is the
single most important thing to learn about *aunque*. Introduced alongside [[mientras]]
and [[sino]] in this batch.

## Examples

| Spanish                                  | English                                       | Notes                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Aunque está cansado, sigue trabajando.        | Although he's tired, he keeps working.               | indicative, known fact           |
| Aunque esté cansado, seguirá trabajando.      | Even if he's tired, he'll keep working.               | subjunctive, hypothetical         |
| Fuimos a la playa aunque hacía frío.          | We went to the beach although it was cold.            | indicative, known fact            |
| Aunque no lo creas, es verdad.                | Believe it or not, it's true.                          | subjunctive, downplayed            |
| Le gusta el plan, aunque tiene algunas dudas. | She likes the plan, although she has some doubts.      | indicative, known fact             |
