# o

## Meaning

*O* joins alternatives — the everyday word for "or." It has the same kind of
spelling-sound switch as [[y]] ("and"): *o* changes to *u* right before a word that
starts with the sound *o* (spelled *o-* or *ho-*), to avoid two *o* sounds running
together.

| Spanish              | English            | Why                           |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| siete u ocho               | seven or eight        | *ocho* starts with the *o* sound    |
| mujer u hombre               | woman or man            | *hombre* starts with the *o* sound (silent *h*) |

*O* also doubles up for exclusive either/or: *o... o...* ("either... or...").

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| ¿Quieres té o café?                         | Do you want tea or coffee?               | joining nouns, question            |
| Podemos ir hoy o mañana.                    | We can go today or tomorrow.             | joining nouns                      |
| O terminas la tarea o no sales.             | Either you finish your homework or you don't go out. | exclusive either/or   |
| Necesito siete u ocho voluntarios.          | I need seven or eight volunteers.        | o-switch, before *o-*              |
| Vino un chico u otro, no recuerdo bien.     | Some guy or another came, I don't remember well. | o-switch, before *o-*      |

## Related Vocabulary

- [[y]] — the same kind of spelling switch, before *i-*/*hi-* instead of *o-*/*ho-*.
