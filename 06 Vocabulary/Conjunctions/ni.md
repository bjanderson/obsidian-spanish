# ni

## Meaning

*Ni* links two negated things — "nor." It shows up most often doubled, *ni...
ni...* ("neither... nor..."), which replaces English's shift from "not" to
"nor": *No quiero ni café ni té* ("I don't want either coffee or tea" / "I want
neither coffee nor tea"). *Ni* can also mean "not even," adding emphasis to a
single negated thing: *Ni siquiera me saludó* ("He didn't even say hi to me").

Unlike English, a Spanish sentence with *ni* still needs *no* before the verb
when *ni* comes after it: *No vino ni Ana ni Rosa* ("Neither Ana nor Rosa
came"). If *ni... ni...* starts the sentence, *no* is dropped: *Ni Ana ni Rosa
vinieron*.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                                | Notes                          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| No tengo ni tiempo ni dinero.                       | I have neither time nor money.                | *no* + *ni... ni...*                  |
| Ni él ni ella sabían la respuesta.                  | Neither he nor she knew the answer.           | *ni... ni...* starting the sentence   |
| Ni siquiera me llamó para avisarme.                 | He didn't even call to let me know.           | "not even"                             |
| No quiero salir, ni tampoco quedarme aquí.          | I don't want to go out, nor stay here either. | *ni* linking two verbs                 |
| ¿No trajiste ni un paraguas?                        | You didn't even bring an umbrella?            | "not even," question                   |
