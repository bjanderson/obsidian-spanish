# y

## Meaning

*Y* joins two words, phrases, or clauses — the everyday word for "and." It's
invariant, and it's one letter, but it has one important spelling-sound rule to
know.

**The e-switch:** *y* changes to *e* right before a word that starts with the sound
*i* — that includes words spelled with *i-* and most words spelled with *hi-*
(the *h* is silent). This is purely about the sound, so it avoids two *i* sounds
running together.

| Spanish              | English            | Why                          |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| padre e hijo               | father and son        | *hijo* starts with the *i* sound   |
| geografía e historia        | geography and history  | *historia* starts with the *i* sound |
| nieve y hielo                 | snow and ice           | exception: *hielo* starts with the *hie-* diphthong, not a plain *i* sound, so *y* stays |

## Examples

| Spanish                                  | English                          | Notes                        |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Compré pan y leche.                             | I bought bread and milk.               | joining nouns                     |
| Ella canta y baila muy bien.                    | She sings and dances very well.        | joining verbs                     |
| Es tarde y todavía no hemos comido.             | It's late and we still haven't eaten.  | joining clauses                   |
| Mi madre e Isabel son amigas.                    | My mother and Isabel are friends.      | e-switch, before *I-*             |
| Necesito aguja e hilo para este arreglo.          | I need needle and thread for this repair. | e-switch, before *hi-*         |
