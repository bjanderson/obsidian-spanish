# hacia

## Meaning

*Hacia* is invariant and marks direction "toward" something — physical
movement (*Caminó hacia la puerta*, "He walked toward the door") or a
figurative direction, like a feeling aimed at someone (*siente cariño
hacia sus abuelos*, "she feels affection toward her grandparents"). It
also marks an approximate point in time (*hacia las ocho*, "around eight
o'clock"). Unlike [[hasta|hasta]], which marks an endpoint actually
reached, *hacia* only points in a direction — it doesn't say whether the
destination was arrived at.

## Examples

| Spanish                                       | English                                    | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| El barco navegaba hacia la isla.                    | The boat was sailing toward the island.             | physical direction                |
| Corrió hacia mí cuando me vio.                      | He ran toward me when he saw me.                    | physical direction, personal *a* n/a — pronoun object |
| Siento mucho respeto hacia mi maestra.              | I feel a lot of respect toward my teacher.          | figurative direction              |
| ¿Hacia dónde vas?                                   | Which way are you headed?                           | question                          |
| Llegaron hacia las nueve de la noche.               | They arrived around nine at night.                  | approximate time                   |

## Related Vocabulary

- [[hasta|hasta]] — a related preposition; *hacia* points toward a direction without implying arrival, while *hasta* marks an endpoint that is reached.
