# del

## Meaning

*Del* is the mandatory contraction of [[de]] + *el* ("of the," "from the," masculine
singular). Whenever *de* is immediately followed by the article *el*, Spanish always
contracts them to *del* — it's never optional and never written as *de el*.

The one exception: when *el* is part of a proper noun's name rather than a plain
article — most notably the country name *El Salvador*. In that case, *de* and *El*
stay separate and *El* keeps its capital letter: *Somos de El Salvador* ("We're from
El Salvador"), not *del Salvador*.

*Del* only contracts with the masculine singular article *el*. It doesn't contract
with *la*, *los*, *las*, or with the accented pronoun *él* ("he"): *el libro de él*
("his book," literally "the book of him") stays as two words.

## Examples

| Spanish                                       | English                                | Notes                              |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Ese es el perro del vecino.                         | That's the neighbor's dog.                   | de + el → del, possession               |
| Venimos del aeropuerto.                             | We're coming from the airport.               | de + el → del, origin                    |
| El centro del pueblo está muy tranquilo.            | The center of the town is very calm.          | de + el → del                           |
| Mi tía es de El Salvador, no del Salvador.          | My aunt is from El Salvador, not "del Salvador." | exception, proper noun               |
| Ese es el carro de él, no del vecino.               | That's his car, not the neighbor's.           | contrast with *de él* (no contraction)   |
