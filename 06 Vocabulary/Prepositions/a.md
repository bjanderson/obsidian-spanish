# a

## Meaning

*A* is invariant — it never changes form. Its two big jobs are marking direction
("to") and marking a point in time or space ("at"). It also has a special grammar
role in Spanish with no direct English equivalent: the **personal *a***, required
before a direct object that's a specific person (see B1.4.2.2 for the full rule).

When *a* is directly followed by the article *el*, the two words always contract to
*al* — *a + el → al* (e.g. *Voy al mercado*, "I'm going to the market"). This is
mandatory, the same way *de + el* contracts to [[del]].

## Core Uses

| Use                | Pattern                     | Example                       | English                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Direction/destination  | ir + a + [place]                | Voy a Bolivia.                    | I'm going to Bolivia.                 |
| Personal *a*             | verb + a + [specific person]     | Veo a María.                      | I see María.                          |
| Indirect object marker     | verb + a + [person]              | Le di el libro a Juan.            | I gave the book to Juan.              |
| Time (clock time)             | a + las + [hour]                  | Salimos a las tres.               | We're leaving at three.               |
| Manner                          | a + [manner]                       | Fuimos a pie.                     | We went on foot.                      |
