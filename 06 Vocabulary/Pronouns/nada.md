# nada

## Meaning

*Nada* means "nothing." Unlike English, Spanish uses a double negative as the
normal, correct pattern: *No sé nada* ("I don't know anything," literally "I
don't know nothing"). If *nada* comes before the verb, *no* is dropped: *Nada
sé de eso* is possible but sounds literary — in everyday speech, *No sé nada de
eso* is far more natural.

*Nada* also works as an intensifying adverb meaning "not at all": *No me gustó
nada la película* ("I didn't like the movie at all").

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| No tengo nada que hacer hoy.                | I don't have anything to do today.       | *no* + *nada*, standard double negative |
| ¿Qué compraste? — Nada.                     | What did you buy? — Nothing.             | standalone answer                   |
| No entendí nada de la explicación.          | I didn't understand any of the explanation. | *no* + *nada*                    |
| Esto no tiene nada de malo.                 | There's nothing wrong with this.         | set phrase                          |
| No me molesta nada tu comentario.           | Your comment doesn't bother me at all.   | intensifying adverb                  |
