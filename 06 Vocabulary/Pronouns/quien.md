# quien

## Meaning

*Quien* (plural *quienes*) is a relative pronoun that refers only to people — "who"
or "whom." It's most common right after a preposition (*la persona con quien
hablé*, "the person I spoke with"); in other positions, [[que]] usually does the
same job more naturally (*la mujer que llegó*, not *la mujer quien llegó*, though
both are technically possible). Don't confuse *quien* (no accent) with *quién*
(accented), the separate interrogative word "who" used in questions (*¿Quién es?*,
"Who is it?").

*Quien* agrees only in number, not gender: *quien* for one person, *quienes* for
more than one, regardless of whether they're men or women.

## Examples

| Spanish                                       | English                                | Notes                          |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| El chico con quien hablé es de Cochabamba.          | The guy I spoke with is from Cochabamba.       | after a preposition                    |
| Mi tía, quien vive en La Paz, nos visitó.           | My aunt, who lives in La Paz, visited us.      | non-restrictive clause                 |
| Las personas a quienes invité no pudieron venir.     | The people I invited couldn't come.            | after a preposition, personal *a*, plural |
| Quien mucho abarca, poco aprieta.                    | Whoever tries to do too much accomplishes little. | proverb, no explicit antecedent    |
| Ese es el profesor de quien te hablé.                | That's the teacher I told you about.           | after a preposition                    |
