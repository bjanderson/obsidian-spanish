# ese / esa

## Meaning

*Ese* and *esa* are demonstratives — "that" — pointing to something farther
from the speaker but often near the listener: *ese* masculine, *esa* feminine.
Spanish has a three-way distance system: [[este-esta|este/esta]] ("this," near
the speaker), *ese/esa* ("that," near the listener or a middle distance), and
*aquel/aquella* ("that," far from both — not yet tracked here).

Like [[este-esta|este/esta]], both forms work as an adjective before a noun
(*ese carro*, "that car") and, on their own, as a pronoun (*Prefiero ese*, "I
prefer that one"). The neuter form *eso* is used when there's no specific noun
in mind: *Eso no me gusta* ("I don't like that").

## Full Paradigm

| Gender / Number   | Form   |
| -------------------- | -------- |
| Masculine singular      | ese      |
| Feminine singular        | esa      |
| Masculine plural           | esos     |
| Feminine plural              | esas     |
| Neuter (no specific noun)      | eso      |

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                          | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Ese perro no es mío.                        | That dog isn't mine.                   | adjective, before a noun           |
| ¿Me pasas esa taza, por favor?              | Can you pass me that cup, please?      | adjective, feminine, question      |
| No me gustan esos zapatos.                  | I don't like those shoes.              | masculine plural                   |
| Esas fotos son de mi viaje a Sucre.         | Those photos are from my trip to Sucre. | feminine plural                    |
| Eso no tiene sentido.                       | That doesn't make sense.               | neuter pronoun                     |
