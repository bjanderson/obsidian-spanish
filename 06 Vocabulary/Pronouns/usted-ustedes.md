# usted / ustedes

## Meaning

*Usted* (singular) and *ustedes* (plural) are the formal/polite second-person
pronouns. They're conjugated like third person — the same verb forms as
[[él]]/[[ella]] and [[ellos]]/[[ellas]] — even though they mean "you":
*¿Cómo está usted?* uses *está*, not *estás*.

In neutral Latin American Spanish (no *vosotros*), *ustedes* also covers
plural "you all" in informal settings, not just formal ones — there's no
separate informal plural. *Usted* itself signals respect or distance, common
with strangers, elders, or formal situations. Regional note: in Bolivia,
*usted* is often used more broadly than elsewhere, including between family
members and close friends in some regions, not strictly reserved for formal
distance.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| ¿Cómo está usted, señora García?            | How are you, Mrs. García?                | singular, question, formal          |
| Ustedes pueden pasar cuando quieran.        | You all can come in whenever you like.   | plural                             |
| ¿Usted sabe dónde queda la farmacia?        | Do you know where the pharmacy is?       | singular, question                  |
| No sabíamos que ustedes ya habían llegado.  | We didn't know you all had already arrived. | plural, negation                 |
| Con gusto los ayudo a ustedes.              | I'm happy to help you all.               | after preposition, plural           |
