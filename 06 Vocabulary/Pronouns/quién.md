# quién

## Meaning

*Quién* (accented, plural *quiénes*) is the interrogative "who" — used in
direct and indirect questions (*¿Quién es?*, "Who is it?"; *No sé quién
llamó*, "I don't know who called"). It agrees only in number, not gender:
*quién* for one person, *quiénes* for more than one.

Don't confuse it with unaccented [[quien]], the relative pronoun that
means the same in English ("who/whom") but works differently — it
introduces a clause referring back to a person already mentioned, usually
right after a preposition (*la persona con quien hablé*, "the person I
spoke with"), rather than asking a question. The accent is the only thing
that tells them apart in writing. This puts *quién* in the same
interrogative cluster as [[cómo|cómo]] and
[[cuánto-cuánta-cuántos-cuántas|cuánto]] — all three always carry the
accent when used to ask or exclaim.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| ¿Quién es esa mujer?                           | Who is that woman?                          | direct question, singular           |
| No sé quiénes vienen a la fiesta.              | I don't know who's coming to the party.     | indirect question, plural           |
| ¿A quién viste en el mercado?                  | Who did you see at the market?              | direct question, personal *a*        |
| ¿Con quién hablaste?                           | Who did you talk to?                        | direct question, after preposition   |
| Dime quién llamó.                              | Tell me who called.                         | indirect question                   |
