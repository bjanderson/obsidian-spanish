## Explanation

*Gustar* ("to like") works backward from how English speakers expect. In English,
the person doing the liking is the subject: "I like coffee." In Spanish, the thing
being liked is the grammatical subject, and the person is expressed with an indirect
object pronoun. *Me gusta el café* literally means "Coffee is pleasing to me."

Once this backward logic clicks, *gustar* becomes simple — you just need the right
indirect object pronoun and the right form of *gustar*.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Indirect object pronouns used with _gustar_:**

| Pronoun | Meaning              |
| --------- | ---------------------- |
| me        | to me                  |
| te        | to you                 |
| le        | to him/her/you (formal) |
| nos       | to us                  |
| les       | to them/you all        |

- Formula: **[indirect pronoun] + gusta/gustan + [thing(s) liked]**
- Use **gusta** when the thing liked is singular, or when it's an infinitive (an
  activity): *Me gusta el café.* / *Me gusta bailar.*
- Use **gustan** when the thing liked is plural: *Me gustan los libros.*
- Even with more than one activity listed, *gusta* stays singular, because infinitives
  are always treated as singular: *Me gusta correr, nadar y esquiar.*
- When using *gustar* with a noun, always include the definite article (*el, la, los,
  las*): *Me gusta el pan*, not *Me gusta pan*.
- The pronoun is sometimes paired with an optional clarifying phrase (*a Juan*, *a
  María*) — this looks redundant but is normal and often needed for clarity, since
  *le* alone could mean "him," "her," or formal "you": *A Juan le gusta el pan.*

**Short answers:**

- *¿Te gusta el pan?* → *Sí, me gusta.*
- *¿Le gustan las fresas a María?* → *Sí, le gustan.*

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                              | Notes                              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Me gusta el chocolate.                    | I like chocolate.                          | singular noun, *me*                 |
| ¿Te gustan los animales?                  | Do you like animals?                       | plural noun, *te*, question         |
| A Juan le gusta el fútbol.                | Juan likes soccer.                         | clarifying phrase + *le*            |
| Nos gusta viajar en verano.               | We like to travel in the summer.           | infinitive, always singular *gusta* |
| A mis papás les gustan las películas de acción. | My parents like action movies.       | clarifying phrase + *les*, plural   |
| No me gusta el ruido.                     | I don't like noise.                        | negation before the pronoun         |

## Common Mistakes

- Matching *gusta/gustan* to the person instead of the thing liked: *gustar* agrees
  with what's liked, never with *me/te/le/nos/les*.
- Dropping the definite article: *Me gusta pan* is wrong — it must be *Me gusta el
  pan*.
- Using *gustan* for a list of activities: *Me gustan correr y nadar* is wrong —
  infinitives always take singular *gusta*.
- Treating the *a Juan* / *a María* clarifier as optional filler to skip — it often
  carries essential information about who *le* refers to.
