## Explanation

Once you know *gustar*, several other high-frequency verbs follow the exact same
pattern — same indirect object pronouns (*me, te, le, nos, les*), same singular/plural
agreement with whatever follows. Only the verb itself changes, along with the feeling
or situation it describes.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Verbs that work like _gustar_:**

| Verb        | Meaning                        | Singular form | Plural form |
| ------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| encantar      | to love, to be delighted by        | encanta          | encantan       |
| doler         | to hurt, to be painful              | duele            | duelen         |
| importar      | to matter, to care about            | importa          | importan       |
| faltar        | to lack, to be missing/needed       | falta            | faltan         |

- Same formula as *gustar*: **[indirect pronoun] + verb form + [thing(s)]**.
- *Encantar* is a stronger version of *gustar* — "to love" rather than just "to like":
  *Me encanta el chocolate* (I love chocolate), *Me encantan los perros* (I love dogs).
- *Doler* is most often used with parts of the body: *Me duele la cabeza* (My head
  hurts / I have a headache), *Me duelen los pies* (My feet hurt).
- *Importar* expresses what matters to someone: *No me importa el precio* (The price
  doesn't matter to me / I don't care about the price).
- *Faltar* expresses something missing or still needed: *Me falta dinero* (I'm short
  on money / I need money), *Nos faltan dos sillas* (We're missing two chairs).
- All four still take the optional clarifying phrase (*a mí, a Juan, a mis papás*) for
  emphasis or to remove ambiguity, exactly like *gustar*.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                            | Notes                              |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Me encanta esta canción.                     | I love this song.                       | singular, *encantar*                |
| A mis papás les encantan los viajes.         | My parents love traveling.              | plural, clarifying phrase           |
| Me duele la garganta.                        | My throat hurts.                        | singular, *doler* + body part       |
| ¿Te duelen los ojos?                         | Do your eyes hurt?                      | plural, question                    |
| No me importa la opinión de los demás.       | Other people's opinions don't matter to me. | singular, *importar*, negation  |
| Nos falta tiempo para terminar el proyecto.  | We're short on time to finish the project. | singular, *faltar*               |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting that these verbs agree with what follows them, not with the person: *Me
  duele las piernas* is wrong — it must be *Me duelen las piernas* (plural *piernas*
  needs *duelen*).
- Confusing *faltar* (to be missing/lacking) with *perder* (to lose) — *faltar* is
  about something not being there, not about losing something you once had.
- Using *importar* only in the negative out of habit — it works fine in the
  affirmative too: *Me importa mucho tu opinión* (Your opinion matters a lot to me).
