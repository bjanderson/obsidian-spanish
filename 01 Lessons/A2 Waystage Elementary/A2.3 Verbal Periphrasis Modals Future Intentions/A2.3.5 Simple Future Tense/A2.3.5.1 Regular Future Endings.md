## Explanation

You already know two ways to talk about the future: *ir a* + infinitive (*voy a
estudiar*) and the present tense with a time word (*trabajo mañana*). Spanish also has
a dedicated future tense, often called the simple future — a single conjugated verb
that means "will do something" on its own, no helper verb needed.

The good news: it is one of the easiest tenses to build. You don't strip off *-ar*,
*-er*, or *-ir* the way you do for most tenses. You just add the ending straight onto
the whole infinitive, and every verb type — *-ar*, *-er*, *-ir* — uses the exact same
set of endings.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Future endings (added to the full infinitive):**

| Subject             | Ending | Note                 |
| -------------------- | ------ | ---------------------- |
| yo                    | -é     | accented              |
| tú                    | -ás    | accented              |
| él / ella / usted     | -á     | accented              |
| nosotros / nosotras   | -emos  | no accent             |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | -án  | accented              |

- The infinitive stays whole — nothing is removed. *Hablar* → *hablaré*, *hablarás*,
  *hablará*, *hablaremos*, *hablarán*.
- Same endings for all three verb types: *hablar* (-ar), *comer* (-er), *vivir*
  (-ir) — compare *hablaré*, *comeré*, *viviré*.
- Every form is accented except *nosotros*. The accent marks the stress, so
  *estudiarás* is stressed es-tu-dia-RÁS, not es-TU-dia-ras.
- Because the ending itself already shows the subject, the subject pronoun is
  usually dropped: *Hablaré con María* (not *Yo hablaré con María*), unless you need
  it for clarity or contrast.
- Skip this tense for polite requests. English uses "will you...?" to ask nicely, but
  Spanish uses the present tense instead: *¿Me traes otra servilleta, por favor?*, not
  a future-tense version. Using the future here can sound odd or overly formal.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                             | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Hablaré con mi jefe mañana.             | I will talk to my boss tomorrow.       | -ar verb, yo                    |
| ¿Comerás en casa esta noche?            | Will you eat at home tonight?          | -er verb, tú, question           |
| Ella vivirá en Bogotá el próximo año.   | She will live in Bogotá next year.     | -ir verb, ella                  |
| Estudiaremos para el examen el sábado.  | We will study for the exam Saturday.   | -ar verb, nosotros, no accent    |
| Mis primos viajarán a Perú en julio.    | My cousins will travel to Peru in July. | -ar verb, ellos                 |
| No trabajaré este fin de semana.        | I won't work this weekend.             | -ar verb, yo, negation           |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the accent mark, especially on *tú* and *él/ella/usted* forms: *hablaras*
  looks like a different tense entirely (imperfect subjunctive) — it must be
  *hablarás*.
- Cutting the infinitive ending before adding the future ending, the way you do for
  the present tense: *habl* + *aré* is wrong. Keep the whole infinitive: *hablar* +
  *é* = *hablaré*.
- Adding an accent to *nosotros*: *hablarémos* is wrong — that form is the only one
  without a written accent, *hablaremos*.
- Using the future tense to make a polite request, copying the English pattern —
  Spanish speakers use the present tense for that instead.
