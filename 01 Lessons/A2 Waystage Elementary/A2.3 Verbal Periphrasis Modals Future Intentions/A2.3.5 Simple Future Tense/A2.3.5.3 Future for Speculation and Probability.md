## Explanation

So far you've used the future tense to talk about things that will actually happen
later — [[A2.3.5.1 Regular Future Endings|regular]] and
[[A2.3.5.2 Irregular Future Stems|irregular]] verbs alike. Spanish also uses this same
tense for something that has nothing to do with the future at all: guessing about the
present.

When you're not sure about something happening right now — who's at the door, how old
someone is, why a friend hasn't texted back — Spanish speakers often reach for the
future tense instead of saying "I think" or "probably." It works like English "must
be" or "I wonder" or "could be." The verb form doesn't change; only how you read it
does, based on context.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- This use is called the future of probability (or conjecture) — the same
  conjugations you already know, used to speculate about the present instead of
  stating a future fact.
- Most common with *ser* and *estar*, since guessing about identity or location or
  state is the most natural use: *¿Quién será?* (Who could that be?), *¿Dónde estará
  Juan?* (Where could Juan be?).
- Also common in questions triggered by something unexpected — a knock at the door, an
  unknown number calling, a strange noise.
- Context tells you which meaning is intended — there's no separate grammar marker.
  *Serán las tres* could mean "It will be three o'clock" (a real future event) or "It's
  probably three o'clock" (a guess), depending on what's being talked about.
- This pattern shows up constantly in casual spoken Spanish. Recognizing it when you
  hear it matters more at this stage than producing it yourself — but it's simple to
  build once you're comfortable with the regular and irregular future forms.
- A close cousin you may hear as an alternative: *deber de* + infinitive (*Debe de ser
  las tres*, "It must be three"), which expresses the same kind of guess without the
  future tense.

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                                | Notes                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| ¿Quién será a esta hora?                 | Who could that be at this hour?         | *ser*, unexpected knock/call     |
| Estará en la oficina todavía.            | She's probably still at the office.     | *estar*, guessing location       |
| Tendrán unos treinta años.               | They're probably around thirty.         | *tener*, guessing age            |
| ¿Qué hora será?                          | What time could it be?                  | *ser*, no clock in sight         |
| No contesta — estará dormido.            | He's not answering — he's probably asleep. | *estar*, explaining silence   |
| ¿Será verdad lo que dice?                | Could what he's saying be true?         | *ser*, doubting a claim          |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming every future-tense sentence refers to a later event — context decides
  whether it's a real future plan or a present-tense guess.
- Overusing this pattern in writing or formal speech. It's a natural, common structure
  in conversation, but *creo que* (I think) or *probablemente* (probably) are more
  common choices outside casual speech.
- Translating "must be" too literally with *deber* + infinitive when the future of
  probability says the same thing more naturally: *Serán las tres* reads more like
  natural spoken Spanish than *Deben de ser las tres* in casual conversation.
- Missing the cue words that signal probability — a question mark, an unanswered
  situation, or a context where the event obviously can't be a real future plan (e.g.
  guessing someone's current age).
