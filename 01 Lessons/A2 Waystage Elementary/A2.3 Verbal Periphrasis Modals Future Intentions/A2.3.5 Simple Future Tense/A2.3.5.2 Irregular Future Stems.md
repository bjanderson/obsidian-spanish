## Explanation

Most verbs build the future tense on the full infinitive — that's what you learned in
[[A2.3.5.1 Regular Future Endings]]. A small set of common, high-frequency verbs breaks
that pattern: instead of the infinitive, they use a shortened or altered stem. The
endings themselves never change — still *-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -án* — only the piece you
attach them to is different.

There are about a dozen of these irregular verbs, and they group into a few easy-to-spot
patterns, so you don't have to memorize each one as a total one-off.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Group 1 — drop the vowel from the infinitive ending:**

| Verb   | Meaning     | Stem   | Example (yo) |
| ------- | ----------- | ------- | -------------- |
| poder   | to be able to | podr-  | podré          |
| querer  | to want     | querr- | querré         |
| saber   | to know     | sabr-  | sabré          |

**Group 2 — the *-e* or *-i* is replaced with a *d*:**

| Verb   | Meaning       | Stem   | Example (yo) |
| ------- | ------------- | ------- | -------------- |
| tener   | to have       | tendr-  | tendré         |
| poner   | to put        | pondr-  | pondré         |
| salir   | to leave      | saldr-  | saldré         |
| venir   | to come       | vendr-  | vendré         |

**Group 3 — the stem is shortened irregularly:**

| Verb   | Meaning     | Stem  | Example (yo) |
| ------- | ----------- | ------ | -------------- |
| hacer   | to do/make  | har-   | haré           |
| decir   | to say/tell | dir-   | diré           |

- The endings stay identical to the regular pattern: *-é, -ás, -á, -emos, -án*. Only
  the stem changes — the ending set never does.
- Every accented form still gets its accent: *tendré, tendrás, tendrá, tendrán* — only
  *tendremos* skips the accent, same rule as regular verbs.
- Verbs built on an irregular verb usually inherit the same irregularity: *tener* →
  *tendré*, and *mantener* (to maintain) → *mantendré*; *poner* → *pondré*, and
  *suponer* (to suppose) → *supondré*.
- *Haber*, the auxiliary "to have," is also irregular here (*habré*) — you'll mostly
  meet it later in compound tenses, but it follows the same pattern as *hacer*.

## Examples

| Spanish                                  | English                            | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Tendré que llamar al médico.                | I will have to call the doctor.          | *tener* → *tendr-*, personal *a* not needed (infinitive object) |
| ¿Podrás venir a la fiesta?                  | Will you be able to come to the party?   | *poder* → *podr-*, tú        |
| Ella pondrá la mesa antes de cenar.         | She will set the table before dinner.    | *poner* → *pondr-*           |
| Saldremos del trabajo a las seis.           | We will leave work at six.               | *salir* → *saldr-*, *del*    |
| Mis amigos vendrán a la fiesta el sábado.   | My friends will come to the party Saturday. | *venir* → *vendr-*        |
| No sabré la respuesta hasta mañana.         | I won't know the answer until tomorrow.  | *saber* → *sabr-*, negation  |
| ¿Qué harás este verano?                     | What will you do this summer?            | *hacer* → *har-*, tú         |
| Él dirá la verdad tarde o temprano.         | He will tell the truth sooner or later.  | *decir* → *dir-*             |

## Common Mistakes

- Adding the endings to the full infinitive out of habit: *tenerá* and *ponerá* are
  wrong — the irregular stem replaces the infinitive ending, giving *tendrá* and
  *pondrá*.
- Mixing up which letter gets inserted for Group 2 verbs: it's always a *d* — *tener*
  → *tendr-*, not *tenr-* or *tenir-*.
- Forgetting that *decir* and *hacer* shorten all the way down to *dir-* and *har-* —
  learners sometimes guess *decirá* or *hacerá*, which don't exist.
- Dropping the accent on the irregular forms just because the stem looks unusual: the
  accent rule (every form but *nosotros*) still applies — *tendrás*, not *tendras*.
