## Explanation

You already know how to build the imperfect. Now the real question: when do
you actually reach for it? Four situations come up constantly, and they all
share the same idea — none of them has a clear "this started, this ended"
edge to it.

**Habits and routines.** Anything you used to do regularly, without a fixed
number of times, calls for the imperfect: *Caminaba al trabajo* ("I used to
walk to work"). Compare that to *Caminé al trabajo* (preterite) — one
specific walk, done and finished.

**Descriptions.** Setting a scene — what something or someone looked like,
what the weather was like, how a place felt — uses the imperfect: *La casa
era grande y tenía un jardín* ("The house was big and had a garden").

**Age.** Spanish always uses the imperfect of *tener* to say how old someone
was at a point in the past: *Tenía diez años cuando nos mudamos* ("I was ten
years old when we moved"). This is a rule, not just a preference — age in the
past is always imperfect.

**Telling time in the past.** Just like age, past clock time always uses the
imperfect of *ser*: *Eran las tres de la tarde* ("It was three in the
afternoon"). Even though a specific moment sounds like it should be
preterite, time-telling is a fixed imperfect construction.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Use                     | Pattern                          | Example                                   |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Habits/routines           | imperfect + optional frequency word | *Comía allí todos los días.*                 |
| Descriptions               | imperfect of *ser*/*estar*/adjectives | *El cielo estaba nublado.*                 |
| Age                         | *tener* in the imperfect            | *Tenía veinte años.*                        |
| Time                        | *ser* in the imperfect              | *Era la una. / Eran las cinco.*             |

- Age and time-telling in the past are **always** the imperfect — this isn't
  a stylistic choice, it's the standard rule.
- *Ser* for time uses *era* (one o'clock) or *eran* (any other hour) —
  singular vs. plural agrees with the number, not the subject.
- Habitual/repeated actions use the imperfect even without an explicit
  frequency word like *siempre* or *todos los días* — the "no clear edge"
  feeling is what matters, not the presence of a specific word.

## Examples

| Spanish                                   | English                              | Notes                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| De niño, comía cereal todos los días.        | As a kid, I used to eat cereal every day. | habit                     |
| Mi abuela era muy cariñosa.                  | My grandmother was very affectionate.  | description                 |
| Tenía quince años cuando aprendí a manejar.  | I was fifteen when I learned to drive. | age                          |
| Eran las diez de la noche cuando llegamos.   | It was ten at night when we arrived.   | time                         |
| Hacía mucho calor ese verano.                | It was very hot that summer.           | weather description          |
| Vivíamos cerca del mar en ese entonces.      | We used to live near the sea back then. | habit/state                |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the preterite for age or time — *tuve* diez años or *fue* la una are
  both wrong; age and time always take the imperfect.
- Switching to the preterite mid-description just because the sentence is
  long — a description stays in the imperfect the whole way through, even
  across several clauses.
- Forgetting that *eran* (plural) is needed for any hour except one o'clock —
  *era la una*, but *eran las dos, eran las tres...*
