## Explanation

You already have one past tense — the preterite — for actions that are
finished and done. The imperfect is Spanish's other past tense, and it works
differently: it describes what was ongoing, repeated, or "in the background"
in the past, without marking a clear beginning or end. Think of it as the
tense for "used to" and "was/were -ing."

Regular *-AR* verbs build the imperfect with the ending *-aba*. Take *hablar*:
drop the *-ar*, and add *-aba* for *yo* (*hablaba*, "I used to speak" or "I
was speaking"), *-abas* for *tú* (*hablabas*), and so on. Every regular *-AR*
verb follows this exact pattern — no exceptions, no stem changes.

Regular *-ER* and *-IR* verbs share a single pattern built on *-ía*. Take
*comer*: drop the *-er*, add *-ía* for *yo* (*comía*, "I used to eat" or "I
was eating"). *Vivir* works the same way: *vivía*, *vivías*... One thing to
notice: the *nosotros* form for *-ER*/*-IR* verbs (*comíamos*, *vivíamos*)
carries a written accent on the *í* — that accent shows up in every form of
the *-ía* pattern, not just *nosotros*.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject             | *-AR* Ending | *hablar*   | *-ER*/*-IR* Ending | *comer*  | *vivir*  |
| -------------------- | ------------- | ---------- | -------------------- | -------- | -------- |
| yo                    | -aba          | hablaba    | -ía                   | comía    | vivía    |
| tú                    | -abas         | hablabas   | -ías                  | comías   | vivías   |
| él / ella / usted     | -aba          | hablaba    | -ía                   | comía    | vivía    |
| nosotros              | -ábamos       | hablábamos | -íamos                | comíamos | vivíamos |
| ellos / ustedes       | -aban         | hablaban   | -ían                  | comían   | vivían   |

- The *-AR* imperfect ending is *-aba* (with variations); every form except
  *nosotros* carries no written accent, but *nosotros* (*-ábamos*) does.
- The *-ER*/*-IR* imperfect ending is *-ía* (with variations); **every** form
  carries a written accent on the *í*, including *nosotros* (*-íamos*).
- Unlike the preterite, *yo* and *él/ella/usted* are identical in the
  imperfect — *hablaba* can mean "I used to speak" or "he/she used to speak,"
  so context or a subject pronoun clarifies which.
- There are no stem changes anywhere in the regular imperfect — the stem
  never shifts, no matter the subject.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| De niño, hablaba mucho con mis abuelos.   | As a kid, I used to talk a lot with my grandparents. | *hablar*, *yo*         |
| ¿Vivías cerca de la escuela?              | Did you use to live near the school?   | *vivir*, *tú*              |
| Ella siempre comía temprano.              | She always used to eat early.          | *comer*, *ella*            |
| Jugábamos en ese parque.                  | We used to play in that park.          | *jugar*, *nosotros*        |
| Mis padres trabajaban en el centro.       | My parents used to work downtown.      | *trabajar*, *ellos*        |
| Cuando era niño, escribía cartas.         | When I was a kid, I used to write letters. | *escribir*, *yo*        |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the accent on the *-ía* pattern's *nosotros* form — it's
  *comíamos*, not *comiamos*.
- Confusing *yo* and *él/ella/usted*, since both look identical (*hablaba*) —
  use a pronoun or context to clarify who's meant.
- Mixing up the imperfect with the preterite for a habitual or ongoing past
  action — "I used to eat there every day" calls for the imperfect
  (*comía*), not the preterite (*comí*).
