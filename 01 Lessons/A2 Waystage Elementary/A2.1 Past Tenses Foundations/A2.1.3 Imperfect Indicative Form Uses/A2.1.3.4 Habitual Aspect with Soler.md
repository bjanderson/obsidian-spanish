## Explanation

You already know two ways to talk about habits: the plain present tense
(*camino al trabajo*) and the imperfect (*caminaba al trabajo*). *Soler* gives
you a third, more explicit option — a verb that means "to usually do" or "to
used to do," built specifically to point at a habit.

*Soler* is a stem-changing verb (*o → ue*), and it's always followed directly
by an infinitive — never conjugated on its own as the main action. In the
present tense, it means "usually": *Suelo caminar al trabajo* ("I usually
walk to work"). In the imperfect, it means "used to": *Solía caminar al
trabajo* ("I used to walk to work").

The advantage of *soler* is that it does the "habitual" work for you, so the
verb after it stays in the simple infinitive — no need to conjugate a second
verb into the imperfect yourself. *Solía estudiar español* says the same
thing as *Estudiaba español (habitualmente)*, but more compactly.

One clarity tip: in the present tense, the *yo* form (*suelo*) is
unambiguous, so you can safely drop the pronoun. In the imperfect, *solía* is
identical for *yo*, *él*, *ella*, and *usted* — so include a subject when it
isn't already clear from context.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject             | Present (*usually*) | Imperfect (*used to*) |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| yo                    | suelo                   | solía                     |
| tú                    | sueles                  | solías                    |
| él / ella / usted     | suele                    | solía                     |
| nosotros              | solemos                 | solíamos                  |
| ellos / ustedes       | suelen                  | solían                    |

- *Soler* is always followed by an infinitive: *suelo* + *comer*, *solía* +
  *comer* — never a conjugated second verb.
- Present *soler* = "usually [does]"; imperfect *soler* = "used to [do]."
- The stem change (*o → ue*) affects *yo, tú, él/ella/usted, ellos/ustedes*
  in the present, but *nosotros* keeps the regular stem (*solemos*) — same
  pattern as other *o → ue* verbs like *poder*.
- The imperfect forms don't stem-change at all (*solía, solías...*) — stem
  changes only apply to the present tense.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Suelo desayunar antes de las siete.       | I usually have breakfast before seven. | present *soler*, *yo*         |
| Mi hermano suele levantarse temprano.     | My brother usually gets up early.      | present *soler*, *él*         |
| Solía leer todas las noches.              | I used to read every night.            | imperfect *soler*, *yo*       |
| Juan solía tocar la guitarra.             | Juan used to play the guitar.          | imperfect *soler*, *él*       |
| Solemos cocinar en casa los domingos.     | We usually cook at home on Sundays.    | present *soler*, *nosotros*   |
| Mis padres solían viajar mucho.           | My parents used to travel a lot.       | imperfect *soler*, *ellos*    |

## Common Mistakes

- Conjugating the verb after *soler* instead of leaving it as an infinitive —
  *suelo como* is wrong; it's *suelo comer*.
- Dropping the subject pronoun in the imperfect when the subject isn't
  already clear — *solía* alone could mean *yo*, *él*, *ella*, or *usted*.
- Forgetting the *o → ue* stem change in the present tense — it's *suelo*,
  not *solo* (which is also a real word meaning "alone").
