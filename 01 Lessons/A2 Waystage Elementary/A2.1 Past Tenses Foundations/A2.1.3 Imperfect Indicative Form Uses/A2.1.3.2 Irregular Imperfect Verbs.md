## Explanation

Good news: out of every verb in Spanish, only three break the regular
imperfect pattern. Everything else — even verbs that are wildly irregular in
the preterite, like *tener* or *hacer* — conjugates completely regularly in
the imperfect. The three exceptions are *ser*, *ir*, and *ver*.

*Ser* (to be) builds its imperfect on the stem *er-*: *era*, *eras*, *era*,
*éramos*, *eran*. *Ir* (to go) builds on *ib-*: *iba*, *ibas*, *iba*,
*íbamos*, *iban*. Neither one looks anything like a regular *-aba*/*-ía*
pattern, so they simply have to be memorized as their own short list.

*Ver* (to see) is irregular in a smaller way: it keeps the *e* from the
infinitive that a regular *-er* verb would normally drop, giving you *veía*
instead of the expected *vía*. Once you have that irregular stem *ve-*, the
rest of the endings are completely regular *-ía* endings: *veía*, *veías*,
*veíamos*...

Because *ser*, *ir*, and *ver* are so common, you'll hear these forms
constantly — especially *era* and *había* (from *haber*, which is regular)
when someone is describing what things used to be like.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject             | *ser*  | *ir*    | *ver*   |
| -------------------- | ------ | ------- | ------- |
| yo                    | era    | iba     | veía    |
| tú                    | eras   | ibas    | veías   |
| él / ella / usted     | era    | iba     | veía    |
| nosotros              | éramos | íbamos  | veíamos |
| ellos / ustedes       | eran   | iban    | veían   |

- These are the **only three** irregular verbs in the entire imperfect tense
  — every other verb, regular or not in other tenses, follows the standard
  *-aba*/*-ía* pattern.
- *Ser* and *ir* each build on their own unique stem (*er-*, *ib-*) with no
  connection to the regular pattern.
- *Ver* keeps the infinitive's *e* (*ve-*) and then adds the completely
  regular *-ía* endings.
- As with the regular imperfect, *yo* and *él/ella/usted* are identical in
  all three verbs (*era*, *iba*, *veía*).

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                             | Notes                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| De joven, era muy tímido.                 | As a young person, I was very shy.     | *ser*, *yo*                  |
| ¿Ibas a la playa cada verano?             | Did you use to go to the beach every summer? | *ir*, *tú*             |
| Ella veía esa serie todas las noches.     | She used to watch that show every night. | *ver*, *ella*              |
| Éramos vecinos hace muchos años.          | We were neighbors many years ago.      | *ser*, *nosotros*            |
| Mis abuelos iban a misa los domingos.     | My grandparents used to go to mass on Sundays. | *ir*, *ellos*          |
| Cuando era niña, veía dibujos animados.   | When I was a girl, I used to watch cartoons. | *ser* + *ver*, *yo*      |

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to build *ser* or *ir* from a regular stem — there's no shortcut;
  *era* and *iba* just have to be memorized.
- Writing *vía* instead of *veía* — the *e* from the infinitive doesn't drop
  the way it would in a fully regular verb.
- Assuming other common irregular-preterite verbs (*tener*, *hacer*, *estar*)
  are also irregular in the imperfect — they're not; only *ser*, *ir*, and
  *ver* break the pattern.
