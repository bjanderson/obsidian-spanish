## Explanation

You now know how to build both past tenses. The real skill is choosing
between them — and there's a simple rule of thumb that gets you there most of
the time: ask whether the action is the *main event* or the *background*.

The preterite is for the main events — the things that happened, one after
another, moving the story forward. *Fui a la tienda* ("I went to the store")
is a single, complete action with a clear start and end. *María se mudó a
México hace cinco años* ("Maria moved to Mexico five years ago") — one
finished move, done.

The imperfect is for the background — the scenery, the ongoing state, the
condition that was already true while the main events happened. *Juan estaba
en el ejército cuando se casó con María* ("Juan was in the Army when he
married Maria") — being in the Army is the background; the wedding is the
main event, so it takes the preterite.

A good way to picture it: the imperfect paints the scene (what was going on,
what things were like), and the preterite is the action that happens on top
of that scene. *Hacía frío y el cielo parecía que iba a nevar* ("It was cold
and the sky looked like it was going to snow") — that's pure scene-setting,
all imperfect, because none of it is a completed event with an endpoint.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Question to Ask                                  | Answer → Tense |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Is this a specific event that happened and finished? | Preterite         |
| Is this the background/scene/ongoing condition?      | Imperfect         |
| Is this a habit or repeated action with no clear end? | Imperfect        |
| Does it move the story forward?                       | Preterite        |

- Preterite = foreground action, imperfect = background scene. Most
  narration mixes both in the same story.
- A repeated or habitual past action (with no specific count of times) is
  imperfect, even if each individual instance was itself completed.
- A single, one-time completed action — even a long one — is preterite:
  *Viví en España por diez años* ("I lived in Spain for ten years") is still
  preterite because it names one finished period, not an ongoing background
  state.

## Examples

| Spanish                                                | English                                       | Notes                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Fui a la tienda y compré pan.                              | I went to the store and bought bread.                | two sequential preterite events  |
| Hacía calor cuando salimos a caminar.                      | It was hot when we went out for a walk.              | imperfect scene + preterite event |
| Mi papá fumaba todos los días.                             | My father used to smoke every day.                   | habitual, imperfect              |
| El cielo estaba gris y la calle parecía vacía.             | The sky was gray and the street seemed empty.         | pure scene-setting, imperfect     |
| Ella llegó tarde porque el tráfico estaba muy pesado.      | She arrived late because traffic was very heavy.      | preterite event + imperfect cause |
| Vivimos en esa casa por cinco años.                        | We lived in that house for five years.                | one finished stretch, preterite   |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the preterite for background description — *El cielo estuvo gris*
  sounds like the sky's grayness itself was a completed event, not a scene;
  *estaba* is usually the natural choice for description.
- Using the imperfect for a single, finished action — *Iba a la tienda* (no
  clear end) is different from *Fui a la tienda* (one completed trip).
- Assuming a long duration always means imperfect — a specific, finished
  stretch of time (*viví diez años*) still takes the preterite.
