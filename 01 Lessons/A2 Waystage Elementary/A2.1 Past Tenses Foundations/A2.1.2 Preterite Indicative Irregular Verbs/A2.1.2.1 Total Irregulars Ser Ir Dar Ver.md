## Explanation

Some verbs don't just tweak the regular endings — they throw the whole pattern
out and build a completely new set of forms for the preterite. *Ser* (to be)
and *ir* (to go) are the most extreme example: in the preterite, they share
the exact same forms, and neither one looks anything like its infinitive.
*Fui* can mean "I was" or "I went" — only context tells you which.

- *Fui a la playa.* ("I went to the beach.") — *ir*, because it's followed by
  *a* + a destination.
- *Fui doctor por veinte años.* ("I was a doctor for twenty years.") — *ser*,
  because it's describing identity, not movement.

*Dar* (to give) and *ver* (to see) are irregular in a quieter way: they take
the regular *-er*/*-ir* endings, but without the written accents. *Di*, *dio*,
*vi*, *vio* — short words that don't need an accent mark to show where the
stress falls, unlike *comí* or *comió*.

None of these four verbs has a stem change the way *tener* or *hacer* does —
they're irregular in their own unique way, which is exactly why they get
memorized as their own small group.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject             | *ser* / *ir* | *dar*  | *ver*  |
| -------------------- | ------------ | ------ | ------ |
| yo                    | fui          | di     | vi     |
| tú                    | fuiste       | diste  | viste  |
| él / ella / usted     | fue          | dio    | vio    |
| nosotros              | fuimos       | dimos  | vimos  |
| ellos / ustedes       | fueron       | dieron | vieron |

- *Ser* and *ir* are identical in the preterite — use context (usually *a* +
  destination for *ir*) to tell them apart.
- *Dar* and *ver* use the regular *-er*/*-ir* endings, but **without** the
  written accent on *yo* and *él/ella/usted* — *di*, *dio*, *vi*, *vio*, not
  *dí*, *dió*.
- None of these four verbs have a stressed stem, so none of them need an
  accent mark anywhere in the preterite.

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Fui al mercado esta mañana.           | I went to the market this morning.  | *ir*, *yo*                  |
| Ella fue mi maestra en la escuela.    | She was my teacher at school.       | *ser*, *ella*                |
| ¿Le diste las llaves a tu hermano?    | Did you give the keys to your brother? | *dar*, *tú*, personal *a* |
| Vimos una película anoche.            | We saw a movie last night.          | *ver*, *nosotros*            |
| Mis padres fueron a Chile en verano.  | My parents went to Chile in the summer. | *ir*, *ellos*            |
| Le di un regalo a mi abuela.          | I gave a gift to my grandmother.    | *dar*, *yo*, personal *a*    |

## Common Mistakes

- Adding an accent to *di*, *dio*, *vi*, or *vio* — these short forms never
  carry a written accent.
- Not being able to tell *ser* apart from *ir* in the preterite — check for
  *a* + destination (*ir*) versus a description of identity or state (*ser*).
- Treating *dar* and *ver* like regular *-AR* verbs — they follow the
  *-ER*/*-IR* pattern (*di*, *diste*, *dio*...), not *-AR* endings.
