## Explanation

Some verbs are perfectly regular in the preterite — the conjugation pattern
doesn't change — but their spelling has to shift in the *yo* form to keep the
pronunciation consistent. This isn't a new grammar rule to learn; it's
Spanish's spelling system doing its job. These verbs all end in *-car*,
*-gar*, or *-zar*, and the change only ever shows up in one form: *yo*.

Take *buscar* (to search for). The regular *yo* ending would be *-é*, giving
you *busqué*... wait, actually *busqé*. But "c" followed by "é" would sound
like an "s," not the hard "k" sound the verb needs. So Spanish spells it
*busqué* instead — the *c* becomes *qu* to keep that hard sound.

The same logic applies to *-gar* verbs like *pagar*: *pagé* would soften the
"g" sound, so it becomes *pagué*, with a silent *u* inserted to keep the "g"
hard. And *-zar* verbs like *empezar* work the opposite direction — Spanish
spelling avoids *z* before *e*, so *empezé* becomes *empecé*, with *z*
swapping to *c*.

None of this changes how the word sounds — it only changes how it's spelled.
Every other form (*tú*, *él/ella/usted*, *nosotros*, *ellos/ustedes*) stays
completely regular.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Ending  | Spelling Change (*yo* only) | Example        | *yo* Form  |
| -------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------- | ---------- |
| -car     | c → qu                          | buscar (to search) | busqué     |
| -gar     | g → gu                          | pagar (to pay)      | pagué      |
| -zar     | z → c                            | empezar (to start)  | empecé     |

- The spelling change happens **only** in the *yo* form — every other subject
  conjugates completely regularly (*buscaste, buscó, buscamos, buscaron*).
- The change preserves the original sound of the infinitive; it never changes
  how the word is pronounced.
- This applies to any verb ending in *-car*, *-gar*, or *-zar* — not just the
  common examples above: *tocar → toqué*, *jugar → jugué*, *cruzar → crucé*.

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Busqué mis llaves por toda la casa.     | I looked for my keys all over the house. | *buscar*, *yo*             |
| Pagué la cuenta con tarjeta.            | I paid the bill with a card.           | *pagar*, *yo*                |
| Empecé un curso de cocina.              | I started a cooking class.             | *empezar*, *yo*              |
| Toqué la puerta tres veces.             | I knocked on the door three times.     | *tocar*, *yo*                |
| Jugué fútbol con mis amigos.            | I played soccer with my friends.       | *jugar*, *yo*                |
| Crucé la calle con cuidado.             | I crossed the street carefully.        | *cruzar*, *yo*                |

## Common Mistakes

- Writing *buscé* or *pagé* instead of *busqué*/*pagué* — the spelling change
  is required, not optional.
- Applying the spelling change to other subjects — *buscaste*, not *busquaste*;
  the change only happens in *yo*.
- Confusing this with a stem-changing or fully irregular verb — the
  pronunciation and meaning never change, only the spelling of one letter.
