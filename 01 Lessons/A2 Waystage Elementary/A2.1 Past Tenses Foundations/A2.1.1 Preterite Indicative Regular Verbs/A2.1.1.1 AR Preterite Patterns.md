## Explanation

Up to now you've only talked about the present — what happens regularly, what's
happening right now. The preterite is the tense that lets you say what happened
and finished: a single, completed event in the past. Think of it as the tense
that answers "what happened?"

For regular *-AR* verbs, the pattern is a new set of endings glued onto the same
stem you already know how to build. Take *hablar*, drop the *-ar*, and you're
left with the stem *habl-*. Add *-é* for *yo* and you get *hablé* ("I spoke").
Add *-aste* for *tú* and you get *hablaste* ("you spoke"). Learn these six
endings and you can put any regular *-AR* verb into the past.

One trap to watch for: the *nosotros* form of the preterite looks identical to
the *nosotros* form of the present. *Hablamos* means both "we speak" and "we
spoke" — context (usually a time word like *ayer*) tells you which one is meant.

Another detail worth noticing: the *yo* and *él/ella/usted* forms carry a written
accent — *hablé*, *habló* — and that accent is not optional. Drop it and the
word can be misread or just looks wrong on the page.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject             | Ending | *hablar*   |
| -------------------- | ------ | ---------- |
| yo                    | -é     | hablé      |
| tú                    | -aste  | hablaste   |
| él / ella / usted     | -ó     | habló      |
| nosotros              | -amos  | hablamos   |
| ellos / ustedes       | -aron  | hablaron   |

- Drop the *-ar* from the infinitive to get the stem, then add the preterite
  ending that matches the subject.
- These endings work for any regular *-AR* verb: *bailar → bailé, bailaste,
  bailó...*; *trabajar → trabajé, trabajaste, trabajó...*
- The *yo* and *él/ella/usted* endings (*-é*, *-ó*) always carry a written accent.
- The *nosotros* form (*-amos*) is identical in the present and the preterite —
  only context tells them apart.
- As always, drop the subject pronoun once the ending makes the subject clear.

## Examples

| Spanish                            | English                       | Notes                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Hablé con mi jefe ayer.             | I spoke with my boss yesterday. | *hablar*, *yo*          |
| ¿Bailaste en la fiesta?             | Did you dance at the party?     | *bailar*, *tú*          |
| Ella trabajó todo el fin de semana. | She worked all weekend.         | *trabajar*, *ella*      |
| Estudiamos para el examen anoche.   | We studied for the exam last night. | *estudiar*, *nosotros* |
| Mis padres viajaron a Perú.         | My parents traveled to Peru.    | *viajar*, *ellos*       |
| Compré un regalo para mi hermana.   | I bought a gift for my sister.  | *comprar*, *yo*         |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the accent on *hablé* or *habló* — without it, the word is
  misspelled and can be confused with a different form.
- Confusing the *nosotros* preterite (*hablamos* = "we spoke") with the
  *nosotros* present (*hablamos* = "we speak") — check the surrounding context
  or time marker.
- Using the present-tense *tú* ending *-as* instead of the preterite ending
  *-aste* — *hablas* ("you speak") is not the same as *hablaste* ("you spoke").
