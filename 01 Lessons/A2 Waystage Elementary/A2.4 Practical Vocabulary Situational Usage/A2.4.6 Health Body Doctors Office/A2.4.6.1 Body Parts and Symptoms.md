## Explanation

Talking about your body and how you feel is one of the most useful everyday skills
in Spanish — you'll need it at the doctor's office, the pharmacy, or just telling a
friend you're not feeling well.

Two things make Spanish body-part talk different from English. First, Spanish uses
the definite article (_el, la, los, las_) instead of a possessive (_my, your_) when
the owner is already clear from context: _Me duele la cabeza_ ("My head hurts"), not
_Me duele mi cabeza_. Second, to say something hurts, Spanish flips the sentence
around: the body part is the subject and the person is an indirect object, the same
pattern as _gustar_. _Me duele el estómago_ literally means "The stomach is hurting
to me."

For symptoms and illness beyond pain, you'll mostly reach for _tener_ (_tener
fiebre_, _tener tos_) or _estar_ (_estar enfermo/a_, _estar resfriado/a_).

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Use the definite article, not a possessive, for body parts: _Me lavo las manos_
  ("I wash my hands"), not _Me lavo mis manos_.
- _Doler_ ("to hurt") works like _gustar_ — conjugate it to match the body part, not
  the person: _Me duele el pie_ (singular) vs. _Me duelen los pies_ (plural).
- _Tener_ + noun for many symptoms: _tener fiebre_ (to have a fever), _tener tos_
  (to have a cough), _tener náuseas_ (to feel nauseous), _tener gripe_ (to have the
  flu).
- _Estar_ + adjective for states of being sick: _estar enfermo/a_ (to be sick),
  _estar resfriado/a_ (to have a cold), _estar mareado/a_ (to feel dizzy).
- Common body parts: _la cabeza_ (head), _el brazo_ (arm), _la pierna_ (leg), _la
  mano_ (hand), _el pie_ (foot), _el estómago_ (stomach), _la espalda_ (back), _la
  garganta_ (throat), _el oído_ (inner ear), _la nariz_ (nose), _el ojo_ (eye), _la
  boca_ (mouth), _el dedo_ (finger/toe), _la rodilla_ (knee), _el hombro_
  (shoulder), _el cuello_ (neck), _el pecho_ (chest).

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Me duele mucho la cabeza. | My head hurts a lot. | _Doler_ + definite article, not possessive. |
| ¿Te duelen los pies después de caminar tanto? | Do your feet hurt after walking so much? | Plural body part, plural verb form. |
| Tengo fiebre y me siento muy mal. | I have a fever and I feel very bad. | _Tener_ + symptom noun. |
| Mi hermano está resfriado desde el lunes. | My brother has had a cold since Monday. | _Estar_ + adjective for illness state. |
| Me corté el dedo cortando verduras. | I cut my finger cutting vegetables. | Reflexive + definite article for body-part actions. |
| Ella tiene tos y dolor de garganta. | She has a cough and a sore throat. | _Dolor de + body part_ as a fixed phrase. |
| Después de la carrera me duelen las piernas. | After the race, my legs hurt. | |
| No fui a trabajar porque estaba mareado. | I didn't go to work because I was dizzy. | |
