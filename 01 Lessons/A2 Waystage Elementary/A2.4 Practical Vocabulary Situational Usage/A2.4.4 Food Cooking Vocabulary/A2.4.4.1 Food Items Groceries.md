## Explanation

A1.5.3.2 covered how to order food at a restaurant and ask for the check, but that
lesson didn't touch the vocabulary you need before you ever sit down at a table —
the food itself. This lesson covers fruits, vegetables, meats, and the words you
need to shop for them at a market or grocery store.

Food vocabulary comes up constantly: reading a menu, following a recipe, telling a
host what you can't eat, or just doing your weekly shopping. Most of these words are
simple nouns with no special grammar attached — the main thing to get comfortable
with is their gender (for the right article) and a few regional differences in what
people call the same thing.

| ------------- | ------------ |

**Grocery-shopping vocabulary:**

| Spanish              | English                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ |
| el supermercado       | supermarket               |
| el mercado             | market                     |
| la lista de compras   | shopping list              |
| el carrito            | shopping cart              |
| la bolsa              | bag                        |
| el pasillo            | aisle                      |
| la caja               | checkout / cash register   |
| fresco / fresca       | fresh                      |
| maduro / madura       | ripe                       |
| congelado / congelada | frozen                     |

- *Plátano* means "banana" across most of Latin America, but in some countries
  (Venezuela, parts of the Caribbean) it specifically means the starchy cooking
  plantain, and *banana* or *guineo* is used for the sweet fruit. Listen for local
  usage.
- *Limón* means "lime" in most of Latin America — "lemon" is usually *limón amarillo*
  where the distinction matters. In Spain, *limón* means "lemon." This vault follows
  the Latin American usage.
- *Papa* (potato) is the neutral Latin American form; *patata* is Spain-specific and
  should be avoided here.
- Use *ir al supermercado* (with the *al* contraction) to say "go to the
  supermarket": *Voy al supermercado los sábados* (I go to the supermarket on
  Saturdays).

## Examples

| Spanish                                             | English                                     | Notes                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Necesito comprar manzanas y plátanos.                | I need to buy apples and bananas.             | fruits                          |
| ¿Tienen tomates frescos?                             | Do you have fresh tomatoes?                    | *fresco/a*                      |
| Voy al mercado a comprar verduras.                   | I'm going to the market to buy vegetables.      | *al mercado*                    |
| Compré pollo y pescado para la cena.                 | I bought chicken and fish for dinner.           | proteins, personal *a* not needed (no specific person) |
| Puse las bolsas en el carrito.                       | I put the bags in the cart.                     | shopping vocabulary             |
| El aguacate todavía no está maduro.                  | The avocado isn't ripe yet.                     | *maduro*, *estar* for a state    |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *patata* instead of *papa* — *patata* is the Spain-specific form; this vault
  uses *papa* for "potato" throughout Latin America.
- Assuming *plátano* always means "banana" — in some countries it means the starchy
  plantain instead, so pay attention to context or ask which fruit someone means.
- Forgetting gender on food nouns that don't end in the usual *-o/-a* pattern, like
  *el pez* vs. *el pescado* (a living fish vs. fish as food) — both are masculine, but
  learners often guess wrong on words like *la carne* (feminine, despite not ending in
  *-a* obviously) or *el pan*.
- Dropping the *al* contraction: *Voy a el supermercado* is wrong — it must be *Voy al
  supermercado*.
