## Explanation

Now that A2.4.4.1 gave you the ingredients, this lesson gives you the verbs for what
to do with them. These come up any time you follow a recipe, describe how a dish is
made, or explain what you're cooking to someone else. Most of these verbs are
regular *-ar* or *-ir* verbs, so once you know the infinitive, conjugating them
follows the patterns you already know.

Recipes and cooking instructions in Spanish very often use the impersonal *se*
(*se hierve el agua* — "the water is boiled" / "you boil the water") instead of a
command or a specific subject, because a recipe describes a general process, not one
particular person doing it. You'll see both the impersonal *se* form and the
*usted* command form (*hierva el agua*) depending on the source — this lesson covers
the verbs themselves so you can recognize and use them either way.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Core cooking verbs:**

| Spanish        | English                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------ |
| cocinar         | to cook (general)          |
| hervir          | to boil                    |
| freír           | to fry                     |
| picar           | to chop, to mince           |
| hornear         | to bake                    |
| asar            | to roast, to grill          |
| mezclar         | to mix                     |
| batir           | to beat, to whisk           |
| pelar           | to peel                    |
| cortar          | to cut                     |
| añadir          | to add                     |
| calentar        | to heat                    |
| revolver        | to stir                    |
| escurrir        | to drain                   |

- *Hervir* (e→ie) and *calentar* (e→ie) are stem-changing verbs: *hiervo, hierves,
  hierve, hervimos, hierven*. *Freír* (e→i) also stem-changes: *frío, fríes, fríe,
  freímos, fríen*.
- *Cocinar* means "to cook" in general (the activity); *cocer* is a narrower verb
  that specifically means "to boil/cook in water" and overlaps with *hervir* — this
  lesson sticks to *cocinar* and *hervir* since they're the more common everyday
  choices.
- The impersonal *se* pattern (*se + [verb in third person]*) is the normal way
  recipes describe steps: *Se pica la cebolla* (The onion is chopped / you chop the
  onion), *Se hornea por 30 minutos* (It's baked for 30 minutes). No subject pronoun
  is needed or used here — the *se* itself signals the impersonal "you/one" meaning.
- To give a direct instruction to one person, use the *usted* command: *hierva*,
  *pique*, *hornee*. (Command forms get their own dedicated lesson — this one is
  about recognizing and using the verbs themselves.)

## Examples

| Spanish                                          | English                                    | Notes                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Voy a hervir el agua para la pasta.                 | I'm going to boil the water for the pasta.        | *hervir*, *ir a*                    |
| Frío el pollo en aceite caliente.                   | I fry the chicken in hot oil.                     | *freír*, present tense              |
| Pica la cebolla antes de agregarla.                 | Chop the onion before adding it.                   | informal command, *picar*           |
| Se hornea el pastel por cuarenta minutos.            | The cake is baked for forty minutes.               | impersonal *se*                     |
| Mezclamos los huevos con el azúcar.                 | We mix the eggs with the sugar.                    | *mezclar*, *nosotros*               |
| Hay que pelar y cortar las papas primero.           | You have to peel and cut the potatoes first.        | *hay que* + infinitive              |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing *cocinar* (to cook, in general) with *cocer* (to boil/cook in water) —
  they overlap but aren't identical; when in doubt, *hervir* is the clearest word for
  "to boil."
- Forgetting the stem change in *hervir* and *freír*: *yo hervo* and *yo freo* are
  wrong — the correct forms are *yo hiervo* and *yo frío*.
- Adding an unnecessary subject pronoun to an impersonal *se* sentence, like
  *Ello se hornea* — the impersonal *se* already carries the meaning; no subject
  pronoun belongs there.
- Using *picar* only for "to chop" and missing its other common meaning ("to
  sting/itch," or "to snack" in some countries) — context makes the meaning clear,
  but it's worth knowing the word does double duty.
