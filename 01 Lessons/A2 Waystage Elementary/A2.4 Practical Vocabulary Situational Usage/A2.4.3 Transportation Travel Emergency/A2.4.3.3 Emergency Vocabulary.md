## Explanation

Nobody wants to need emergency Spanish, but having it ready can make a real difference
when something goes wrong while traveling — losing your passport, getting sick, or
needing urgent help. This lesson covers the essential words and phrases for exactly
those moments.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Calling for help:**

| Spanish                    | English            |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| ¡Ayuda!                          | Help!                    |
| ¡Auxilio!                        | Help! (more urgent)        |
| ¡Llame a la policía!             | Call the police!           |
| ¡Llame a una ambulancia!         | Call an ambulance!          |
| Es una emergencia.               | It's an emergency.          |

**Reporting a problem:**

| Spanish                    | English            |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Perdí mi pasaporte.              | I lost my passport.        |
| Me robaron la cartera.           | Someone stole my wallet.     |
| Tuve un accidente.               | I had an accident.          |
| Necesito un médico.              | I need a doctor.            |
| Me siento mal.                   | I feel sick.               |

**At the hospital or with authorities:**

| Spanish                    | English            |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| el hospital                      | hospital                 |
| la sala de emergencias           | emergency room             |
| la comisaría                     | police station             |
| el seguro médico                 | health insurance            |
| soy alérgico/a a...               | I'm allergic to...          |

- *Perdí* (I lost) uses the preterite of *perder* — a completed, one-time event, which
  is exactly the kind of situation emergencies call for.
- *Me robaron* uses the impersonal "they" construction — you don't need to know who
  robbed you, the sentence just states what happened to you: literally "they stole
  from me the wallet."
- *Necesito* + noun or infinitive covers most urgent requests: *Necesito un médico*,
  *Necesito ayuda*.
- *Soy alérgico/a a* + allergen is essential for medical situations — always mention
  allergies clearly and early if you need care.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| ¡Ayuda! ¡Llame a la policía!                  | Help! Call the police!                       | urgent command                  |
| Perdí mi pasaporte en el aeropuerto.          | I lost my passport at the airport.           | *perder*, past                  |
| Me robaron la mochila en la calle.            | Someone stole my backpack on the street.      | *robar*, impersonal              |
| Necesito un médico, me siento muy mal.        | I need a doctor, I feel very sick.            | *necesitar*, *sentirse*         |
| ¿Dónde está la sala de emergencias?           | Where is the emergency room?                   | question                        |
| Soy alérgico a la penicilina.                 | I'm allergic to penicillin.                    | *soy alérgico a*                |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing *auxilio* and *ayuda* — both mean "help," but *auxilio* is reserved for
  true emergencies (like calling out when in danger), while *ayuda* is used more
  broadly, including everyday requests for assistance.
- Forgetting the personal *a* before *policía* as a specific target: *Llame policía*
  is incomplete — it needs *Llame a la policía*.
- Using *soy alérgico* without adjusting for gender: a woman says *soy alérgica*, not
  *soy alérgico*.
- Saying *Yo perdí* out of habit — the subject pronoun is unnecessary since *perdí*
  already shows *yo*; just say *Perdí mi pasaporte*.
