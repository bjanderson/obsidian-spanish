## Explanation

Comparing two things — who's taller, which house is bigger, whose car is faster — is
one of the most useful everyday sentence patterns. Spanish builds comparisons with
three simple formulas: one for "more," one for "less," and one for "as...as," when
things are equal. Once you know the pattern, you can plug in almost any adjective.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Comparisons of inequality — "more than" / "less than":**

- **más + adjective + que** = more ___ than
- **menos + adjective + que** = less ___ than

*Mi casa es más grande que la tuya* (My house is bigger than yours). *Este carro es
menos caro que ese* (This car is less expensive than that one).

**Comparisons of equality — "as...as":**

- **tan + adjective + como** = as ___ as

*Ana es tan alta como su hermano* (Ana is as tall as her brother).

**Irregular comparatives** — these don't use *más* + adjective, they have their own
short form:

| Adjective       | Irregular comparative | Meaning        |
| ----------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------- |
| bueno (good)       | mejor                     | better           |
| malo (bad)         | peor                      | worse            |
| grande (big/old, age) | mayor                  | older/bigger     |
| pequeño (small/young, age) | menor             | younger/smaller  |

- The adjective always agrees in gender and number with the noun it describes, just
  like normal: *más altos*, *tan interesantes*.
- *Que* introduces the second half of an unequal comparison; *como* introduces the
  second half of an equal comparison. Mixing them up (*más alto como*, *tan alto que*)
  is a common learner slip.
- *Mayor* and *menor* usually refer to age (older/younger) rather than physical size
  when comparing people — for physical size, *más grande* and *más pequeño* are more
  common.
- Comparisons don't require a second noun spelled out if it's clear from context: *Mi
  hermano es mayor* (My brother is older — older than someone already mentioned).

## Examples

| Spanish                                        | English                              | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Mi hermano es más alto que yo.                      | My brother is taller than me.               | *más...que*                   |
| Este libro es menos interesante que el otro.        | This book is less interesting than the other. | *menos...que*                |
| Mi perro es tan grande como el tuyo.                | My dog is as big as yours.                  | *tan...como*                  |
| Ella es mayor que su prima.                         | She's older than her cousin.                 | irregular, *mayor*             |
| Este restaurante es mejor que aquel.                | This restaurant is better than that one.     | irregular, *mejor*             |
| Los niños son tan curiosos como los adultos.        | Children are as curious as adults.           | *tan...como*, plural agreement |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *que* with an equal comparison: *tan alto que* is wrong — equal comparisons
  need *como*: *tan alto como*.
- Using *más bueno* or *más malo* instead of the irregular forms *mejor*/*peor* — both
  exist, but *mejor*/*peor* are far more common and natural in everyday speech.
- Forgetting adjective agreement in a comparison: *Mis hermanas son más alto que yo*
  is wrong — the adjective must agree with *hermanas*: *más altas*.
- Mixing up *mayor/menor* (age) with *más grande/más pequeño* (size) — both exist, but
  swapping them changes the meaning: *mi hermano mayor* (my older brother) versus *mi
  hermano más grande* (my physically bigger brother).
