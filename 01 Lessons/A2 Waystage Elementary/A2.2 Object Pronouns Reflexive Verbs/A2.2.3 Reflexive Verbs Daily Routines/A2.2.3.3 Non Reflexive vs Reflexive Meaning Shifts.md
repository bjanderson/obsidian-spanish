## Explanation

So far, adding a reflexive pronoun has meant "doing the action to yourself"
— *lavar* ("to wash something") becomes *lavarse* ("to wash yourself"). But
for a specific group of verbs, adding *-se* doesn't just make the action
reflexive — it actually shifts the meaning into something related but
distinct.

Take *dormir* ("to sleep") versus *dormirse* ("to fall asleep"). *Dormir*
describes the state of being asleep — *Duermo ocho horas* ("I sleep eight
hours"). *Dormirse* describes the moment of crossing into that state — *Me
dormí durante la película* ("I fell asleep during the movie"). Same root,
related idea, but a real difference in meaning, not just "to myself."

*Ir* ("to go") versus *irse* ("to leave") work the same way. *Ir* points
toward a destination — *Voy al supermercado* ("I'm going to the
supermarket"). *Irse* emphasizes departing from where you currently are —
*Me voy ahora* ("I'm leaving now") — no destination needed, just the act of
leaving.

*Poner* ("to put") versus *ponerse* is especially productive: *ponerse* can
mean "to put on" (clothing) — *Me puse un abrigo* ("I put on a coat") — or
"to become" (a temporary state, especially emotions) — *Se puso triste* ("He/
she became sad"). Two related but distinct meanings from the same reflexive
form, decided by context.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Non-Reflexive          | Meaning                | Reflexive             | Meaning                          |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| dormir                    | to sleep                   | dormirse                  | to fall asleep                         |
| ir                         | to go (toward somewhere)   | irse                      | to leave / go away                     |
| levantar                   | to lift (something)        | levantarse                | to get (oneself) up                    |
| poner                      | to put (something)         | ponerse                   | to put on (clothes) / to become        |
| llamar                     | to call (someone)          | llamarse                  | to be named/called                     |
| comer                      | to eat                     | comerse                   | to eat up/finish completely            |
| quedar                     | to have left, to remain    | quedarse                  | to stay (in a place)                   |
| encontrar                  | to find                    | encontrarse (con)         | to run into, to meet up (with)         |

- The non-reflexive form isn't wrong or old-fashioned — both forms are
  correct Spanish, they just mean different things.
- Some of these meaning shifts are close relatives ("to sleep" → "to fall
  asleep"), while others feel more different ("to lift" → "to get up").
- Context and the surrounding sentence usually make clear which meaning is
  intended, especially with a verb like *ponerse* that has more than one
  reflexive meaning.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                             | Notes                        |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Duermo ocho horas cada noche.             | I sleep eight hours every night.       | *dormir*, state                    |
| Me dormí durante la película.             | I fell asleep during the movie.        | *dormirse*, crossing into sleep     |
| Voy a la tienda.                          | I'm going to the store.                | *ir*, destination                   |
| Me voy, ya es tarde.                      | I'm leaving, it's already late.        | *irse*, departure                   |
| Voy a levantar la caja.                   | I'm going to lift the box.             | *levantar*, transitive              |
| Me levanto a las siete.                   | I get up at seven.                     | *levantarse*, reflexive             |
| Me puse un abrigo porque hacía frío.      | I put on a coat because it was cold.   | *ponerse*, "to put on"              |
| Se puso muy nervioso antes del examen.    | He got very nervous before the exam.   | *ponerse*, "to become"              |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *dormir* when you mean the moment of falling asleep — *Dormí durante
  la película* sounds like you slept the whole time, not that you drifted off.
- Assuming *irse* always needs a destination — it doesn't; the point is
  leaving, not arriving somewhere.
- Confusing the two meanings of *ponerse* without enough context — check
  whether it's followed by a clothing item ("to put on") or an adjective/
  emotion ("to become").
