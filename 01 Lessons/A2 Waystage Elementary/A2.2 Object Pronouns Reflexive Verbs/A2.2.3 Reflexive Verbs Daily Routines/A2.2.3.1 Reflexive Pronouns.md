## Explanation

A reflexive verb is one where the subject does the action *to themselves* —
the doer and the receiver of the action are the same person. In English we
sometimes add "myself," "yourself," etc. (*I hurt myself*), but Spanish uses
this pattern far more often, including for everyday routines like washing up
or getting dressed.

The signal that a verb is reflexive is the little *-se* attached to the end
of its infinitive form in the dictionary: *lavarse* ("to wash oneself"),
*levantarse* ("to get (oneself) up"). When you actually conjugate the verb,
that *-se* becomes a separate reflexive pronoun that matches the subject and
goes in front of the verb, just like a direct or indirect object pronoun.

Here are the reflexive pronouns:

| Subject             | Reflexive Pronoun |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| yo                    | me                     |
| tú                    | te                     |
| él / ella / usted     | se                     |
| nosotros              | nos                    |
| ellos / ustedes       | se                     |

Take *lavarse*: *Yo me lavo las manos* ("I wash my hands"). Notice the
pronoun *me* comes before the verb, and *se* is the same for both *él/ella/
usted* and *ellos/ustedes* — no separate plural form needed. Just like direct
and indirect object pronouns, reflexive pronouns can attach to an infinitive
or gerund when there's a second verb involved: *Me voy a lavar las manos* /
*Voy a lavarme las manos*.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- A reflexive verb's infinitive form always ends in *-se*: *bañarse,
  vestirse, despertarse*.
- The reflexive pronoun (*me, te, se, nos, se*) goes before a single
  conjugated verb, matching the subject.
- *Se* covers both *él/ella/usted* and *ellos/ustedes* — there's no gender or
  extra number distinction beyond singular/plural.
- With a conjugated verb + infinitive or gerund, the pronoun can go before
  the conjugated verb or attach to the infinitive/gerund — same flexibility
  as direct and indirect object pronouns.
- The reflexive verb still conjugates normally for its subject — only the
  extra pronoun is new.

## Examples

| Spanish                          | English                              | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Me lavo las manos antes de comer.    | I wash my hands before eating.              | *lavarse*, *yo*                  |
| ¿Te despiertas temprano los lunes?   | Do you wake up early on Mondays?            | *despertarse*, *tú*              |
| Ella se viste rápido en la mañana.   | She gets dressed quickly in the morning.    | *vestirse*, *ella*                |
| Nos sentamos en la primera fila.     | We sit down in the front row.               | *sentarse*, *nosotros*           |
| Mis hijos se acuestan a las nueve.   | My kids go to bed at nine.                  | *acostarse*, *ellos*              |
| Voy a bañarme antes de salir.        | I'm going to take a bath before going out.  | *bañarse*, infinitive, pronoun attached |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the reflexive pronoun entirely — *Lavo las manos* (missing
  *me*) sounds incomplete for "I wash my hands."
- Using the wrong pronoun for the subject — *te* only pairs with *tú*, never
  with *yo* or *él*.
- Forgetting that *se* covers both singular and plural third person — there's
  no separate form for "themselves" versus "him/herself."
