## Explanation

Now that you know how reflexive pronouns work, it's time to build a working
vocabulary of the reflexive verbs you'll actually use every day — the ones
that describe your morning and evening routine, from waking up to going to
bed.

These verbs all conjugate like any regular verb, plus the reflexive pronoun
you already learned. Take *despertarse* ("to wake up"): drop the *-se*,
conjugate *despertar* normally (it's a stem-changing *e→ie* verb), and add
the matching pronoun — *me despierto*, *te despiertas*, *se despierta*...

Notice that many of these verbs describe an action you do *to a specific
part of your body* — *lavarse las manos* ("to wash your hands"), *cepillarse
el pelo* ("to brush your hair"). In these cases, Spanish uses the definite
article (*las manos*, *el pelo*), not a possessive like "my" — the reflexive
pronoun already makes clear whose hands or hair you mean, so adding "mis
manos" would be redundant.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Spanish            | English                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| despertarse           | to wake up                            |
| levantarse             | to get up                             |
| ducharse               | to shower                             |
| bañarse                | to take a bath                        |
| lavarse (las manos, la cara) | to wash (your hands, face)      |
| cepillarse (los dientes, el pelo) | to brush (your teeth, hair)|
| peinarse               | to comb your hair                     |
| afeitarse              | to shave                              |
| maquillarse            | to put on makeup                      |
| vestirse               | to get dressed                        |
| ponerse (la ropa)      | to put on (clothes)                   |
| quitarse (la ropa)     | to take off (clothes)                 |
| prepararse             | to get ready                          |
| acostarse              | to go to bed                          |

- Use the definite article (*el/la/los/las*), not a possessive, when the
  reflexive verb already acts on a body part: *me lavo las manos*, not *me
  lavo mis manos*.
- Several of these verbs are stem-changing: *despertarse* (e→ie), *acostarse*
  (o→ue), *vestirse* (e→i) — the stem change follows the usual rules on top
  of the reflexive pronoun.
- These verbs work in any tense — present, future, or past — the reflexive
  pronoun stays, only the verb ending changes.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Me despierto a las seis todos los días.   | I wake up at six every day.                 | *despertarse*, stem-changing     |
| Se ducha antes de ir al trabajo.          | He/she showers before going to work.        | *ducharse*                        |
| Nos cepillamos los dientes tres veces al día. | We brush our teeth three times a day.  | *cepillarse*, definite article    |
| ¿Ya te vestiste para la fiesta?           | Have you already gotten dressed for the party? | *vestirse*, stem-changing    |
| Mi papá se afeita todas las mañanas.      | My dad shaves every morning.                | *afeitarse*                       |
| Los niños se acuestan a las ocho.         | The kids go to bed at eight.                | *acostarse*, stem-changing        |

## Common Mistakes

- Using a possessive instead of the definite article — *me lavo mis manos*
  should be *me lavo las manos*.
- Forgetting the stem change on verbs like *despertarse*, *acostarse*, or
  *vestirse* — the reflexive pronoun doesn't replace the normal stem-change
  rule.
- Mixing up *ponerse* (to put on) and *quitarse* (to take off) — they're
  opposites, and both are reflexive.
