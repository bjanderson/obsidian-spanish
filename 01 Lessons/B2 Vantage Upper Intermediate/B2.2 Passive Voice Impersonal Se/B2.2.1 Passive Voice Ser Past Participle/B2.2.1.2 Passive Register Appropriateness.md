## Explanation

Here's something that surprises a lot of learners: the *ser* + past
participle passive you just learned is grammatically correct, but native
speakers barely use it out loud. It's real, it's not a mistake, but it
sounds like something you'd read in a newspaper, not something you'd say
to a friend. Knowing *when* to use it — and what to say instead in
everyday conversation — matters just as much as knowing how to build it.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Reach for *ser* + participle** in **formal, written** contexts: news
  articles, history books, academic writing, official announcements. It
  fits naturally there: *El tratado fue firmado por ambos países* (The
  treaty was signed by both countries) sounds right in a history text.
- **In everyday spoken Spanish**, the same idea usually comes out one of
  three other ways: the **passive *se*** (*Se firmó el tratado*), the
  **active voice** with the doer named (*Ambos países firmaron el
  tratado*), or an **unspecified third-person plural** (*Firmaron el
  tratado*) when the doer isn't the point.
- **A simple rule of thumb**: if you can rephrase a passive sentence in
  Spanish using something other than *ser* + participle, it's usually
  worth doing — especially in casual speech. Save the *ser* passive for
  when you're writing formally or specifically want to emphasize the
  action and its agent.
- **When the agent truly matters**, *ser* + participle is still the
  natural choice, even in speech: *El puente fue diseñado por un ingeniero
  muy famoso* works fine when the identity of the designer is the point of
  the sentence.
- This isn't a hard rule with sharp edges — it's a **tendency in register**.
  You'll hear the *ser* passive in speech sometimes, especially in more
  formal spoken contexts (news broadcasts, presentations), but defaulting
  to it in casual conversation will sound stiff.

## Examples

| Spanish                                                     | English                                            | Notes                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| El tratado fue firmado por ambos países. (written)             | The treaty was signed by both countries.               | *ser* passive, formal/written             |
| Se firmó el tratado. (spoken)                                  | The treaty was signed.                                   | passive *se*, everyday speech               |
| Firmaron el tratado. (spoken)                                  | They signed the treaty. / The treaty got signed.          | unspecified plural, everyday speech           |
| El puente fue diseñado por un ingeniero famoso. (either)        | The bridge was designed by a famous engineer.               | agent matters, natural in speech too            |
| Se construyó el puente el año pasado. (spoken)                 | The bridge was built last year.                              | passive *se*, everyday speech                     |
| La decisión fue tomada por el comité directivo. (written)       | The decision was made by the board.                            | *ser* passive, formal/written                       |

## Common Mistakes

- Defaulting to *ser* + participle in casual conversation: *La cena fue
  preparada por mi mamá* sounds formal for a dinner-table comment — more
  natural in speech: *Mi mamá preparó la cena* or *Se preparó la cena.*
- Assuming the *ser* passive is wrong or nonstandard: it's fully correct
  grammar, just register-mismatched for casual speech — it's the right
  choice in formal writing.
- Forgetting that the agent's importance changes the calculation: if who
  did it matters, *ser* + participle stays natural even out loud — don't
  force a *se* construction that hides an agent you actually want to
  mention.
- Treating this as an absolute rule instead of a tendency: native speakers
  do use the *ser* passive in speech sometimes, especially in more formal
  spoken situations — it's about matching the overall tone, not following
  a strict ban.
