## Explanation

You already know the present perfect indicative — *he hablado* (I have
spoken) — built from *haber* plus a past participle. When you want to talk
about that same kind of recent action but frame it through doubt, emotion,
or a wish instead of stating it as plain fact, you need the same *haber* +
participle pattern — just in the subjunctive. That's the **present perfect
subjunctive**. The only real change is swapping the indicative form of
*haber* for its subjunctive form.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **The formula**: [*haber* conjugated in the **present subjunctive**] +
  [past participle]. The present subjunctive of *haber* is **haya, hayas,
  haya, hayamos, hayan** — memorize this short list once, and it covers
  every present perfect subjunctive sentence you'll ever build.
- The **past participle never changes** in this construction — it stays in
  its default form (usually ending in *-ado* or *-ido*) regardless of the
  subject's gender or number: *Espero que hayas comido* (I hope you've
  eaten), *Espero que hayan comido* (I hope they've eaten) — *comido* stays
  the same either way.
- Regular past participles follow the familiar pattern: *-ar* verbs →
  *-ado* (*hablado*, *trabajado*); *-er*/*-ir* verbs → *-ido* (*comido*,
  *vivido*). Irregular participles you already know from the present
  perfect indicative carry over unchanged: *hecho* (hacer), *dicho*
  (decir), *visto* (ver), *escrito* (escribir), *puesto* (poner), *abierto*
  (abrir).
- This tense needs the same **subjunctive triggers** you already know —
  doubt, emotion, wish, impersonal expressions — just applied to an action
  that's already finished by the time the sentence is spoken: *Dudo que
  haya llegado* (I doubt he has arrived) — the doubt is happening now, but
  the arrival would already be complete.
- Don't confuse this with the **imperfect subjunctive**: the present
  perfect subjunctive is for a completed action still relevant to the
  present moment; the imperfect subjunctive is for describing the past
  itself, often from an even earlier vantage point.

## Examples

| Spanish                                            | English                                            | Notes                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Espero que hayas terminado la tarea.                 | I hope you have finished the homework.                  | *hayas* + *terminado*                 |
| Dudo que ellos hayan llegado ya.                     | I doubt they have arrived yet.                           | *hayan* + *llegado*                     |
| Me alegra que hayamos resuelto el problema.          | I'm glad we have solved the problem.                       | *hayamos* + *resuelto*                    |
| Es posible que Marta haya hecho el pastel.           | It's possible Marta has made the cake.                      | *haya* + *hecho*, irregular participle       |
| No creo que él haya visto la película.               | I don't think he has seen the movie.                          | *haya* + *visto*, irregular participle         |
| Ojalá hayas dormido bien anoche.                     | I hope you slept well last night.                              | *hayas* + *dormido*                              |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the indicative form of *haber* after a trigger: *Dudo que ha
  llegado* is wrong once a subjunctive trigger is present — it needs
  *haya*: *Dudo que haya llegado.*
- Changing the past participle to agree with gender or number: *Espero que
  hayan comida* is wrong — the participle never agrees in this
  construction: *Espero que hayan comido.*
- Confusing the present perfect subjunctive with the imperfect subjunctive
  for a recent, still-relevant action: *Espero que llegaras a tiempo*
  sounds like a distant past event; for something recent and connected to
  now, the present perfect subjunctive fits better: *Espero que hayas
  llegado a tiempo.*
- Forgetting an irregular participle carries over unchanged from the
  indicative: *Dudo que haya escribido* is wrong — the irregular
  participle is *escrito*: *Dudo que haya escrito la carta.*
