## Explanation

You've spent this whole section building subjunctive forms for
hypothetical and unreal situations. But not every "if" sentence in Spanish
is unreal — plenty of them describe something genuinely likely to happen.
For those everyday, realistic conditions, Spanish doesn't reach for the
subjunctive at all: it just uses the present tense on both sides, the way
you'd expect a plain statement of cause and effect to work.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **The pattern**: *si* + **present indicative** → **present, future, or
  command** in the result clause. This is for conditions that are real,
  likely, or habitual — not hypothetical: *Si llueve, no voy a la playa*
  (If it rains, I'm not going to the beach).
- **No subjunctive here** — this is the one *si*-clause pattern where the
  indicative handles everything. The unreal patterns you'll see in the next
  two lessons are the ones that call for the subjunctive.
- The result clause can be **present** (for habitual truths): *Si llueve,
  me quedo en casa* (If it rains, I stay home); **future** (for a specific
  upcoming outcome): *Si llueve, no iré a la playa* (If it rains, I won't
  go to the beach); or a **command** (for instructions): *Si llueve, trae
  un paraguas* (If it rains, bring an umbrella).
- **Never use the future or the conditional right after *si*** in this
  pattern. English allows "if it will rain," but Spanish doesn't carry the
  future into the *si* clause itself — the present indicative does that
  job: *Si lloverá* is wrong; *Si llueve* is correct.
- The *si* clause can come first or second with no change in meaning: *Si
  me llamas, te ayudo* and *Te ayudo si me llamas* both work — only the
  comma changes, appearing after the *si* clause when it leads.

## Examples

| Spanish                                             | English                                            | Notes                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Si llueve, no voy a la playa.                            | If it rains, I'm not going to the beach.                    | present → present                     |
| Si me llamas esta tarde, te cuento todo.                 | If you call me this afternoon, I'll tell you everything.     | present → present                       |
| Si terminas temprano, iremos al cine.                    | If you finish early, we'll go to the movies.                   | present → future                          |
| Si tienes dudas, pregúntame.                             | If you have questions, ask me.                                   | present → command                           |
| Si no estudias, no aprobarás el examen.                  | If you don't study, you won't pass the exam.                       | present → future                              |
| Si hace buen tiempo, salimos a caminar.                  | If the weather's nice, we go for a walk.                             | present → present, habitual                     |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the future after *si*: *Si lloverá, no voy a la playa* is wrong —
  the present indicative always follows *si* in this pattern: *Si llueve,
  no voy a la playa.*
- Reaching for the subjunctive out of habit from earlier lessons: *Si
  llueva, no voy* is wrong — this pattern is fully indicative, with no
  subjunctive at all.
- Using the conditional after *si*: *Si llovería, no iría* is wrong for a
  real/possible condition — that structure belongs to the unreal
  conditionals in the next two lessons, not this one.
- Forgetting the comma when the *si* clause leads: *Si llueve no voy* is
  missing the comma that should follow the *si* clause: *Si llueve, no
  voy.*
