## Explanation

Everyone has said "I wish I had..." about something that's already too
late to change. Spanish reaches for the pluperfect subjunctive you just
learned to build exactly that feeling — a wish about something that
**didn't happen and can't happen now**, because the moment for it has
already passed. This is the subjunctive's version of looking back with
regret.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Ojalá + pluperfect subjunctive** is the most direct way to express
  regret about something that didn't happen: *Ojalá hubiera estudiado más*
  (I wish I had studied more) — the wish is about a door that's already
  closed.
- Compare this to *ojalá* + imperfect subjunctive, which wishes for
  something **still possible** right now: *Ojalá tuviera más tiempo* (I
  wish I had more time — could still happen) versus *Ojalá hubiera tenido
  más tiempo* (I wish I had had more time — that window is gone).
- **Ojalá que** works exactly the same way — the *que* is optional and
  doesn't change the meaning: *Ojalá que hubieras venido a la fiesta* (I
  wish you had come to the party).
- Other regret expressions pair naturally with this same pattern: *Cómo me
  gustaría que hubieras estado ahí* (How I wish you had been there); *Si
  tan solo hubiera sabido* (If only I had known).
- This same *hubiera* + participle structure is also the backbone of
  unreal past conditionals with *si* — covered in B2.1.4.3 — but on its
  own, after *ojalá* or similar expressions, it's simply a way to voice
  regret without needing a full conditional sentence.

## Common Mistakes

- Using *ojalá* + imperfect subjunctive for something already impossible:
  *Ojalá estudiara más* sounds like a wish that could still happen; for
  something already too late, the pluperfect subjunctive is needed: *Ojalá
  hubiera estudiado más.*
- Reaching for the conditional perfect instead of the pluperfect
  subjunctive after *ojalá*: *Ojalá habría estudiado más* is wrong — *ojalá*
  always takes the subjunctive, never the conditional: *Ojalá hubiera
  estudiado más.*
- Forgetting that the regret is about something finished and unchangeable:
  if the situation could still be resolved, a different structure fits
  better — the pluperfect subjunctive specifically signals "too late now."
- Dropping *que* inconsistently and assuming it changes meaning: *Ojalá
  hubieras venido* and *Ojalá que hubieras venido* mean exactly the same
  thing — *que* is optional here, not a separate construction.
