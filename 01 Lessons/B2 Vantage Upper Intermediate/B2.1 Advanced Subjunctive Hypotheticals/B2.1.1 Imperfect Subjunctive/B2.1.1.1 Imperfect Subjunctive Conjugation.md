## Explanation

You already know how to build the present subjunctive. When those same
triggers — wishes, doubts, emotions, impersonal expressions — show up
talking about the **past** instead of the present, you reach for a
different form: the **imperfect subjunctive**, also called the past
subjunctive: *Quería que vinieras* (I wanted you to come). The good news is
the conjugation itself follows one simple, totally reliable recipe, no
matter how irregular the verb is.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **The formula**: take the **ellos/ellas/ustedes** form of the **preterite**
  tense, drop the **-ron** ending, and add the imperfect subjunctive
  ending. That's it — this works for every verb, regular or irregular.
- The endings are **-ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -ran** (for yo, tú, él/ella/usted,
  nosotros, ellos/ellas/ustedes). Spanish also has an alternate *-se* set of
  endings, but *-ra* is far more common in everyday Latin American speech,
  so that's the one to learn first.
- The **nosotros** form always carries a written accent on the vowel right
  before the ending: *habláramos*, *comiéramos*, *viviéramos*.
- Because the stem comes from the **preterite**, any irregular preterite
  stem carries straight over. If you know the preterite is irregular, the
  imperfect subjunctive will be irregular in exactly the same way — no new
  irregularities to memorize: *tuvieron* (preterite) → *tuviera* (imperfect
  subjunctive); *dijeron* → *dijera*; *fueron* → *fuera*.
- Regular verbs follow the pattern cleanly by conjugation group:
  *hablar* → *hablara*, *comer* → *comiera*, *vivir* → *viviera*. Notice
  *-er* and *-ir* verbs share the same endings in this tense.

## Examples

| Spanish                                            | English                                       | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Si yo hablara español mejor, viajaría más.               | If I spoke Spanish better, I would travel more.      | *hablar* → *hablara*                 |
| Esperaba que comiéramos juntos esa noche.                | I was hoping we would eat together that night.       | *comer* → *comiéramos*                 |
| Quería que vivieras cerca de mí.                         | I wanted you to live close to me.                      | *vivir* → *vivieras*                     |
| Dudaba que tuvieran tiempo para venir.                   | I doubted they would have time to come.                 | irregular stem *tuvieron* → *tuvieran*     |
| Me pidió que dijera la verdad.                           | He asked me to tell the truth.                           | irregular stem *dijeron* → *dijera*          |
| Ojalá fuéramos de vacaciones ese año.                    | I wish we had gone on vacation that year.                 | irregular stem *fueron* → *fuéramos*           |

## Common Mistakes

- Building the stem from the **infinitive** instead of the preterite:
  *tener* doesn't give you *tenera* — you must go through the preterite
  first: *tuvieron* → *tuviera.*
- Forgetting the accent mark on the **nosotros** form: *hablaramos* is
  wrong — it needs the accent: *habláramos.*
- Mixing up *-ra* and *-se* endings mid-sentence: both sets are valid, but
  pick one form and stay consistent — don't write *hablase* in one clause
  and *comiéramos* in the next within the same sentence.
- Assuming a verb irregular in the present tense is also irregular here:
  what matters is the **preterite** stem, not the present-tense stem. A
  verb like *pedir* is regular in the imperfect subjunctive stem-building
  step once you start from *pidieron* → *pidiera.*
