## Explanation

The great news about the imperfect subjunctive is that you don't need to
learn a new set of rules for *when* to use it. If a sentence would have
triggered the present subjunctive — a wish, a doubt, an emotion, an
impersonal expression — moving that same sentence into the past just means
swapping the subjunctive verb from present to imperfect. The trigger logic
carries over completely; only the tense of the main clause changes.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **The pattern**: [main clause verb, in a past tense] + **que** + [verb in
  the imperfect subjunctive]. If the main clause is in the preterite,
  imperfect, or conditional, and it's a trigger verb (wanting, doubting,
  feeling, requesting...), the subordinate clause takes the imperfect
  subjunctive: *Quería que vinieras* (I wanted you to come).
- **Ojalá** works the same way in the past as it does in the present, just
  with a shift in meaning: *ojalá* + present subjunctive expresses a **hope**
  for something realistic (*Ojalá tengamos tiempo* — I hope we have time),
  while *ojalá* + imperfect subjunctive expresses a **wish** for something
  unlikely, impossible, or contrary to how things actually are: *Ojalá
  tuviera más tiempo* (I wish I had more time).
- Words that connect one past action to another — *cuando*, *en cuanto*,
  and *una vez que* — also shift into the imperfect subjunctive when the
  whole sentence is set in the past and one action depended on another
  happening first: *Le dije que me avisara cuando llegara* (I told him to
  let me know when he arrived) — both the indirect command and the time
  clause take the imperfect subjunctive here. These are sometimes called
  **time-clause triggers**.
- **Antes de que** (before) always triggers the subjunctive, in the past
  exactly as in the present, with no exceptions for habitual actions:
  *Siempre limpiaba la casa antes de que Juan llegara* (I always used to
  clean the house before Juan arrived).
- As in the present tense, the subject pronoun before the trigger verb and
  the subordinate verb is usually dropped unless needed for clarity — the
  conjugation already carries that information.

## Examples

| Spanish                                                    | English                                             | Notes                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| María quería que fuera a la tienda antes de que lloviera.     | Maria wanted me to go to the store before it rained.   | past main clause + *que* + trigger      |
| Esperaba que los estudiantes tuvieran tiempo para terminar.   | I was hoping the students would have time to finish.   | *esperar* trigger                         |
| Ojalá tuviera más tiempo para viajar.                         | I wish I had more time to travel.                       | *ojalá* + imperfect subjunctive               |
| Le dije que me avisara en cuanto llegara.                     | I told him to let me know as soon as he arrived.         | time-clause trigger, past narration             |
| Siempre limpiaba la casa antes de que Juan llegara.           | I always used to clean the house before Juan arrived.     | *antes de que*, habitual action, still subjunctive |
| Dudaba que Juan fuera el que dejó la puerta abierta.          | I doubted Juan was the one who left the door open.        | doubt trigger                                        |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the indicative after a past-tense trigger verb: *Quería que
  venías* is wrong — a trigger verb in the past still calls for the
  subjunctive in the subordinate clause: *Quería que vinieras.*
- Treating *antes de que* like *cuando* and switching to the indicative for
  habitual past actions: *antes de que* always takes the subjunctive, even
  for routines: *Siempre limpiaba la casa antes de que Juan llegara* (not
  *llegaba*).
- Forgetting that *ojalá* changes meaning with the tense that follows it:
  *ojalá* + present subjunctive is a realistic hope; *ojalá* + imperfect
  subjunctive is a wish for something unlikely or untrue right now — mixing
  them up changes what you're actually saying.
- Switching tenses mid-sentence: once the main clause sets the scene in the
  past, the subordinate clause should stay in the imperfect subjunctive,
  not slip back into the present subjunctive: *Quería que tengas suerte* is
  inconsistent — it should be *Quería que tuvieras suerte.*
