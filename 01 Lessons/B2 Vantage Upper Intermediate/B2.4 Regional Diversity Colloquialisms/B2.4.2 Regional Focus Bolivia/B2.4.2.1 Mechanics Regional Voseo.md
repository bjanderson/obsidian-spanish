## Explanation

Up to now, you've learned *tú* as the informal "you." But in large parts
of Latin America — including Bolivia, this vault's target dialect — many
speakers use *vos* instead of, or alongside, *tú*. This is called
*voseo*, and it's not slang or a mistake — it's the standard informal
"you" in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, much of Central America, and
parts of Bolivia, especially in everyday spoken Bolivian Spanish.

The good news is that *voseo* doesn't require learning a whole new verb
system. It mostly just shifts the stress and ending in the present tense
and commands, while everything else — including all the object pronouns
you already know (*te, tu, tuyo*) — stays exactly the same as with *tú*.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Present tense**: drop the *-ar/-er/-ir* ending and add *-ás/-és/-ís*,
  with the stress moving to that final syllable: *vos hablás* (you
  speak), *vos comés* (you eat), *vos vivís* (you live). Compare to *tú
  hablas, tú comes, tú vives* — same meaning, different stress and
  ending.
- **Voseo present-tense forms are mostly regular** — even verbs that are
  stem-changing with *tú* often become regular with *vos*: *tú quieres*
  (stem-changing) becomes *vos querés* (no stem change), *tú puedes*
  becomes *vos podés*.
- **Commands (imperative)** drop the final *-r* of the infinitive and add
  an accent: *hablar* → *hablá* (speak!), *comer* → *comé* (eat!),
  *vivir* → *viví* (live!). This is simpler than the *tú* command, which
  often has an irregular form (*tú ven*, *tú pon*, *tú haz*).
- **Everything else stays the same as tú**: object pronouns (*te vi* — I
  saw you), possessives (*tu casa* — your house), and reflexives (*te
  levantás* — you get up) don't change at all.
- **Other tenses barely change.** Preterite, imperfect, future, and the
  subjunctive use the same forms as *tú* in most voseo regions — *voseo*
  is really a present-tense and command phenomenon.
- **Pragmatics**: *vos* isn't "less correct" or "less polite" than *tú* —
  it's simply the regional norm. In Bolivia, *vos* and *tú* often coexist,
  with *vos* more common in casual, everyday speech and *tú* still heard
  in some contexts — this varies by region and generation within the
  country.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| ¿Vos hablás español? | Do you speak Spanish? | Present tense, -ar verb |
| Vos comés muy rápido. | You eat very fast. | Present tense, -er verb |
| ¿Dónde vivís vos? | Where do you live? | Present tense, -ir verb |
| ¡Hablá más despacio! | Speak more slowly! | Command form |
| Vos querés ir al cine, ¿no? | You want to go to the movies, right? | No stem change with vos, unlike tú quieres |
| Te espero afuera. | I'll wait for you outside. | Object pronoun te — identical to tú form |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming *voseo* changes every tense — it mostly affects the present
  tense and commands; the rest of the conjugation system is shared with
  *tú*.
- Mixing *vos* verb forms with *tú* pronouns, or vice versa — within one
  sentence, stay consistent: *vos hablás*, not *tú hablás* or *vos
  hablas*.
- Treating *voseo* as incorrect or "bad Spanish." It's the standard
  informal form across huge stretches of Latin America and carries no
  less prestige than *tú*.
- Forgetting the accent on command forms — *hablá*, *comé*, and *viví*
  all need the written accent to mark where the stress falls.
