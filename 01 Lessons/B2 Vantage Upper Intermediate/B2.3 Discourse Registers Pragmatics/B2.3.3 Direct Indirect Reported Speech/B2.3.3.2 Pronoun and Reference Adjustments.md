## Explanation

Tense isn't the only thing that shifts when you report what someone said —
the whole point of view moves too. If your friend Sara says "I'm going
here today," and you tell someone else about it later, you don't say "Sara
said she's going here today" — you say "Sara said she was going there that
day." Spanish makes exactly the same kind of shift: pronouns, possessives,
and time/place words all adjust to the new vantage point of the person
reporting.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Subject and object pronouns shift** to match who's actually being
  talked about: *yo* → *él/ella* (or whatever fits), *mi/mío* → *su/suyo*:
  *Sara dijo: "Voy a mi casa"* becomes *Sara dijo que iba a su casa* (Sara
  said she was going to her house).
- **Time expressions move to match the new reference point**: *hoy*
  (today) → *ese día* (that day); *ayer* (yesterday) → *el día anterior*
  (the day before); *mañana* (tomorrow) → *al día siguiente* (the next
  day): *María dijo: "Llego hoy"* becomes *María dijo que llegaba ese
  día.*
- **Place words shift outward from the speaker's original position**:
  *aquí* (here) → *allí/ahí* (there); *este/esta* (this) → *ese/esa*
  (that): *Sara dijo: "Voy aquí"* becomes *Sara dijo que iba allí* (Sara
  said she was going there).
- **Demonstratives generally move away from "near me" toward "over
  there"** for the same reason — you're no longer standing where the
  original statement was made, so *este libro* (this book) can become *ese
  libro* (that book) in the reported version.
- Not every adjustment applies in every sentence — only make the shift
  that's actually relevant. If you're reporting something someone said
  **to you, right now, about right now**, some of these markers might not
  need to change at all — it depends on how far the reporting has moved
  from the original moment and place.

## Examples

| Direct                                     | Indirect                                                    | Notes                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Sara dijo: "Voy a mi casa."                        | Sara dijo que iba a su casa.                                   | possessive shift: *mi* → *su*           |
| María dijo: "Llego hoy."                           | María dijo que llegaba ese día.                                  | time shift: *hoy* → *ese día*             |
| Juan dijo: "Te vi ayer."                           | Juan dijo que me había visto el día anterior.                     | time shift: *ayer* → *el día anterior*      |
| Sara dijo: "Voy aquí."                             | Sara dijo que iba allí.                                            | place shift: *aquí* → *allí*                  |
| Pedro dijo: "Este libro es mío."                   | Pedro dijo que ese libro era suyo.                                   | demonstrative + possessive shift                |
| Ana dijo: "Nos vemos mañana."                      | Ana dijo que nos veríamos al día siguiente.                            | time shift: *mañana* → *al día siguiente*         |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to shift the possessive along with the pronoun: *Sara dijo
  que iba a mi casa* is wrong if you're reporting Sara's words as a third
  party — it should be *su casa*, not *mi casa*, unless the house is
  actually yours.
- Leaving time words unadjusted: *María dijo que llegaba hoy* only works
  if you're reporting this on the very same day — otherwise, *ese día* is
  needed to keep the timeline accurate.
- Keeping *aquí* when the location has changed for the listener: if
  you're telling someone else about it somewhere else, *allí* reflects
  that shift — *aquí* only stays correct if you're still standing in the
  same spot the original statement referred to.
- Over-adjusting when it's not actually needed: if you're reporting
  something said moments ago, in the same place, about the same day,
  some of these shifts might sound unnatural — use judgment based on how
  far removed the report is from the original context.
