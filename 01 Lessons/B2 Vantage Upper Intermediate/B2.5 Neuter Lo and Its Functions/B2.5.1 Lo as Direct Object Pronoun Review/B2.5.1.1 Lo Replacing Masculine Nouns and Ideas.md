## Explanation

You already know *lo* as the direct object pronoun for "him" or "it" when
the thing being replaced is masculine and singular: *el libro* becomes
*lo*, *mi jefe* becomes *lo*. This lesson is a deeper look at that
familiar use, plus a close cousin of it — using *lo* to stand in for a
whole action, idea, or situation that was just mentioned, not just a
single noun.

In both cases, *lo* is doing the same basic job: standing in for
something already on the table so you don't have to repeat it.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Lo replaces a masculine singular noun** that is the direct object of
  the verb: *¿Le diste el libro a Juan? Sí, lo hice* (I gave it to him —
  more literally, "I did it"). *¿Cuándo conociste a mi jefe? Lo conocí
  ayer* (I met him yesterday).
- **Lo goes right before a conjugated verb**, just like other direct
  object pronouns: *Lo conocí*, not *Conocí lo*.
- **Lo can also replace an entire action, idea, or situation** already
  mentioned — not just a single masculine noun. It works like "it" in
  English when "it" refers to a whole event: *Miguel hizo un excelente
  trabajo. Sí, lo hizo* (Miguel did an excellent job. Yes, he did — "it"
  referring to the whole act of doing a good job, not one noun).
- **This "whole idea" use of lo doesn't change for gender or number**,
  since it isn't replacing a specific noun at all — it's standing in for
  the abstract situation.
- **Context tells you which job lo is doing.** If there's a clear
  masculine singular noun nearby, *lo* is likely replacing that noun. If
  the previous sentence described an action or event, *lo* is likely
  standing in for that whole idea.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| ¿Le diste el libro a Juan? Sí, lo hice. | Did you give the book to Juan? Yes, I did. | Lo = el libro |
| ¿Cuándo conociste a mi jefe? Lo conocí ayer. | When did you meet my boss? I met him yesterday. | Lo = mi jefe |
| ¿Viste el partido anoche? Sí, lo vi. | Did you see the game last night? Yes, I saw it. | Lo = el partido |
| Miguel hizo un excelente trabajo. Sí, lo hizo. | Miguel did an excellent job. Yes, he did. | Lo = the whole idea of doing a good job |
| María dio una buena presentación. Sí, lo hizo. | María gave a good presentation. Yes, she did. | Lo = the whole idea of giving a good presentation |

## Common Mistakes

- Placing *lo* after the conjugated verb instead of before it: *Conocí
  lo ayer* is wrong — it should be *Lo conocí ayer*.
- Trying to make *lo* agree in gender or number when it's standing in
  for a whole idea rather than a specific noun — in that use, *lo* never
  changes.
- Confusing this direct-object *lo* with the resumptive *lo* used to
  reply to a question about an adjective (*¿Es aburrida la película? No,
  no lo es*) — that's a related but distinct use, covered in
  [[B2.5.1.2 Lo Referring Back to Stated Concepts]].
- Forgetting that *lo* only replaces masculine singular direct objects —
  feminine nouns need *la*, and plural nouns need *los/las*.
