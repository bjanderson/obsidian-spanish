## Explanation

English "what" does two different jobs, and it's easy to miss that they're
different. In "What do you want?" it's a question word. But in "I don't
know what you want," it's not really asking anything — it means
something closer to "that which." Spanish keeps these two jobs separate:
the question word is *qué*, and "that which" is *lo que*.

A simple test: if you can swap "what" for "that which" in the English
sentence without it sounding broken, you need *lo que* in Spanish, not
*qué*.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Lo que means "what" in the sense of "that which"** — it refers to an
  idea, thing, or situation without naming it directly: *Nunca sabes lo
  que tienes hasta que lo pierdes* (You never know what you have until
  you lose it).
- **Use the "that which" test** to decide between *qué* and *lo que*: if
  "that which" sounds natural in English, use *lo que*. If only the
  question word "what" works, use *qué*.
- **Lo que often starts a sentence** when the whole clause it introduces
  is the subject: *Lo que más me gusta es leer novelas* (What I like most
  is reading novels).
- **Lo que can also come in the middle of a sentence**, introducing a
  clause that's the object of another verb: *Ella siempre hace lo que
  dice* (She always does what she says).
- **Lo que never changes form** — it doesn't agree with gender or number,
  since it isn't referring to any specific noun.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Nunca sabes lo que tienes hasta que lo pierdes. | You never know what you have until you lose it. | lo que = that which |
| Lo que más me gusta es leer novelas. | What I like most is reading novels. | lo que starts the sentence as the subject |
| Lo que necesito es un poco de tiempo. | What I need is a bit of time. | lo que as subject |
| Ella siempre hace lo que dice. | She always does what she says. | lo que as the object of hace |
| No entiendo lo que quieres decir. | I don't understand what you mean. | lo que as the object of entiendo |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *qué* instead of *lo que* when no question is actually being
  asked: *No sé qué tienes* sounds like the start of a question; *No sé
  lo que tienes* is the correct way to say "I don't know what you have."
- Forgetting the "that which" test — when in doubt, try substituting
  "that which" in the English sentence. If it still makes sense, *lo que*
  is what you need.
- Adding an accent to *que* in *lo que* — unlike the question word *qué*,
  *lo que* never takes a written accent on *que*.
- Confusing *lo que* with *lo* + adjective (see
  [[B2.5.2.1 Forming Abstract Nouns with Lo Plus Adjective]]) — *lo que*
  introduces a whole clause, while *lo* + adjective creates an abstract
  noun phrase on its own.
