## Explanation

English has "no matter how" for emphasizing a degree of something: "No
matter how tired you are, keep going." Spanish has its own formula for
this exact idea, built with *lo*: *No importa lo cansado que estés,
sigue adelante*. Instead of just saying something is a certain way,
this structure emphasizes *how much* it is that way.

This is one of the more advanced uses of *lo*, since it pairs with the
subjunctive mood — but once you learn the formula, it's a reliable
pattern you can drop into a lot of different sentences.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **The formula is: lo + adjective/adverb + que + subjunctive verb.**
  This expresses "how [adjective/adverb]" something is, with emphasis:
  *No importa lo inteligente que seas* (It doesn't matter how
  intelligent you are).
- **This construction almost always follows a phrase like "no importa"**
  ("it doesn't matter") or similar expressions of indifference or
  emphasis, setting up the idea that the degree doesn't change the
  outcome.
- **The adjective or adverb agrees with the subject being described**,
  unlike *lo* + adjective for abstract nouns (see
  [[B2.5.2.1 Forming Abstract Nouns with Lo Plus Adjective]]), where the
  adjective always stays masculine singular. Here, *lo cansada que
  estés* is correct if talking about a woman.
- **The verb after que is in the subjunctive** when this formula follows
  a concessive expression like *no importa* ("it doesn't matter") or
  *por mucho que* ("no matter how much") — the subjunctive fits because
  the sentence is saying the outcome holds regardless of the degree:
  *No importa lo rápido que corras* (It doesn't matter how fast you
  run). Outside a concessive expression — for example after *me
  sorprende* or a plain statement — the indicative is used instead,
  since then you're just stating a fact about the degree, not
  discounting it.
- **This structure works with both adjectives and adverbs** — *lo
  inteligente que seas* (adjective, describing a person) and *lo rápido
  que corras* (adverb, describing how an action is done).

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| No importa lo inteligente que seas. | It doesn't matter how intelligent you are. | lo + adjective + que + subjunctive |
| No importa lo rápido que corras. | It doesn't matter how fast you run. | lo + adverb + que + subjunctive |
| No importa lo cansado que estés, sigue adelante. | No matter how tired you are, keep going. | Used to give advice regardless of degree |
| Me sorprende lo difícil que es este examen. | I'm surprised how hard this exam is. | Indicative (es) — no concessive expression, just a fact |
| No importa lo feliz que estés, siempre puedes estar más feliz. | No matter how happy you are, you can always be happier. | Subjunctive (estés) after no importa |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the indicative instead of the subjunctive after a concessive
  expression like *no importa*: *No importa lo cansado que estás* sounds
  off — it should be *estés*.
- Forgetting that the adjective/adverb *does* agree with gender and
  number here, unlike the neuter *lo* + adjective construction from
  [[B2.5.2.1 Forming Abstract Nouns with Lo Plus Adjective]] — *lo
  cansada que estés* is correct for a woman, not *lo cansado*.
- Dropping *que* entirely: *lo cansado estés* is incomplete — *que* is a
  required part of the formula.
- Confusing this intensifier structure with the resumptive *lo* from
  [[B2.5.1.2 Lo Referring Back to Stated Concepts]] — that structure
  answers a question with *Sí, lo es*; this one builds a whole emphatic
  clause with *que* + subjunctive.
