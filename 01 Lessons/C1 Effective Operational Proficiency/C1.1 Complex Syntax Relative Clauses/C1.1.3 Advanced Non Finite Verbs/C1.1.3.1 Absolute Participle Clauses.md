## Explanation

There's a compact way to say "once X happened" without using a full
"once/after" sentence. You take a past participle (*terminado, cerrado,
resuelto*) and put it at the front of the sentence, right before the noun it
describes — no conjugated verb, no *cuando* or *una vez que* needed. Grammar
calls this an **absolute participle clause**. It's a shortcut Spanish uses
constantly in writing and formal narration to link one finished event to what
happens next.

Because the participle is doing the job of a whole clause ("once the meeting
ended..."), it has to agree in gender and number with the noun that follows
it — just like an adjective would.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Structure: **[past participle] + [noun]**, comma, then the main clause.
  *Terminada la reunión, nos fuimos.* ("The meeting having ended, we left" /
  "Once the meeting ended, we left.")
- The participle **agrees in gender and number** with the noun right after it:
  *terminada la reunión* (fem. sing.), *terminados los exámenes* (masc. pl.).
- This is different from the participle in a compound tense (*he terminado*),
  which never changes form. An absolute participle always agrees, because
  here it's functioning like an adjective, not part of a verb tense.
- Only verbs that have a standard past participle work this way — irregular
  participles apply too: *resuelto* (resolver), *dicho* (decir), *hecho*
  (hacer).
- This construction implies the action happened **before** the main clause's
  action, and is usually interchangeable with *una vez que* or *después de
  que* + a full clause — the absolute participle is just the more compact,
  more formal/literary version.
- Common in news writing, narration, and formal reports — less common in
  casual spoken Spanish, where people usually just say *cuando terminó la
  reunión...* instead.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Terminada la reunión, nos fuimos a casa. | Once the meeting ended, we went home. | *Terminada* agrees with *la reunión* (fem.). |
| Firmado el contrato, empezamos a trabajar. | Once the contract was signed, we started working. | *Firmado* agrees with *el contrato* (masc.). |
| Resueltos los problemas técnicos, la reunión pudo continuar. | Once the technical problems were resolved, the meeting could continue. | *Resueltos* agrees with *los problemas* (masc. pl.). |
| Cerradas las puertas, el guardia apagó las luces. | Once the doors were closed, the guard turned off the lights. | *Cerradas* agrees with *las puertas* (fem. pl.). |
| Dicho esto, procedamos con la votación. | That said, let's proceed with the vote. | Common formal/meeting phrase; *dicho* agrees with the implied neuter *esto*. |
| Publicados los resultados, los estudiantes revisaron sus notas. | Once the results were published, the students checked their grades. | *Publicados* agrees with *los resultados* (masc. pl.). |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting agreement and defaulting to the masculine singular: ~~Terminado
  la reunión~~ → **Terminada la reunión** (must agree with *la reunión*).
- Confusing this with the compound-tense participle, which never changes:
  don't write ~~Ha terminada la reunión~~ — that participle stays *terminado*
  in compound tenses; only the absolute/adjectival use agrees.
- Overusing this structure in casual speech, where *cuando* + a conjugated
  verb sounds far more natural.
- Placing the noun before the participle instead of after it: ~~La reunión
  terminada, nos fuimos~~ is not the standard word order — keep the
  participle first: **Terminada la reunión...**
