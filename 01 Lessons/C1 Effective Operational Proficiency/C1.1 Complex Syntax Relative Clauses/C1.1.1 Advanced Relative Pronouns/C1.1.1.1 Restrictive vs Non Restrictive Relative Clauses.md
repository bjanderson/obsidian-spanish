## Explanation

Some clauses narrow down exactly which noun you mean — remove them and the
sentence loses essential meaning. Grammar calls these **restrictive clauses**.
Other clauses just add extra information about a noun you already identified —
remove them and the core meaning survives; these are **non-restrictive
clauses**. In writing, a non-restrictive clause is set off by commas; in
speech, it is set off by a pause.

*Que* works for both kinds of clause in everyday Spanish. *El cual / la cual /
los cuales / las cuales* are reserved for more formal or more precise contexts —
they agree in gender and number with the noun they replace, so they remove
ambiguity when more than one noun could be the antecedent, and they read more
naturally after a preposition or when several words separate the pronoun from
its antecedent.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Restrictive** (no commas): *que* is the default. *El cual* is possible but
  sounds stiff/formal — most speakers stick with *que* or *el que* here.
- **Non-restrictive** (set off by commas): both *que* and *el cual* work, but
  *el cual* is preferred in formal writing (reports, essays, contracts) and
  after a preposition.
- **Gender/number agreement**: *el cual* → masc. sing., *la cual* → fem. sing.,
  *los cuales* → masc. pl., *las cuales* → fem. pl. Match the antecedent, not
  the speaker.
- **Disambiguation**: when a sentence has two possible antecedents of different
  genders, *el cual* / *la cual* shows which one the clause refers to — *que*
  cannot.
- **After a preposition**: *el cual* forms are the natural choice, especially
  with compound prepositions (*a través del cual*, *por medio de la cual*,
  *detrás de los cuales*).
- *El cual* is not used to start a restrictive clause directly after its
  antecedent with no comma and no preposition — that slot belongs to *que*.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| El informe, el cual revisamos ayer, tiene varios errores. | The report, which we reviewed yesterday, has several errors. | Non-restrictive — comma before *el cual*; extra info about a report already identified. |
| La empresa contrató a la asistente del director, la cual habla tres idiomas. | The company hired the director's assistant, who speaks three languages. | *La cual* clarifies the clause refers to the assistant (fem.), not the director (masc.). |
| Estos son los documentos sin los cuales no podemos continuar. | These are the documents without which we cannot continue. | *El cual* after the compound preposition *sin*. |
| Los estudiantes que llegaron tarde no pudieron entrar. | The students who arrived late could not get in. | Restrictive — *que*, no commas; identifies which students. |
| La reunión, durante la cual se tomaron varias decisiones importantes, duró dos horas. | The meeting, during which several important decisions were made, lasted two hours. | Non-restrictive with the compound preposition *durante*. |
| Vi el edificio en el cual trabaja mi hermana. | I saw the building in which my sister works. | *El cual* after the simple preposition *en*, common in formal register. |
| El proyecto que presentamos fue aprobado sin cambios. | The project that we presented was approved without changes. | Restrictive — *que* identifies which project. |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *el cual* to open a restrictive clause right after the noun with no
  preposition and no comma: ~~El libro el cual leí~~ → **El libro que leí**.
- Forgetting to match gender/number: ~~la reunión, el cual duró...~~ → **la
  reunión, la cual duró...**
- Dropping the comma before a non-restrictive clause, which turns it into a
  restrictive one and changes the meaning.
- Using *que* directly after a compound preposition instead of *el cual*:
  ~~la razón por que~~ (unclear/incomplete) → **la razón por la cual**.
