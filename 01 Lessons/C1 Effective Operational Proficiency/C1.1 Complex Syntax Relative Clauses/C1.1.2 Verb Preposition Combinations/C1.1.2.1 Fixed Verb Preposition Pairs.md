## Explanation

Some Spanish verbs are glued to one specific preposition — not because of a
rule you can derive, but because that is simply how native speakers pair them.
English does the same thing ("depend **on**," "dream **of**," "fall **in**
love **with**") — the trap is that English and Spanish rarely pick the same
preposition for the same verb, so you cannot translate word for word. You have
to learn each verb together with its preposition, as a single unit, the way
you'd learn a vocabulary word.

A few of these verbs even change meaning depending on which preposition
follows — so the preposition is not just decoration, it is doing real
grammatical work.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Learn the verb and its preposition as one chunk, not as two separate pieces —
  *soñar con*, not *soñar* + "figure out the preposition later."
- The preposition after these verbs is always followed by an **infinitive**,
  never a gerund: *sueño con viajar*, not *sueño con viajando*.
- Some verbs shift meaning by preposition:
  - *pensar en* (to think about) vs. *pensar de* (to have an opinion about)
    vs. *pensar* + infinitive (to plan to do something).
- Common fixed pairs at this level: *soñar con* (to dream of), *depender de*
  (to depend on), *incurrir en* (to incur/fall into), *contar con* (to count
  on/have), *quejarse de* (to complain about), *enamorarse de* (to fall in
  love with), *cansarse de* (to get tired of), *burlarse de* (to make fun of).
- Don't assume the English preposition transfers — *depend on* → *depender
  de*, not *depender en*; *think about* → *pensar en*, not *pensar sobre*
  (which exists but sounds more like "to think deeply/ponder").

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Sueño con vivir en otro país algún día. | I dream of living in another country someday. | *Soñar con* + infinitive, not *soñar de*. |
| El equipo incurrió en varios errores durante el proyecto. | The team incurred several errors during the project. | *Incurrir en*, not *incurrir con*. |
| El precio final depende de la cantidad que compres. | The final price depends on the amount you buy. | *Depender de*, not *depender en*. |
| Siempre puedo contar con mis amigos en momentos difíciles. | I can always count on my friends in difficult times. | *Contar con* = to count on/rely on. |
| Mi hermana se enamoró de un compañero de trabajo. | My sister fell in love with a coworker. | *Enamorarse de*, not *enamorarse con*. |
| Los vecinos se quejan del ruido todas las noches. | The neighbors complain about the noise every night. | *Quejarse de* + *el* contracts to *del*. |
| Me cansé de esperar y decidí irme. | I got tired of waiting and decided to leave. | *Cansarse de* + infinitive. |

## Common Mistakes

- Translating the English preposition literally: ~~depender en~~ → **depender
  de**; ~~soñar de~~ → **soñar con**.
- Following the preposition with a gerund instead of an infinitive: ~~sueño
  con viajando~~ → **sueño con viajar**.
- Confusing *pensar en* (think about) with *pensar de* (opinion about) — only
  use *pensar de* when someone is directly asking for or giving an opinion.
- Forgetting the *de* + *el* → *del* contraction after these verbs: ~~se queja
  de el ruido~~ → **se queja del ruido**.
