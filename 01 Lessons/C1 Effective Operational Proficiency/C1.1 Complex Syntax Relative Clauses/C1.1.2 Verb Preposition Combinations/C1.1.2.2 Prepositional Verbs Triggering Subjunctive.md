## Explanation

A group of Spanish prepositions — *antes de* (before), *para* (in order to),
*sin* (without), *después de* (after), *con tal de* (provided that) — connect
two actions. Whether you follow them with a plain infinitive or switch to
*que* + subjunctive depends on one thing: **does the subject stay the same
across both actions, or does it change?**

If the same person is doing both things, Spanish stays simple: preposition +
infinitive, no conjugation needed. If a *different* person or thing is doing
the second action, Spanish adds *que* and switches to the subjunctive, because
you are now talking about something outside your own direct experience — the
hallmark trigger for subjunctive in Spanish.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Same subject → preposition + infinitive.**
  *Antes de salir, compré pan.* (I left, I bought bread — same "I.")
- **Different subject → preposition + que + subjunctive.**
  *Apagué la luz para que los niños durmieran.* (I turned off the light; the
  children sleep — two different subjects.)
- This same-subject/different-subject rule applies across all of these
  prepositions: *antes de / antes de que*, *después de / después de que*,
  *para / para que*, *sin / sin que*, *con tal de / con tal de que*, *a menos
  de / a menos que*.
- *Al + infinitive* is a related, simpler construction meaning "upon doing
  something" — it doesn't have a subjunctive counterpart because it always
  keeps the same subject: *Al llegar a casa, me acosté.*
- Reflexive pronouns attach to the infinitive and still match the subject:
  *Antes de acostarme...* (I), *antes de acostarte...* (you).
- The tense of the subjunctive verb follows the main clause: present tense
  main clause → present subjunctive; past tense main clause → past
  (imperfect) subjunctive.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Antes de salir, compré pan. | Before leaving, I bought bread. | Same subject ("I") — infinitive. |
| Antes de que salieras, ya había comprado el pan. | Before you left, I had already bought the bread. | Different subjects — *antes de que* + subjunctive. |
| Compré flores para decorar la casa. | I bought flowers to decorate the house. | Same subject — *para* + infinitive. |
| Compré flores para que decoraras la casa. | I bought flowers for you to decorate the house. | Different subjects — *para que* + subjunctive. |
| Ella se fue sin despedirse. | She left without saying goodbye. | Same subject — *sin* + infinitive. |
| Ella se fue sin que nadie se diera cuenta. | She left without anyone noticing. | Different subjects — *sin que* + subjunctive. |
| Apagué la luz para que los niños durmieran. | I turned off the light so the children could sleep. | Past tense, different subjects — imperfect subjunctive. |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the infinitive when the subject changes: ~~Compré flores para
  decores la casa~~ → **Compré flores para que decores la casa.**
- Using *que* + subjunctive when the subject stays the same — this sounds
  overly formal or simply wrong: ~~Antes de que yo salga, compro pan~~ →
  **Antes de salir, compro pan.**
- Matching the wrong subjunctive tense to the main clause's tense — present
  main clause needs present subjunctive, past needs imperfect subjunctive.
- Forgetting to attach and match the reflexive pronoun on the infinitive:
  ~~antes de acostar~~ → **antes de acostarme** (matching "yo").
