## Explanation

Once you're reading Spanish news, contracts, or academic reports instead of
textbook dialogues, you run into a whole layer of vocabulary that everyday
conversation never touches — the specialized words you'll hit reading the
news, a legal document, or a lab report. This lesson pulls together the core
terms from four domains you'll meet constantly in formal or specialized
Spanish-language media: **finanzas** (finance), **derecho** (law),
**política** (politics), and **ciencia** (science).

You don't need to master an entire technical dictionary — you need the
handful of terms that show up over and over in headlines, official
statements, and reports, plus the fixed phrases (*collocations*) native
speakers pair them with. Spanish leans heavily on set phrases like *dictar un
fallo* (to hand down a ruling) or *aprobar una ley* (to pass a law) — knowing
the noun alone isn't enough if you don't also know which verb goes with it.

A quick note on register: this vocabulary belongs to formal, written, or
broadcast Spanish. You won't hear *el electorado* or *el incumplimiento* in
casual conversation, but you'll see them constantly in *El País*, *BBC
Mundo*, or a company's annual report — which is exactly the kind of media
this lesson prepares you to read.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

### Finanzas (Finance)

- **la inflación** — inflation. Feminine, like most nouns ending in
  *-ción*/*-sión* (though not every *-ión* ending is — *el avión*, *el
  camión* are masculine). Common collocation: *contener la inflación* (to
  curb inflation).
- **la tasa de interés** — interest rate. Always feminine (*la tasa*);
  don't confuse with *el interés* (interest, the concept), which is
  masculine. Collocation: *subir/bajar la tasa de interés*.
- **los activos** — assets. Masculine plural; contrasts with **los
  pasivos** (liabilities). Collocation: *activos financieros*, *congelar los
  activos* (to freeze assets).
- **el presupuesto** — budget. Masculine. Collocation: *aprobar/recortar el
  presupuesto* (to approve/cut the budget).
- **el déficit** — deficit. Masculine, invariable in the plural (*los
  déficit*). Collocation: *reducir el déficit fiscal*.
- **la deuda** — debt. Feminine. Collocation: *contraer una deuda* (to take
  on debt), *la deuda pública* (public debt).

### Derecho (Law)

- **la demanda** — lawsuit/claim. Feminine. Watch the false-friend trap:
  *demanda* is also "demand" in an economic sense (*oferta y demanda*), but
  in a legal context it means a suit filed in court. Collocation: *presentar
  una demanda* (to file a lawsuit), *interponer una demanda contra alguien*.
- **el fallo** — ruling/verdict. Masculine. Collocation: *dictar un fallo*
  (to hand down a ruling); *el fallo* can also mean "flaw/failure" in a
  non-legal context, so context matters.
  - Personal *a* note: when a court issues a ruling against a specific
    person, the sentence needs the personal *a* on that person — *El
    tribunal condenó al acusado* (not *condenó el acusado*).
- **la jurisdicción** — jurisdiction. Feminine (*-ión* pattern).
  Collocation: *estar bajo la jurisdicción de*, *ejercer jurisdicción sobre
  algo*.
- **el incumplimiento** — breach/non-compliance. Masculine. Collocation:
  *el incumplimiento de contrato* (breach of contract), *incurrir en
  incumplimiento*.
- **la sentencia** — sentence/judgment. Feminine. Collocation: *cumplir una
  sentencia* (to serve a sentence).
- **el testigo / la testigo** — witness. Same form for both genders in most
  of Latin America (*el/la testigo*); article changes, not the noun.
  Collocation: *citar a un testigo* (personal *a* required — a specific
  person).

### Política (Politics)

- **el proyecto de ley** — bill (proposed legislation). Masculine.
  Collocation: *presentar/aprobar un proyecto de ley* — once passed, it
  becomes **la ley** (the law).
- **el escaño** — legislative seat. Masculine. Collocation: *ganar/perder
  un escaño*, *obtener escaños en el Congreso*.
- **el electorado** — the electorate. Masculine, singular even though it
  refers to a group of people (like *el pueblo*) — the verb stays singular:
  *el electorado decidió*, not *decidieron*.
- **el mandato** — term of office/mandate. Masculine. Collocation: *durante
  su mandato* (during their term), *renovar el mandato*.
- **la candidatura** — candidacy. Feminine. Collocation: *lanzar/retirar su
  candidatura*.
- **el escrutinio** — vote count/scrutiny. Masculine. Collocation: *el
  escrutinio de los votos*.

### Ciencia (Science)

- **la hipótesis** — hypothesis. Feminine, invariable in the plural (*las
  hipótesis*). Collocation: *plantear/comprobar una hipótesis*.
- **el ensayo clínico** — clinical trial. Masculine. Collocation: *realizar
  un ensayo clínico*, *los resultados del ensayo*.
- **la variable** — variable. Feminine. Collocation: *controlar/aislar una
  variable*.
- **el hallazgo** — finding/discovery. Masculine. Collocation: *un hallazgo
  significativo*, *publicar un hallazgo*.
- **la muestra** — sample. Feminine. Collocation: *tomar una muestra*,
  *el tamaño de la muestra* (sample size).
- **el estudio revisado por pares** — peer-reviewed study. Masculine phrase
  built around *el estudio*. Collocation: *publicar un estudio revisado por
  pares*.

## Common Mistakes

- **False cognate: *actualmente* ≠ "actually."** *Actualmente* means
  "currently/at present" (*actualmente, la inflación está bajando* —
  "inflation is currently going down"). "Actually" (in the sense of
  correcting or emphasizing) is *en realidad* or *de hecho*.
- **False cognate: *asistir* ≠ "assist."** *Asistir (a algo)* means "to
  attend" (*asistió a la audiencia* — "he/she attended the hearing"). "To
  assist/help" is *ayudar*.
- **Confusing *demanda* (lawsuit) with *demanda* (economic demand).** Both
  spellings are identical, but the meaning depends entirely on context —
  *la oferta y la demanda* (supply and demand) is economics, *presentar una
  demanda* (to file a lawsuit) is law. Don't assume one meaning carries
  across domains.
- **Confusing *sentencia* (judgment/sentence) with *oración* (grammatical
  sentence).** *Sentencia* is a legal ruling, not a sentence in the
  grammar sense — a common mix-up for English speakers since "sentence" in
  English covers both meanings.
- **Wrong gender on *-ción*/*-sión* nouns.** *Inflación* and *jurisdicción*
  are feminine — don't default to masculine just because a word sounds
  technical or official. The same default applies to *-cia*/*-encia* nouns
  like *sentencia*.
- **Dropping the personal *a* with legal verbs.** Verbs like *citar*,
  *condenar*, and *demandar* almost always take a specific person as their
  object in legal contexts, so the personal *a* is mandatory: *demandó al
  proveedor*, not *demandó el proveedor*.
- **Treating *el electorado* as plural.** It's a singular collective noun —
  pair it with a singular verb (*el electorado votó*, not *votaron*), the
  same pattern as *el pueblo* or *el gobierno*.
