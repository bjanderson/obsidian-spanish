## Explanation

Picture two ways of turning down a client's proposal. One is "No, that
doesn't work for us." The other is "Entendemos su propuesta, sin embargo, en
este momento no nos sería posible aceptarla en esos términos." Both mean
roughly the same thing, but only one of them keeps the business relationship
intact. Spanish business correspondence and negotiation leans hard on the
second style — softened, indirect, cushioned — and this lesson teaches the
toolkit that makes that style work.

The core trick is that Spanish uses grammar itself to soften a statement.
Where English softens mostly through word choice ("it might be a bit
difficult" instead of "no"), Spanish reaches for the **conditional mood**
(*sería, tendríamos, nos gustaría*) and the **subjunctive** after impersonal
or suggestion-type expressions (*sería conveniente que revisáramos...*) to
put distance between the speaker and the blunt version of what they're
saying. Learning this lesson's phrases means learning to reach for those
moods automatically whenever you're asking for something, disagreeing with
someone, or delivering unwelcome news in a professional context.

This is a **formula-based** lesson, like the openings and closings in
C1.2.1.1: you're memorizing a set of ready-made phrases and slotting them
into the right moment, more than building new sentences from scratch. Native
speakers use these constructions constantly in professional email threads
and negotiation meetings — mastering them is often the single biggest jump
between "grammatically correct but blunt" Spanish and Spanish that reads as
polished and professional.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Conditional mood softeners** — reach for the conditional (not the present
tense) whenever you're making a polite request or floating an opinion in a
professional context; it's your default mood here, not an occasional
flourish.

- **Nos gustaría + infinitive** — "we would like to." Use this as your
  all-purpose opener whenever you're making a request and want to sound
  collaborative rather than demanding: *Nos gustaría conocer más detalles
  sobre su oferta.*
- **Sería conveniente/preferible/oportuno + infinitive or que + subjunctive**
  — "it would be advisable/preferable/opportune (to)." Reach for this when
  you want a suggestion to land as a shared observation rather than an
  order: *Sería conveniente revisar el contrato antes de firmarlo.*
- **Podría ser + adjective/infinitive** — "it could be." Use this when
  you're floating an idea tentatively and don't want to assert it as fact:
  *Podría ser útil reconsiderar el plazo de entrega.*
- **Quisiéramos sugerir/proponer/solicitar** — "we would like to
  suggest/propose/request," a more formal cousin of *nos gustaría* that you'll
  see constantly in written correspondence: *Quisiéramos sugerir una fecha
  alternativa para la reunión.*

**Hedged disagreement and diplomatic pushback**

- **Entendemos su posición, sin embargo...** — "we understand your position,
  however..." Always validate the other party before you introduce your
  counterpoint — jumping straight to the objection is what reads as
  confrontational, not the disagreement itself.
- **Si bien es cierto que..., también es importante señalar que...** —
  "while it's true that..., it's also important to point out that..." Use
  this concessive structure when you want to acknowledge a point before you
  counter it.
- **Nos preocupa un poco que + subjunctive** — "we're a bit concerned
  that..." Reach for this when you need to raise a concern without pointing
  a finger at anyone: *Nos preocupa un poco que el plazo propuesto no sea
  suficiente.*
- **Nos permitimos discrepar respetuosamente** — "we would respectfully
  disagree." Keep this set formula in your back pocket for when you need to
  flag outright disagreement while staying fully polite.

**Polite/indirect refusals**

- **Lamentablemente, no nos sería posible + infinitive** — "unfortunately, it
  wouldn't be possible for us to..." This is your go-to formula for a soft
  refusal; notice the conditional (*sería*, not *es*) is what keeps it
  gentle instead of blunt.
- **En este momento no estamos en condiciones de + infinitive** — "at this
  time we're not in a position to..." Use this when you want to decline
  without a flat *no*, in a way that saves face for both sides.
- **Preferiríamos + infinitive** — "we would prefer to." Reach for this when
  you want to redirect a request toward a counter-offer instead of refusing
  outright: *Preferiríamos mantener los términos originales del contrato.*

**Subjunctive after impersonal expressions of suggestion**

- Watch for impersonal expressions that recommend, suggest, or evaluate —
  *es importante que, sería conveniente que, es preferible que, conviene
  que* — because all of them trigger the **subjunctive** in the following
  clause when there's a named or implied subject: *Es importante que ambas
  partes revisen el acuerdo antes de la próxima reunión.* If you slip into
  the indicative here (*revisan*), that's one of the most common errors at
  this level — see Common Mistakes below.

**Cushioning bad news**

- **Nos vemos en la necesidad de + infinitive** — "we find ourselves needing
  to..." Use this when you have to introduce unwelcome news (a price
  increase, a delay, a rejected request) without sounding abrupt.
- **Lamentamos informarle que...** — "we regret to inform you that..." Reach
  for this as your standard formal opener whenever you're delivering bad
  news in writing.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Lamentablemente, no nos sería posible aceptar la propuesta en los términos actuales, pero quisiéramos explorar otras opciones. | Unfortunately, it wouldn't be possible for us to accept the proposal under the current terms, but we would like to explore other options. | Declining a proposal politely; conditional *sería* softens the refusal, and the sentence pivots immediately toward continued negotiation. |
| Entendemos que el cronograma actual es ajustado; sin embargo, nos gustaría solicitar una prórroga de dos semanas para completar la revisión. | We understand the current schedule is tight; however, we would like to request a two-week extension to complete the review. | Requesting a deadline extension; validates the other party's constraint before making the request. |
| Nos preocupa un poco que el presupuesto propuesto no cubra los costos de implementación. ¿Sería posible revisarlo juntos? | We're a bit concerned that the proposed budget doesn't cover implementation costs. Would it be possible to review it together? | Raising a concern diplomatically; subjunctive *cubra* after the impersonal concern, followed by a conditional question to invite collaboration. |
| Si bien valoramos su oferta inicial, consideramos que un precio de 15,000 dólares refleja mejor el alcance del proyecto. | While we value your initial offer, we believe that a price of 15,000 dollars better reflects the project's scope. | Countering an offer; the concessive opener softens the counter-proposal that follows. |
| Nos vemos en la necesidad de informarle que el lanzamiento se pospondrá una semana debido a factores fuera de nuestro control. | We find ourselves needing to inform you that the launch will be postponed by one week due to factors beyond our control. | Softening bad news; the set phrase distances the writer from being the direct cause of the delay. |
| Sería conveniente que el equipo legal revisara la cláusula 4 antes de que ambas partes firmen el contrato. | It would be advisable for the legal team to review clause 4 before both parties sign the contract. | Suggestion in a negotiation; subjunctive *revisara* is required after the impersonal *sería conveniente que*. |
| Agradecemos su paciencia y le aseguramos que estamos trabajando para resolver el inconveniente a la brevedad posible. | We appreciate your patience and assure you that we are working to resolve the issue as soon as possible. | Cushioning a delay or complaint response; keeps tone cooperative rather than defensive. |
| Nos permitimos discrepar respetuosamente con el plazo sugerido, ya que consideramos que dos semanas no son suficientes. | We would respectfully disagree with the suggested deadline, as we believe two weeks are not enough. | Formal disagreement; the set phrase *nos permitimos discrepar respetuosamente* is the safe, professional way to push back. |

## Common Mistakes

- **Using the indicative instead of the subjunctive after impersonal
  suggestion expressions.** *Es importante que el equipo revise el
  contrato*, not *Es importante que el equipo revisa el contrato* — the
  suggestion/evaluation meaning of *es importante que* always triggers the
  subjunctive in the following clause.
- **Skipping the conditional softener and going straight to the present
  tense.** *Queremos que nos den más tiempo* is grammatically fine but reads
  as blunt in a formal negotiation; *Nos gustaría solicitar más tiempo* is
  the polished equivalent. At this level, the missing conditional is often
  the only thing separating "correct" from "professional."
- **Translating English business hedges too literally.** English "we might
  need to revisit this" doesn't map word-for-word — a literal *podríamos
  necesitar revisar esto* is understandable but stiff; native correspondence
  favors set formulas like *sería conveniente revisar esto* or *convendría
  revisarlo*.
- **Refusing without a buffer phrase.** Opening a refusal with *No podemos
  aceptar* instead of *Lamentablemente, no nos sería posible aceptar* skips
  the cushioning that neutral Latin American business register expects —
  the buffer isn't optional politeness, it's the expected structure.
- **Disagreeing without first validating the other party's point.** Jumping
  straight to *sin embargo* or *pero* without an opening acknowledgment
  (*Entendemos su posición...*, *Si bien es cierto que...*) reads as
  confrontational even when the grammar is correct.
