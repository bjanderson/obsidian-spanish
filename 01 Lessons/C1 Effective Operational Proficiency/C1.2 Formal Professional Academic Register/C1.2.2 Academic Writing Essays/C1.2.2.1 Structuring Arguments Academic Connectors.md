## Explanation

Think about the last time you read a well-argued opinion piece or academic essay
in English. It probably didn't jump from idea to idea — little linking words
carried you from one point to the next, told you when the writer was about to
contrast an idea, add support, or wrap up. Spanish academic and persuasive
writing leans on this same toolkit, just with its own set of phrases. Linguists
call these words **discourse connectors** (*conectores del discurso*), and in
formal writing they do a lot of heavy lifting: they signal the shape of your
argument before the reader even gets to the content.

The connectors in this lesson are specifically the "essay register" ones —
phrases like *en suma*, *por consiguiente*, and *cabe destacar* that you'd use
in a university paper, an opinion column, a formal report, or a structured
debate, but almost never in a text message or a casual chat. Using the wrong
register connector (a spoken one like *entonces* or *o sea* in a formal essay)
is one of the fastest ways to make an otherwise polished piece of writing sound
out of place — like showing up to a job interview in flip-flops.

Each connector below does one specific structural job: it adds a point, it
contrasts one idea against another, it signals a consequence, it draws
attention to something important, or it wraps up an argument. Learning them by
function — not just as a random vocabulary list — makes it much easier to pick
the right one when you're actually writing.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Adding a point** (building on what was just said)

- *además* — besides, furthermore
- *asimismo* — likewise, also (slightly more formal than *además*)
- *por otro lado* — on the other hand (introduces an additional, not
  necessarily contrasting, point)
- *en este sentido* — in this regard / along these lines — ties the next
  sentence back to the idea just introduced

**Contrasting** (setting one idea against another)

- *no obstante* — nonetheless, however (more formal than *sin embargo*)
- *sin embargo* — however — the everyday-formal default; safe in nearly any
  written register
- *aun así* — even so, even then
- *dicho esto* — that said, having said that — concedes a point before
  pivoting

**Citing cause or consequence**

- *por consiguiente* — consequently, therefore
- *a tenor de* — in light of, in accordance with — introduces a fact or
  source that the following claim is based on
- *de ahí que* — hence, which is why (triggers the subjunctive in the clause
  that follows: *de ahí que sea necesario...*)

**Emphasizing** (flagging something as especially important)

- *cabe destacar (que)* — it's worth noting / highlighting (that)
- *cabe señalar (que)* — it's worth pointing out (that)
- *vale la pena mencionar (que)* — it's worth mentioning (that)

**Concluding** (wrapping up an argument)

- *en suma* — in short, to sum up
- *en definitiva* — ultimately, in the end
- *en última instancia* — in the final analysis, when it comes down to it —
  slightly stronger than *en definitiva*, often used right before the
  writer's strongest or final claim
- *para concluir* — to conclude

- All of these connectors are invariable — they never change form for gender
  or number, no matter what they refer to.
- Most sit at the start of a sentence or clause, followed by a comma:
  *No obstante, los datos muestran...*
- A given connector should not be repeated more than once or twice in the
  same essay — vary the connector even when the logical relationship (e.g.
  contrast) repeats, the same way you'd avoid repeating "however" five times
  in an English essay.
- *cabe destacar que*, *cabe señalar que*, and *vale la pena mencionar que*
  introduce a full clause and are commonly followed by the indicative when
  stating a fact (*cabe destacar que el estudio incluyó...*), though the
  subjunctive appears when the writer is expressing a value judgment about
  the fact rather than just reporting it.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| El banco central subió la tasa de interés; **por consiguiente**, el consumo se desaceleró en el segundo trimestre. | The central bank raised the interest rate; consequently, consumption slowed in the second quarter. | *por consiguiente* links a cause to its economic effect. |
| **Cabe destacar que** los estudiantes que participaron en el programa piloto mejoraron su rendimiento en un veinte por ciento. | It's worth noting that the students who took part in the pilot program improved their performance by twenty percent. | *cabe destacar que* flags a key finding before the writer builds on it. |
| Reducir las emisiones industriales es urgente; **no obstante**, cualquier política debe considerar el impacto en el empleo local. | Reducing industrial emissions is urgent; nonetheless, any policy must consider the impact on local employment. | *no obstante* introduces a formal counterpoint, more literary than *sin embargo*. |
| La inteligencia artificial promete mayor eficiencia. **En este sentido**, varias empresas ya han automatizado sus procesos de atención al cliente. | Artificial intelligence promises greater efficiency. In this regard, several companies have already automated their customer service processes. | *en este sentido* extends the previous idea rather than contrasting it. |
| **A tenor de** los datos publicados por la organización, la desigualdad de ingresos ha aumentado en la última década. | In light of the data published by the organization, income inequality has increased over the last decade. | *a tenor de* cites a source or basis for the claim that follows. |
| El dilema ético no tiene una solución sencilla; **dicho esto**, la mayoría de los especialistas coincide en que la transparencia es el primer paso. | The ethical dilemma has no easy solution; that said, most specialists agree that transparency is the first step. | *dicho esto* concedes a point before pivoting to the writer's main claim. |
| **En última instancia**, ningún sistema educativo puede tener éxito sin la participación activa de las familias. | In the final analysis, no educational system can succeed without the active participation of families. | *en última instancia* signals the writer's strongest or closing claim. |
| **En suma**, los tres factores —el costo, el acceso y la calidad— determinan el éxito de la reforma sanitaria. | In short, the three factors — cost, access, and quality — determine the success of the healthcare reform. | *en suma* summarizes multiple points already made. |

## Common Mistakes

- **Using a spoken-register connector in formal writing.** *Entonces*, *o
  sea*, and *bueno* are extremely common in conversation but read as
  underdeveloped in an essay or report. Use *por lo tanto*/*por consiguiente*
  instead of *entonces*, and *es decir* instead of *o sea*.
- **Repeating the same connector throughout an essay.** Leaning on *sin
  embargo* for every contrast (instead of rotating in *no obstante*, *aun
  así*, *por el contrario*) makes formal writing feel repetitive and
  underdeveloped, the same way overusing "however" does in English.
- **Using a concluding connector mid-argument.** *En suma* and *en última
  instancia* signal to the reader that the argument is wrapping up — dropping
  one in the middle of a paragraph with more points still to come confuses
  the essay's structure, even if the sentence itself is grammatical.
- **Treating *a tenor de* like a casual "according to."** It specifically
  introduces a formal basis (data, a study, a legal text) for a claim — it
  sounds stilted attached to a personal opinion (*a tenor de mí* is not
  idiomatic; use *en mi opinión* or *a mi parecer* for personal views).
- **Dropping *que* after *cabe destacar/señalar*.** These phrases need *que*
  before the clause they introduce: *cabe destacar que...*, not *cabe
  destacar los datos muestran...*.
- **Forgetting the comma after a sentence-initial connector.** *No obstante*,
  *en suma*, and similar connectors are set off with a comma when they open a
  sentence or clause.
