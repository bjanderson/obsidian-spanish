## Explanation

Before you can shop for clothes in Spanish, you need the basic vocabulary for
the items themselves, their colors, and their sizes — this lesson covers all
three so you can describe what you're looking for or already own.

Clothing nouns follow the same gender and number rules as any other noun:
_la camisa_ (the shirt), _los pantalones_ (the pants, always plural in
Spanish), _el vestido_ (the dress), _las zapatillas_ (the sneakers). Learn
each item with its article so the gender sticks from the start.

Colors used as adjectives usually agree in gender and number with the noun
they describe: _una camisa roja_ (a red shirt), _unos zapatos rojos_ (red
shoes). Some colors — often ones borrowed from a fixed noun like a fruit or
material — don't change form at all, and Spanish speakers frequently fall
back on the safe _color + [color noun]_ pattern instead (_una camisa color
naranja_). See [[A1.2.3.3 Invariable Adjectives]] for the full rule on which
colors stay fixed and why.

For sizes, clothing uses _la talla_ (¿Qué talla usas? — "What size do you
wear?") while shoes use _el número_ (¿Qué número calzas? — "What shoe size
do you wear?") — Spanish keeps these two words separate, unlike English
"size" for both. _Pequeño/a, mediano/a,_ and _grande_ cover the basic
small/medium/large range.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Phrase                    | Meaning                       | Notes                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| la camisa / la blusa       | the shirt / the blouse         | _blusa_ typically for women's tops         |
| los pantalones             | the pants                      | always plural in Spanish                   |
| el vestido                 | the dress                      | —                                           |
| la falda                   | the skirt                      | —                                           |
| los zapatos / las zapatillas | the shoes / the sneakers      | —                                           |
| la chaqueta / el abrigo    | the jacket / the coat          | _abrigo_ is heavier, for cold weather       |
| ¿Qué talla usas?           | What size do you wear? (clothes) | uses _talla_, not _número_               |
| ¿Qué número calzas?        | What shoe size do you wear?    | uses _número_ and _calzar_ ("to wear shoes") |
| pequeño/a, mediano/a, grande | small, medium, large          | agree in gender with the noun               |

## Examples

| Spanish                                          | English                                     | Notes                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Necesito una camisa azul para la entrevista.       | I need a blue shirt for the interview.         | color agrees with _camisa_ (feminine)      |
| ¿Tienes estos pantalones en talla mediana?         | Do you have these pants in medium size?        | _talla_ for clothing                       |
| Mi hermana siempre usa vestidos de color verde.    | My sister always wears green dresses.          | _color verde_, a safe invariable pattern   |
| ¿Qué número calzas? Calzo el 40.                   | What shoe size do you wear? I wear a 40.       | _número_ + _calzar_ for shoes              |
| Prefiero los zapatos negros a los cafés.           | I prefer the black shoes to the brown ones.    | _café_ stays invariable                    |
| Esta chaqueta me queda pequeña; necesito una grande. | This jacket fits me small; I need a large one. | _quedar_ describes fit, not just "to stay" |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _número_ for clothing size instead of _talla_ — Spanish splits
  "size" into two separate words depending on whether it's clothes or shoes.
- Forgetting that _pantalones_ is always plural, even for a single pair —
  saying _el pantalón_ for one pair is also correct, but treating it as
  grammatically singular ("el pantalones") is not.
- Not matching a color adjective's gender and number to the noun, or
  forgetting that some colors (like _café_ or _naranja_) don't change at
  all — see [[A1.2.3.3 Invariable Adjectives]].
