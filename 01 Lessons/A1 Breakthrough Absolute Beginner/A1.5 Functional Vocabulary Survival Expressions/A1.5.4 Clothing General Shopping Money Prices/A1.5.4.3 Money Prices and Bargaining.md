## Explanation

Asking a price is one of the most common things you'll do while shopping, and
Spanish has a few standard ways to do it. _¿Cuánto cuesta?_ ("How much does
it cost?") and _¿Cuánto vale?_ ("How much is it worth?") are both neutral,
widely understood options — use whichever noun's already in your head, since
they mean the same thing here. You'll also hear _¿A cuánto sale?_ in some
countries (especially the Southern Cone), which works the same way but is
more regional than the first two.

Once you hear the price, _Es muy caro_ ("That's very expensive") and _Es muy
barato_ ("That's very cheap") let you react to it. You need the numbers from
[[A1.5.2.1 Cardinal Numbers 0 to 100]] to actually understand and repeat back
a price, since prices are almost always given as plain numbers: _Cuesta
treinta pesos_ ("It costs thirty pesos").

Bargaining isn't appropriate everywhere — fixed-price stores (_tiendas_,
malls, supermarkets) don't expect it, but open-air markets (_mercados_,
_ferias_) often do, including in Bolivia. _¿Me puede hacer un descuento?_
("Can you give me a discount?") is a polite way to open that conversation,
and a seller might respond with _Le hago un descuento_ ("I'll give you a
discount") or _Es lo último que le puedo dar_ ("That's the last price I can
give you") to signal they won't go lower. _¿Cuál es su mejor precio?_ ("What's
your best price?") is a softer opener than asking for a discount outright.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Phrase                         | Meaning                            | Notes                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta? / ¿Cuánto vale?    | How much does it cost? / How much is it worth? | both neutral, interchangeable        |
| Cuesta + [number].                 | It costs [number].                    | pairs with [[A1.5.2.1 Cardinal Numbers 0 to 100]] |
| Es muy caro/barato.                | It's very expensive/cheap.            | reacting to a price                          |
| ¿Me puede hacer un descuento?      | Can you give me a discount?           | polite bargaining opener                     |
| ¿Cuál es su mejor precio?          | What's your best price?               | softer than asking directly for a discount   |
| Le hago un descuento.              | I'll give you a discount.             | seller's response, agreeing                  |
| Es lo último que le puedo dar.     | That's the last price I can give you. | seller's response, declining to go lower      |

## Examples

| Spanish                                             | English                                        | Notes                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta esta chaqueta?                              | How much does this jacket cost?                       | basic price question                        |
| Cuesta ochenta bolivianos.                                 | It costs eighty bolivianos.                            | number + currency                           |
| ¡Es muy caro! ¿No tiene algo más barato?                   | That's very expensive! Don't you have something cheaper? | reacting + follow-up                     |
| En el mercado, ¿me puede hacer un descuento?               | At the market, can you give me a discount?             | bargaining, market context                  |
| ¿Cuál es su mejor precio si compro dos?                    | What's your best price if I buy two?                   | bargaining with a condition                 |
| Le hago un descuento del diez por ciento.                  | I'll give you a ten percent discount.                  | seller's response with a specific number    |

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to bargain in a fixed-price store or supermarket — this is normal
  in open-air markets (_mercados_, _ferias_) but not expected in a regular
  retail shop.
- Answering a price question with just the number and no verb — Spanish
  needs _Cuesta..._ or _Vale..._ before the number, not the bare number
  alone as a full sentence.
- Confusing _caro_ ("expensive") and _barato_ ("cheap") — a common mix-up
  for English speakers since neither resembles its English counterpart.
