## Explanation

A trip to a clothing store follows its own small script, separate from the
general _necesito/quiero_ requests from [[A1.5.3.1 Expressing Basic Needs]].
This lesson covers the fixed phrases specific to browsing, trying things on,
and getting help from a salesperson.

When a salesperson greets you, _Solo estoy mirando, gracias_ ("I'm just
looking, thanks") is the standard, polite way to decline help while you
browse — it's not rude, it's expected. When you do want to try something on,
_¿Puedo probármelo/la?_ ("Can I try it on?") uses the reflexive pronoun
attached to _probar_ (_probármelo_ = "try it on myself") — the pronoun
changes to match the gender of the item: _probármelo_ for _el vestido_,
_probármela_ for _la camisa_.

_¿Dónde está el probador?_ ("Where is the fitting room?") gets you there, and
once inside, _¿Tiene esto en otra talla/color?_ ("Do you have this in
another size/color?") is the single most useful follow-up question. Since
you're speaking with someone providing a service, this whole exchange stays
in _usted_ by default — see [[A1.5.1.3 Pragmatics Tu vs Usted]].

To wrap up, _Me lo llevo_ ("I'll take it") signals you're ready to buy, while
_Lo voy a pensar_ ("I'll think about it") is the polite way to walk away
without committing.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Phrase                              | Meaning                              | Notes                                    |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Solo estoy mirando, gracias.           | I'm just looking, thanks.               | standard response to a greeting              |
| ¿Puedo probármelo/la?                  | Can I try it on?                        | reflexive pronoun agrees with item's gender  |
| ¿Dónde está el probador?               | Where is the fitting room?              | —                                              |
| ¿Tiene esto en otra talla/color?       | Do you have this in another size/color? | most common follow-up question               |
| Me lo llevo. / Me la llevo.            | I'll take it.                           | signals you're ready to buy                  |
| Lo voy a pensar.                       | I'll think about it.                    | polite way to decline without buying         |

## Examples

| Spanish                                              | English                                       | Notes                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Le puedo ayudar en algo? — Solo estoy mirando, gracias.  | Can I help you with something? — I'm just looking, thanks. | typical opening exchange              |
| ¿Puedo probarme esta chaqueta?                            | Can I try on this jacket?                          | _probarme_ + noun                           |
| ¿Dónde está el probador, por favor?                       | Where is the fitting room, please?                 | —                                              |
| Me queda un poco grande. ¿Tiene esto en otra talla?       | It fits me a little big. Do you have this in another size? | combines fit + follow-up question    |
| Este color no me convence. ¿Lo tiene en azul?              | This color doesn't quite work for me. Do you have it in blue? | asking for a different color         |
| Está perfecto — me lo llevo.                                | It's perfect — I'll take it.                        | closing the interaction                     |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _tú_ with store staff by default — this interaction is a service
  context and defaults to _usted_, per [[A1.5.1.3 Pragmatics Tu vs Usted]].
- Forgetting to match the reflexive pronoun in _probármelo/la_ to the
  gender of the item — _probármelo_ for _el vestido_, _probármela_ for
  _la camisa_.
- Reaching for _necesito_ or _quiero_ alone in a fitting-room context instead
  of the fixed shopping phrases above — _¿Puedo probármelo?_ is the natural
  phrasing, not _Quiero probar esto_.
