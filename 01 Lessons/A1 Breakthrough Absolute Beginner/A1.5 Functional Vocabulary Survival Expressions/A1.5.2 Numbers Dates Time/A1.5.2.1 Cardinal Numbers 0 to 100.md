## Explanation

Spanish numbers 0-30 are mostly one-word forms you just have to memorize, but
from 30 onward a clear pattern takes over that makes the rest of the system
fast to learn. Once you know the ten "decade" words (_treinta, cuarenta,
cincuenta..._), you build every number in between with _y_ ("and"): _treinta y
uno_ (31), _cuarenta y siete_ (47), _cincuenta y dos_ (52).

The 16-29 range is a special case worth knowing by sight: these are written as
one fused word rather than three separate ones — _dieciséis_ (16, from _diez y
seis_), _veintidós_ (22, from _veinte y dos_). From 30 up, the pattern goes back
to three separate words joined by _y_, and that three-word pattern is what you
actually use, not a fused version like the teens and twenties have.

_Uno_ shortens to _un_ before a masculine noun the same way it does on its own —
_treinta y un libros_ ("thirty-one books"), not _treinta y uno libros_ — and
becomes _una_ before a feminine noun: _treinta y una personas_. This is the same
shortening you already know from the indefinite article _un/una_.

_Cien_ is used for exactly 100 on its own or directly before a noun (_cien
dólares_, "one hundred dollars"), while _ciento_ is the form used when a number
continues past 100: _ciento uno_ (101), _ciento veinte_ (120). You won't need
numbers much past 100 at this level, but recognizing that split is worth
having early.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Range      | Pattern                                    | Example                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| 0-15          | memorize individually                             | doce (12), quince (15)           |
| 16-29         | fused single word                                 | dieciséis (16), veintidós (22)   |
| 30, 40, 50... | decade word, memorize                             | treinta (30), cuarenta (40)      |
| 31-99         | decade + _y_ + unit, three separate words         | treinta y cinco (35), sesenta y ocho (68) |
| 100           | _cien_ alone or before a noun                     | cien libros (100 books)          |
| 101+          | _ciento_ + rest                                   | ciento diez (110)                |

- _Uno_ becomes _un_ before a masculine noun and _una_ before a feminine noun,
  even inside a compound number: _veintiún años_ (21 years), _treinta y una
  sillas_ (31 chairs).
- 16-29 fuse into one word; 30+ stay as separate words joined by _y_ — don't
  mix the two patterns.
- Use _cien_ for exactly 100; switch to _ciento_ once the number continues past
  it.

## Examples

| Spanish                             | English                          | Notes                          |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Tengo veintitrés años.                     | I'm twenty-three years old.            | fused 16-29 form                     |
| Hay cuarenta y cinco estudiantes en la clase. | There are forty-five students in the class. | 30+ pattern, decade + y + unit |
| Cuesta cien pesos.                         | It costs one hundred pesos.            | *cien* alone                          |
| Vivo en el piso ciento cinco.              | I live on floor one hundred five.      | *ciento* once past 100                |
| Compré treinta y un boletos.               | I bought thirty-one tickets.           | *un* before masculine noun            |
| Había setenta y ocho personas en la fiesta. | There were seventy-eight people at the party. | decade + y + unit           |

## Common Mistakes

- Writing 16-29 as three separate words like the 30+ pattern — _diez y seis_
  instead of _dieciséis_ mixes up the two systems.
- Forgetting to shorten _uno_ to _un/una_ before a noun inside a compound number
  — _treinta y uno libros_ should be _treinta y un libros_.
- Using _ciento_ for exactly 100 — _ciento dólares_ is wrong; it should be
  _cien dólares_.
