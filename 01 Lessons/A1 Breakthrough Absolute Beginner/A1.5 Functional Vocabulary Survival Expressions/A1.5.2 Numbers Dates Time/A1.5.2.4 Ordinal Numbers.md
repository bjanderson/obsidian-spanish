## Explanation

Ordinal numbers are the words that tell you where something falls in a
sequence — first, second, third — instead of just how many there are. In
Spanish these words, called _números ordinales_, change a little depending
on what they describe, the same way any other Spanish adjective does.

The first ten are the ones you'll actually use day to day: _primero_
(first), _segundo_ (second), _tercero_ (third), _cuarto_ (fourth), _quinto_
(fifth), _sexto_ (sixth), _séptimo_ (seventh), _octavo_ (eighth), _noveno_
(ninth), and _décimo_ (tenth). Because they act as adjectives, each one has
a masculine and a feminine form — _primero/primera_, _segundo/segunda_, and
so on — and it has to match the noun it describes.

Two of them shrink in one specific spot: _primero_ and _tercero_ drop the
final _-o_ and become _primer_ and _tercer_ right before a masculine
singular noun. You say _el primer día_ ("the first day"), not _el primero
día_. This only happens directly before a masculine singular noun — the
feminine forms _primera_ and _tercera_ never change.

Ordinal numbers usually go before the noun they describe, the same spot as
in English: _la segunda casa_ ("the second house"), not _la casa segunda_.

One thing worth knowing: past _décimo_ (10th), Spanish speakers mostly stop
using ordinals in casual conversation and switch to cardinal numbers
instead. Nobody says _el undécimo piso_ for "the eleventh floor" in everyday
speech — they say _el piso once_. You'll run into the higher ordinals in
formal writing (centuries, official documents), but for now _décimo_ is the
practical stopping point.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Number | Ordinal (m./f.)     |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| 1st    | primero / primera     |
| 2nd    | segundo / segunda      |
| 3rd    | tercero / tercera      |
| 4th    | cuarto / cuarta        |
| 5th    | quinto / quinta        |
| 6th    | sexto / sexta          |
| 7th    | séptimo / séptima      |
| 8th    | octavo / octava        |
| 9th    | noveno / novena        |
| 10th   | décimo / décima        |

- Ordinal numbers agree in gender and number with the noun they describe,
  like any other adjective.
- _Primero_ and _tercero_ shorten to _primer_ and _tercer_ directly before a
  masculine singular noun: _el primer día_, _el tercer piso_.
- Ordinals normally come before the noun: _la segunda casa_, not _la casa
  segunda_.
- Past _décimo_, everyday speech switches to cardinal numbers instead: _el
  piso once_, not _el undécimo piso_.

## Examples

| Spanish                                        | English                                          | Notes                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Vivo en el segundo piso.                       | I live on the second floor.                      | masculine agreement with _piso_          |
| Es mi primera vez en Bolivia.                  | It's my first time in Bolivia.                   | feminine _primera_ with _vez_            |
| Llegó en tercer lugar en la carrera.           | He finished in third place in the race.          | _tercer_ before masc. singular _lugar_   |
| Es el cuarto capítulo del libro.               | It's the fourth chapter of the book.             | _del_ = _de + el_                        |
| Vi al primer corredor cruzar la meta.          | I saw the first runner cross the finish line.    | personal _a_ + _al_ = _a + el_           |
| La primera pregunta del examen fue difícil.    | The first question on the exam was difficult.    | feminine _primera_, _del_ = _de + el_    |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _primero_ or _tercero_ unshortened before a masculine singular
  noun — _el primero día_ should be _el primer día_.
- Forgetting gender agreement — _la primero vez_ should be _la primera
  vez_.
- Reaching for an ordinal past _décimo_ in everyday speech instead of the
  cardinal number a native speaker would actually use — _el onceavo piso_
  sounds unnatural; say _el piso once_.
