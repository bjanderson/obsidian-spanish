## Explanation

Telling time in Spanish uses _ser_ ("to be"), but with a quirk: it's singular
for one o'clock and plural for every other hour. _Es la una_ ("It's one
o'clock") uses the singular _es_ because you're talking about "the one hour."
Every other hour uses the plural _son las_: _Son las tres_ ("It's three
o'clock"), _Son las diez_ ("It's ten o'clock"). Think of it as "it is [singular:
the one] / [plural: the several] o'clock" — the number of hours decides
singular vs. plural, not the fact that you're stating a time.

For minutes past the hour, add _y_ ("and") plus the number of minutes: _Son las
tres y diez_ ("It's 3:10"). Two minute-counts get their own special words
instead of a number: _y cuarto_ ("quarter past," :15) and _y media_ ("half
past," :30) — from _cuarto_ ("quarter") and _media_ ("half"). Past the
half-hour mark, Spanish usually counts backward from the next hour using
_menos_ ("minus/to"): _Son las ocho menos veinte_ isn't "8:20," it's "twenty
minutes to eight" (7:40).

To ask what time it is, use _¿Qué hora es?_ — a fixed question, always singular
regardless of the answer. To say when something happens, use _a la/las_
("at"): _a la una_ ("at one o'clock"), _a las siete_ ("at seven o'clock").

Beyond the clock, a few fixed phrases round out everyday time expressions:
_de la mañana_ / _de la tarde_ / _de la noche_ attach to a specific time to
mean "a.m." / "in the afternoon" / "p.m." (_Son las nueve de la noche_, "It's
9 p.m."), while _en punto_ means "on the dot," ("Son las cinco en punto," "It's
five o'clock sharp").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Pattern                     | Example                          | Meaning                     |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Es la una                      | Es la una.                              | It's 1:00 — singular only for one o'clock |
| Son las + hour                 | Son las cinco.                          | It's 5:00                          |
| Son las + hour + y + minutes   | Son las tres y diez.                    | It's 3:10                          |
| Son las + hour + y cuarto      | Son las seis y cuarto.                  | It's 6:15                          |
| Son las + hour + y media       | Son las seis y media.                   | It's 6:30                          |
| Son las + next hour + menos + minutes | Son las ocho menos veinte.       | It's 7:40 ("twenty to eight")      |
| ¿Qué hora es?                  | —                                        | "What time is it?" — always singular |
| a la/las + time                | a las siete                              | "at seven o'clock"                 |

- Use _es_ only for one o'clock (_Es la una_); every other hour uses _son_.
- Past the half hour, count down to the next hour with _menos_ rather than
  counting up past 30.
- Attach _de la mañana/tarde/noche_ to specify a.m./p.m. when it's not obvious
  from context.

| Son las diez en punto.                      | It's ten o'clock sharp.                    | *son las* + *en punto*                   |

| Son las nueve menos cinco.                  | It's 8:55 ("five to nine").                | *menos*, counting down                    |
| Llego a la una de la tarde.                 | I arrive at 1 p.m.                          | *a la una*, singular hour                 |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _son_ with one o'clock — _Son la una_ is wrong; it has to be _Es la una_.
- Counting minutes past the half hour instead of switching to _menos_ — saying
  _Son las siete y cuarenta_ instead of the more natural _Son las ocho menos
  veinte_ (both are technically understood, but the *menos* pattern is the
  standard way to express it).
- Forgetting _de la mañana/tarde/noche_ when the time alone is ambiguous between
  a.m. and p.m.
