## Explanation

Days of the week and months of the year in Spanish are both straightforward
vocabulary lists, with one habit that trips up English speakers immediately:
neither is capitalized. _lunes_ (Monday) and _enero_ (January) stay lowercase
even at the start of a sentence's subject, unlike their English counterparts.

The week in Spanish calendars and everyday speech starts on Monday, not Sunday —
so _lunes_ is the first day, and _domingo_ (Sunday) is the last. To say "on
Monday" or "on Mondays," Spanish uses the definite article instead of a
preposition like English "on": _el lunes_ ("on Monday," a specific one) or
_los lunes_ ("on Mondays," as a habit/pattern) — see
[[A1.2.2.1 Definite Articles]] for the article forms themselves.

For dates, Spanish uses a fixed formula: _el_ + day number + _de_ + month +
_de_ + year — _el 15 de marzo de 2026_ ("March 15th, 2026"). Notice the day
comes before the month, opposite of the common US "month/day" order, and
regular cardinal numbers are used for the day (_dos, tres, catorce..._) rather
than ordinals like "second" or "third" — the one exception is the first of the
month, which commonly uses _el primero_ ("the first").

Asking "what day is it" and "what's the date" use two different question
patterns: _¿Qué día es hoy?_ ("What day is it today?" — answered with the day
of the week) versus _¿Cuál es la fecha (de hoy)?_ ("What's the date?" —
answered with the full date). Keep them separate; mixing them up is a common
learner slip.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Category    | Spanish                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Days (Mon-Sun)    | lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo                                          |
| Months            | enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre |
| Date formula      | _el_ + day number + _de_ + month + _de_ + year                                                     |
| "On Monday(s)"    | _el lunes_ (a specific Monday) / _los lunes_ (every Monday, as a habit)                            |
| "What day is it?" | ¿Qué día es hoy?                                                                                    |
| "What's the date?"| ¿Cuál es la fecha (de hoy)?                                                                        |

- Days and months are never capitalized in Spanish.
- The week starts on _lunes_ (Monday); _domingo_ (Sunday) is the last day.
- Dates use cardinal numbers for the day, except _el primero_ for the 1st.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                                | Notes                             |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Hoy es martes.                                     | Today is Tuesday.                             | lowercase day name                       |
| Los sábados voy al mercado.                        | On Saturdays I go to the market.              | *los* + day, habitual                    |
| Mi cumpleaños es el 12 de julio.                   | My birthday is July 12th.                     | date formula, day before month           |
| La reunión es el primero de octubre.               | The meeting is on October 1st.                | *el primero* for the 1st                 |
| ¿Qué día es hoy? —Es jueves.                       | "What day is it today?" "It's Thursday."      | asking the day of the week                |
| ¿Cuál es la fecha de hoy? —Es el 3 de mayo de 2026. | "What's today's date?" "It's May 3rd, 2026." | asking the full date                      |

## Common Mistakes

- Capitalizing days and months — _Lunes_ and _Enero_ are wrong at the start of a
  sentence's subject; they stay lowercase.
- Saying the month before the day, following English word order — _julio 12_
  should be _el 12 de julio_.
- Using an ordinal number for dates other than the 1st — _el dos de marzo_ uses
  the cardinal _dos_, not an ordinal "second."
