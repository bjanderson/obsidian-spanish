## Explanation

Three simple sentence starters cover most everyday situations where you need
help, want something, or are looking for something: _Necesito..._ ("I
need..."), _Quiero..._ ("I want..."), and _¿Dónde está...?_ ("Where is...?").
Each attaches directly to a noun or a verb in its base form (infinitive):
_Necesito agua_ ("I need water"), _Necesito ir al baño_ ("I need to go to the
bathroom").

English speakers often reach for "need" when Spanish speakers would actually
say "want." _Necesito_ implies something closer to a real requirement — _Necesito
un médico_ ("I need a doctor") suggests genuine urgency. _Quiero_ is far more
common in everyday requests, even ones that sound like needs in English —
ordering food, asking for a favor, or expressing a preference: _Quiero un café_
("I want a coffee") is the natural way to order, not _Necesito un café_, which
would sound oddly urgent.

_¿Dónde está...?_ asks where a specific thing is located, and pairs with _hay_
("there is/are") when you're asking whether something exists nearby rather than
where a specific known thing is: _¿Hay un baño cerca?_ ("Is there a bathroom
nearby?") versus _¿Dónde está el baño?_ ("Where is the bathroom?" — once you
know one exists and want its location).

_Puedo..._ ("I can...") rounds out this group for polite requests — _¿Puedo
usar su teléfono?_ ("Can I use your phone?") is a softer, more polite way to
ask for something than a flat statement of need.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Phrase           | Meaning              | Use it for                                   |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Necesito + noun/infinitive | I need...              | genuine requirements, urgency                        |
| Quiero + noun/infinitive   | I want...               | everyday requests, orders, preferences               |
| ¿Dónde está...?      | Where is...?                | locating a specific, known thing                     |
| ¿Hay...?              | Is/Are there...?            | asking whether something exists nearby                |
| ¿Puedo...?            | Can I...?                   | polite requests, asking permission                    |

- _Necesito_ and _quiero_ both attach directly to a noun or an infinitive verb —
  no extra connecting word needed.
- Reach for _quiero_ in everyday requests (ordering, asking for things) even
  where English instinct says "need" — _necesito_ reads as more urgent.
- _¿Hay...?_ asks about existence; _¿Dónde está...?_ asks about the location of
  something already known to exist.

## Examples

| Spanish                            | English                              | Notes                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Necesito ayuda, por favor.                 | I need help, please.                        | *necesito* + noun                        |
| Quiero un jugo de naranja.                 | I want an orange juice.                     | *quiero*, everyday request               |
| ¿Dónde está la estación de tren?           | Where is the train station?                 | *dónde está*, specific location          |
| ¿Hay una farmacia cerca de aquí?           | Is there a pharmacy near here?              | *hay*, asking about existence            |
| ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?                  | Can I pay with a card?                       | *puedo*, polite request                   |
| Necesito llamar a mi familia.              | I need to call my family.                    | *necesito* + infinitive                   |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _necesito_ for a casual order or preference — _Necesito un café_ sounds
  urgent; _Quiero un café_ is the natural everyday phrasing.
- Confusing _¿Hay...?_ and _¿Dónde está...?_ — asking _¿Dónde está un baño?_
  when you don't yet know one exists nearby should instead be _¿Hay un baño
  cerca?_
- Adding an unnecessary preposition after _necesito_ or _quiero_ — both attach
  directly to the noun or infinitive with no "to" or "for" in between.
