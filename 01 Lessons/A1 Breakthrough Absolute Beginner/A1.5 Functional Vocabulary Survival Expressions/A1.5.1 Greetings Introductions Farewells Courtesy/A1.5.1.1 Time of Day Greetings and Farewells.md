## Explanation

Spanish greetings change with the time of day, the same way English switches
between "good morning" and "good evening." The three you'll use constantly are
_buenos días_ (morning, until roughly midday), _buenas tardes_ (afternoon, into
early evening), and _buenas noches_ (night — used both as a greeting and as a
farewell when parting for the night, like "good night").

Notice _buenos_ pairs with _días_ (masculine, plural) while _buenas_ pairs with
_tardes_ and _noches_ (feminine, plural) — the greeting agrees with the gender of
the noun that follows it, the same agreement pattern you already know from
adjectives. There's no hard cutoff time between them; treat late morning /
early afternoon and early evening / night as flexible, judged by what feels
natural in the moment rather than a strict clock.

_Hola_ ("hi/hello") works at any time of day and can stack with the time-based
greeting — _¡Hola, buenos días!_ is common and friendly, not redundant. For
farewells, _adiós_ ("goodbye") is the general all-purpose one, while _hasta
luego_ ("see you later"), _hasta mañana_ ("see you tomorrow"), and _nos vemos_
("we'll see each other" / "see you") are more common in casual daily
conversation than a flat _adiós_, which can sound slightly more final.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Spanish        | English                        | When to use                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Buenos días         | Good morning                          | morning, until around midday              |
| Buenas tardes       | Good afternoon                        | afternoon into early evening              |
| Buenas noches       | Good evening / Good night             | night — both a greeting and a farewell    |
| Hola                | Hi / Hello                            | any time, often combined with the above   |
| Adiós               | Goodbye                               | general farewell, any time                |
| Hasta luego         | See you later                         | casual farewell, expecting to meet again  |
| Hasta mañana        | See you tomorrow                      | farewell at the end of the day            |
| Nos vemos           | See you / We'll see each other        | casual, common among friends              |

- _Buenos_ agrees with the masculine plural _días_; _buenas_ agrees with the
  feminine plural _tardes_ and _noches_.
- _Buenas noches_ does double duty — it greets someone at night and also says
  goodbye when the visit or day is ending.
- There's no strict clock cutoff between _tardes_ and _noches_ — use judgment
  based on when it feels like evening has set in.

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                              | Notes                          |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| ¡Buenos días! ¿Cómo amaneciste?             | Good morning! How did you wake up?            | morning greeting, common courtesy phrase |
| Buenas tardes, ¿tiene un momento?           | Good afternoon, do you have a moment?         | formal-leaning afternoon greeting    |
| Buenas noches, que descanses.               | Good night, sleep well.                        | night farewell                       |
| Hola, ¿qué tal todo?                        | Hi, how's everything?                          | *hola* alone, casual                 |
| Nos vemos el lunes, que tengas buen fin de semana. | See you Monday, have a good weekend.    | casual farewell with well-wishing    |
| Hasta luego, fue un placer conocerte.       | See you later, it was a pleasure meeting you. | farewell after a first meeting       |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _bueno días_ instead of _buenos días_ — the greeting must agree in gender
  and number with the noun, not stay in a fixed singular form.
- Treating _buenas noches_ as only a farewell — it's also the standard greeting
  when arriving somewhere at night.
- Overusing _adiós_ in everyday casual exchanges — _hasta luego_ or _nos vemos_
  sound more natural when you expect to see the person again soon.
