## Explanation

Introducing yourself in Spanish leans on a small set of fixed phrases you'll use
constantly, so it's worth learning them as whole chunks rather than building them
word by word every time. The most common way to give your name is _Me llamo..._
("My name is..." — literally "I call myself..."), though _Soy..._ ("I am...")
works just as well and is a little more direct.

Asking someone else's name mirrors the same structure: _¿Cómo te llamas?_
(informal, _tú_) or _¿Cómo se llama usted?_ (formal). Once introductions are
underway, _Mucho gusto_ ("Nice to meet you," literally "much pleasure") is the
standard response — you'll hear it constantly and should have it ready without
thinking. _Encantado_ (said by a man) or _encantada_ (said by a woman) is a
slightly more polished alternative, and it's the speaker's own gender that
determines the ending, not the other person's.

Beyond your name, a basic self-introduction usually covers where you're from —
_Soy de..._ ("I'm from...") — and sometimes what you do — _Soy..._ plus a
profession, or _Trabajo en/como..._ ("I work at/as..."). Stringing a few of
these together (_Me llamo Ana, soy de Perú, y trabajo como maestra_) is enough
for a natural, complete self-introduction at this level.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Spanish                     | English                       | Notes                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Me llamo...                     | My name is...                        | most common way to give your name             |
| Soy...                           | I am...                              | also works for name, origin, or profession    |
| ¿Cómo te llamas?                | What's your name?                    | informal (_tú_)                                |
| ¿Cómo se llama usted?           | What's your name?                    | formal (_usted_)                               |
| Mucho gusto                     | Nice to meet you                     | standard response after an introduction        |
| Encantado / Encantada           | Delighted (to meet you)              | ending matches the *speaker's* own gender      |
| Soy de...                       | I'm from...                          | stating origin                                  |
| Trabajo en/como...              | I work at/as...                      | stating profession or workplace                |

- _Encantado/encantada_ agrees with the gender of the person saying it, not the
  person being addressed.
- _Me llamo_ and _¿Cómo te llamas?_ use a reflexive structure ("I call myself" /
  "what do you call yourself") — you'll learn the grammar behind reflexive verbs
  later; for now, just learn these as fixed phrases.
- Combine name, origin, and profession into one flowing introduction rather than
  three separate disconnected sentences.

## Examples

| Spanish                                          | English                                     | Notes                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Hola, me llamo Carlos. Mucho gusto.                   | Hi, my name is Carlos. Nice to meet you.          | *me llamo* + *mucho gusto*              |
| ¿Cómo te llamas?                                      | What's your name?                                  | informal                                |
| Soy Marta, encantada de conocerte.                    | I'm Marta, delighted to meet you.                  | *soy* + *encantada*, female speaker     |
| Soy de Bolivia y trabajo como ingeniero.               | I'm from Bolivia and I work as an engineer.        | origin + profession                     |
| Mucho gusto, señora. ¿Cómo se llama usted?            | Nice to meet you, ma'am. What's your name?         | formal register                         |
| Me llamo Diego, soy de Colombia, y estudio medicina.  | My name is Diego, I'm from Colombia, and I study medicine. | name + origin + activity        |

## Common Mistakes

- Saying _Yo llamo..._ instead of _Me llamo..._ — the reflexive _me_ is required;
  dropping it changes the meaning to "I call [someone/something]."
- Using _encantado_ regardless of the speaker's own gender — a woman introducing
  herself says _encantada_, not _encantado_.
- Mixing _tú_ and _usted_ forms in the same exchange — _¿Cómo te llamas, señor?_
  mismatches an informal verb form with a formal address.
