## Explanation

In English, you only find out a sentence is a question when you reach the
question mark at the end. Spanish doesn't make you wait. Every question
starts with an upside-down question mark, *¿*, and every exclamation starts
with an upside-down exclamation mark, *¡*. The normal mark still closes the
sentence, so the punctuation comes in a matched pair — one at the start, one
at the end.

This pair is called *puntuación de apertura y cierre* ("opening and closing
punctuation"). The opening mark exists purely to warn the reader ahead of
time: "get ready, this is a question" or "get ready, this is an
exclamation." That's especially useful in Spanish because word order alone
often can't tell a question from a statement — *¿Vienes?* and *Vienes.* are
built from the exact same words.

The opening mark doesn't always sit at the very start of the sentence. If
only part of a sentence is the question or exclamation, only that part gets
bracketed: *Oye, ¿vienes?* ("Hey, are you coming?") keeps *Oye* outside the
question marks because *Oye* itself isn't part of the question. The same
rule applies to exclamations mid-sentence.

A sentence can also mix both marks when it's an exclamatory question — a
question asked with real surprise or emotion. Native speakers commonly write
this as *¿¡Qué dices!?* or *¡¿Qué dices?!*, with the marks nested in either
order.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Every question opens with *¿* and closes with *?*.
- Every exclamation opens with *¡* and closes with *!*.
- If the question or exclamation is only part of the sentence, the marks
  wrap only that part — not the whole sentence.
- Exclamatory questions can combine both marks at each end: *¿¡...!?* or
  *¡¿...?!*.
- Never drop the opening mark, even though English has no equivalent — this
  is one of the most common mistakes English speakers make when writing
  Spanish.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| ¿Dónde está el baño? | Where is the bathroom? | Whole sentence is the question, so both marks bracket it. |
| ¡Qué calor hace hoy! | It's so hot today! | Whole sentence is the exclamation. |
| Oye, ¿tienes hambre? | Hey, are you hungry? | *Oye* sits outside the bracket because it isn't part of the question. |
| Bueno, ¡vámonos ya! | Okay, let's go already! | *Bueno* sits outside the bracket; only the exclamation itself is marked. |
| ¿Vas a venir a la fiesta, o no? | Are you coming to the party, or not? | The question mark opens right where the interrogative meaning starts. |
| ¡¿Ganamos?! | We won?! | Exclamatory question — both marks appear at each end, nested. |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the opening *¿* or *¡* because English writing never uses one.
- Bracketing the whole sentence when only part of it is the question or
  exclamation, instead of opening the mark right where the question or
  exclamation actually starts.
- Using a regular *!* or *?* instead of the inverted mark at the start —
  autocorrect and English-language keyboards often make this the default.
- Forgetting that a sentence can carry both a comma-separated statement and
  a bracketed question or exclamation in the same line, as in *Oye, ¿vienes?*
