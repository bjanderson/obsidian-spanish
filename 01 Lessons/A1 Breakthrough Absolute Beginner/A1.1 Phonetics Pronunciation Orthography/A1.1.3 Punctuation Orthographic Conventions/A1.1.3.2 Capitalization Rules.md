## Explanation

English capitalizes a lot of things by habit — days of the week, months,
languages, nationalities, and people's titles. Spanish capitalizes far
fewer of them. The good news is the underlying rule is simple: Spanish only
capitalizes true proper nouns — specific names — and treats everything else
as an ordinary word.

That means days of the week (*lunes, martes*), months (*enero, febrero*),
seasons (*invierno, verano*), languages (*español, inglés*), and
nationalities or other words derived from a place (*mexicano, francesa*)
all stay lowercase in Spanish, even at the start of a sentence's subject —
unless the word happens to start the sentence itself, in which case it's
capitalized only because it's the first word, not because of what it is.

Titles like *señor, señora, doctor,* and *profesora* also stay lowercase
when written out in full and used with someone's name (*el doctor
Ramírez*). They're only capitalized when abbreviated (*Sr., Sra., Dr.*).

What Spanish *does* capitalize matches English closely: the first word of a
sentence, people's names, place names (cities, countries, rivers), and the
names of specific organizations or institutions. So the rule to hold onto
is: capitalize the name of a specific, one-of-a-kind thing; leave everything
else — including categories of time, language, and nationality — in
lowercase.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Days of the week and months stay lowercase: *lunes, viernes, marzo,
  diciembre*.
- Seasons stay lowercase: *primavera, verano, otoño, invierno*.
- Languages stay lowercase: *hablo español, estudio inglés*.
- Nationalities and other place-derived adjectives/nouns stay lowercase:
  *soy mexicana, es un libro francés*.
- Titles stay lowercase when spelled out with a name: *el señor García, la
  doctora Pérez*. Only their abbreviations are capitalized: *Sr., Dra.*
- Proper nouns still capitalize as expected: people's names, cities,
  countries, and specific organizations.
- The first word of a sentence is always capitalized, regardless of what
  kind of word it is.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Hoy es lunes. | Today is Monday. | *lunes* stays lowercase even though it's a day of the week. |
| Mi cumpleaños es en abril. | My birthday is in April. | *abril* stays lowercase. |
| Ella habla portugués y español. | She speaks Portuguese and Spanish. | Both language names stay lowercase. |
| Somos colombianos. | We are Colombian. | Nationality stays lowercase. |
| Vino a verme el doctor Salas. | Doctor Salas came to see me. | *doctor* stays lowercase when spelled out with the name. |
| El Dr. Salas vive en Bogotá. | Dr. Salas lives in Bogotá. | The abbreviation *Dr.* is capitalized; *Bogotá* is a proper noun. |
| Prefiero el invierno al verano. | I prefer winter to summer. | Both season names stay lowercase. |

## Common Mistakes

- Capitalizing days and months out of English habit — *Lunes* and *Abril*
  are both wrong; they should be *lunes* and *abril*.
- Capitalizing language and nationality words — *Español* and *Mexicano*
  should be *español* and *mexicano*.
- Capitalizing a spelled-out title like *Doctor* or *Señor* when it appears
  with someone's name, instead of only capitalizing its abbreviation.
- Assuming every word that's capitalized in the English translation must
  also be capitalized in the Spanish sentence.
