## Explanation

Spanish has five vowel sounds — *a, e, i, o, u* — and each one has exactly one
pronunciation, always. That's the single biggest difference from English
vowels, where a letter like "a" can sound like the "a" in "cat," "father,"
or "about," depending on the word. In Spanish, *a* always sounds the same,
no matter what word it's in or where the stress falls.

These are called "pure" vowel sounds because each one stays a single,
steady sound from start to finish — it doesn't glide into a second sound.
English speakers often add that glide without noticing, because most
English vowels do it naturally:

- **a** — like the "a" in "father." (*casa*)
- **e** — like the "e" in "they," but held pure, without sliding into
  a "y" sound at the end. (*bebé*)
- **i** — like the "ee" in "machine." (*sí*)
- **o** — like the "o" in "go," but held pure, without sliding into
  a "w" sound at the end. (*oso*)
- **u** — like the "oo" in "flu." (*tú*)

Another key habit to unlearn: English often blurs an unstressed vowel into
a neutral "uh" sound (the schwa) — think of the "a" in "about." Spanish
vowels never do this. Even in an unstressed syllable, *a, e, i, o, u* keep
their full, clear sound. Say every vowel in *pronunciación* as clearly as
the one you'd stress, and you're already closer to a native accent than
most beginners.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Spanish has exactly five vowel sounds, one per vowel letter: *a, e, i, o, u*.
- Each vowel has one pronunciation that never changes with context or stress.
- *E* and *o* are pure sounds in Spanish — don't let them glide into
  "ey" or "ow" the way English "ay" and "oh" do.
- Unstressed vowels stay full and clear; Spanish has no schwa sound.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| casa | house | Pure *a*, like "ah" in "father." |
| bebé | baby | Pure *e*, like "eh" in "they" — no glide into "ee" at the end. |
| sí | yes | Pure *i*, like "ee" in "machine." |
| oso | bear | Pure *o*, like "oh" in "go" — no glide into "oo" at the end. |
| tú | you (informal) | Pure *u*, like "oo" in "flu." |
| Voy a comer una manzana. | I'm going to eat an apple. | Every vowel in this sentence keeps its full sound, even the unstressed ones. |

## Common Mistakes

- Turning *e* into a glide, so *bebé* sounds like "beh-BEY" with an added
  "y" sound at the end instead of a clean, pure *e*.
- Turning *o* into a glide, so *oso* sounds like "OH-woh-so" instead of a
  clean, pure *o*.
- Reducing an unstressed vowel to a schwa, the way English does with the
  "a" in "about" — Spanish vowels don't soften like that, stressed or not.
- Assuming a vowel's sound depends on the letters around it, like in
  English (compare "cat," "father," and "any"). In Spanish, each vowel
  keeps the same sound in every word.
