## Explanation

Spanish has five vowel sounds: *a, e, i, o, u*. Each one has exactly one sound,
and that sound never changes, no matter where the vowel sits in a word or
whether the syllable is stressed.

English vowels do not work this way. The letter "a" in English can sound like
"cat," "father," or "cake," depending on the word. Spanish vowels never shift
like that — *a* always sounds like the "a" in "father," never like the "a" in
"cake."

English speakers also tend to turn a single vowel sound into two sounds
glued together, called a diphthong. Say the English word "no" out loud — it
starts as "oh" and glides into "oo." The Spanish word *no* uses only the pure
"oh" sound, held steady, with no glide at the end. Keeping each Spanish vowel
short, pure, and unchanging is the single biggest habit to build in this
lesson.

Here are the five pure vowel sounds:

- ***a*** — like the "a" in "father." Mouth open wide.
- ***e*** — like the "e" in "bed," but shorter and crisper. Lips relaxed, mouth
  slightly open.
- ***i*** — like the "ee" in "see." Lips pulled back, mouth barely open.
- ***o*** — like the "o" in "for," without the "w" glide English adds at the
  end. Lips rounded.
- ***u*** — like the "oo" in "food." Lips rounded and nearly closed.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Each vowel keeps its one sound in every word, in every position, stressed
  or unstressed.
- Do not glide a vowel into a second sound. *No* is one short "oh," not
  "oh-oo."
- Do not reduce unstressed vowels to a mushy "uh" sound the way English
  does (compare the "a" in "about" to the full, clear *a* in Spanish
  *casa*). Spanish vowels stay clear and full-value even when unstressed.
- Vowels are always short and crisp — Spanish does not lengthen a vowel for
  emphasis the way English draws out "no" or "yes."

## Examples

| Spanish | English    | Notes                                                      |
| ------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| casa    | house      | Pure *a* in both syllables — no glide, no reduction.         |
| mesa    | table      | *e* stays crisp in both the stressed and unstressed syllable. |
| libro   | book       | *i* is short and tight, not drawn out like English "ee."    |
| perro   | dog        | *e* and *o* both stay pure — no diphthong on the final *o*.  |
| uno     | one        | *u* is fully rounded; *o* has no "w" glide at the end.       |
| azul    | blue       | Stressed final *u* — hold it short and rounded, not long.    |

## Common Mistakes

- Turning *o* into "oh-oo" (adding an English-style glide). Say *no* with one
  short, pure sound.
- Turning *e* into "ey" (like the English letter name). Spanish *e* is closer
  to the "e" in "bed."
- Reducing an unstressed vowel to "uh," as English does. In *casa*, both *a*
  sounds stay full and clear — neither one turns into a schwa.
- Lengthening a vowel for emphasis, as in stretching *sí* into "seeee." Keep
  Spanish vowels short even when the speaker is excited.
