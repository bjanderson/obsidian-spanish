## Explanation

The Spanish alphabet (*el abecedario* or *el alfabeto*) looks almost like the
English one, but it has one extra letter and every letter has its own Spanish
name. Knowing these names matters more than it seems — you need them to spell
your name out loud, to understand someone spelling a word for you, and to
follow along when a Spanish speaker says a word letter by letter.

Spanish has 27 letters: the 26 you already know from English, plus **Ñ**
(*eñe*). The Ñ is a distinct letter, not a decorated N — it changes the
meaning of a word (*ano* = anus, *año* = year) and gets its own place in the
dictionary, right after N.

A few other letters are worth flagging early because their names or sounds
often surprise English speakers:

- **H** (*hache*) is always silent in Spanish — *hola* starts with the vowel
  sound, not an "h" sound.
- **J** (*jota*) has a harsh, breathy sound close to the English "h" in "hot,"
  not the English "j" sound.
- **LL** and **RR** used to be counted as their own letters in the alphabet.
  Modern spelling reform (2010) dropped that — they're now treated as
  two-letter combinations, alphabetized under L and R. You'll still hear
  older materials list them separately, so don't be confused if you see that.
- **K** and **W** exist in the alphabet but appear almost only in loanwords
  (*kilómetro*, *whisky*) — Spanish itself rarely needs them.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Spanish has 27 letters: the 26 English letters plus **Ñ**.
- Every letter has its own name, and those names are what you say when
  spelling a word aloud — not the letter's sound in isolation.
- **H** is always silent.
- **Ñ** is a separate letter from N, both in the alphabet and in meaning.
- **LL** and **RR** are two-letter combinations, not separate alphabet
  letters, under current spelling rules (post-2010).

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| ¿Cómo se escribe tu nombre? | How do you spell your name? | The question you'll hear before someone spells a word for you. |
| Se escribe con "eñe", no con "ene". | It's written with "eñe," not "ene." | Distinguishing Ñ from N by name. |
| Mi apellido se deletrea A-N-D-E-R-S-O-N. | My last name is spelled A-N-D-E-R-S-O-N. | *Deletrear* = to spell out loud, letter by letter. |
| La hache es muda en español. | The H is silent in Spanish. | States the silent-H rule directly. |
| "Jamón" empieza con jota, no con ge. | "Jamón" starts with a J, not a G. | J and G can both make the harsh "h" sound before e/i, so Spanish speakers often clarify which one. |

## Common Mistakes

- Reading Spanish letter names as if they were English ones — e.g. saying
  "aitch" for H instead of *hache*, or "double-u" for W instead of *doble ve*.
- Pronouncing the H in words like *hola* or *hotel* — it must stay silent.
- Treating Ñ as an accented N instead of its own letter — this changes word
  meaning, not just pronunciation.
- Skipping straight to sounding out words without first learning to spell out
  loud — *deletrear* is a skill you'll need constantly (phone numbers,
  emails, forms, names).
