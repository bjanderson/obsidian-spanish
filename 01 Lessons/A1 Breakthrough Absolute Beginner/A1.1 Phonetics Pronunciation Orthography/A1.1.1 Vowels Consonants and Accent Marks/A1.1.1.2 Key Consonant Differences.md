## Explanation

Most Spanish consonants sound close to their English equivalents. A few need
special attention because they don't behave the way an English speaker would
expect:

- **h** is always silent: *hola*, *hijo*.
- **j** sounds like the English "h": *jugo*, *ojo*.
- **ll** sounds like the English "y": *llamar*, *pollo*.
- **ñ** adds an "ny" sound: *niño*, *año*.
- **rr** is a strong rolled "r": *perro*.

These five differences cover most of the consonant sounds that trip up new
learners, since the rest of the Spanish consonants map closely enough to
English to not need separate rules.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- *h* is silent; *j* sounds like "h"; *ll* sounds like "y"; *ñ* adds "ny"; *rr* is
  a rolled "r".
- These five letters/combinations are the main places Spanish consonants diverge
  from what an English speaker would expect.
