## Explanation

In English, the adjective comes before the noun: "the red car." In Spanish,
the standard, default order is the opposite — the noun comes first and the
adjective follows it: *el carro rojo*, literally "the car red." This is the
pattern you should reach for by default whenever you describe something
with a regular, descriptive adjective.

This noun-first order applies to the vast majority of descriptive
adjectives — color, size, shape, nationality, and most physical or
personality traits all follow the noun: *una mesa grande* (a big table),
*un libro interesante* (an interesting book), *una mujer mexicana* (a
Mexican woman). Once you've matched the adjective's ending to the noun
using [[A1.2.3.1 Gender and Number Agreement]], placing it right after the
noun completes the phrase.

You will eventually meet a small set of adjectives that can go before the
noun instead — usually to add emphasis, express something more subjective,
or when the adjective is one of a handful of common short words like
*bueno, malo, grande,* or a number. Those cases shift meaning or tone in
ways that are worth their own lesson later; for now, the reliable default
you'll use constantly is noun first, adjective second, and that default is
correct for the overwhelming majority of everyday descriptions.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Default order: noun, then adjective — *el carro rojo*, not *el rojo
  carro*.
- This applies to color, size, shape, nationality, and most descriptive
  adjectives.
- Multiple adjectives can stack after the noun, usually joined with *y*:
  *una casa grande y bonita* (a big, pretty house).
- The adjective still has to agree in gender and number with the noun it
  follows, per [[A1.2.3.1 Gender and Number Agreement]] — placement and
  agreement are two separate rules working together.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| el carro rojo | the red car | Noun *carro*, then adjective *rojo*. |
| una mesa grande | a big table | Noun *mesa*, then adjective *grande*. |
| un libro interesante | an interesting book | Noun *libro*, then adjective *interesante*. |
| una mujer mexicana | a Mexican woman | Noun *mujer*, then nationality adjective *mexicana*. |
| unos zapatos negros | some black shoes | Noun *zapatos*, then adjective *negros*, both plural. |
| una casa grande y bonita | a big, pretty house | Two adjectives stacked after the noun with *y*. |

## Common Mistakes

- Translating English word order directly and putting the adjective before
  the noun: saying *el rojo carro* instead of *el carro rojo*.
- Forgetting to also apply gender/number agreement once the adjective is
  correctly placed after the noun.
- Overcorrecting and assuming every adjective must go after the noun,
  without knowing that a small set of common short adjectives can
  precede it in certain contexts (a topic for a later lesson).
- Dropping the *y* when stacking two adjectives, producing an awkward run
  of words instead of *grande y bonita*.
