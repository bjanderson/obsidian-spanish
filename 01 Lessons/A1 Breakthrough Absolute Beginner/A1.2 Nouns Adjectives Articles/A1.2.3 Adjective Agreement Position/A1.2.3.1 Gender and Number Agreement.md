## Explanation

In English, an adjective never changes — "red" describes a red car and a
red house the exact same way. In Spanish, adjectives change their ending to
match the noun they describe, in both gender and number. This is called
agreement, and once you get the pattern, it becomes automatic.

Most adjectives that end in *-o* in their base (dictionary) form have four
possible endings: *-o* for masculine singular, *-a* for feminine singular,
*-os* for masculine plural, *-as* for feminine plural. Take *rojo* (red):
*el carro rojo* (the red car), *la casa roja* (the red house), *los carros
rojos* (the red cars), *las casas rojas* (the red houses). The adjective is
tracking the noun, not deciding anything on its own — you already know the
noun's gender and number from [[A1.2.1.1 Standard Gender Endings]], and the
adjective just falls in line.

Adjectives that end in a consonant or in *-e* in their base form only
change for number, not gender — they use the same word for masculine and
feminine: *azul* (blue) → *el carro azul, la casa azul* (no change for
gender), but *los carros azules, las casas azules* (add *-es* for plural,
following the same consonant-ending pluralization rule from
[[A1.2.1.3 Pluralization Rules]]). Adjectives ending in *-e* work the same
way: *inteligente* → *el chico inteligente, la chica inteligente*, both
identical, with *-s* added for the plural forms.

The rule to hold onto: find the noun's gender and number first, then match
the adjective's ending to it. If the adjective's base form ends in *-o*,
it has four forms to choose from. If it ends in a consonant or *-e*, it
only changes for number.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- *-o*-ending adjectives have four forms: *-o, -a, -os, -as* (*rojo, roja,
  rojos, rojas*).
- Consonant- or *-e*-ending adjectives only change for number, not gender:
  *azul → azul, azules*; *inteligente → inteligente, inteligentes*.
- The adjective always matches the noun's gender and number, never the
  other way around.
- Adding *-es* for plural on consonant-ending adjectives follows the same
  rule as pluralizing nouns.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| el carro rojo | the red car | Masc. sing. → *-o*. |
| la casa roja | the red house | Fem. sing. → *-a*. |
| los carros rojos | the red cars | Masc. plural → *-os*. |
| las casas rojas | the red houses | Fem. plural → *-as*. |
| el vestido azul | the blue dress | *azul* doesn't change for gender. |
| las paredes azules | the blue walls | *azul* adds *-es* for plural only. |
| una chica inteligente | a smart girl | *-e* ending adjective, no gender change. |

## Common Mistakes

- Adding *-a* to a consonant- or *-e*-ending adjective to try to make it
  feminine, producing a wrong form like *azula* instead of *azul*.
- Forgetting to pluralize the adjective when the noun is plural: saying
  *las casas roja* instead of *las casas rojas*.
- Matching the adjective's ending to English gender assumptions instead of
  the actual Spanish noun's gender.
- Using only *-s* to pluralize a consonant-ending adjective instead of
  *-es*, mirroring the same mistake possible with nouns.
