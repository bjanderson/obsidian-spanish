## Explanation

English uses "a" or "an" for one unspecified thing, and "some" for more
than one. Spanish does the same job with four words that, like the
definite articles in [[A1.2.2.1 Definite Articles]], have to match the
noun's gender and number: *un, una, unos,* and *unas*.

*Un* goes with singular masculine nouns: *un libro* (a book). *Una* goes
with singular feminine nouns: *una mesa* (a table). *Unos* goes with plural
masculine nouns and means "some": *unos libros* (some books). *Unas* goes
with plural feminine nouns: *unas mesas* (some tables).

The pattern for choosing the right one is identical to definite articles —
figure out the noun's gender and number first, then pick the matching
word. The only new piece here is the plural forms, *unos/unas*, which don't
have a direct one-word English equivalent; "some" is the closest match, but
Spanish speakers also use *unos/unas* loosely to mean "a few" or
"approximately," as in *unos veinte minutos* ("about twenty minutes").

One more thing worth noting now, even though it's covered in full in
[[A1.2.2.3 Basic Article Omission]]: Spanish often drops the indefinite
article entirely in places English would keep "a," most commonly when
stating someone's profession after *ser*: *Soy profesor* ("I'm a
teacher"), not *Soy un profesor*. Don't worry about memorizing every
omission case yet — just notice that "a/an" doesn't always have a Spanish
counterpart.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- *un* → singular masculine: *un libro*.
- *una* → singular feminine: *una mesa*.
- *unos* → plural masculine, "some": *unos libros*.
- *unas* → plural feminine, "some": *unas mesas*.
- *unos/unas* can also mean "about" or "approximately" with numbers: *unos
  veinte minutos*.
- Spanish frequently drops the indefinite article where English keeps it —
  see [[A1.2.2.3 Basic Article Omission]] for the full pattern.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Tengo un perro. | I have a dog. | *un* (masc. sing.). |
| Compré una camisa nueva. | I bought a new shirt. | *una* (fem. sing.). |
| Hay unos libros en la mesa. | There are some books on the table. | *unos* (masc. plural). |
| Necesito unas ideas. | I need some ideas. | *unas* (fem. plural). |
| Llegan en unos diez minutos. | They're arriving in about ten minutes. | *unos* meaning "approximately." |
| Vive en un apartamento pequeño. | She lives in a small apartment. | *un* (masc. sing.). |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *un/una* with a plural noun instead of switching to *unos/unas*.
- Forgetting that *unos/unas* can mean "approximately," and misreading it
  as always meaning "some."
- Mismatching gender, like saying *un mesa* instead of *una mesa*.
- Adding *un/una* after *ser* with a profession, where Spanish usually
  drops it — see [[A1.2.2.3 Basic Article Omission]].
