## Explanation

"The" in English is a single word no matter what it's attached to — the
book, the books, the table. Spanish spreads "the" across four different
words, because the article has to match the noun's gender and number at
the same time. Those four words are *el, la, los,* and *las*.

*El* goes with singular masculine nouns: *el libro* (the book). *La* goes
with singular feminine nouns: *la mesa* (the table). *Los* goes with plural
masculine nouns: *los libros* (the books). *Las* goes with plural feminine
nouns: *las mesas* (the tables). Once you know a noun's gender — from
[[A1.2.1.1 Standard Gender Endings]] — and whether it's singular or plural,
picking the right article is mechanical.

Spanish also uses definite articles in a few places where English drops
"the" entirely. Talking about a general category — not one specific thing,
but the whole idea of it — still takes the article: *Me gusta la música*
("I like music," literally "I like the music"). Days of the week take the
article when you're talking about something happening on that day: *Nos
vemos el lunes* ("We'll see each other on Monday") — but not right after
*ser*: *Hoy es lunes* ("Today is Monday"), no article. You'll meet more of
these general-category and day-of-week patterns as you read and listen
more; for now, the main job is locking in *el/la/los/las* and matching
them correctly to gender and number.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- *el* → singular masculine: *el libro*.
- *la* → singular feminine: *la mesa*.
- *los* → plural masculine: *los libros*.
- *las* → plural feminine: *las mesas*.
- Use the definite article for general categories, even where English
  drops it: *Me gusta la música*.
- Use the definite article with a day of the week when saying something
  happens on that day, but not right after *ser*: *Nos vemos el lunes*
  vs. *Hoy es lunes*.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| el libro está en la mesa | the book is on the table | *el* (masc. sing.), *la* (fem. sing.). |
| los libros están en la mesa | the books are on the table | *los* (masc. plural). |
| las ventanas están abiertas | the windows are open | *las* (fem. plural). |
| Me gusta la música clásica. | I like classical music. | General category still takes the article. |
| Trabajo los sábados. | I work on Saturdays. | Day of the week takes the article for a recurring event. |
| Hoy es sábado. | Today is Saturday. | No article right after *ser*. |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *el* or *la* without checking the noun's actual gender, instead of
  matching it the way [[A1.2.1.1 Standard Gender Endings]] describes.
- Dropping the article for general categories because English does: saying
  *Me gusta música* instead of *Me gusta la música*.
- Adding an article after *ser* with a day of the week: saying *Hoy es el
  sábado* instead of *Hoy es sábado*.
- Mismatching number, like pairing *los* with a singular noun.
