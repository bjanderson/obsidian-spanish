## Explanation

Making a Spanish noun plural is more predictable than English, once you
know which ending the singular word has. The rule splits into two clean
cases based on the noun's last letter.

If the noun ends in a vowel (*a, e, i, o, u*), just add *-s*: *casa* →
*casas*, *coche* → *coches*, *libro* → *libros*. This covers the majority
of nouns you'll meet, since so many Spanish words already end in a vowel.

If the noun ends in a consonant, add *-es* instead of just *-s*: *ciudad*
→ *ciudades*, *papel* → *papeles*, *reloj* → *relojes*. The extra *-e*
makes the plural pronounceable — Spanish doesn't stack consonants at the
end of a word the way *-ds* or *-ls* would in English.

Two smaller wrinkles are worth knowing early. First, a noun ending in *-z*
changes that *z* to *c* before adding *-es*: *lápiz* (pencil) → *lápices*,
*vez* (time/occasion) → *veces*. Second, when a noun's stress would
otherwise shift when you add a syllable, Spanish sometimes adds or drops a
written accent to keep the stress in the same place: *joven* (young
person) → *jóvenes* keeps the stress on the same syllable by adding an
accent, while *nación* → *naciones* drops its accent because the stress
naturally lands there anyway once *-es* is added.

The article in front of the noun becomes plural too, and it has to match:
*el libro* → *los libros*, *la casa* → *las casas*. Gender and plural
travel together, so once you know a noun's gender from
[[A1.2.1.1 Standard Gender Endings]], pluralizing the article is automatic.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Noun ends in a vowel → add *-s*: *casa* → *casas*.
- Noun ends in a consonant → add *-es*: *ciudad* → *ciudades*.
- Noun ends in *-z* → change *z* to *c*, then add *-es*: *lápiz* →
  *lápices*.
- A written accent may appear or disappear in the plural to keep the
  stress on the same syllable: *joven* → *jóvenes*, *nación* → *naciones*.
- The article always agrees in number with the noun: *el/la* → *los/las*.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| la casa → las casas | the house → the houses | Ends in a vowel, add *-s*. |
| el coche → los coches | the car → the cars | Ends in a vowel, add *-s*. |
| la ciudad → las ciudades | the city → the cities | Ends in a consonant, add *-es*. |
| el papel → los papeles | the paper → the papers | Ends in a consonant, add *-es*. |
| el lápiz → los lápices | the pencil → the pencils | *z* changes to *c* before *-es*. |
| el joven → los jóvenes | the young man → the young men | Accent added to keep the original stress. |
| la nación → las naciones | the nation → the nations | Accent dropped; stress falls naturally on the added syllable. |

## Common Mistakes

- Adding only *-s* to a noun that ends in a consonant, producing an
  unpronounceable form like *ciudads* instead of *ciudades*.
- Forgetting to change *-z* to *-c* before adding *-es*: writing *lapizes*
  instead of *lápices*.
- Leaving the article singular while the noun becomes plural: *el casas*
  instead of *las casas*.
- Ignoring the accent shift that some words need to keep their original
  stress in the plural, such as writing *jovenes* without the accent.
