## Explanation

Now that you can conjugate *estar* (see
[[A1.3.2.1 Estar Conjugation Present Indicative]]), it's time to nail down when to
actually reach for it. Where *ser* handles the lasting, defining stuff — remember
[[A1.3.1.2 Ser Core Uses]] — *estar* covers two very different jobs: saying *where*
something is, and describing a state that could change by tomorrow (or by the hour).

Location is the easier one to get used to: no matter how permanent a building or a
city feels, Spanish always uses *estar* to say where it is. *El hotel está en el
centro* — the hotel doesn't move, but you still use *estar*, because you're pointing
at its location, not defining what it fundamentally is.

The second job — temporary states — is where *estar* really earns its keep.
Emotions, physical conditions, and even how food tastes right now all take *estar*,
because none of them are permanent facts about the thing they describe. Someone can
be *cansado* (tired) today and rested tomorrow; a soup can be *caliente* now and cold
in twenty minutes.

One more piece worth learning alongside *estar*: the word *hay* ("there is / there
are"). It's easy to mix up with *estar* because both can translate to "there is/are"
in English, but they answer different questions. Use *hay* to say whether something
exists at all (*¿Hay café?* — "Is there coffee?"), and use *estar* once you already
know it exists and want to say where it is (*El café está en la cocina* — "The coffee
is in the kitchen").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Location** — *estar* always states where something is, even for permanent
  places: *El museo está cerca del centro.*
- **Temporary physical or emotional states** — *Estoy cansado. Está feliz hoy.*
- **How food tastes or feels right now** — *La sopa está deliciosa. El café está
  frío.*
- **Hay vs. *estar***: use *hay* to say something exists (no article on a specific,
  known item), use *estar* to say where a specific, already-known thing is located.
- Common prepositions of place pair with *estar*: *en, sobre, debajo de, cerca de,
  lejos de, al lado de, entre, detrás de, delante de*.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                             | Notes                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| ¿Dónde está el hotel?                       | Where is the hotel?                      | location                             |
| Está cerca del centro.                      | It's near downtown.                      | location + *cerca de* + *el* → *del* |
| Estoy muy nervioso hoy.                     | I'm very nervous today.                  | temporary emotional state            |
| La comida está deliciosa.                   | The food is delicious.                   | how food tastes right now            |
| ¿Hay café?                                  | Is there coffee?                         | existence, *hay*                     |
| Sí, está en la cocina.                      | Yes, it's in the kitchen.                | existence confirmed, then location   |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *hay* when the thing has already been established and you just need to say
  where it is — switch to *estar* at that point (*¿Dónde están mis llaves?*, not
  *¿Dónde hay mis llaves?*).
- Forgetting the *de + el → del* contraction after prepositions like *cerca de*,
  *lejos de*, or *a la derecha de*.
- Using *ser* for how food tastes at the moment — *está rica* (it tastes good right
  now), not *es rica*, which would describe the dish's inherent quality rather than
  this particular serving.
