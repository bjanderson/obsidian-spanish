## Explanation

You already met Spanish's other "to be" verb in
[[A1.3.1.1 Ser Conjugation Present Indicative]]. Now it's *estar*'s turn. Like *ser*,
*estar* is irregular, so you'll want to memorize these forms as their own set rather
than trying to derive them from a pattern.

One detail makes *estar* easy to spot once you know it: every form except *yo* carries
a written accent mark on the last syllable — *estás, está, están*. That accent isn't
decorative — it shows you where the stress falls, and it's what separates *está* ("he
is") from words that look similar but stress a different syllable. Get used to
writing it in from the start.

The uses of *estar* — location, temporary states, how something feels or tastes right
now — are covered in the next lesson,
[[A1.3.2.2 Estar Core Uses]].

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject           | Form    |
| ------------------ | ------- |
| yo                  | estoy   |
| tú                  | estás   |
| él / ella / usted   | está    |
| nosotros            | estamos |
| ellos / ustedes     | están   |

- *Estar* is irregular in the present indicative.
- Every form except *yo* has a written accent on the stressed syllable — don't drop
  it when writing.
- *Usted* and *ustedes* share forms with *él/ella* and *ellos*, same as with *ser*.

## Examples

| Spanish                       | English                   | Notes                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| Estoy en casa.                     | I'm at home.                   | *yo* form                          |
| ¿Cómo estás?                       | How are you?                   | *tú* form, very common greeting    |
| Ella está cansada.                 | She's tired.                   | *ella* form                        |
| Estamos listos.                    | We're ready.                   | *nosotros* form                    |
| Ellos están en el trabajo.         | They're at work.               | *ellos* form                       |
| Usted está muy ocupado hoy.        | You're very busy today.        | formal *usted*, same form as *él*  |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the accent mark on *estás, está,* or *están* — this isn't just a spelling
  detail, it marks the stressed syllable.
- Confusing *estar* with *ser*: *estar* handles location and temporary states, while
  *ser* handles identity and lasting traits — see
  [[A1.3.1.1 Ser Conjugation Present Indicative]].
- Writing *esta* (this) when you mean *está* (is) — the accent is the only thing that
  tells them apart.
