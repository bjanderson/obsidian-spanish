## Explanation

So far you've learned two irregular verbs, *ser* and *estar*, which had to be
memorized form by form. Good news: most Spanish verbs aren't like that. The vast
majority follow a predictable pattern based on how their infinitive ends, and *-AR*
verbs are the biggest and most common group of all — think *hablar* (to speak),
*bailar* (to dance), *trabajar* (to work).

Here's the trick that makes regular verbs manageable: you take the infinitive, chop
off the *-ar*, and glue on a new ending depending on who's doing the action. Once you
know the six endings, you can conjugate hundreds of verbs the same way, without
memorizing each one separately.

Take *hablar*. Strip the *-ar* and you're left with the stem *habl-*. Add *-o* for
*yo*, and you get *hablo* ("I speak"). Add *-as* for *tú*, and you get *hablas* ("you
speak"). Every regular *-AR* verb works exactly the same way — learn the pattern once,
and it unlocks a huge chunk of the language.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject           | Ending | *hablar*  |
| ------------------ | ------ | --------- |
| yo                  | -o     | hablo     |
| tú                  | -as    | hablas    |
| él / ella / usted   | -a     | habla     |
| nosotros            | -amos  | hablamos  |
| ellos / ustedes     | -an    | hablan    |

- Drop the *-ar* from the infinitive to get the stem, then add the ending that
  matches the subject.
- These endings work for any regular *-AR* verb: *bailar → bailo, bailas, baila...*;
  *trabajar → trabajo, trabajas, trabaja...*
- As always, drop the subject pronoun once the ending makes the subject clear.

## Examples

| Spanish                         | English                       | Notes                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Hablo español.                        | I speak Spanish.                     | *hablar*, *yo*                   |
| ¿Bailas salsa?                        | Do you dance salsa?                  | *bailar*, *tú*                   |
| Ella trabaja en un hospital.          | She works at a hospital.             | *trabajar*, *ella*               |
| Estudiamos todos los días.            | We study every day.                  | *estudiar*, *nosotros*           |
| Mis padres viajan mucho.              | My parents travel a lot.             | *viajar*, *ellos*                |
| ¿Escuchas música?                     | Do you listen to music?              | *escuchar*, *tú*                 |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to drop the *-ar* before adding the ending — *hablar* + *-o* should give
  *hablo*, not *hablaro*.
- Mixing up the *tú* ending *-as* with the *él/ella/usted* ending *-a* — the *-s* is
  what marks *tú*.
- Applying *-AR* endings to a verb that actually ends in *-er* or *-ir* — always
  check the infinitive first.
