## Explanation

Last stop in the regular-verb family: *-IR* verbs, like *vivir* (to live), *escribir*
(to write), and *abrir* (to open). You've already seen this dance twice in
[[A1.3.3.1 AR Conjugation Endings]] and
[[A1.3.3.2 ER Conjugation Endings]] — strip the infinitive ending, add the ending
that matches the subject.

Here's the part that trips people up: *-IR* verbs share almost all their endings
with *-ER* verbs. Compare *como* (I eat) and *vivo* (I live) — both end in *-o*.
Compare *comes* and *vives* — both end in *-es*. The only place they actually
differ is *nosotros*: *-ER* verbs use *-emos* (*comemos*), while *-IR* verbs use
*-imos* (*vivimos*). Everywhere else, if you know your *-ER* endings, you already
know your *-IR* endings.

That means by the end of this lesson, you'll have a single mental toolkit that
covers the huge majority of Spanish verbs in the present tense — three infinitive
types, three sets of six endings, and one shared shortcut between two of them.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject           | Ending | *vivir*  |
| ------------------ | ------ | -------- |
| yo                  | -o     | vivo     |
| tú                  | -es    | vives    |
| él / ella / usted   | -e     | vive     |
| nosotros            | -imos  | vivimos  |
| ellos / ustedes     | -en    | viven    |

- Drop the *-ir* from the infinitive to get the stem, then add the matching ending.
- *-IR* endings match *-ER* endings everywhere except *nosotros*: *-imos* instead of
  *-emos*.
- These endings work for any regular *-IR* verb: *escribir → escribo, escribes,
  escribe...*; *abrir → abro, abres, abre...*

## Examples

| Spanish                        | English                       | Notes                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Vivo en Bogotá.                       | I live in Bogotá.                    | *vivir*, *yo*                     |
| ¿Escribes en tu diario?               | Do you write in your journal?        | *escribir*, *tú*                  |
| Ella abre la tienda a las nueve.      | She opens the store at nine.         | *abrir*, *ella*                   |
| Compartimos un apartamento.           | We share an apartment.               | *compartir*, *nosotros*           |
| Mis padres reciben visitas hoy.       | My parents are receiving guests today. | *recibir*, *ellos*              |
| ¿Asistes a la universidad?            | Do you attend the university?        | *asistir*, *tú*                   |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *-emos* instead of *-imos* for the *nosotros* form — *vivimos*, not
  *vivemos*.
- Mixing up *-IR* verbs with *-ER* verbs when the stems look similar (*vivir* vs.
  *ver*, for example) — always check the actual infinitive ending.
- Forgetting the personal *a* when the direct object is a specific person: *Recibo a
  mis padres* ("I'm receiving my parents"), not *Recibo mis padres*.
