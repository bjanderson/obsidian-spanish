## Explanation

Spanish actually has two verbs that both translate to "to be" in English — *ser* and
*estar* — and that can feel strange at first if your own language only has one.
You'll get the full picture of when to use each later; for now, this lesson is just
about learning to say *ser* itself in the present tense, so you can recognize and
produce it.

Here's the catch: *ser* is irregular, which means its forms don't come from a
predictable pattern the way regular verbs do. There's no shortcut — you just have to
sit with these five forms until they're automatic. The good news is that *ser* is one
of the most common verbs in the language, so you'll get plenty of natural repetition
just from talking about yourself and the people around you.

Once you have the forms down, the next lesson,
[[A1.3.1.2 Ser Core Uses]], covers what *ser* is actually used for — identity,
origin, profession, and traits.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Subject           | Form  |
| ------------------ | ----- |
| yo                  | soy   |
| tú                  | eres  |
| él / ella / usted   | es    |
| nosotros            | somos |
| ellos / ustedes     | son   |

- *Ser* is irregular — none of its forms come from a predictable stem, so they're
  worth memorizing as a set.
- Once the verb ending makes the subject obvious, drop the subject pronoun — that's
  how Spanish is normally spoken, not a shortcut you're taking.
- *Usted* and *ustedes* borrow the same forms as *él/ella* and *ellos* — you don't
  need to learn separate endings for the formal "you."

## Examples

| Spanish                    | English                  | Notes                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Soy de Colombia.            | I am from Colombia.       | *yo* form, dropped pronoun      |
| ¿Eres estudiante?           | Are you a student?        | *tú* form, question             |
| Mi hermano es doctor.       | My brother is a doctor.   | *él* form with a named subject  |
| Nosotros somos amigos.      | We are friends.           | *nosotros* form                 |
| Ellos son de Perú.          | They are from Peru.       | *ellos* form                    |
| Usted es muy amable.        | You are very kind.        | formal *usted*, same form as *él* |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing *ser* with *estar*: both mean "to be," but *estar* is for location and
  temporary states (see [[A1.3.2.1 Estar Conjugation Present Indicative]]).
- Using *tu eres* with a written accent error — the subject pronoun is *tú*, not the
  possessive *tu*.
- Adding an extra subject pronoun in every sentence (*yo soy, yo soy...*) instead of
  dropping it once the subject is clear.
