## Explanation

Now that you can say *soy, eres, es, somos, son* from
[[A1.3.1.1 Ser Conjugation Present Indicative]], the real question is: what do you
actually use *ser* for? A helpful way to think about it is that *ser* describes what
something *is* at its core — the kind of information that would still be true on a
different day. Where you're from, what you do for work, your nationality, your
name — none of that changes just because it's Tuesday instead of Monday.

That's the dividing line you'll lean on constantly once you meet *estar* in the next
section: *ser* is for the lasting, defining stuff, and *estar* is for the temporary
stuff — locations, moods, conditions that could flip by tomorrow. You don't need to
master that contrast yet, just keep it in the back of your mind as you go through
these uses.

One quirk worth flagging early: when you use *ser* to state someone's job, Spanish
usually drops the article that English would use. You say *Es profesora* ("She's a
teacher"), not *Es una profesora* — the article only comes back once you add a
descriptive adjective, like *Es una buena profesora* ("She's a good teacher").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Identity** — who or what someone/something is: *Soy Ana. Es un libro.*
- **Origin** — where someone/something is from: *Soy de Perú.*
- **Nationality** — *Es mexicana.*
- **Profession or role** — drop *un/una* unless you add a descriptive adjective:
  *Es profesora.* but *Es una buena profesora.*
- **Physical or personality traits that define the person** — *Es alto. Es
  simpático.*
- Descriptive adjectives usually follow the noun (*la casa roja*), but a few
  subjective, opinion-based ones can come before it (*un excelente cantante*).

## Examples

| Spanish                        | English                        | Notes                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Soy de Estados Unidos.              | I'm from the United States.        | origin                             |
| Es ingeniera.                       | She's an engineer.                 | profession, no article             |
| Es una excelente ingeniera.         | She's an excellent engineer.       | profession + adjective, article returns |
| Mi tío es colombiano.               | My uncle is Colombian.             | nationality                        |
| Ella es alta y muy amable.          | She is tall and very kind.         | physical trait + personality trait |
| Los hermanos son estadounidenses.   | The brothers are American.         | nationality, plural agreement      |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *ser* for a temporary state or feeling — *estoy cansado* ("I'm tired" right
  now), not *soy cansado*.
- Keeping *un/una* before an unmodified profession: *Es un profesor* should be *Es
  profesor* unless an adjective is added.
- Forgetting that alive/dead always take *estar*, even though they seem permanent:
  *Está viva*, not *Es viva*.
