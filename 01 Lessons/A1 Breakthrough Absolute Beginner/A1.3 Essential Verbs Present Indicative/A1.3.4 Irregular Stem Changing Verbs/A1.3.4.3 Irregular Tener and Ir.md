## Explanation

*Tener* (to have) and *ir* (to go) are two of the most-used verbs in Spanish, and
both are irregular enough to deserve their own spotlight, separate from the rest of
the [[A1.3.4.2 Yo GO Irregular Verbs|"yo-GO" group]].

*Tener* actually belongs to that same *-go* family in its *yo* form — *tengo*, not
*teno* — but it also changes its stem in the *tú, él/ella/usted,* and
*ellos/ustedes* forms (*e → ie*, just like the stem-changers from
[[A1.3.4.1 Stem Changers e ie o ue e i]]). So *tener* is really combining two
patterns you've already learned into one verb.

*Ir* is on a whole different level: it's completely irregular, with forms that don't
resemble a normal *-IR* verb at all. *Voy, vas, va, vamos, van* — there's no shortcut
here, just memorization. The payoff is huge, though: *ir* is the backbone of two
constructions you'll use constantly. *Tener que* + infinitive means "to have to do
something" (*Tengo que trabajar* — "I have to work"), and *ir a* + infinitive means
"going to do something" (*Voy a estudiar* — "I'm going to study"). Between the two,
you can talk about obligations and near-future plans in almost any conversation.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Tener** (to have)

| Subject           | Form    |
| ------------------ | ------- |
| yo                  | tengo   |
| tú                  | tienes  |
| él / ella / usted   | tiene   |
| nosotros            | tenemos |
| ellos / ustedes     | tienen  |

**Ir** (to go)

| Subject           | Form  |
| ------------------ | ----- |
| yo                  | voy   |
| tú                  | vas   |
| él / ella / usted   | va    |
| nosotros            | vamos |
| ellos / ustedes     | van   |

- *Tener* combines the *-go* irregular *yo* form with an *e → ie* stem change in
  three other forms (*tú, él/ella/usted, ellos/ustedes*); only *nosotros*
  (*tenemos*) stays fully regular.
- *Ir* is completely irregular — memorize all five forms as a set.
- *Tener que* + infinitive = "to have to..." — *Tengo que estudiar.*
- *Ir a* + infinitive = "going to..." — *Voy a estudiar.*

## Examples

| Spanish                          | English                          | Notes                        |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Tengo dos hermanos.                      | I have two brothers.                        | *tener*, *yo*                        |
| ¿Tienes tiempo hoy?                      | Do you have time today?                     | *tener*, *tú*                        |
| Tengo que trabajar mañana.               | I have to work tomorrow.                    | *tener que* + infinitive             |
| Voy al parque los domingos.              | I go to the park on Sundays.                | *ir*, *yo*, *al* contraction         |
| Vamos a estudiar esta noche.             | We're going to study tonight.               | *ir a* + infinitive                  |
| Mis padres van a Colombia en junio.      | My parents are going to Colombia in June.   | *ir*, *ellos*                        |

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the stem change in *tener*: *tú tenes* is wrong — the correct form is
  *tienes*.
- Confusing the near-future *ir a* + infinitive with the simple present — *voy a
  estudiar* ("I'm going to study") is different from *estudio* ("I study").
- Dropping the *que* after *tener* when expressing obligation: *tengo trabajar* is
  incomplete — it needs to be *tengo que trabajar*.
- Forgetting the *a + el → al* contraction: *voy al parque*, not *voy a el parque*.
