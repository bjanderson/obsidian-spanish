## Explanation

Here's a group of verbs that make life easier once you notice the pattern: they're
completely regular in every form *except* *yo*. Take *hacer* (to do/make). *Tú, él,
nosotros,* and *ellos* all conjugate exactly like a normal *-ER* verb. But *yo* breaks
the pattern and becomes *hago* instead of the expected *haco*.

What makes this group especially friendly is that the irregular *yo* forms share a
family resemblance: a lot of them end in *-go*. *Hacer → hago. Poner → pongo. Salir →
salgo. Traer → traigo.* Once that *-go* sound is in your ear, these verbs start to
feel like a set rather than a pile of separate exceptions to memorize.

A couple of verbs in this same "irregular only in *yo*" family don't end in *-go* but
follow the same one-form-only pattern: *saber → sé* and *ver → veo*. You'll meet
*tener* and its *-go* ending in the next lesson, along with the fully irregular
*ir* — they get their own lesson because they carry extra quirks beyond just the *yo*
form.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Hacer** (to do/make)

| Subject | Form |
| --- | --- |
| yo | hago |
| tú | haces |
| él/ella/usted | hace |
| nosotros | hacemos |
| ellos/ustedes | hacen |

**Poner** (to put)

| Subject | Form |
| --- | --- |
| yo | pongo |
| tú | pones |
| él/ella/usted | pone |
| nosotros | ponemos |
| ellos/ustedes | ponen |

**Salir** (to leave/go out)

| Subject | Form |
| --- | --- |
| yo | salgo |
| tú | sales |
| él/ella/usted | sale |
| nosotros | salimos |
| ellos/ustedes | salen |

- Only the *yo* form is irregular — every other form follows the regular pattern for
  that verb's ending.
- Common *-go* verbs: *hacer → hago, poner → pongo, salir → salgo, traer → traigo*.
- A couple of look-alikes break the *-go* pattern but still only change *yo*: *saber
  → sé, ver → veo*.

## Examples

| Spanish                     | English                       | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Hago la tarea por la tarde.        | I do homework in the afternoon.        | *hacer*, *yo*                     |
| ¿Qué haces los fines de semana?    | What do you do on weekends?            | *hacer*, *tú*, regular             |
| Pongo la mesa todos los días.      | I set the table every day.             | *poner*, *yo*                     |
| Salgo de casa a las siete.         | I leave the house at seven.            | *salir*, *yo*                     |
| Traigo el postre a la fiesta.      | I'm bringing dessert to the party.     | *traer*, *yo*                     |
| No sé la respuesta.                | I don't know the answer.               | *saber*, *yo*                     |

## Common Mistakes

- Applying the irregular *yo* form to other subjects — *tú* stays *haces*, not
  *hagos*; the irregularity belongs to *yo* alone.
- Forgetting that the rest of the verb stays completely regular — *hacemos, ponemos,
  salimos* don't change at all.
- Mixing up *sé* (I know, from *saber*) with *se* (the reflexive pronoun) — they're
  spelled almost the same but mean very different things.
