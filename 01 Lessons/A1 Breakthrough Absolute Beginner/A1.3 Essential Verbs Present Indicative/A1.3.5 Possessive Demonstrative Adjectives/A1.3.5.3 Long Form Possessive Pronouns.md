## Explanation

The short possessives from the last lesson (_mi, tu, su_) always sit right before a
noun: _mi casa_, _tu libro_. But what happens when you don't want to repeat the noun
at all — like answering "Whose is this?" with just "Mine," not "My book"? That's what
the **long-form possessive pronouns** are for: _mío, tuyo, suyo, nuestro_, and so on.
They stand in for "the noun + whose it is" all by themselves, the way English "mine,"
"yours," and "his" replace "my book," "your book," and "his book."

These long forms show up in two situations. First, standing completely alone, usually
after the verb _ser_: —¿De quién es este celular? —Es mío. ("Whose phone is this?"
—"It's mine.") Second, placed right after a noun (instead of before it) to mean "of
mine / of yours," often to single something out among several options: un amigo mío
("a friend of mine"), esta casa nuestra ("this house of ours").

Unlike the short forms, every long-form possessive agrees in both **gender and
number** with the thing owned — not just _nuestro_, but all of them: mío/mía/míos/
mías, tuyo/tuya/tuyos/tuyas, and so on. And just like _su_ in the short form, _suyo/
suya/suyos/suyas_ is ambiguous on its own — it can mean "his," "hers," "yours
(formal)," or "theirs." When that's unclear, Spanish speakers swap it for _de él, de
ella, de usted,_ or _de ellos_ instead: no es suyo → es de ella ("it's not his/hers —
it's hers").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Meaning                          | Masc. Singular | Fem. Singular | Masc. Plural | Fem. Plural |
| --------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------- | ------------- | ------------ |
| mine                               | mío             | mía            | míos          | mías         |
| yours (tú)                        | tuyo            | tuya           | tuyos         | tuyas        |
| his / hers / yours (usted) / theirs | suyo          | suya           | suyos         | suyas        |
| ours                               | nuestro         | nuestra        | nuestros      | nuestras     |

- Long-form possessives agree in **both** gender and number with the noun they refer
  to — every form in the table above changes for both, unlike the short forms where
  only _nuestro_ changes for gender.
- Standing alone (usually after _ser_): —¿Es tuya esta mochila? —Sí, es mía. ("Is
  this backpack yours?" —"Yes, it's mine.")
- After a noun, to mean "of mine/yours/etc.": un colega mío ("a colleague of mine"),
  esa idea tuya ("that idea of yours"). This position often implies "one among
  several," the way English "a friend of mine" implies you have more than one.
- _Suyo/suya/suyos/suyas_ is ambiguous the same way _su_ is — clarify with _de él, de
  ella, de usted,_ or _de ellos_ when it isn't obvious from context.
- With the definite article (_el mío, la tuya, los suyos_), the long form works like
  a full pronoun replacing the noun entirely: Mi carro es rojo; el tuyo es azul. ("My
  car is red; yours is blue.")

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —¿De quién es esta chaqueta? —Es mía. | "Whose jacket is this?" — "It's mine." | Standing alone after _ser_, agreeing with feminine _chaqueta_. |
| Mi teléfono no funciona; ¿me prestas el tuyo? | My phone isn't working; can you lend me yours? | _el tuyo_ replaces "tu teléfono" entirely. |
| Estos libros son míos, esos son tuyos. | These books are mine, those are yours. | Plural agreement — _míos/tuyos_ match plural _libros_. |
| Un amigo mío trabaja en esa empresa. | A friend of mine works at that company. | Long form after the noun, implying "one of several friends." |
| Nuestra casa es pequeña; la suya es enorme. | Our house is small; theirs is huge. | Contrasts _nuestra_ (short, before noun) with _la suya_ (long, standing alone). |
| No sé si este paraguas es de ella o si es suyo. | I don't know if this umbrella is hers or if it's his. | Shows _de ella_ clarifying ambiguity that _suyo_ alone wouldn't resolve. |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the long form only for number, forgetting gender — saying _esta mochila es
  mío_ instead of _es mía_, since _mochila_ is feminine.
- Placing the long form before the noun like the short form — _mío carro_ is wrong;
  the long form after a noun needs no article (_un carro mío_), and standing alone it
  usually takes the definite article (_el mío_).
- Treating _suyo_ as automatically clear — like _su_, it can mean "his," "hers,"
  "yours (formal)," or "theirs," so it needs _de él/de ella/de usted/de ellos_ when
  context doesn't already make the owner obvious.
- Confusing when to use the short form vs. the long form — the short form (_mi
  casa_) always comes before the noun; the long form either stands alone (_es mía_)
  or comes after the noun (_una casa mía_), never before it.
