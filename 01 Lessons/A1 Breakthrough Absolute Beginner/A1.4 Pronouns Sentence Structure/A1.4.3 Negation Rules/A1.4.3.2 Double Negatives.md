## Explanation

In English, two negatives are a mistake — "I don't have nothing" is considered
incorrect; you're supposed to say "I don't have anything." Spanish works the
opposite way: stacking _no_ together with a negative word like _nada_ ("nothing"),
_nadie_ ("nobody"), or _nunca_ ("never") isn't a mistake, it's required. _No tengo
nada_ ("I don't have anything," literally "I don't have nothing") is the normal,
correct way to say it.

The pattern is: **no + verb + negative word**, built right on top of the basic
negation you already know from [[A1.4.3.1 Simple Negation with No]]. You keep the
_no_ before the verb and add the negative word after it: _No conozco a nadie
aquí_ ("I don't know anybody here"), _No vamos nunca a ese restaurante_ ("We never
go to that restaurant").

There's a shortcut: if the negative word comes before the verb instead of after
it, you drop the _no_ entirely — one negative marker per clause is enough as long
as one of them sits in front of the verb. _Nadie llegó a tiempo_ ("Nobody arrived
on time") skips _no_ because _nadie_ is already doing that job up front. Compare
that to _No llegó nadie a tiempo_, which says the same thing with _no_ + verb +
_nadie_ after. Both orders are correct; putting the negative word first just
means _no_ isn't needed.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Negative word | Meaning     | Pattern (after the verb)         | Pattern (before the verb, no "no")  |
| ---------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| nada             | nothing       | No tengo nada.                       | Nada me interesa.                      |
| nadie            | nobody        | No vino nadie.                       | Nadie vino.                            |
| nunca            | never         | No como carne nunca.                 | Nunca como carne.                      |
| tampoco          | neither/either | No me gusta tampoco.                 | Tampoco me gusta.                      |
| ningún/ninguna   | none/not any  | No tengo ningún problema.            | Ningún problema tengo. *(rare word order)* |

- Rule of thumb: at least one negative word must come before the verb. If it's
  _no_, add the negative word after the verb. If the negative word itself comes
  first, skip _no_.
- _Ningún_ and _ninguna_ agree in gender with the noun and drop the final _-o_
  before a masculine singular noun, the same way _un_ does: _ningún libro_,
  _ninguna idea_.
- More than two negative words can stack in the same sentence: _Nunca le digo
  nada a nadie_ ("I never tell anybody anything").

## Examples

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| No hay nadie en la oficina.                  | There's nobody in the office.            | no + verb + _nadie_                        |
| Nadie sabe la respuesta.                     | Nobody knows the answer.                 | _nadie_ first, no _no_                     |
| No como carne nunca.                         | I never eat meat.                        | no + verb + _nunca_                        |
| Nunca he viajado a Europa.                   | I've never traveled to Europe.           | _nunca_ first, no _no_                     |
| A mí tampoco me gusta el café frío.          | I don't like cold coffee either.         | _tampoco_, agreeing negative response       |
| No tengo ningún problema con eso.            | I don't have any problem with that.      | _ningún_, agrees with _problema_           |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the negative word after _no_ out of habit from English — _No tengo
  nada_ needs both _no_ and _nada_; _No tengo_ alone just means "I don't have."
- Keeping _no_ when the negative word already comes before the verb — _No nadie
  vino_ is wrong; it's just _Nadie vino_.
- Using _ningún_ without matching the noun's gender — _ningún idea_ is wrong; it
  has to be _ninguna idea_.
