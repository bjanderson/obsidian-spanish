## Explanation

Making a Spanish sentence negative is simpler than in English — there's no "don't"
or "doesn't" to conjugate. You just put the word _no_ right before the verb, and
that's it: _Hablo español_ ("I speak Spanish") becomes _No hablo español_ ("I
don't speak Spanish"). One word does the whole job that English splits across
"do" + "not."

The key rule is placement: _no_ goes immediately before the conjugated verb, with
nothing in between except an object pronoun if the sentence has one (you'll meet
those in a later lesson). It never goes after the verb, and it never attaches to
anything the way "n't" does in English: _No trabajo los domingos_ ("I don't work
on Sundays"), not _Trabajo no los domingos_.

If the subject pronoun is stated (which, remember, is often skipped — see
[[A1.4.1.2 Subject Pronoun Omission]]), _no_ still comes right before the verb,
after the subject: _Ella no vive aquí_ ("She doesn't live here"). And when you
answer a yes/no question negatively, Spanish repeats _no_ twice in casual speech —
once as the standalone "no," once again before the verb: _¿Vienes a la fiesta? —
No, no puedo_ ("Are you coming to the party? — No, I can't"). That's not a
mistake or an error to avoid; it's just how a plain "no" answer sounds natural.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Place _no_ directly before the conjugated verb: **no + verb**.
- _No_ comes after the subject, if the subject is stated: _Yo no sé_ ("I don't
  know").
- A negative answer to a question often uses _no_ twice — once alone, once before
  the verb — and that's normal, not redundant: _No, no quiero_ ("No, I don't want
  to").
- This lesson covers the basic _no_ + verb pattern; for negative words like
  _nada, nadie, nunca_ that combine with _no_, see
  [[A1.4.3.2 Double Negatives]].

## Examples

| Spanish                          | English                       | Notes                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| No entiendo la pregunta.            | I don't understand the question.  | _no_ + verb                       |
| ¿No tienes hambre?                  | Aren't you hungry?                | negative question                 |
| Mi hermano no maneja todavía.       | My brother doesn't drive yet.     | subject + _no_ + verb              |
| No podemos salir hoy.               | We can't go out today.            | _no_ + verb, plural subject        |
| Ustedes no trabajan los domingos.   | You all don't work on Sundays.    | subject + _no_ + verb              |
| —¿Quieres postre? —No, no quiero.   | "Do you want dessert?" "No, I don't." | doubled _no_ in a negative answer |

## Common Mistakes

- Putting _no_ after the verb instead of before it — _Hablo no inglés_ is wrong;
  it has to be _No hablo inglés_.
- Trying to build a contraction like English "don't" — Spanish has no equivalent;
  _no_ always stays a separate word in front of the verb.
- Treating the doubled _no_ in an answer (_No, no puedo_) as a mistake and cutting
  it down to one — both are needed for a natural negative answer.
