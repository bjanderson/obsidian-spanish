## Explanation

English speakers usually reach for _qué_ every time they mean "what," but Spanish
splits that job between two words: _qué_ and _cuál_. Getting this right is less
about translating "what" vs. "which" and more about knowing what each word is
actually asking for.

Use _qué_ when you want a definition or an open-ended answer — asking someone to
explain what something is, or asking about a noun in general: _¿Qué es esto?_
("What is this?"), _¿Qué quieres comer?_ ("What do you want to eat?"). _Qué_ also
goes directly in front of a noun: _¿Qué película viste?_ ("What movie did you
watch?") — when a noun follows immediately, _qué_ is almost always the right
choice, even in cases where English would say "which."

Use _cuál_ (plural _cuáles_) when you're asking someone to pick one option out of
a set, even an implied one — asking for specific information like a name, a
number, or an address counts as "picking from a set" of possible answers: _¿Cuál
es tu nombre?_ ("What is your name?"), _¿Cuál prefieres?_ ("Which one do you
prefer?"). _Cuál_ pairs naturally with _de_ to narrow the set explicitly:
_¿Cuál de los dos quieres?_ ("Which of the two do you want?").

The one place these overlap and cause real confusion is right before _ser_
("to be"). _¿Cuál es tu nombre?_ is the natural, most common phrasing — treat
asking for a name, number, or address as "picking the answer from a set," so
_cuál_ + _ser_ is the default there. _¿Qué es...?_ before _ser_ is reserved for
asking someone to define or explain something: _¿Qué es el amor?_ ("What is
love?").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Pattern                     | Use it for                          | Example                              |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Qué + verb (not _ser_)        | open-ended action or definition question | ¿Qué quieres? — What do you want?       |
| Qué + noun                    | asking about a noun directly            | ¿Qué libro lees? — What book are you reading? |
| Cuál(es) + verb (not _ser_)   | choosing from a set                    | ¿Cuál prefieres? — Which one do you prefer? |
| Cuál(es) de + noun            | choosing from an explicit set          | ¿Cuál de las opciones prefieres? — Which of the options do you prefer? |
| Qué + es/son                  | asking for a definition                | ¿Qué es la inflación? — What is inflation? |
| Cuál/Cuáles + es/son          | asking for specific information (name, number, address) | ¿Cuál es tu número? — What's your number? |

- Directly before a noun, default to _qué_ — _cuál_ + noun exists in some dialects
  but isn't the neutral, everyday choice taught here.
- Before _ser_, the split is definition (_qué_) vs. specific-answer-from-a-set
  (_cuál_) — not literally "what" vs. "which."
- _Cuáles_ is the plural of _cuál_, matching a plural expected answer:
  _¿Cuáles son tus platillos favoritos?_ ("What are your favorite dishes?").

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Qué quieres hacer hoy?                 | What do you want to do today?         | _qué_ + verb, open-ended                     |
| ¿Cuál es tu color favorito?             | What's your favorite color?           | _cuál_ + _ser_, specific answer              |
| ¿Qué carro es ese?                      | What car is that?                     | _qué_ + noun                                 |
| ¿Cuál de estos carros prefieres?         | Which of these cars do you prefer?    | _cuál de_ + noun, narrowed set               |
| ¿Qué es un sustantivo?                  | What is a noun?                       | _qué_ + _ser_, definition                    |
| ¿Cuáles son tus planes para el fin de semana? | What are your plans for the weekend? | _cuáles_ + _ser_, plural specific answer |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _qué_ before _ser_ when asking for a name, number, or address —
  _¿Qué es tu nombre?_ sounds foreign; _¿Cuál es tu nombre?_ is the natural phrasing.
- Using _cuál_ directly before a noun the way English uses "which" — _¿Cuál
  libro?_ isn't the neutral form here; _¿Qué libro?_ is.
- Forgetting the plural _cuáles_ when the expected answer has more than one item.
