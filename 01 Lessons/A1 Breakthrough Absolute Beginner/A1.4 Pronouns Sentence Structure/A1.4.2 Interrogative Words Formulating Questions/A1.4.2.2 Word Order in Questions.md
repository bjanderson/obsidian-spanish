## Explanation

In English, turning a statement into a question usually means adding a helper verb:
"You speak Spanish" becomes "Do you speak Spanish?" Spanish doesn't use a helper
verb like "do" at all — instead, it flips the subject and verb, and wraps the whole
thing in upside-down and regular question marks. _Tú hablas español_ ("You speak
Spanish") becomes _¿Hablas tú español?_ or, more commonly, just _¿Hablas español?_
with the subject dropped entirely, the same way it usually is in statements — see
[[A1.4.1.2 Subject Pronoun Omission]].

When a question word is involved, it always comes first, immediately followed by
the verb, then the subject if it's stated at all: _¿Dónde vive tu hermano?_
("Where does your brother live?") — question word, verb, subject, never subject
before the verb. This subject-after-verb order is required with a question word; it
isn't optional the way it is in a plain yes/no question.

For a simple yes/no question with no question word, Spanish often doesn't move
anything at all — it just adds question marks and relies on rising intonation
in speech: _¿Tú hablas español?_ works fine without inverting _tú_ and _hablas_.
Both the inverted and non-inverted order are natural; inversion becomes more
noticeable, and more common, once a question word is added.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- With a question word: **question word + verb + subject (if stated)** —
  _¿Cuándo llega tu vuelo?_ ("When does your flight arrive?").
- Without a question word (yes/no questions): subject-verb order can stay the same
  as a statement, or invert — both _¿Tú vives aquí?_ and _¿Vives tú aquí?_ are
  correct; the subject is usually dropped either way.
- Every written question opens with an inverted question mark (¿) and closes with a
  regular one (?) — Spanish marks the question at both ends, not just at the end.
- The subject almost always comes after the verb once a question word starts the
  sentence, even when the subject is a full noun phrase, not just a pronoun:
  _¿A qué hora cierra la tienda?_ ("What time does the store close?").

## Examples

| Spanish                             | English                          | Notes                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| ¿Dónde trabaja tu papá?               | Where does your dad work?           | question word + verb + subject        |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta este boleto?           | How much does this ticket cost?     | question word + verb + subject        |
| ¿Vienes a la reunión mañana?          | Are you coming to the meeting tomorrow? | no question word, subject dropped |
| ¿Habla usted inglés?                  | Do you speak English?               | subject after verb, formal            |
| ¿A qué hora empieza la película?      | What time does the movie start?     | question phrase + verb + subject       |
| ¿Tienen ustedes tiempo hoy?           | Do you all have time today?         | subject after verb, plural             |

## Common Mistakes

- Adding an English-style helper verb — there's no Spanish equivalent of "do" for
  forming questions; the verb itself moves, nothing gets inserted.
- Putting the subject before the verb after a question word — _¿Dónde tu hermano
  vive?_ is wrong; it has to be _¿Dónde vive tu hermano?_
- Forgetting the opening ¿ — Spanish needs both punctuation marks, not just the one
  at the end.
