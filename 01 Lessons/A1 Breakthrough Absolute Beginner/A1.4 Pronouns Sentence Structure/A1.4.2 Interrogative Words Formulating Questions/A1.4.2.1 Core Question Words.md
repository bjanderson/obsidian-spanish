## Explanation

Question words are the small words that open an information question — "what,
who, where, when, why, how, how much." Spanish has a matching set: _qué, quién,
dónde, cuándo, por qué, cómo, cuánto_. Learn these seven first — they cover almost
everything you'll need to ask in everyday conversation.

The one rule that trips up new learners: every Spanish question word carries a
written accent mark, even though the same word without an accent can mean
something else entirely. _Como_ (no accent) means "like" or "as," but _cómo_ (with
accent) means "how." _Que_ (no accent) is "that," but _qué_ (with accent) is
"what." The accent is what signals "this word is asking a question," so don't
drop it — it isn't decorative, it changes the word's job in the sentence.

A couple of these words change form to match what they're asking about. _Quién_
becomes _quiénes_ when you expect the answer to involve more than one person:
_¿Quiénes son ellos?_ ("Who are they?"). _Cuánto_ agrees in gender and number
with the noun that follows it, just like an adjective: _cuánto dinero_ ("how much
money"), _cuánta agua_ ("how much water"), _cuántos libros_ ("how many books"),
_cuántas personas_ ("how many people").

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Word     | Meaning        | Notes                                      |
| -------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| qué      | what            | always accented — see [[A1.4.2.3 Que vs Cual Distinction]] for _qué_ vs. _cuál_ |
| quién / quiénes | who      | _quiénes_ for a plural answer               |
| dónde    | where           | pair with _a_ for "to where": _adónde_       |
| cuándo   | when            | —                                             |
| por qué  | why             | two words, both accented — don't confuse with _porque_ ("because") |
| cómo     | how             | —                                             |
| cuánto / cuánta / cuántos / cuántas | how much / how many | agrees in gender and number with the noun |

- All Spanish question words carry a written accent — that's what marks them as
  interrogatives instead of their unaccented lookalikes.
- These words also work inside indirect questions: _No sé dónde está_ ("I don't
  know where it is") — still accented even without question marks.
- Spanish questions open and close with punctuation: ¿...? — see
  [[A1.4.2.2 Word Order in Questions]] for how the rest of the sentence is built.

## Examples

| Spanish                          | English                       | Notes                     |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| ¿Qué quieres comer?                | What do you want to eat?        | _qué_                        |
| ¿Quién es esa persona?             | Who is that person?              | _quién_, singular             |
| ¿Dónde está la farmacia?           | Where is the pharmacy?           | _dónde_                      |
| ¿Cuándo llega el vuelo?            | When does the flight arrive?     | _cuándo_                     |
| ¿Por qué no vienes a la fiesta?    | Why aren't you coming to the party? | _por qué_                 |
| ¿Cuántas hermanas tienes?          | How many sisters do you have?    | _cuántas_, agrees with _hermanas_ |

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the accent mark — writing _como_ instead of _cómo_ makes the sentence
  read as a statement, not a question.
- Confusing _por qué_ (why, two words) with _porque_ (because, one word) — they
  sound alike but serve opposite roles in a sentence.
- Forgetting that _cuánto_ has to agree with the noun it modifies — _cuántos
  chicos_, not _cuánto chicos_.
