## Explanation

Subject pronouns are the words that tell you who's doing the action — "I, you, he,
she, we, they." In Spanish they're _yo, tú, él, ella, usted, nosotros, ustedes, ellos_
(and a few more), and they line up with the six "boxes" of a verb conjugation, the
same six slots you'll see in every verb chart from now on.

Spanish splits "you" into two pronouns where English only has one. _Tú_ is informal —
use it with friends, family, peers, and anyone your own age or younger. _Usted_ is
formal — use it with strangers, elders, bosses, or anyone you'd address respectfully.
Choosing wrong isn't a grammar error exactly, but it can sound too casual or too
stiff, so it's worth getting a feel for early.

Spanish also has no single word for "it" as a subject — a thing is just talked about
by leaving the pronoun out entirely and using the right verb form, or with _él/ella_
if it's standing in for a specific masculine or feminine noun. And _nosotros_ becomes
_nosotras_ when the group is all female; the same pattern applies to _ellos/ellas_.

One more regional note: this vault uses _ustedes_ as the only plural "you," the way
most of Latin America does. Spain also has _vosotros/vosotras_ for informal plural
"you," but that form doesn't come up in neutral Latin American Spanish.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

| Person             | Pronoun         | Notes                                  |
| ------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 1st singular         | yo              | I                                        |
| 2nd singular informal | tú              | you (friends, family, peers)             |
| 3rd singular          | él / ella / usted | he / she / you (formal)               |
| 1st plural           | nosotros / nosotras | we (nosotras if all-female group)   |
| 2nd/3rd plural        | ustedes         | you all — the only plural "you" in LatAm Spanish |
| 3rd plural            | ellos / ellas   | they (ellas if all-female group)         |

- Spanish verb endings already show who the subject is, so subject pronouns are
  often left out — more on that in [[A1.4.1.2 Subject Pronoun Omission]].
- _Usted_ and _ustedes_ take the same verb forms as _él/ella_ and _ellos/ellas_,
  even though they mean "you."
- Latin American Spanish has no _vosotros_ — _ustedes_ covers plural "you" in every
  register, formal or informal.

## Examples

| Spanish                          | English                      | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Yo trabajo en un hospital.        | I work at a hospital.         | _yo_, 1st singular               |
| ¿Tú vives cerca de aquí?          | Do you live near here?        | _tú_, informal                   |
| Usted es muy amable.              | You are very kind.            | _usted_, formal                  |
| Nosotras somos hermanas.          | We are sisters.                | _nosotras_, all-female group     |
| ¿Ustedes quieren café?            | Do you all want coffee?       | _ustedes_, plural "you"          |
| Ellos llegan mañana.              | They arrive tomorrow.          | _ellos_, 3rd plural               |

## Common Mistakes

- Using _tú_ with someone who calls for _usted_ — a new boss, an elder, or a stranger
  in a formal setting.
- Reaching for _vosotros_ from an app or textbook written for Spain — this vault
  treats _ustedes_ as the only plural "you."
- Adding a subject pronoun to every sentence out of habit from English — Spanish
  speakers leave it out once the verb ending makes the subject clear.
