## Explanation

In English you always need a subject: "I eat," "she eats," "we eat" — drop the "I"
or "she" and the sentence falls apart. Spanish doesn't work that way. Each verb
ending already tells you who's doing the action, so the pronoun is extra
information the listener usually doesn't need. _Como_ already means "I eat" all by
itself; adding _yo_ in front doesn't make it any clearer.

This is called being a "pro-drop" language — a language whose verb endings are rich
enough to let you drop ("pro-drop" = pronoun-drop) the subject pronoun. Because
every person (_yo, tú, él..._) has its own distinct ending in most tenses, the
verb alone carries that information. Leaving pronouns out isn't lazy or informal —
it's the normal, unmarked way to speak Spanish. Piling them into every sentence is
what actually sounds off, like a foreign accent in grammar form.

So when do you keep the pronoun? Mainly to clear up ambiguity or to add emphasis.
_Él_, _ella_, and _usted_ all share the same verb form (_habla_ can mean "he
speaks," "she speaks," or "you speak"), so if context doesn't make the subject
obvious, keep the pronoun in: _Ella habla francés, pero él no_ ("She speaks French,
but he doesn't"). You'll also see it kept for contrast or emphasis — _Yo sí quiero
ir_ ("I, for one, want to go") — or simply to introduce who you're talking about
before switching to drop it in the sentences that follow.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Drop the subject pronoun by default — the verb ending already shows the subject.
- Keep the pronoun when the verb form is ambiguous (especially _él/ella/usted_,
  which all share one form) and context alone doesn't clarify who's meant.
- Keep the pronoun for contrast or emphasis: _Yo trabajo, tú descansas_ ("I work,
  you rest").
- It's common to state the pronoun once to introduce a subject, then drop it in the
  sentences that follow once the subject is established.

## Examples

| Spanish                            | English                          | Notes                              |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Vivo en Bolivia.                    | I live in Bolivia.                | no _yo_ needed — ending is enough    |
| ¿Hablas español?                    | Do you speak Spanish?              | no _tú_ needed                       |
| Trabajamos los sábados.              | We work on Saturdays.              | no _nosotros_ needed                 |
| Ella cocina y él limpia.             | She cooks and he cleans.           | both pronouns kept for contrast      |
| Usted es de aquí, ¿verdad?           | You're from here, right?            | _usted_ kept — ambiguous without it  |
| Yo sí quiero postre, gracias.        | I do want dessert, thanks.          | _yo_ kept for emphasis               |

## Common Mistakes

- Translating English word-for-word and keeping _yo, tú, él..._ in front of every
  verb — it reads as overly emphatic or unnatural in Spanish.
- Dropping the pronoun in a sentence where _él/ella/usted_ share a form and context
  doesn't make the subject clear, leaving the listener unsure who's being discussed.
- Forgetting that dropping the pronoun is the default even in questions: ¿Hablas
  español? not ¿Tú hablas español? for a plain, unemphatic question.
