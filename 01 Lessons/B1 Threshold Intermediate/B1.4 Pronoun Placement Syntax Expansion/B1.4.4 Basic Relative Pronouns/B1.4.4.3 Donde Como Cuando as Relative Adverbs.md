## Explanation

You've seen how *que* and *quien/quienes* connect a description back to a
person or thing. Spanish has three more connecting words that work the same
way, but each one specializes in a different kind of detail: **donde**
(where) links back to a place, **cuando** (when) links back to a time, and
**como** (how) links back to a manner or way of doing something. Grammar
books call these **relative adverbs**, because instead of standing in for a
noun, they describe the circumstances — place, time, or manner — of the
clause they connect.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Donde** (where) refers back to a **place**: *La casa donde crecí ya no
  existe* (The house where I grew up no longer exists). It can replace *en
  la que/en el que* + place, and often sounds more natural in everyday
  speech.
- **Cuando** (when) refers back to a **time**: *Recuerdo el día cuando nos
  conocimos* (I remember the day when we met). In careful writing, *en que*
  is also common here (*el día en que nos conocimos*) — both are correct,
  and *cuando* is the more conversational choice.
- **Como** (how) refers back to a **manner or way** something is done: *Me
  gusta como cocinas* (I like how you cook). *Como* here has no accent mark
  — accented *cómo* is the question word ("how?"), unaccented *como* is the
  relative adverb.
- When the clause is essential to knowing which place, time, or way you
  mean, there's no comma — grammar books call this a **restrictive clause**.
  When the clause just adds extra detail and isn't needed to identify what
  you're talking about, it's set off by commas — a **non-restrictive
  clause**. All three adverbs work in both kinds, just like *que* and
  *quien*.
- A common alternative to *donde*, *cuando*, and *como* is the equivalent
  preposition + *que* form: *en que/en la que* for place, *en que* for time,
  *en que* for manner. These aren't wrong, but *donde/cuando/como* are the
  more natural, everyday choice a native speaker reaches for first.

## Examples

| Spanish                                                    | English                                            | Notes                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| La casa donde crecí ya no existe.                          | The house where I grew up no longer exists.          | *donde*, place                       |
| El restaurante donde cenamos anoche cerró.                 | The restaurant where we had dinner last night closed. | *donde*, place                         |
| Recuerdo el día cuando nos conocimos.                      | I remember the day when we met.                       | *cuando*, time                           |
| Llegó la época cuando los precios subieron mucho.          | The period when prices rose a lot arrived.            | *cuando*, time                             |
| Me gusta como cocinas.                                     | I like how you cook.                                   | *como*, manner                               |
| No entiendo como resolviste el problema tan rápido.        | I don't understand how you solved the problem so fast. | *como*, manner                                 |

## Common Mistakes

- Writing *cómo, dónde, cuándo* with an accent when they're relative
  adverbs, not questions: *La forma cómo lo hizo* is wrong — no accent when
  it's connecting a clause: *La forma como lo hizo.*
- Using *donde* for something that isn't a place: *El motivo donde se fue*
  is wrong — a reason isn't a place, so *que* fits better: *El motivo por el
  que se fue.*
- Forgetting that *donde* already means "where": adding a redundant *en*
  before it (*en donde vivo*) isn't wrong exactly, but *donde vivo* alone is
  the more natural, streamlined form most speakers use.
- Using *cuando* for a place or *donde* for a time — each one is
  specialized, so *el lugar cuando fuimos* and *el año donde nací* both
  swap the wrong adverb in: *el lugar donde fuimos*, *el año cuando nací.*
