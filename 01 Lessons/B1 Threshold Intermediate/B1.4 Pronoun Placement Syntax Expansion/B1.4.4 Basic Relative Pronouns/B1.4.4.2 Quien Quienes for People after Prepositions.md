## Explanation

You already know that **que** covers almost every case of connecting a
description back to a noun. But there's one spot where *que* steps aside:
right after a preposition, when you're talking about a person. In English
you might say "the woman **with whom** I work" or, more casually, "the woman
**I work with**." Spanish has its own word for that "whom" slot when the
sentence is about a person — **quien** (singular) and **quienes** (plural).

The other thing to know up front: in Spanish, **the preposition always
comes before the relative pronoun**, never dangling at the end of the
sentence the way it can in casual English.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Quien** (singular) and **quienes** (plural) are relative pronouns used
  **only for people** — never for things. They agree in number with the
  person or people they refer to, not in gender.
- The main job of *quien/quienes* is standing in right **after a
  preposition** (*a, con, de, para, por...*) when the person came up earlier
  in the sentence: *La mujer **con quien** trabajo es ingeniera* (The woman
  **with whom** I work is an engineer).
- Spanish never leaves a preposition stranded at the end of a clause the
  way English does. English allows *the woman I work with*; Spanish requires
  the preposition up front with *quien*: *la mujer con quien trabajo* — never
  *la mujer trabajo con*.
- *Quien/quienes* also appears in **non-restrictive clauses** — extra,
  parenthetical information about a person, set off by commas: *Mi hermano,
  **quien** vive en Bogotá, viene de visita* (My brother, **who** lives in
  Bogotá, is coming to visit). Here *que* would also be acceptable, but
  *quien* sounds a little more polished.
- **Never use *quien* in a restrictive clause with no preposition** —
  that job belongs to *que*. *La mujer quien trabaja aquí* sounds off to a
  native speaker; say *La mujer que trabaja aquí* instead.
- After the personal *a*, use *a quien* for a direct object that's a
  specific person: *La persona **a quien** vi ayer trabaja aquí* (The
  person **whom** I saw yesterday works here).

## Examples

| Spanish                                                        | English                                                | Notes                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| La mujer con quien trabajo es ingeniera.                        | The woman with whom I work is an engineer.               | *con quien*, singular                 |
| Los vecinos con quienes cenamos siempre son muy amables.         | The neighbors with whom we always have dinner are very kind. | *con quienes*, plural                 |
| La persona a quien vi ayer trabaja aquí.                        | The person whom I saw yesterday works here.               | *a quien*, personal *a* + direct object |
| Mi hermano, quien vive en Bogotá, viene de visita la próxima semana. | My brother, who lives in Bogotá, is coming to visit next week. | *quien*, non-restrictive clause        |
| El profesor para quien escribí el ensayo lo revisó con cuidado.  | The teacher for whom I wrote the essay reviewed it carefully. | *para quien*, singular                 |
| Las estudiantes de quienes hablamos ya se graduaron.             | The students whom we talked about already graduated.       | *de quienes*, plural                  |

## Common Mistakes

- Stranding the preposition like English does: *La mujer quien trabajo
  con* is wrong — the preposition must come before *quien*: *La mujer con
  quien trabajo.*
- Using *quien* in a restrictive clause with no preposition: *La mujer
  quien trabaja aquí* sounds wrong — restrictive clauses with no
  preposition call for *que*: *La mujer que trabaja aquí.*
- Forgetting the plural: after a plural person or group, use *quienes*, not
  *quien*: *los amigos con quienes salgo*, not *los amigos con quien salgo.*
- Using *quien/quienes* for things: *quien* is for people only — *la casa
  en que vivo* (not *la casa en quien vivo*), since a house isn't a person.
- Dropping the *a* before *quien* when the person is a direct object: *La
  persona quien vi ayer* skips the personal *a* — it should be *La persona
  **a quien** vi ayer.*
