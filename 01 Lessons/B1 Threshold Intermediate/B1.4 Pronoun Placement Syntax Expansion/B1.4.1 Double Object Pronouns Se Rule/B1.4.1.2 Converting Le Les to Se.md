## Explanation

[[B1.4.1.1 Order of Pronouns|Last lesson]] you learned that indirect always
comes before direct — *me lo, te la, nos los*. But there's one indirect
pronoun that can never appear directly in front of a direct object pronoun:
*le* or *les*. Spanish doesn't allow *le lo* or *les la* — it sounds
awkward, so the language has a fix: swap *le/les* for **se**.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- The rule is simple and has no exceptions: whenever **le** or **les** would
  come right before **lo, la, los,** or **las**, it changes to **se**.

| Would-be combination | Actual combination |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| le + lo                        | se lo                        |
| le + la                        | se la                        |
| les + lo                       | se lo                         |
| les + las                      | se las                         |

- This change is purely about **sound**, not meaning — *se* here has
  nothing to do with reflexive *se* (as in *se levanta*) or impersonal *se*
  (as in *se habla español*). Context always makes the difference clear.
- Because *se* replaces both *le* and *les*, a sentence like *Se lo di* is
  ambiguous on its own — it could mean "I gave it to him," "to her," "to
  you (usted)," or "to them." Spanish resolves this the same way it resolves
  other *le/les* ambiguity: by adding a clarifying phrase with *a*: *Se lo
  di a ella* (I gave it to her).
- This rule applies no matter where the pronouns end up — before a
  conjugated verb, attached to an infinitive, gerund, or command — the
  *le/les* → *se* swap always happens first, before anything else.

## Examples

| Spanish                    | English                       | Notes                         |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Se lo di a Juan.                 | I gave it to Juan.                    | *le* → *se*, clarified with *a Juan*   |
| Se la mandé ayer.                | I sent it to him/her yesterday.        | *le* → *se*                              |
| Se los expliqué con calma.       | I explained them to them calmly.        | *les* → *se*                               |
| ¿Se lo dijiste a tu hermana?     | Did you tell it to your sister?          | *le* → *se*, clarified with *a tu hermana*   |
| Se las prestamos sin problema.   | We lent them to him/her without a problem. | *le* → *se*, *nosotros*                        |
| Se lo entregaron a los clientes. | They delivered it to the customers.       | *les* → *se*, clarified with *a los clientes*    |

## Common Mistakes

- Keeping *le/les* in front of a direct object pronoun: *Le lo di* is
  always wrong in Spanish — it must become *Se lo di.*
- Assuming *se* here is reflexive: *Se lo di* doesn't mean "I gave it to
  myself" — this *se* is just a sound-avoidance substitute for *le/les*,
  unrelated to reflexive verbs.
- Forgetting to clarify an ambiguous *se* when it matters: if it's not
  clear from context who *se* refers to, add *a él, a ella, a usted, a
  ellos,* etc.
- Applying the swap to the wrong pronoun: only *le* and *les* change to
  *se* — the direct object pronoun (*lo, la, los, las*) never changes.
