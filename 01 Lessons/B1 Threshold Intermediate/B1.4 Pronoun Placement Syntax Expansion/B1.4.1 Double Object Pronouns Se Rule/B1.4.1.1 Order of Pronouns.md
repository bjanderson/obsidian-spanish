## Explanation

You already know indirect object pronouns (*me, te, le, nos, les*) and
direct object pronouns (*me, te, lo, la, nos, los, las*) separately. Now
you'll combine them in the same sentence — something Spanish does constantly
in everyday speech: *I gave it to him*, *She sent them to us*, *We showed it
to her*. The tricky part isn't picking the right pronouns — it's knowing
which order they go in.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- When a sentence has both an indirect object pronoun (*to/for whom*) and a
  direct object pronoun (*what*), the order is always **indirect before
  direct**. A handy way to remember it: think **"I.D."** — indirect, then
  direct — then the verb.
- Both pronouns go together as a unit, either before the conjugated verb or
  attached to an infinitive/gerund/command: *Me lo dio* (He/she gave it to
  me).
- Most of the pronouns you already know work exactly the same way here — the
  only wrinkle is what happens when *le* or *les* would come right before
  *lo, la, los,* or *las*, which you'll cover in the next lesson.

## Examples

| Spanish                  | English                        | Notes                     |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Me lo dio ayer.                | He/she gave it to me yesterday.        | indirect *me* + direct *lo*          |
| Te la mandé por correo.        | I sent it to you by mail.               | indirect *te* + direct *la*            |
| Nos los mostraron en la tienda. | They showed them to us at the store.     | indirect *nos* + direct *los*            |
| ¿Me las prestas?               | Will you lend them to me?                | indirect *me* + direct *las*               |
| Te lo explico ahora mismo.     | I'll explain it to you right now.         | indirect *te* + direct *lo*                  |
| Nos la trajeron temprano.      | They brought it to us early.               | indirect *nos* + direct *la*                   |

## Common Mistakes

- Reversing the order: *Lo me dio* is wrong — it's always **indirect →
  direct**, no exceptions: *Me lo dio.*
- Splitting the pronouns apart in the sentence: they always travel together
  as a pair, right next to each other, right before the verb (or attached
  together after it).
- Forgetting that these two pronouns act as a single unit for placement
  purposes — wherever one goes, the other goes with it.
- Mixing up which pronoun is which: the indirect pronoun answers "to/for
  whom," the direct pronoun answers "what" — if you're not sure which is
  which, ask those two questions about the sentence first.
