## Explanation

You've learned the order ([[B1.4.1.1 Order of Pronouns|indirect before direct]])
and the *le/les* → *se* swap ([[B1.4.1.2 Converting Le Les to Se|last lesson]]).
Now let's cover the last piece: **where** that pronoun pair actually goes in
the sentence. Good news — this follows the exact same placement rules you
already know from single object pronouns, so nothing new to learn here,
just applying the pattern to two pronouns instead of one.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Conjugated verb:** the pronoun pair goes **before** the verb, as
  separate words: *Se lo di.* (I gave it to him.)
- **Infinitive:** the pair can **attach to the end** of the infinitive,
  forming one word: *Voy a dárselo.* (I'm going to give it to him.) — notice
  the written accent added to keep the original stress.
- **Gerund:** the pair can also attach to the end of a gerund: *Estoy
  dándoselo.* (I'm giving it to him.) — again with an accent added.
- **Affirmative command:** the pair attaches to the end, just like with a
  single pronoun: *Dáselo.* (Give it to him.)
- **Negative command:** the pair goes **before** the verb, exactly like with
  a single pronoun: *No se lo des.* (Don't give it to him.)
- When a sentence has an infinitive or gerund paired with a conjugated
  helper verb (like *ir a* or *estar*), you actually have a **choice**:
  attach the pair to the infinitive/gerund, or place it before the
  conjugated verb instead: *Voy a dárselo* = *Se lo voy a dar.* Both are
  correct and equally common.

## Examples

| Spanish                        | English                            | Notes                       |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Se lo di ayer.                       | I gave it to him/her yesterday.               | conjugated verb                       |
| Voy a dárselo mañana.                | I'm going to give it to him/her tomorrow.       | infinitive, attached                     |
| Se lo voy a dar mañana.              | I'm going to give it to him/her tomorrow.        | same meaning, before conjugated verb       |
| Estoy explicándoselo ahora.          | I'm explaining it to him/her right now.           | gerund, attached                             |
| Dáselo cuando lo veas.               | Give it to him/her when you see him/her.           | affirmative command, attached                  |
| No se lo des todavía.                | Don't give it to him/her yet.                       | negative command, precedes                       |

## Common Mistakes

- Attaching the pair to a negative command: *No dáselo* is wrong — negative
  commands always require the pair in front: *No se lo des.*
- Forgetting the written accent when attaching two pronouns to an
  infinitive, gerund, or command: *Estoy explicandoselo* is missing the
  accent — it must be *explicándoselo* to keep the stress in the right
  place.
- Splitting the pronoun pair between the two positions: *Se voy a darlo* is
  wrong — the pair stays together, either both before the conjugated verb
  or both attached to the infinitive.
- Thinking there's only one correct placement with infinitives/gerunds:
  both *Voy a dárselo* and *Se lo voy a dar* are correct — Spanish gives you
  the choice.
