## Explanation

You already know *pero* means "but." So what's *sino*? Spanish actually
splits English "but" into two different words depending on what kind of
contrast you're making. Get this wrong and a sentence can sound off or even
confusing, so it's worth learning the rule precisely.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Pero** works after both positive and negative statements — it's the
  general-purpose "but," adding a contrast or limitation: *Me gusta el
  fútbol, pero prefiero el básquetbol* (I like soccer, but I prefer
  basketball).
- **Sino** only appears after a **negative** statement, and it doesn't just
  contrast — it **replaces** the negated thing with the correct alternative:
  *No quiero pizza, sino pasta* (I don't want pizza, but [rather] pasta).
  Notice the first part is negative (*no quiero pizza*) and *sino*
  introduces what you actually want instead.
- **Sino que** is the version you need when **both parts of the sentence
  have their own conjugated verb**: *No compramos una casa, sino que
  alquilamos un condominio* (We didn't buy a house, but instead we rented a
  condo). Since *alquilamos* is its own conjugated verb, *sino* alone isn't
  enough — it needs *que*.
- Quick test: after a negative statement, if what follows is just a noun,
  adjective, or infinitive (no new conjugated verb), use **sino**. If what
  follows has its own conjugated verb, use **sino que**.
- If the first part of the sentence isn't negative, none of this applies —
  just use **pero**, regardless of what follows.

## Examples

| Spanish                                        | English                                    | Notes                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Este restaurante es bueno, pero es muy caro.          | This restaurant is good, but it's very expensive. | *pero*, positive statement          |
| No quiero pizza, sino pasta.                          | I don't want pizza, but pasta instead.             | *sino*, no new conjugated verb        |
| No compramos una casa, sino que alquilamos un condominio. | We didn't buy a house, but instead we rented a condo. | *sino que*, new conjugated verb        |
| Su perro no es negro, sino dorado.                    | Their dog isn't black, but golden.                  | *sino*, adjective, no new verb           |
| El perro no atacó a los patos, sino que nadó con ellos. | The dog didn't attack the ducks, but instead swam with them. | *sino que*, new conjugated verb             |
| La película es interesante, pero demasiado larga.     | The movie is interesting, but too long.              | *pero*, positive statement                    |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *pero* after a negative when you mean "instead": *No quiero pizza,
  pero pasta* sounds wrong — since you're replacing the negated thing, it
  needs *sino*: *No quiero pizza, sino pasta.*
- Using plain *sino* when the second part has its own conjugated verb: *No
  compramos una casa, sino alquilamos un condominio* is missing *que* —
  it should be *sino que alquilamos.*
- Adding *que* when it isn't needed: *No quiero pizza, sino que pasta* is
  wrong — there's no conjugated verb after *sino* here, so *que* doesn't
  belong.
- Using *sino* after a positive statement: *Me gusta el café, sino el té*
  is wrong — *sino* only ever follows a negative: use *pero* instead if
  the first part is positive.
