## Explanation

[[B1.4.3.1 Additive Contrastive Connectors|Last lesson]] covered connectors
for adding on or contrasting. This lesson covers the other essential pair:
words that explain **why** something happened (cause) and words that
introduce **what happened as a result** (consequence). These show up
constantly in explanations, stories, and arguments — anywhere you're
connecting a reason to its outcome.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Ya que** (since, given that) introduces a **cause**, usually at the
  start of a sentence, and works a lot like *como* (since): *Ya que
  llueve, nos quedamos en casa* (Since it's raining, we're staying home).
- **Por eso** (that's why, so) introduces a **consequence** — the result
  that follows from what was just said: *Llovía mucho. Por eso, nos
  quedamos en casa* (It was raining a lot. That's why we stayed home).
- **Por lo tanto** (therefore, consequently) also introduces a
  **consequence**, like *por eso*, but is noticeably more formal — you'll
  see it in academic writing, reports, and formal speech more than casual
  conversation: *Los resultados fueron positivos. Por lo tanto, continuamos
  con el proyecto* (The results were positive. Therefore, we continued with
  the project).
- Think of it as cause **first**, result **second**: *ya que* points
  backward at the reason (cause), while *por eso* and *por lo tanto* point
  forward at the outcome (consequence) — usually starting a new sentence
  right after the cause has been stated.
- All three usually take a comma right after them when they open a
  sentence, same as *sin embargo* and *no obstante* from the last lesson.

## Examples

| Spanish                                        | English                                  | Notes                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Ya que tienes tiempo, ayúdame con esto.               | Since you have time, help me with this.         | *ya que*, cause                    |
| No estudié. Por eso, reprobé el examen.              | I didn't study. That's why I failed the exam.    | *por eso*, consequence                |
| Los precios subieron. Por lo tanto, vendimos menos.  | Prices went up. Therefore, we sold less.          | *por lo tanto*, consequence, formal      |
| Ya que estás aquí, quédate a cenar.                  | Since you're here, stay for dinner.               | *ya que*, cause                            |
| Hacía mucho calor. Por eso, abrimos las ventanas.    | It was very hot. That's why we opened the windows. | *por eso*, consequence                       |
| El sistema falló. Por lo tanto, perdimos los datos.  | The system failed. Therefore, we lost the data.    | *por lo tanto*, consequence, formal             |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *por lo tanto* in casual conversation where *por eso* would sound
  more natural: telling a friend a story with *por lo tanto* sounds
  stiff — save it for formal or written contexts.
- Confusing *ya que* (cause) with *por eso* (consequence) — they point in
  opposite directions: *ya que* explains the reason, *por eso* states the
  result.
- Forgetting the comma after these connectors when they open a sentence:
  *Por eso nos quedamos* reads awkwardly — it should be *Por eso, nos
  quedamos.*
- Treating *ya que* as identical to *porque*: they're close in meaning, but
  *ya que* usually leads the sentence and states a reason the listener
  probably already knows or can infer, while *porque* more often answers a
  direct "why" question and can go anywhere in the sentence.
