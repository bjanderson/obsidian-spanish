## Explanation

So far your sentences have mostly connected with simple words like *y* (and)
and *pero* (but). Now it's time to add some more sophisticated tools —
**connectors** that let you add extra information or introduce a contrast,
the kind of vocabulary that makes your Spanish sound more fluent and less
choppy. This lesson covers two families: words that **add** to what you just
said, and words that **contrast** with it.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Además** (moreover, also, in addition) adds extra information to what
  was just said: *Me gusta el café. Además, tomo té todas las mañanas* (I
  like coffee. I also drink tea every morning).
- **Sin embargo** (however, nevertheless) introduces a contrast — it's more
  formal and a bit stronger than *pero*: *Quería ir a la fiesta. Sin
  embargo, tenía que trabajar* (I wanted to go to the party. However, I had
  to work).
- **No obstante** (nonetheless) works almost identically to *sin embargo*
  and is interchangeable with it — it just leans slightly more formal,
  common in writing and professional speech.
- **Aunque** (although, even though) is different from the others: it joins
  two ideas **inside one sentence**, rather than connecting two separate
  sentences: *Aunque llovió, fuimos al parque* (Although it rained, we went
  to the park).
- Most of these connectors (*además, sin embargo, no obstante*) typically
  start a new sentence and are followed by a comma; *aunque* sits inside a
  single sentence and usually doesn't need one right after it.

## Examples

| Spanish                                       | English                                  | Notes                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Estudio mucho. Además, practico todos los días.      | I study a lot. I also practice every day.       | *además*, addition                  |
| Hace frío. Sin embargo, salimos a caminar.           | It's cold. However, we went out for a walk.       | *sin embargo*, contrast               |
| El plan era arriesgado. No obstante, funcionó.       | The plan was risky. Nonetheless, it worked.        | *no obstante*, contrast                 |
| Aunque estaba cansado, terminé el proyecto.          | Although I was tired, I finished the project.      | *aunque*, within one sentence              |
| Me encanta viajar. Además, hablo tres idiomas.       | I love traveling. I also speak three languages.     | *además*, addition                           |
| No tenía dinero. Sin embargo, encontró una solución. | He/she didn't have money. However, he/she found a solution. | *sin embargo*, contrast                        |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *aunque* like *sin embargo*, as a sentence-starter linking two
  separate sentences: *Estaba cansado. Aunque terminé el proyecto* sounds
  unnatural — *aunque* belongs inside one sentence: *Aunque estaba cansado,
  terminé el proyecto.*
- Treating *además* as a contrast word: it adds information, it doesn't
  contrast — don't use it where you mean "but" or "however."
- Overusing *no obstante* in casual speech: it's correct but sounds
  formal/bookish in everyday conversation — *sin embargo* or *pero* usually
  fit better with friends.
- Forgetting the comma after *sin embargo* and *no obstante* when they
  start a sentence: *Sin embargo salimos* reads awkwardly — it should be
  *Sin embargo, salimos.*
