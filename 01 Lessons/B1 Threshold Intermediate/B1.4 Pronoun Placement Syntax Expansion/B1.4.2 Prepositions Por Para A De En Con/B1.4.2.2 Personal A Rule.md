## Explanation

You've probably noticed this little word popping up in example sentences
throughout the vault: *Veo a Juan* (I see Juan). That extra *a* isn't a
preposition meaning "to" — it doesn't translate into English at all. It's
the **personal a**, and Spanish requires it whenever the thing or person
receiving the action of a verb — the **direct object** — is a specific
person or a pet. It's easy to forget because English simply doesn't have
anything like it.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- Use **a** before a direct object that's a specific person: *Veo a María*
  (I see María). This applies to plural people too: *Veo a los hombres* (I
  see the men).
- **Pets get it too**, since Spanish treats them like people: *Veo a mi
  perro* (I see my dog). Other animals don't take it: *Veo dos delfines* (I
  see two dolphins).
- **No personal a for things**: *Veo las casas* (I see the houses), *Veo la
  playa* (I see the beach) — inanimate direct objects never take it.
- A sentence can have several *a*'s that mean different things — only spot
  the one marking a specific-person direct object: *Voy a invitar a mi
  hermano a la fiesta* (I'm going to invite my brother to the party) — the
  first *a* is part of *ir a + infinitive*, the second is the personal *a*
  (brother = direct object), the third is the preposition "to."
- With verbs like *buscar* (to look for), *necesitar* (to need), and
  *contratar* (to hire), the personal *a* often **drops** when you mean a
  non-specific person: *Buscamos un profesor* (We're looking for a
  professor — any professor) vs. *Buscamos a un profesor que conocemos*
  (We're looking for a professor we know — a specific one).
- Words like *alguien* (someone) and *nadie* (no one) usually still take
  the personal *a*, even though they don't name a specific individual:
  *¿Conoces a alguien que sepa cocinar?* (Do you know someone who knows how
  to cook?)
- **Tener is the big exception**: even when the object is a person or pet,
  *tener* normally skips the personal *a*: *Tengo dos hijos* (I have two
  children), *Tengo un perro* (I have a dog).

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the personal *a* before a specific person: *Veo María* is
  missing it — should be *Veo a María.*
- Adding it before a thing: *Veo a la playa* is wrong — inanimate objects
  never take the personal *a*: *Veo la playa.*
- Using it with *tener* by default: *Tengo a un perro* sounds off in the
  ordinary sense of ownership — plain *Tengo un perro* is correct, unless
  you specifically mean someone acting in a role (*Tengo a mi tío como
  garante* — I have my uncle as a guarantor).
- Keeping the personal *a* with a non-specific object after verbs like
  *buscar*: *Busco a un profesor* implies you're looking for one particular
  professor you already have in mind — drop the *a* for any professor:
  *Busco un profesor.*
