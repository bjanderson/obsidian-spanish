## Explanation

Here's one of the trickiest pairs in Spanish: **por** and **para** both
often translate as "for" in English, but they express very different ideas.
Rather than memorizing a giant list, it helps to boil it down to one core
contrast: **por looks backward, to a cause or reason; para looks forward, to
a purpose or destination.**

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- When you're pointing at the reason something happened — the cause behind
  it, not what you were aiming for — reach for **por**: *Juan lloró por lo
  que pasó ayer* (Juan cried because of what happened yesterday).
- When you're pointing at a goal you're working toward, use **para**: *Ahorro
  dinero para comprar una casa* (I'm saving money to buy a house).
- Talking about how long something lasted? That's **por**: *Hablamos por
  tres horas* (We talked for three hours). Talking about a due date instead?
  That's **para**: *Necesito el informe para el viernes* (I need the report
  by Friday).
- Trading something or naming a price uses **por**: *Pagué mucho por la
  casa* (I paid a lot for the house). Naming who something is meant for
  uses **para**: *Este regalo es para ti* (This gift is for you).
- Passing through a place calls for **por**: *El pájaro entró por la
  ventana* (The bird came in through the window). Naming where you're
  headed calls for **para**: *Salió para Madrid* (He/she left for Madrid).
- If you work *for* someone as their employee, that's **para**: *Trabajo
  para ella* (I work for her, she's my boss). If you're acting on someone's
  behalf or because of them, that's **por**: *Trabajo por ella* (I work
  because of her/in her place).

## Examples

| Spanish                              | English                              | Notes                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| No pudimos salir por la nieve.             | We couldn't go out because of the snow.      | *por*, cause                        |
| Ahorro dinero para viajar el próximo año.  | I'm saving money to travel next year.         | *para*, purpose                       |
| Estuve en la fiesta por dos horas.         | I was at the party for two hours.              | *por*, duration                         |
| Necesito la tarea para el lunes.           | I need the homework by Monday.                  | *para*, deadline                          |
| Cambié mi carro por uno más nuevo.         | I traded my car for a newer one.                 | *por*, exchange                             |
| Este café es para ti.                      | This coffee is for you.                          | *para*, recipient                             |

## Common Mistakes

- Using *para* when the meaning is "because of": *Lloró para lo que pasó*
  is wrong — cause always takes *por*: *Lloró por lo que pasó.*
- Using *por* for a deadline: *Necesito esto por el viernes* is wrong when
  you mean a due date — deadlines take *para*: *Necesito esto para el
  viernes.*
- Confusing "work for" as employer vs. cause: *Trabajo por mi jefe* (unusual
  — implies cause/on his behalf) when you mean *Trabajo para mi jefe* (he's
  my employer).
- Treating *por*/*para* as interchangeable "for": English uses one word for
  many ideas Spanish splits in two — always ask "is this a reason (por) or
  a goal (para)?"
