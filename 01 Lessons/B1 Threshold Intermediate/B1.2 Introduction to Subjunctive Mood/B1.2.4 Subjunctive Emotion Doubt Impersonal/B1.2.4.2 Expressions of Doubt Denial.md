## Explanation

This one has a twist you haven't seen yet: **the trigger itself can flip
whether you use the subjunctive or not**, depending on whether the main clause
expresses certainty or doubt. **Dudar que** (to doubt that) and **no creer que**
(to not believe that) trigger the subjunctive, because they signal you're
not sure something is true. But **creer que** (to believe/think that), used on
its own without a negative, expresses certainty — so it takes the regular
indicative, not the subjunctive.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **Dudar que** (to doubt that) always triggers the subjunctive: *Dudo que
  llegue a tiempo* (I doubt he'll arrive on time).
- **Creer que** (to believe/think that) in a positive sentence takes the
  **indicative**, because you're stating something you consider a fact:
  *Creo que Juan está en casa* (I think Juan is at home).
- **No creer que** (to not believe/think that) flips to the **subjunctive**,
  because now you're expressing doubt instead of certainty: *No creo que Juan
  esté en casa* (I don't think Juan is at home).
- The same flip applies to **parecer** (to seem): *parece que* + indicative
  (it seems that...) versus *no parece que* + subjunctive (it doesn't seem
  that...).
- **Negar** (to deny) also triggers the subjunctive, since denying something
  is a form of doubting its truth: *Niego que sea mi culpa* (I deny that it's
  my fault).
- The rule of thumb: **certainty → indicative, doubt or denial →
  subjunctive.** When a verb of belief gets negated, it usually stops
  expressing certainty and starts expressing doubt — and the mood switches
  with it.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                               | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Creo que Juan está en casa.                   | I think Juan is at home.                   | *creer que*, indicative (certainty) |
| No creo que Juan esté en casa.                | I don't think Juan is at home.              | *no creer que*, subjunctive (doubt)  |
| Dudo que María quiera ir a la tienda.         | I doubt that Maria wants to go to the store. | *dudar que*                          |
| No parece que Linda quiera comprar el carro.  | It doesn't seem like Linda wants to buy the car. | *no parecer que*                    |
| Parece que va a llover.                       | It looks like it's going to rain.            | *parecer que*, indicative (no negation) |
| Niego que haya roto el jarrón.                | I deny that I broke the vase.                | *negar que*                            |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the subjunctive after plain *creer que* (no negation): *Creo que esté
  en casa* is wrong — with a positive belief, use the indicative: *Creo que
  está en casa.*
- Using the indicative after *no creer que*: *No creo que está en casa* is
  wrong — negating *creer* flips it to subjunctive: *No creo que esté en
  casa.*
- Forgetting that *dudar* always takes the subjunctive, even without
  negation: *Dudo que* is already a doubt-expression on its own, so it
  doesn't need "no" in front of it to trigger the subjunctive.
- Assuming every negated verb triggers the subjunctive — this rule is
  specific to verbs of belief/certainty (*creer, parecer*) being negated, not
  a universal rule for all negation.
