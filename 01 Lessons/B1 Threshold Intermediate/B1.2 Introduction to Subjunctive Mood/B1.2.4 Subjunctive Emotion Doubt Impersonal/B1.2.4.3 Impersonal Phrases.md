## Explanation

This is the last of the WEIRDO-style subjunctive triggers in this section, and
it's one you'll hear constantly: opinions or judgments that don't belong to
any one person — no *yo, tú, él* — just a general statement built with **es +
adjective + que**, what Spanish calls an **impersonal expression**. Because
these expressions are judgments rather than plain facts, the clause that
follows almost always takes the subjunctive.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- The pattern is: **es + adjective + que + [verb in subjunctive]**.
- **Es importante que** (it's important that): *Es importante que estudies
  para el examen* (It's important that you study for the exam).
- **Es necesario que** (it's necessary that): *Es necesario que lleguemos
  temprano* (It's necessary that we arrive early).
- Other common impersonal expressions work the same way: **es bueno que**
  (it's good that), **es malo que** (it's bad that), **es raro que** (it's
  strange that), **es mejor que** (it's better that).
- **Subject change isn't required here** the way it is for verbs like
  *querer* — impersonal expressions don't have a personal subject in the
  first clause to begin with, so the subjunctive always applies to the second
  clause regardless: *Es importante que estudie* can mean "it's important
  that I study" or "that you/he/she study," depending on context.
- When an impersonal expression states a **fact or certainty** instead of a
  judgment — like **es verdad que** (it's true that) or **es cierto que**
  (it's certain that) — it takes the **indicative**, not the subjunctive,
  because now you're stating something as fact, not evaluating it.
- If there's no *que* and no specific subject at all, Spanish often uses the
  **infinitive** instead: *Es importante estudiar* (It's important to study)
  — a general statement with no particular person in mind.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                               | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Es importante que llegues a tiempo.           | It's important that you arrive on time.    | *es importante que*                  |
| Es necesario que todos participen.            | It's necessary that everyone participate.   | *es necesario que*                    |
| Es bueno que hables de tus sentimientos.      | It's good that you talk about your feelings. | *es bueno que*                          |
| Es raro que no haya contestado todavía.       | It's strange that he/she hasn't answered yet. | *es raro que*                            |
| Es verdad que hace frío hoy.                  | It's true that it's cold today.              | *es verdad que*, indicative (fact)         |
| Es importante estudiar todos los días.        | It's important to study every day.            | no *que*, general statement, infinitive      |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the indicative after a judgment-based impersonal expression: *Es
  importante que llegas a tiempo* should be *Es importante que llegues a
  tiempo* — judgments trigger the subjunctive.
- Using the subjunctive after a fact-based impersonal expression: *Es verdad
  que haga frío* is wrong — *es verdad que* states a fact, so it takes the
  indicative: *Es verdad que hace frío.*
- Adding *que* to a general statement with no subject: *Es importante que
  estudiar* mixes two structures — either drop *que* and use the infinitive
  (*Es importante estudiar*) or keep *que* and conjugate a subject (*Es
  importante que estudies*).
- Forgetting that impersonal expressions don't need a subject change to
  trigger the subjunctive, unlike *querer que* or *pedir que* — the
  subjunctive applies here by default whenever there's a specific subject in
  the second clause.
