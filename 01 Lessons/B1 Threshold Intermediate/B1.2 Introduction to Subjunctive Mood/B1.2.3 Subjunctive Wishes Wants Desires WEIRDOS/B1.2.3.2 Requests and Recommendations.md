## Explanation

[[B1.2.3.1 Verbs of Desire and Will|Last lesson]] covered verbs that express what
you personally want or hope for. This lesson covers a related but distinct group:
verbs you use when you're asking someone else to do something, or giving them
advice. **Pedir que** (to ask/request that), **recomendar que** (to recommend
that), and **sugerir que** (to suggest that) all trigger the subjunctive the same
way — they just carry a slightly different flavor: a request, a recommendation, or
a suggestion, instead of a personal wish.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- The pattern is the same one you already know: **[trigger verb] + que +
  [subjunctive verb]**, used only when the subject changes between clauses.
- **Pedir que** (to ask/request that) is for direct requests: *Te pido que me
  ayudes* (I'm asking you to help me).
- **Recomendar que** (to recommend that) and **sugerir que** (to suggest that)
  are softer — you're offering advice, not making a demand.
- These three verbs usually point at the person being asked, recommended to,
  or given a suggestion with a small pronoun — *me, te, le, nos, les* — called
  the **indirect object pronoun**: *Te sugiero que* (I suggest to you that...),
  *Le recomiendo que* (I recommend to him/her that...).
- Don't confuse **pedir** (to ask for/request) with **preguntar** (to ask a
  question). Only *pedir* triggers the subjunctive here, because only *pedir*
  expresses wanting someone to do something — *preguntar* just asks for
  information and doesn't take this pattern.
- **Recomendar** and **sugerir** are close in meaning, but *recomendar* leans
  toward "this is a good idea" (like a recommendation you'd give a friend),
  while *sugerir* leans toward "here's an idea to consider" — a lighter
  suggestion. In everyday speech the two overlap heavily, and either works in
  most contexts.

## Examples

| Spanish                                     | English                                | Notes                          |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Te pido que llegues a tiempo.                 | I'm asking you to arrive on time.            | *pedir que*                         |
| Le recomiendo que pruebe el ceviche.          | I recommend that he/she try the ceviche.     | *recomendar que*, *usted*/*él/ella*   |
| Les sugiero que salgan más temprano.          | I suggest that you all leave earlier.        | *sugerir que*, plural                 |
| Mi doctor me recomienda que duerma más.       | My doctor recommends that I sleep more.       | *recomendar que*, third person          |
| Nos piden que traigamos algo de comer.        | They're asking us to bring something to eat.  | *pedir que*, *nosotros*                 |
| Te sugiero que hables con ella primero.       | I suggest you talk to her first.              | *sugerir que*                            |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing *pedir* with *preguntar*: *Le pregunto que venga* is wrong —
  *preguntar* doesn't trigger the subjunctive this way. It should be *Le pido
  que venga* (I'm asking him/her to come).
- Forgetting the indirect object pronoun: *Sugiero que vengas* is understandable
  but sounds incomplete — Spanish speakers usually include it: *Te sugiero que
  vengas.*
- Using the indicative in the subordinate clause: *Te pido que llegas a
  tiempo* should be *Te pido que llegues a tiempo* — the trigger verb still
  forces subjunctive.
- Treating *recomendar* and *sugerir* as requiring different structures — both
  follow the exact same [trigger + que + subjunctive] pattern as *pedir*.
