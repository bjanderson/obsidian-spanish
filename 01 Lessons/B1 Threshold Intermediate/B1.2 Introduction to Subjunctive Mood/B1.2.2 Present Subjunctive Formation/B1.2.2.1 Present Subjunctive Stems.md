## Explanation

Now that you know when and why to use the subjunctive, it's time to build it. The
good news: there's one reliable shortcut that generates the subjunctive stem for the
overwhelming majority of Spanish verbs, regular and irregular alike. Once you know
this shortcut, you already know most of what you need — the next lesson covers the
endings you attach to this stem.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**The shortcut: take the *yo* form of the present indicative, drop the final -o.**

| Step                              | Example (*hablar*) | Example (*tener*) | Example (*hacer*) |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| 1. Start with the *yo* present indicative | hablo                    | tengo                    | hago                    |
| 2. Drop the final -o                    | habl-                    | teng-                    | hag-                     |
| 3. This is your subjunctive stem        | habl-                    | teng-                    | hag-                     |

- This shortcut explains why so many "irregular" verbs actually behave predictably in
  the subjunctive: any verb with an irregular *yo* form in the present indicative
  (the *-go* verbs like *tener → tengo*, *hacer → hago*, *poner → pongo*, *salir →
  salgo*, *decir → digo*) simply carries that same irregularity into the subjunctive
  stem — *tenga, haga, ponga, salga, diga*.
- Stem-changing verbs (*e→ie*, *o→ue*, *e→i*) also follow this shortcut, since their
  *yo* form already shows the stem change: *pensar → pienso → piens-*, *poder →
  puedo → pued-*, *pedir → pido → pid-*.
- A handful of verbs don't follow this shortcut at all — their *yo* form doesn't
  predict the subjunctive stem (*ser*, *ir*, *saber*, *estar*, *haber*, and *dar*).
  Those six are covered as a group later in
  [[B1.2.2.3 Irregular Subjunctive Stems DISHES|the DISHES lesson]] — for now, just
  know they're the exception to this otherwise reliable rule.
- Once you have the stem, you attach an ending that depends on whether the verb was
  originally *-ar* or *-er/-ir* — that's the subject of the next lesson,
  [[B1.2.2.2 Opposite Vowel Ending Patterns]].

## Examples

| Infinitive | Yo (indicative) | Subjunctive stem | Notes                    |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| hablar         | hablo                  | habl-                      | regular -ar                     |
| comer          | como                   | com-                       | regular -er                     |
| vivir          | vivo                   | viv-                       | regular -ir                     |
| tener          | tengo                  | teng-                      | irregular *yo*, *-go* verb       |
| conocer        | conozco                | conozc-                    | irregular *yo*, spelling change  |
| pensar         | pienso                 | piens-                     | stem-changing (e→ie)             |

## Common Mistakes

- Building the subjunctive stem from the infinitive instead of the *yo* form: *hacer*
  → wrongly guessing *hac-* instead of the correct *hag-* (from *hago*).
- Forgetting that *-go* verbs keep their irregularity: assuming *salir* produces a
  regular subjunctive stem (*sal-*) instead of the correct irregular one (*salg-*,
  from *salgo*).
- Applying this shortcut to *ser, ir, saber, estar, haber, dar* — these six don't
  follow the *yo*-form rule at all and must be memorized separately.
- Stopping at the stem and forgetting it still needs an ending — the stem alone
  (*habl-*, *teng-*) isn't a complete, usable verb form.
