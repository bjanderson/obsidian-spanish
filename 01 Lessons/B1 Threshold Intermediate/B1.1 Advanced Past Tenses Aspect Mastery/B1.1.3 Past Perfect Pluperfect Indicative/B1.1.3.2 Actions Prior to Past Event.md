## Explanation

You now know how to build the past perfect. This lesson is about putting it to work:
using it to show that one past action happened *before* another past action — a
"past before the past." Spanish stories often need three layers of time (something
that had already happened, something that was happening, and something that
happened), and the past perfect is what handles that first, earliest layer.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**The core pattern: past perfect (earlier event) + preterite or imperfect (later
reference point):**

*Ya había comido cuando llegó.* (I had already eaten when he arrived.) Here, eating
happened first (past perfect), and arriving is the reference point (preterite).

- The past perfect always refers to something finished *before* another past moment —
  it never stands alone as "the most recent" past event in a sequence.
- Common connector words that set up this two-layer past: *cuando* (when), *antes de
  que* (before), *ya* (already), *todavía no* (not yet).
- *Antes de que* + subjunctive is common when both clauses share the same subject
  timeframe reference, but with the past perfect you'll most often just see *antes
  de* + infinitive or a simple *cuando* clause — save the subjunctive-heavy version
  for later lessons.
- Word order is flexible: the past perfect clause can come first or second, as long
  as the meaning ("this happened earlier") stays clear: *Cuando llegó, ya había
  comido* works just as well as *Ya había comido cuando llegó*.
- *Todavía no* + past perfect is common for saying something *hadn't* happened yet by
  a certain point: *Todavía no había llegado cuando empezó la reunión.* (He still
  hadn't arrived when the meeting started.)

## Examples

| Spanish                                                | English                                          | Notes                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Cuando llegué a la fiesta, todos ya habían empezado a bailar. | When I arrived at the party, everyone had already started dancing. | *cuando*, *ya*      |
| Ella ya había salido cuando la llamé.                        | She had already left when I called her.                     | *cuando*, *ya*                 |
| Todavía no había terminado la tarea cuando se fue la luz.    | I still hadn't finished my homework when the power went out. | *todavía no*, *cuando*         |
| Antes de mudarnos, ya habíamos visitado la ciudad varias veces. | Before we moved, we had already visited the city several times. | *antes de*                   |
| Cuando el médico llegó, el paciente ya había mejorado.       | By the time the doctor arrived, the patient had already improved. | *cuando*                     |
| No había estudiado nada antes del examen.                    | I hadn't studied at all before the exam.                     | *antes de*, negation            |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the past perfect for the most recent event in a sequence: if two things
  happened, and this is the *second* one, use preterite/imperfect, not past perfect —
  past perfect is reserved for whichever event came first.
- Dropping *ya* or *todavía no* and expecting the past perfect alone to carry the
  "already/not yet" nuance — while grammatically fine without them, these words make
  the sequencing much clearer and more natural in conversation.
- Overusing *antes de que* + subjunctive when a simpler *antes de* + infinitive or
  *cuando* clause would sound more natural and match what you've learned so far.
- Losing track of which event is "first" in a long sentence — if you're not sure,
  ask yourself which action was already finished when the other one started; that
  finished-first action is the one that takes the past perfect.
