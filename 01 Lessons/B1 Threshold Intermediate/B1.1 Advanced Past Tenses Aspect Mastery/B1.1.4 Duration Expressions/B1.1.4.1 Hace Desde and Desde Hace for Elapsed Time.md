## Explanation

English speakers often try to translate "for" and "since" word-for-word into Spanish,
and it usually comes out wrong. Spanish has its own set of patterns for talking about
time — how long ago something happened, when something started, and how long
something has been going on. Once you learn the patterns, they're easy to reuse.

**How long ago something happened — *hace* + preterite:**
Put *hace* and a length of time in front of (or after) a preterite verb to say "X
[time] ago." *Hace* here doesn't change form — it's always *hace*, no matter who's
doing the action.

**When something started — *desde*:**
*Desde* means "since" and points to the specific moment something began. The verb
that follows stays in the present tense, because the action is still going on right
now: *Vivo aquí desde 2020* (I've been living here since 2020) — you're still living
there.

**How long something has been going on — *desde hace*:**
*Desde hace* focuses on duration instead of a starting point. It also pairs with a
present-tense verb: *Trabajo aquí desde hace cinco años* (I've been working here for
five years).

**An alternate way to say duration — *hace* + time + *que* + present:**
You can flip the *hace* pattern around to express duration too, this time with *que*
and a present-tense verb: *Hace cinco años que trabajo aquí* means the same thing as
*Trabajo aquí desde hace cinco años*. It literally translates as "it makes five years
that I work here" — odd in English, completely natural in Spanish.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- **"X ago"**: *hace* + time + preterite verb (or preterite verb + *hace* + time).
  *Fui a la tienda hace dos años.* (I went to the store two years ago.)
- **"Since [point in time]"**: present tense + *desde* + specific point in time.
  *¿Desde cuándo estás aquí?* (Since when have you been here?)
- **"For [duration], ongoing"**: present tense + *desde hace* + time.
  *Vivimos en esta casa desde hace diez años.* (We've been living in this house for
  ten years.)
- **Same duration meaning, flipped order**: *hace* + time + *que* + present tense.
  *Hace diez años que vivimos en esta casa.* (same meaning as above.)
- *Hace* never conjugates for person in any of these patterns — it stays *hace*
  whether the subject is *yo, tú, ellos*, or anyone else.
- *Desde* points to a starting moment (a date, an event); *desde hace* points to a
  length of time. Don't mix them: *desde hace 2020* is wrong — use *desde 2020* or
  *desde hace seis años*, not both together.

## Examples

| Spanish                                          | English                                       | Notes                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Llegamos a la ciudad hace tres meses.              | We arrived in the city three months ago.       | *hace* + preterite           |
| Mi hermana estudia español desde enero.            | My sister has been studying Spanish since January. | *desde* + present tense  |
| Vivo en este barrio desde hace ocho años.          | I've been living in this neighborhood for eight years. | *desde hace* + present |
| Hace dos años que no veo a mis abuelos.            | I haven't seen my grandparents in two years.   | *hace* + *que*, negation      |
| ¿Desde cuándo trabajas en esta empresa?            | Since when have you worked at this company?    | *desde cuándo* question       |
| Nos conocimos hace mucho tiempo.                   | We met a long time ago.                        | *hace* + preterite, vague duration |

## Common Mistakes

- Translating "for" as *para* in a duration expression — Spanish uses *desde hace* or
  *hace... que*, not *para*, to talk about how long something has lasted.
- Using the preterite with *desde* or *desde hace* — both of these describe something
  still ongoing, so the verb stays in the present tense: *vivo*, not *viví*.
- Mixing *desde* and *desde hace* in the same phrase, or using *desde* with a
  duration instead of a starting point (*desde cinco años* is wrong — say *desde hace
  cinco años*).
- Forgetting that *hace* + preterite and *desde hace* + present express two different
  ideas — "three years ago" (a finished, one-time event) versus "for three years, and
  still going" (an ongoing state). Mixing them up changes the meaning.
