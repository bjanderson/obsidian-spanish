## Explanation

You already know how to talk about duration with *desde hace* and *hace... que*
(see [[B1.1.4.1 Hace Desde and Desde Hace for Elapsed Time]]). This lesson gives you
another way to say the same kind of thing — and it's the one you'll hear most often
in everyday conversation.

*Llevar* usually means "to carry" or "to take," but paired with a gerund (the *-ando*
/ *-iendo* form of a verb), it means "to have been doing something for [an amount of
time]." Picture it as "carrying" a stretch of time while an action keeps happening:
*Llevo tres años viviendo aquí* — literally "I carry three years living here," which
means "I've been living here for three years."

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Formula:** *llevar* (conjugated to match the subject) + time expression + gerund.

- *Llevar* is the only word that conjugates here — it changes for person and number
  just like any regular *-ar* verb: *llevo, llevas, lleva, llevamos, llevan*.
- The gerund never changes form: *trabajando, viviendo, estudiando*, etc. — it stays
  the same no matter who the subject is.
- The time expression sits between *llevar* and the gerund: *Llevo* **cinco años**
  *trabajando aquí.*
- You can drop the time expression to ask or say that something is *still* going on,
  without stating how long: *Todavía llevo esperando.* (I'm still waiting.)
- To make it negative, put *sin* + infinitive after *llevar* instead of a gerund:
  *Llevo dos días sin dormir bien.* (I haven't slept well in two days.)
- **Regional note:** in Mexico and other parts of Latin America, *tener* can replace
  *llevar* in this same pattern: *Tengo cinco años trabajando aquí* means the same
  thing as *Llevo cinco años trabajando aquí*. Both are correct — *llevar* is
  understood everywhere, so it's the safer default if you're not sure which region
  you're talking to.

## Examples

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Llevo dos años estudiando español. | I've been studying Spanish for two years. | *llevar* + time + gerund |
| Mi hermano lleva seis meses buscando trabajo. | My brother has been looking for a job for six months. | third person singular |
| Llevamos una hora esperando el autobús. | We've been waiting for the bus for an hour. | *nosotros* form |
| ¿Cuánto tiempo llevas viviendo en esa ciudad? | How long have you been living in that city? | question form |
| Llevo dos días sin dormir bien. | I haven't slept well in two days. | negative pattern with *sin* + infinitive |
| Ella lleva diez años trabajando en el mismo hospital. | She's been working at the same hospital for ten years. | third person singular |

## Common Mistakes

- Conjugating the gerund instead of *llevar* — only *llevar* changes form; the
  gerund (*viviendo, trabajando*) always stays the same.
- Using the infinitive instead of the gerund: *Llevo tres años vivir aquí* is wrong —
  it must be *Llevo tres años viviendo aquí*.
- Forgetting *sin* + infinitive for the negative version and instead trying *llevar*
  + *no* + gerund, which isn't how Spanish speakers say it.
- Placing the time expression after the gerund instead of between *llevar* and the
  gerund — the natural order is *llevar* + time + gerund, not *llevar* + gerund +
  time.
