## Explanation

Spanish has a whole family of tenses built the same way English builds "have eaten,"
"has seen," or "had gone" — a form of *haber* plus a past participle. This is the
present perfect: *haber* conjugated in the present, paired with a past participle,
used to talk about actions connected to now. It's important to know right away that
*haber* here has nothing to do with possession — that's *tener*'s job. *Haber* is a
pure grammar tool for building these compound tenses.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Haber in the present tense (auxiliary use):**

| Subject             | Form  |
| --------------------- | ------- |
| yo                    | he      |
| tú                    | has     |
| él / ella / usted      | ha      |
| nosotros/nosotras      | hemos   |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | han    |

**Present perfect = haber (present) + past participle:**

*¿Has visto esa película?* (Have you seen that movie?) *Hemos comido mucho.* (We've
eaten a lot.)

- *Haber* and the past participle always stay glued together — nothing can come
  between them. *No he todavía comido* is wrong; it must be *No he comido todavía*.
- The past participle never changes for gender or number in this construction — *ella
  ha comido*, *ellos han comido* — the participle stays *comido* regardless of who's
  doing the eating.
- Object pronouns attach before *haber*, not between *haber* and the participle: *Lo
  he visto* (I've seen it), not *He lo visto*.
- In neutral Latin American Spanish, the present perfect works essentially like
  English — for actions connected to the present moment. This contrasts with Spain
  (and parts of Peru and Bolivia), where the present perfect also covers recent
  completed actions that would take simple past in most of Latin America — worth
  recognizing if you hear it, but stick to the Latin American pattern in your own
  speech.

## Examples

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| ¿Has comido hoy?                           | Have you eaten today?                        | *has*, question                 |
| He terminado el libro.                     | I have finished the book.                     | *he*                            |
| Ella ha abierto la ventana.                | She has opened the window.                    | *ha*                            |
| Hemos hablado con el maestro.              | We have spoken with the teacher.              | *hemos*                         |
| Ellos han escrito una carta.               | They have written a letter.                    | *han*                           |
| ¿Lo has visto?                             | Have you seen it?                             | object pronoun before *haber*    |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing *haber* with *tener*: *He un carro* is wrong — possession always uses
  *tener*: *Tengo un carro*. *Haber* is only for building compound tenses (and the
  impersonal *hay*).
- Inserting a word between *haber* and the past participle: *He ya comido* is wrong —
  keep them glued together and put the adverb elsewhere: *Ya he comido*.
- Making the past participle agree with the subject: *Ella ha comida* is wrong — the
  participle never changes here, it's always *comido*.
- Placing an object pronoun after *haber* instead of before it: *He visto lo* is
  wrong — it must come before: *Lo he visto*.
