## Explanation

Now that you can build the present perfect, it's worth being precise about when to
actually reach for it instead of the preterite. The clearest use case is life
experience — talking about whether you've ever done something, within an open,
ongoing timeframe (your whole life so far), rather than a specific, closed moment.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Open timeframe = present perfect; closed timeframe = preterite:**

| Timeframe          | Tense       | Example |
| --------------------- | ------------- | --------- |
| Open (life so far, this year/week if still ongoing) | present perfect | *He viajado a Colombia dos veces.* (I've traveled to Colombia twice.) |
| Closed (a specific finished moment)                  | preterite       | *Viajé a Colombia en 2019.* (I traveled to Colombia in 2019.) |

**Common life-experience markers that trigger the present perfect:**

| Word/phrase   | Meaning              |
| --------------- | ------------------------ |
| nunca            | never                     |
| alguna vez        | ever (in questions)         |
| ya                | already                    |
| todavía no        | not yet                     |
| últimamente       | lately                      |

- *Nunca he* + past participle is the classic "I've never..." pattern: *Nunca he
  visitado Japón.* (I've never visited Japan.)
- *¿Alguna vez has...?* is the standard way to ask "Have you ever...?": *¿Alguna vez
  has comido insectos?* (Have you ever eaten insects?)
- Time expressions that are still open (*este año*, *esta semana*, *este mes*) pair
  naturally with the present perfect, since the period they refer to hasn't closed
  yet: *Este año he leído diez libros.* (This year I've read ten books — the year
  isn't over).
- The same time expression can take either tense depending on whether the period is
  over: *este año he leído* (the year is still ongoing) vs. *el año pasado leí* (last
  year is closed, so preterite).
- Remember the regional note from [[B1.1.2.1 Auxiliary Haber Present]]: this
  open/closed distinction is the neutral Latin American pattern. In Spain (and parts
  of Peru and Bolivia), the present perfect also covers recently-finished, closed
  moments — worth recognizing, but not the pattern to imitate here.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Nunca he probado el ceviche.                  | I've never tried ceviche.                     | *nunca he*                     |
| ¿Alguna vez has estado en Argentina?          | Have you ever been to Argentina?               | *alguna vez has*, question      |
| Ya he visto esa serie dos veces.              | I've already watched that series twice.        | *ya*                            |
| Todavía no hemos decidido a dónde ir.         | We still haven't decided where to go.           | *todavía no*                    |
| Esta semana he trabajado mucho.               | I've worked a lot this week.                    | open timeframe                  |
| La semana pasada trabajé todos los días.      | Last week I worked every day.                    | closed timeframe → preterite    |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the preterite for a lifetime experience: *Nunca probé el ceviche* sounds odd
  for "I've never tried ceviche" in this open-ended sense — the present perfect fits
  better: *Nunca he probado el ceviche*.
- Using the present perfect for a clearly closed period: *Ayer he ido al médico* is
  wrong in neutral Latin American Spanish — *ayer* is a closed moment, so it needs the
  preterite: *Ayer fui al médico*.
- Forgetting *alguna vez* in questions about experience and defaulting to a plain
  yes/no present perfect question, which can sound less natural: *¿Has estado en
  Perú?* works, but *¿Alguna vez has estado en Perú?* is the more idiomatic way to ask
  about lifetime experience.
- Mixing open and closed time markers in the same clause: *Este año fui a la playa
  tres veces* clashes if the year is still ongoing — prefer *Este año he ido a la
  playa tres veces*.
