## Explanation

Now that you know how to conjugate *haber* for the present perfect, the other half of
the equation is the past participle — the "eaten," "spoken," "written" part. Most
verbs build it with a totally predictable pattern. A smaller set of very common verbs
have irregular participles you just have to memorize, but there aren't many of them,
and several follow patterns of their own once you learn the base form.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Regular past participles:**

| Verb type   | Rule                    | Example |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | --------- |
| -ar verbs     | drop -ar, add -ado          | *hablar → hablado*, *probar → probado* |
| -er verbs     | drop -er, add -ido          | *comer → comido*, *aprender → aprendido* |
| -ir verbs     | drop -ir, add -ido          | *vivir → vivido*, *decidir → decidido* |

**Irregular past participles (memorize these):**

| Verb     | Past participle |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| abrir      | abierto              |
| decir      | dicho                |
| escribir   | escrito              |
| hacer      | hecho                |
| poner      | puesto               |
| romper     | roto                 |
| ver        | visto                |
| volver     | vuelto               |

**Irregular participles carry over to compound verbs built on the same root:**

| Verb              | Past participle |
| -------------------- | ------------------ |
| descubrir (uncover)   | descubierto           |
| devolver (return)     | devuelto              |
| describir (describe)  | descrito              |

- The past participle in the present perfect never changes for gender or number —
  it's always the same form no matter who's doing the action.
- Some -er/-ir verbs need a written accent on the *í* to avoid an awkward vowel
  combination: *leer → leído*, *traer → traído*, *oír → oído*, *creer → creído*.
- Recognizing the pattern in *descubrir* (des- + *cubrir*, to cover) → *descubierto*
  and *devolver* (de- + *volver*) → *devuelto* means you don't have to memorize every
  compound verb separately — once you know the root irregular, the compound inherits
  it.
- Past participles aren't just for the present perfect — they also work as adjectives
  (*la puerta está abierta*), where they DO agree in gender and number, unlike inside
  a compound tense. That's a different use covered elsewhere; for the present perfect
  specifically, the form is always fixed.

## Examples

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| He hablado con ella esta mañana.              | I've talked to her this morning.             | regular -ar                    |
| ¿Has comido algo?                             | Have you eaten anything?                      | regular -er                    |
| Hemos vivido aquí cinco años.                 | We've lived here for five years.              | regular -ir                    |
| Ella ha dicho que va a venir.                 | She's said she's coming.                       | irregular, *decir*             |
| ¿Has visto mi mochila?                        | Have you seen my backpack?                     | irregular, *ver*               |
| No he leído ese libro todavía.                | I haven't read that book yet.                  | -er verb needing accent (*leído*) |

## Common Mistakes

- Regularizing an irregular participle: *hacido* instead of *hecho*, *escribido*
  instead of *escrito* — these verbs simply don't follow the regular pattern and must
  be memorized.
- Forgetting the accent on -er/-ir verbs whose stem ends in a vowel: *leido* (no
  accent) looks wrong — it needs *leído*.
- Making the participle agree with the subject inside the present perfect: *Ellas han
  escritas* is wrong — it's always *han escrito*, invariable.
- Assuming a compound verb is regular just because it doesn't look like the base verb
  at first glance: *devolver* still inherits *volver*'s irregularity, giving
  *devuelto*, not *devolvido*.
