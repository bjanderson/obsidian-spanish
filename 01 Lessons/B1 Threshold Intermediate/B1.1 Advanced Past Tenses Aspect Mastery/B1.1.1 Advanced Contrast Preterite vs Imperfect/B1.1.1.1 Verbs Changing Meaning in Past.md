## Explanation

You already know that the preterite is for completed actions and the imperfect is for
ongoing or habitual ones. Most verbs just slot into that pattern predictably. But a
small set of very common verbs — *conocer*, *saber*, *querer*, and *poder* — actually
shift their English translation depending on which past tense you pick. This isn't a
grammar quirk to memorize by rote; it makes sense once you see it as the same
preterite/imperfect logic applied to verbs about knowledge, desire, and ability.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

**Conocer** (to know a person/place):

| Tense     | Meaning                    | Example |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | --------- |
| Imperfect   | knew (already, ongoing state)  | *María me dijo que conocía a Juan.* (María told me she knew Juan.) |
| Preterite   | met (for the first time)       | *Conocí a Juan en 2010.* (I met Juan in 2010.) |

**Saber** (to know information):

| Tense     | Meaning                    | Example |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | --------- |
| Imperfect   | knew (already, ongoing state)  | *No sabía que querías ir al concierto.* (I didn't know you wanted to go to the concert.) |
| Preterite   | found out, learned             | *¿Cuándo supiste que Juan te mintió?* (When did you find out Juan lied to you?) |

**Querer** (to want):

| Tense                | Meaning                    | Example |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------- |
| Imperfect                | wanted (ongoing)               | *Nunca me dijiste que querías comprar una casa.* (You never told me you wanted to buy a house.) |
| Preterite (affirmative)  | tried to (and attempted it)     | *Al escuchar la noticia, quise gritar.* (Upon hearing the news, I wanted/tried to scream.) |
| Preterite (negative)     | refused to                     | *No quise decirle mi nombre.* (I refused to tell him my name.) |

**Poder** (to be able to):

| Tense     | Meaning                    | Example |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | --------- |
| Imperfect   | was capable of (general ability) | *Juan me dijo que podía trabajar el sábado.* (Juan told me he was able to work Saturday.) |
| Preterite   | managed to / actually did it     | *Pude levantar la caja yo mismo.* (I was able to — and did — lift the box myself.) |

- The imperfect describes a state that was simply true at the time — knowing,
  wanting, being capable — with no focus on a specific starting or ending point.
- The preterite marks a specific moment where that state changed or resolved into an
  event: knowledge arrived (*supe*), acquaintance began (*conocí*), desire turned into
  an attempt (*quise*), or ability turned into an accomplishment (*pude*).
- Negative preterite forms of *querer* and *poder* carry extra meaning: *no quise* =
  "I refused," and *no pude* = "I tried and failed" — stronger than a simple "didn't
  want to" or "wasn't able to."
- This same logic extends to *tener*: imperfect *tenía* (had, ongoing) vs. preterite
  *tuve* (got, received) — *Tuve la impresión de que...* (I got the impression that...).

## Examples

| Spanish                                       | English                                     | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Conocí a mi esposa en la universidad.             | I met my wife in college.                            | *conocer*, preterite            |
| Ya conocía este restaurante.                      | I already knew this restaurant.                      | *conocer*, imperfect            |
| Supimos la noticia por las redes sociales.        | We found out the news through social media.          | *saber*, preterite              |
| No sabía que hablabas francés.                    | I didn't know you spoke French.                       | *saber*, imperfect              |
| Quise llamarte, pero se me hizo tarde.            | I tried to call you, but it got late on me.           | *querer*, preterite affirmative |
| No pudimos entrar porque el lugar estaba cerrado. | We couldn't get in (and failed to) because it was closed. | *poder*, preterite negative |

## Common Mistakes

- Using the imperfect for a one-time event: *Conocía a Juan en la fiesta* is wrong if
  it's the first time you met him — that calls for the preterite: *Conocí a Juan en la
  fiesta*.
- Translating *no pude* as simply "I didn't want to" instead of "I tried and failed" —
  the preterite negative carries the stronger, more specific meaning.
- Overcorrecting and always using preterite for these four verbs because they "feel
  like actions" — most of the time you still want the imperfect, since ongoing states
  (already knowing someone, already wanting something) are far more common in
  storytelling than the sudden-change moments.
- Missing that *no quise* means "I refused," not just "I didn't want to" — using it
  loosely can sound more defiant than intended.
