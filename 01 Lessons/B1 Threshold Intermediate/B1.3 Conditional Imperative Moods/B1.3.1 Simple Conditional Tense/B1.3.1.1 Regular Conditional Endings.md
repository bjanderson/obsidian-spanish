## Explanation

You've already used the future tense to talk about what *will* happen. The
**conditional tense** is its close cousin — it talks about what *would*
happen. In English, this is the word "would": *I would travel, she would
help, we would eat.* The good news is that the conditional is one of the
easiest tenses to form in Spanish: most verbs follow one simple, unchanging
pattern.

## Key Points / Conjugation / Rules

- For regular verbs, you take the **infinitive** (the unconjugated -ar/-er/-ir
  form) and add one of these endings — no other changes to the verb:

| Subject               | Ending |
| ------------------------ | -------- |
| yo                        | -ía      |
| tú                        | -ías     |
| él/ella/usted             | -ía      |
| nosotros/nosotras         | -íamos   |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes       | -ían     |

- These endings are **identical for -ar, -er, and -ir verbs** — unlike the
  present tense, there's only one pattern to memorize here.
- Notice that every ending carries a written accent on the *í* — that accent
  never moves or disappears, no matter the subject.
- The *yo* and *él/ella/usted* forms are identical (*hablaría*), just like in
  the imperfect tense — context or a subject pronoun tells you which one is
  meant.

## Examples

| Spanish                                   | English                             | Notes                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Yo hablaría con ella si pudiera.              | I would talk to her if I could.           | *hablar*, -ar verb              |
| ¿Comerías insectos?                           | Would you eat insects?                     | *comer*, -er verb                |
| Ella viviría en la playa si tuviera dinero.   | She would live at the beach if she had money. | *vivir*, -ir verb                  |
| Viajaríamos más si tuviéramos tiempo.         | We would travel more if we had time.        | *viajar*, *nosotros*                 |
| Ellos comprarían la casa si fuera más barata. | They would buy the house if it were cheaper. | *comprar*, *ellos*                    |
| ¿Ustedes trabajarían los fines de semana?     | Would you all work on weekends?             | *trabajar*, plural formal              |

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing the conditional with the imperfect tense: *hablaría* (would
  speak) vs. *hablaba* (used to speak/was speaking) — these describe very
  different things, so double-check which meaning you actually intend.
- Dropping the accent on the *í*: writing *hablaria* instead of *hablaría*
  changes the stress and isn't correct written Spanish.
- Trying to change the verb stem for regular verbs: the whole infinitive
  stays intact — *hablar* + *ía*, not some shortened or altered form.
- Mixing up *yo* and *él/ella/usted* forms since they're identical
  (*hablaría*) — rely on context or an explicit subject to know who's meant.
