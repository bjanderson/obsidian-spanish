# Formal Openings Closings — Conversational Sentences

## Formal Opening Lines

The standard way to greet a recipient at the top of a formal email or letter,
adjusted to who the recipient is and how much you know about them.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Estimado señor Castillo: Le escribo para confirmar los detalles de nuestra reunión del próximo lunes. | Dear Mr. Castillo: I am writing to confirm the details of our meeting next Monday. | Standard formal opening for a known male recipient. |
| Estimada doctora Fuentes: Le agradezco su tiempo y quisiera solicitar una cita para la próxima semana. | Dear Dr. Fuentes: I appreciate your time, and I would like to request an appointment for next week. | Title + last name; *doctora* takes the feminine ending to agree with the recipient. |
| Distinguidos miembros del comité: Me dirijo a ustedes para presentar mi propuesta de investigación. | Distinguished committee members: I am writing to you to present my research proposal. | *Distinguidos* signals extra respect for an institutional audience. |
| A quien corresponda: Solicito información sobre los requisitos para el trámite de visa. | To whom it may concern: I am requesting information about the requirements for the visa process. | Used when there's no specific named recipient. |
| Estimados señores: Adjunto el currículum solicitado para el puesto de gerente de proyecto. | Dear Sirs/Madams: I am attaching the résumé requested for the project manager position. | Plural, gender-neutral opening for an unknown group of recipients. |

## Formal Closing Lines

The standard sign-offs that match a formal opening, used to end the letter
or email on the same register it started with.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Sin otro particular, le saluda atentamente. | With nothing further to add, sincerely. | Common closing pair; *sin otro particular* signals the letter is wrapping up. |
| Reciba un cordial saludo y quedo atento a sus comentarios. | Please accept a cordial greeting, and I remain attentive to your comments. | Slightly warmer closing, common between ongoing business contacts. |
| Quedo a su disposición para cualquier aclaración que necesite. Atentamente, | I remain at your disposal for any clarification you may need. Sincerely, | Offers further help before the sign-off. |
| Le agradezco de antemano su atención a la presente. | Thank you in advance for your attention to this letter. | Common closing line just before the final sign-off. |
| Atentamente, | Sincerely, | The safest, shortest formal sign-off; works in almost any formal context. |

## Requesting Action Formally

Polite, high-register ways to ask the recipient to do something, typically
placed in the body just before the closing.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Le agradecería que me confirmara la recepción de este correo. | I would appreciate it if you could confirm receipt of this email. | Conditional *agradecería* + subjunctive softens the request. |
| Quisiera solicitar una prórroga para la entrega del informe. | I would like to request an extension for the report's submission. | *Quisiera* (conditional of *querer*) is standard for polite formal requests. |
| Le ruego que revise el documento adjunto a la brevedad posible. | I kindly ask that you review the attached document as soon as possible. | *Le ruego* is a stronger, more formal request verb than *pedir*. |
| Mucho le agradecería que me indicara los pasos a seguir. | I would greatly appreciate it if you could indicate the steps to follow. | Common opening for a formal request buried mid-letter. |
| ¿Podría usted enviarme la documentación pendiente antes del viernes? | Could you send me the pending documentation before Friday? | *Usted* form of *poder* keeps the request formal even as a direct question. |

## Expressing Regret or Apology Formally

High-register ways to apologize or express regret in professional
correspondence, without sounding overly casual.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Lamento informarle que no podremos cumplir con la fecha acordada. | I regret to inform you that we will not be able to meet the agreed-upon date. | *Lamento informarle* is the standard formal way to deliver bad news. |
| Le ofrecemos una disculpa por los inconvenientes ocasionados. | We offer our apologies for the inconvenience caused. | Common in customer-service or complaint-response letters. |
| Sentimos mucho el retraso en nuestra respuesta y agradecemos su paciencia. | We are very sorry for the delay in our response, and we appreciate your patience. | *Sentimos* (formal "we regret") pairs naturally with a request for patience. |
| Reconocemos el error cometido y estamos tomando las medidas necesarias para corregirlo. | We acknowledge the mistake made and are taking the necessary steps to correct it. | Formal acknowledgment of fault, common in institutional replies. |
| Le pedimos disculpas por cualquier malentendido que haya podido surgir. | We apologize for any misunderstanding that may have arisen. | *Que haya podido surgir* uses the subjunctive after an indefinite antecedent. |
