# Traditional Proverbs (Refranes) — Conversational Sentences

## Choosing the Safe Sure Thing

Using *más vale pajarito en mano que cien volando* to advise taking a smaller
guaranteed option over a bigger, uncertain one.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —No sé si vender la casa ahora o esperar a que suba más el precio. —Yo la vendería ya. Más vale pajarito en mano que cien volando. | "I don't know whether to sell the house now or wait for the price to go up more." — "I'd sell it now. A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush." | Applied to a real estate decision — takes the sure profit over a speculative future gain. |
| —Me ofrecen dos trabajos: uno seguro y otro con mejor sueldo pero es contrato temporal. —Más vale pajarito en mano que cien volando, yo tomaría el seguro. | "I've got two job offers: one stable, one with better pay but it's a temporary contract." — "A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush — I'd take the stable one." | Common workplace framing — job security over a riskier, higher-paying option. |
| —¿Aceptas la oferta por el carro o esperas a otro comprador? —Acepto. Más vale pájaro en mano que cien volando. | "Are you accepting the offer on the car, or waiting for another buyer?" — "I'm accepting. A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush." | Uses the shortened variant *pájaro* (no diminutive) — shows both forms are interchangeable in speech. |
| —Podríamos esperar a una beca mejor para la maestría. —No, ya tengo esta asegurada. Más vale pajarito en mano. | "We could wait for a better scholarship for the master's program." — "No, I already have this one secured. A bird in the hand." | Speakers often clip the refrán to just the first half when the second half is already understood from context. |
| —¿Negocias el precio o compras ya al precio que piden? —Compro ya, más vale pajarito en mano que cien volando. | "Are you negotiating the price, or buying now at the asking price?" — "I'm buying now — a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush." | Applied to a straightforward purchase decision, showing the refrán's flexibility across very different situations. |

## Urging Quick Action Before It's Too Late

Using *camarón que se duerme, se lo lleva la corriente* to push someone toward
acting fast on a fading opportunity.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —Todavía no mando mi solicitud para el puesto. —¡Mándala ya! Camarón que se duerme, se lo lleva la corriente. | "I still haven't sent my application for the position." — "Send it now! You snooze, you lose." | Direct imperative urging, the most common context for this refrán. |
| —Esa mesa de segunda mano está bien barata, pero la vi hace tres días. —Seguro ya no está. Camarón que se duerme... | "That secondhand table is really cheap, but I saw it three days ago." — "It's probably gone by now. You snooze, you lose..." | Trailing off mid-refrán — the listener is expected to finish it mentally, a common conversational shortcut. |
| —Dejé pasar la promoción del gimnasio, ahora cuesta el doble. —Ahí tienes, camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente. | "I let the gym promotion pass, now it costs double." — "There you go — you snooze, you lose." | Used after the fact, as commentary on a missed opportunity rather than as a warning beforehand. |
| —Voy a esperar una semana más para decidir si compro las entradas. —Yo no esperaría, camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente. | "I'm going to wait one more week to decide whether to buy the tickets." — "I wouldn't wait — you snooze, you lose." | Used to gently push back on someone's plan to delay. |
| —¿Por qué te apuras tanto en responder el correo? —Porque camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente, y ya perdí una oportunidad así antes. | "Why are you rushing so much to answer the email?" — "Because you snooze, you lose, and I already missed an opportunity that way before." | Used to explain one's own urgency, citing the refrán as the underlying life lesson. |

## Reacting to Free Gifts and Earned Reputations

Using *a caballo regalado no se le mira el diente* and *cría fama y échate a
dormir* to comment on gifts and reputations.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —El celular que me regaló mi hermano ya es modelo viejo. —Bueno, a caballo regalado no se le mira el diente. | "The phone my brother gave me is already an old model." — "Well, don't look a gift horse in the mouth." | Classic use — gently checking someone for criticizing something they got for free. |
| —No me encantó el color del sofá que nos regalaron los abuelos. —Ni digas nada, a caballo regalado no se le mira el diente. | "I didn't love the color of the couch our grandparents gave us." — "Don't even say anything — don't look a gift horse in the mouth." | Preemptive use — said before the criticism is fully voiced, to head it off. |
| —Desde que llegó tarde a esa boda, todos piensan que siempre es impuntual. —Cría fama y échate a dormir. | "Ever since he showed up late to that wedding, everyone thinks he's always late." — "Build a reputation and it sticks." | Commenting on how one incident cemented a lasting (negative) reputation. |
| —Ella ayudó una vez con un proyecto y ahora todos le piden ayuda con todo. —Cría fama y échate a dormir, ya la conocen como la que siempre resuelve todo. | "She helped once with a project and now everyone asks her for help with everything." — "Build a reputation and it sticks — now they know her as the one who always fixes everything." | Shows the refrán applies to positive reputations too, not only negative ones. |
| —¿Por qué siguen invitando a Marcos a organizar las fiestas? —Porque organizó una excelente hace años. Cría fama y échate a dormir. | "Why do they keep inviting Marcos to organize the parties?" — "Because he organized a great one years ago. Build a reputation and it sticks." | Explains a present-day pattern by pointing back to a single past event, the refrán's typical logic. |

## Finding the Silver Lining in a Setback

Using *no hay mal que por bien no venga* and *al mal tiempo, buena cara* to
reframe bad news or push through a hard situation.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —Me despidieron del trabajo, pero así encontré uno que pagaba mejor. —Ya ves, no hay mal que por bien no venga. | "I got fired from my job, but that's how I found one that paid better." — "See, every cloud has a silver lining." | Retrospective use — said once the good outcome is already visible. |
| —Se me canceló el vuelo, pero conocí a alguien increíble en el aeropuerto mientras esperaba. —No hay mal que por bien no venga. | "My flight got canceled, but I met someone amazing at the airport while I waited." — "Every cloud has a silver lining." | Personal-story context, common in casual retellings of how something bad turned out well. |
| —Se inundó el sótano, pero al final remodelamos toda esa parte de la casa. —Bueno, no hay mal que por bien no venga. | "The basement flooded, but in the end we remodeled that whole part of the house." — "Well, every cloud has a silver lining." | Applied to a home-repair setback that led to an improvement. |
| —Perdimos el contrato grande, pero ahora tenemos tiempo para mejorar el producto. —Al mal tiempo, buena cara: aprovechemos el tiempo. | "We lost the big contract, but now we have time to improve the product." — "Chin up — let's make the most of the time." | Forward-looking use, said while still in the middle of the hard situation. |
| —No sé cómo vamos a terminar la mudanza con esta lluvia. —Al mal tiempo, buena cara, nos organizamos y ya. | "I don't know how we're going to finish the move with this rain." — "Chin up — we'll get organized and that's that." | Used as active encouragement mid-crisis, not just after the fact. |

## Commenting on Promises, Overreach, and Careless Talk

Using *del dicho al hecho hay mucho trecho*, *el que mucho abarca poco
aprieta*, *en boca cerrada no entran moscas*, and *más vale tarde que nunca*
across everyday judgment calls.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —Dice que va a correr un maratón el próximo año sin haber corrido nunca. —Del dicho al hecho hay mucho trecho. | "He says he's going to run a marathon next year without ever having run before." — "Easier said than done." | Skeptical reaction to an ambitious claim, before it's been tested. |
| —Está manejando tres negocios a la vez y ninguno va bien. —El que mucho abarca poco aprieta. | "She's running three businesses at once and none of them are doing well." — "Jack of all trades, master of none." | Diagnostic use — explaining why something is failing, not just describing it. |
| —Casi le comento a mi suegra lo que pienso de su comida, pero mejor no dije nada. —Hiciste bien, en boca cerrada no entran moscas. | "I almost told my mother-in-law what I think of her cooking, but I decided not to say anything." — "Good call — silence is golden." | Approval of a wise silence, in a sensitive family context. |
| —Por fin me inscribí al curso de inglés que quería tomar hace años. —Más vale tarde que nunca. | "I finally signed up for the English course I wanted to take years ago." — "Better late than never." | Encouraging response to someone finally acting on a long-delayed goal. |
| —Quiere aprender a tocar cuatro instrumentos, aprender tres idiomas y sacar un doctorado, todo este año. —El que mucho abarca poco aprieta, mejor que elija una cosa. | "He wants to learn four instruments, learn three languages, and get a doctorate, all this year." — "Jack of all trades, master of none — he'd do better to pick one thing." | Advisory use, suggesting a course of action based on the refrán's lesson. |
| —No debí haber comentado nada sobre el divorcio de mis vecinos. —Ahora ya sabes: en boca cerrada no entran moscas. | "I shouldn't have said anything about my neighbors' divorce." — "Now you know: silence is golden." | Used as a lesson learned after the fact, reinforcing the refrán as retrospective wisdom. |
