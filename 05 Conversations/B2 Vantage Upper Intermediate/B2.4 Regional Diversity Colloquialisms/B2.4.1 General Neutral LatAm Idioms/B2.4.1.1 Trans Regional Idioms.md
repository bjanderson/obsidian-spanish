# Trans-Regional Idiomatic Expressions — Conversational Sentences

## Reacting to a Coincidence

Used when you unexpectedly run into someone you know, especially far from home.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| ¡El mundo es un pañuelo! Nunca pensé verte aquí. | It's a small world! I never thought I'd see you here. | Classic reaction to a surprise encounter |
| Qué casualidad, el mundo es un pañuelo, ¿no? | What a coincidence, small world, right? | Casual, conversational tag |
| Resulta que mi vecino es primo de mi jefe — el mundo es un pañuelo. | It turns out my neighbor is my boss's cousin — small world. | Used to comment on an unexpected connection |

## Talking About Price

Used to complain about or comment on how expensive something is.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Ese apartamento me costó un ojo de la cara. | That apartment cost me an arm and a leg. | Past tense, personal complaint |
| No compres ese celular, cuesta un ojo de la cara. | Don't buy that phone, it costs an arm and a leg. | Giving advice |
| Todo en esa tienda cuesta un ojo de la cara. | Everything in that store costs an arm and a leg. | General complaint about prices |

## Saying Something Is Easy

Used to describe a task with little difficulty.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Tranquilo, este examen es pan comido. | Relax, this exam is a piece of cake. | Reassuring someone |
| Para ella, cocinar es pan comido. | For her, cooking is a piece of cake. | Describing someone's skill |
| Pensé que sería difícil, pero fue pan comido. | I thought it would be hard, but it was a piece of cake. | Reflecting after the fact |

## When Someone Shows Up Mid-Conversation

Used the moment a person you were just discussing appears.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Hablando del rey de Roma, ahí viene Carlos. | Speak of the devil, here comes Carlos. | Shortened, common spoken form |
| Justo estábamos hablando de ti — hablando del rey de Roma. | We were just talking about you — speak of the devil. | Direct address to the person who appeared |

## Describing Reactions and Blame

Used for overreactions, final straws, and staying out of other people's business.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| No hagas una montaña de un grano de arena, fue solo un error pequeño. | Don't make a mountain out of a molehill, it was just a small mistake. | Calming someone down |
| Ya sé que estás molesto, pero esto fue la gota que colmó el vaso. | I know you're upset, but this was the last straw. | Explaining why patience ran out |
| Prefiero no opinar — no tengo vela en este entierro. | I'd rather not weigh in — it's none of my business. | Politely declining to get involved |
| Mi tío está de malas hoy, mejor no le preguntes nada. | My uncle is in a bad mood today, better not ask him anything. | *Estar de malas* is the more typical Latin American phrasing; *tener mala leche* also works but leans toward Spain |
