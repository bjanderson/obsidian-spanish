# Shifting Fault away from the Speaker — Conversational Sentences

## Explaining a Small Mistake at Home

Everyday sentences softening a small household accident.

| Spanish                                            | English                                        | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Se me rompió tu taza favorita, lo siento.            | Your favorite mug broke on me, I'm sorry.                 | accidental *se*, softened blame    |
| Se me quemó el arroz, perdón.                        | The rice burned on me, sorry.                              | accidental *se*, softened blame      |
| Se me cayó el jarrón sin querer.                     | The vase fell out of my hands without meaning to.            | accidental *se*, softened blame        |

## Explaining a Mistake at Work or School

Everyday sentences softening a mistake in a professional or academic context.

| Spanish                                            | English                                        | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Se me olvidó enviar el correo, lo mando ahora.       | The email slipped my mind, I'm sending it now.              | accidental *se*, softened blame      |
| Se me perdieron los documentos, los estoy buscando.  | The documents got lost on me, I'm looking for them.           | accidental *se*, softened blame        |
| Se nos acabó el tiempo antes de terminar.            | We ran out of time before finishing.                            | accidental *se*, softened blame          |

## Contrasting an Accident with Something Deliberate

Pairs showing when to use the direct version versus the accidental *se* version.

| Spanish                                            | English                                        | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Se me rompió el plato. → Rompí el plato a propósito. | The plate broke on me. → I broke the plate on purpose. | accident vs. deliberate            |
| Se me cayó el vaso. → Tiré el vaso al suelo a propósito. | The glass fell out of my hands. → I threw the glass on the floor on purpose. | accident vs. deliberate |

## Reassuring Someone It Wasn't Intentional

Casual conversational exchanges clarifying that something wasn't on purpose.

| Spanish                                            | English                                        | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| No fue a propósito, se me cayó de las manos.         | It wasn't on purpose, it fell out of my hands.              | accidental *se*, clarifying intent     |
| Fue sin querer, se me olvidó por completo.           | It was unintentional, it completely slipped my mind.          | accidental *se*, clarifying intent       |

## Everyday Apologies Using This Pattern

Common apologies built on the accidental *se* construction.

| Spanish                                            | English                                        | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Perdón, se me hizo tarde por el tráfico.             | Sorry, I ended up running late because of traffic.            | accidental *se*, apology              |
| Disculpa, se me pasó tu mensaje entre tantas cosas.  | Sorry, your message slipped by me with everything going on.     | accidental *se*, apology                |
