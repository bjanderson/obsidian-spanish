# Natural Stress Rules — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Everyday words ending in a vowel (stress on the second-to-last syllable)

Common nouns and adjectives that stress naturally, no accent needed.

| Spanish                    | English                | Notes                                  |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Mi casa está cerca.         | My house is close by.   | *casa* → **ca**-sa                     |
| Tengo mucha tarea hoy.      | I have a lot of homework today. | *tarea* → ta-**re**-a               |
| Ella trabaja en una oficina. | She works in an office. | *oficina* → o-fi-**ci**-na             |
| Necesito una silla nueva.   | I need a new chair.     | *silla* → **si**-lla                   |
| Vivimos en la misma calle.  | We live on the same street. | *calle* → **ca**-lle               |

## Everyday words ending in -n or -s (stress on the second-to-last syllable)

Plurals and verb forms ending in *n* or *s* still stress the second-to-last
syllable — the same rule, just a different final letter.

| Spanish                       | English                    | Notes                              |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Los niños comen a las ocho.     | The kids eat at eight.       | *comen* → **co**-men               |
| ¿Ustedes hablan español?        | Do you all speak Spanish?    | *hablan* → **ha**-blan             |
| Tengo tres exámenes esta semana. | I have three exams this week. | *exámenes* keeps its written accent; compare regular *hablan* |
| Mis padres viven en Bolivia.    | My parents live in Bolivia.  | *viven* → **vi**-ven               |
| Necesitamos más vasos.          | We need more glasses.        | *vasos* → **va**-sos               |

## Everyday words ending in a consonant other than n/s (stress on the last syllable)

Words ending in *l*, *r*, *d*, or *z* push the stress to the final syllable.

| Spanish                          | English                     | Notes                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Voy a hablar con el profesor.       | I'm going to talk to the professor. | *hablar*, *profesor* → last syllable |
| ¿A qué hora regresas de la ciudad?  | What time do you get back from the city? | *ciudad* → ciu-**dad**    |
| El doctor llega a las nueve.        | The doctor arrives at nine.  | *doctor* → doc-**tor**          |
| Ese señor es muy amable.            | That man is very kind.       | *señor* → se-**ñor**             |
| Estoy feliz por ti.                 | I'm happy for you.           | *feliz* → fe-**liz**             |

## Mixed sentences (both stress patterns together)

Real speech mixes both patterns constantly — practice hearing the shift.

| Spanish                                | English                             | Notes                                        |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| El profesor trabaja en la ciudad.         | The professor works in the city.       | *profesor* (last), *trabaja* (2nd-to-last), *ciudad* (last) |
| Mis hermanos comen pan con mantequilla.   | My siblings eat bread with butter.     | *hermanos*, *comen* (2nd-to-last); *pan* is one syllable |
| ¿Ustedes van a hablar con el doctor hoy?  | Are you all going to talk to the doctor today? | *hablan*→*hablar* (last), *doctor* (last)  |
| La silla está cerca de la pared.          | The chair is close to the wall.        | *silla* (2nd-to-last), *pared* (last)          |
