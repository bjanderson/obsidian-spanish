# Natural Stress Rules — Conversational Sentences

## Everyday words ending in a vowel (stress on the second-to-last syllable)

Common nouns and adjectives that stress naturally, no accent needed.

| Spanish                      | English                         | Notes                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Mi casa está cerca.          | My house is close by.           | _casa_ → **ca**-sa         |
| Tengo mucha tarea hoy.       | I have a lot of homework today. | _tarea_ → ta-**re**-a      |
| Ella trabaja en una oficina. | She works in an office.         | _oficina_ → o-fi-**ci**-na |
| Necesito una silla nueva.    | I need a new chair.             | _silla_ → **si**-lla       |
| Vivimos en la misma calle.   | We live on the same street.     | _calle_ → **ca**-lle       |

## Everyday words ending in -n or -s (stress on the second-to-last syllable)

Plurals and verb forms ending in _n_ or _s_ still stress the second-to-last
syllable — the same rule, just a different final letter.

| Spanish                          | English                       | Notes                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Los niños comen a las ocho.      | The kids eat at eight.        | _comen_ → **co**-men                                          |
| ¿Ustedes hablan español?         | Do you all speak Spanish?     | _hablan_ → **ha**-blan                                        |
| Tengo tres exámenes esta semana. | I have three exams this week. | _exámenes_ keeps its written accent; compare regular _hablan_ |
| Mis padres viven en Bolivia.     | My parents live in Bolivia.   | _viven_ → **vi**-ven                                          |
| Necesitamos más vasos.           | We need more glasses.         | _vasos_ → **va**-sos                                          |

## Everyday words ending in a consonant other than n/s (stress on the last syllable)

Words ending in _l_, _r_, _d_, or _z_ push the stress to the final syllable.

| Spanish                            | English                                  | Notes                                |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Voy a hablar con el profesor.      | I'm going to talk to the professor.      | _hablar_, _profesor_ → last syllable |
| ¿A qué hora regresas de la ciudad? | What time do you get back from the city? | _ciudad_ → ciu-**dad**               |
| El doctor llega a las nueve.       | The doctor arrives at nine.              | _doctor_ → doc-**tor**               |
| Ese señor es muy amable.           | That man is very kind.                   | _señor_ → se-**ñor**                 |
| Estoy feliz por ti.                | I'm happy for you.                       | _feliz_ → fe-**liz**                 |

## Mixed sentences (both stress patterns together)

Real speech mixes both patterns constantly — practice hearing the shift.

| Spanish                                  | English                                        | Notes                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| El profesor trabaja en la ciudad.        | The professor works in the city.               | _profesor_ (last), _trabaja_ (2nd-to-last), _ciudad_ (last) |
| Mis hermanos comen pan con mantequilla.  | My siblings eat bread with butter.             | _hermanos_, _comen_ (2nd-to-last); _pan_ is one syllable    |
| ¿Ustedes van a hablar con el doctor hoy? | Are you all going to talk to the doctor today? | *hablan*→*hablar* (last), _doctor_ (last)                   |
| La silla está cerca de la pared.         | The chair is close to the wall.                | _silla_ (2nd-to-last), _pared_ (last)                       |
