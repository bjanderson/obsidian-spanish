# Pitch and Cadence — Conversational Sentences

## Statements (falling pitch at the end)

Plain declarative sentences, said with a settled drop in pitch.

| Spanish                            | English                            | Notes                            |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Vamos a llegar tarde.              | We're going to be late.            | Falling pitch, sense of closure. |
| Mi hermana trabaja en un hospital. | My sister works at a hospital.     | Falling pitch on _hospital_.     |
| Hoy no tengo ganas de salir.       | I don't feel like going out today. | Falling pitch on _salir_.        |
| El café está muy caliente.         | The coffee is very hot.            | Falling pitch on _caliente_.     |

## Yes/no questions (rising pitch at the end)

Same word order as a statement, but the pitch rises to signal a question.

| Spanish                             | English                              | Notes                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| ¿Vamos a llegar tarde?              | Are we going to be late?             | Same words as the statement above, rising pitch. |
| ¿Tu hermana trabaja en un hospital? | Does your sister work at a hospital? | Rising pitch turns it into a question.           |
| ¿Tienes hambre?                     | Are you hungry?                      | Rising pitch at the end.                         |
| ¿Puedes ayudarme un momento?        | Can you help me for a moment?        | Rising pitch on _momento_.                       |
| ¿Ya terminaste la tarea?            | Did you already finish the homework? | Rising pitch at the end.                         |

## Information questions (rise on the question word, fall by the end)

Questions starting with _qué_, _dónde_, _cuándo_, _cómo_, _por qué_, or _quién_.

| Spanish                        | English                      | Notes                               |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| ¿Dónde trabaja tu hermana?     | Where does your sister work? | Rise on _dónde_, fall by the end.   |
| ¿Cuándo empieza la reunión?    | When does the meeting start? | Rise on _cuándo_, fall by the end.  |
| ¿Cómo se llama tu perro?       | What's your dog's name?      | Rise on _cómo_, fall by the end.    |
| ¿Por qué estás tan cansado?    | Why are you so tired?        | Rise on _por qué_, fall by the end. |
| ¿Quién dejó la puerta abierta? | Who left the door open?      | Rise on _quién_, fall by the end.   |

## Mixed pairs in conversation

The same core sentence shifting between statement and question by pitch alone.

| Spanish                                    | English                                                    | Notes                                            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Está lloviendo. / ¿Está lloviendo?         | It's raining. / Is it raining?                             | Identical words, only the pitch differs.         |
| Vienes a la fiesta. / ¿Vienes a la fiesta? | You're coming to the party. / Are you coming to the party? | Falling vs. rising pitch signals the difference. |
| Ya comiste. / ¿Ya comiste?                 | You already ate. / Did you already eat?                    | Same words, statement vs. question by pitch.     |
