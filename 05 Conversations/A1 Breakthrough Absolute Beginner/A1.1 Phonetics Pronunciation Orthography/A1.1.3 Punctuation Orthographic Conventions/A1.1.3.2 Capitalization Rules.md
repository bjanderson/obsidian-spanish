# Capitalization Rules — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually write, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Naming the day or making weekend plans

Days of the week stay lowercase even when they're the main subject of the sentence.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Hoy es miércoles, ¿verdad? | Today is Wednesday, right? | *miércoles* stays lowercase. |
| Nos vemos el sábado. | See you on Saturday. | *sábado* stays lowercase after *el*. |
| Los lunes siempre tengo mucho trabajo. | I always have a lot of work on Mondays. | *lunes* stays lowercase even though it opens the clause. |
| ¿Trabajas los domingos? | Do you work on Sundays? | *domingos* stays lowercase. |

## Talking about birthdays, holidays, or the calendar

Months and seasons stay lowercase too, even in dates and casual small talk.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Mi cumpleaños es en octubre. | My birthday is in October. | *octubre* stays lowercase. |
| Nací un 15 de marzo. | I was born on March 15th. | *marzo* stays lowercase. |
| Aquí el verano es súper caluroso. | Summer is really hot here. | *verano* stays lowercase. |
| Ya casi llega la primavera. | Spring is almost here. | *primavera* stays lowercase. |
| Nos casamos en diciembre. | We're getting married in December. | *diciembre* stays lowercase. |

## Talking about what languages someone speaks

Language names stay lowercase, whether used as a noun or right after a verb like *hablar*.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Hablo español e inglés. | I speak Spanish and English. | Both stay lowercase; *e* replaces *y* before the *i* sound. |
| Está aprendiendo alemán. | She's learning German. | *alemán* stays lowercase. |
| ¿Hablas portugués? | Do you speak Portuguese? | *portugués* stays lowercase. |
| Mi mamá habla francés perfectamente. | My mom speaks French perfectly. | *francés* stays lowercase. |

## Saying where someone is from or their nationality

Nationalities stay lowercase, unlike in English.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Soy colombiana. | I'm Colombian. | *colombiana* stays lowercase. |
| Mi esposo es mexicano. | My husband is Mexican. | *mexicano* stays lowercase. |
| Somos venezolanos. | We're Venezuelan. | *venezolanos* stays lowercase. |
| Ella es chilena, pero vive en Perú. | She's Chilean, but she lives in Peru. | *chilena* stays lowercase; *Perú* is capitalized as a place name. |

## Referring to someone by their title

Titles stay lowercase when spelled out with a name, but their abbreviations are
capitalized.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Fui al consultorio del doctor Ramírez. | I went to Doctor Ramírez's office. | *doctor* stays lowercase before the name. |
| La profesora García nos dio mucha tarea. | Professor García gave us a lot of homework. | *profesora* stays lowercase before the name. |
| Le escribí un correo a la Sra. Molina. | I wrote an email to Mrs. Molina. | *Sra.* is capitalized because it's the abbreviation. |
| El Dr. Ortiz llega a las nueve. | Dr. Ortiz arrives at nine. | *Dr.* is capitalized because it's the abbreviation. |
