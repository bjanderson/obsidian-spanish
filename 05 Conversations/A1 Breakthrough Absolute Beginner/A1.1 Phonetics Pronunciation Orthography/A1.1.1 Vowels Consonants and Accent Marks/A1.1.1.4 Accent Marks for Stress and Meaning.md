# Accent Marks for Stress and Meaning — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, chosen to highlight
accent marks that carry real weight — either overriding the default stress
rule or telling apart two otherwise-identical words.

## El vs. él

The most common accent-driven mix-up: the article *el* (the) and the
pronoun *él* (he/him) sound the same but are never interchangeable in
writing.

| Spanish                 | English                | Notes                                         |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| El perro está en el jardín. | The dog is in the yard.  | Both *el* here are the article "the," no accent. |
| Él vive en Bogotá.        | He lives in Bogotá.      | *Él* here is the pronoun "he," with the accent.   |
| El libro es de él.        | The book is his.         | Article *el* and pronoun *él* side by side — same sentence, different roles. |
| ¿Dónde está él ahora?     | Where is he now?         | *Él* stands alone as the subject.                |
| El carro de él es nuevo.  | His car is new.          | *el* (article, no accent) modifies *carro*; *él* (pronoun, accent) refers to the owner. |

## Tú vs. tu, mi vs. mí

The same pattern shows up with other short word pairs — pronoun gets the
accent, possessive doesn't.

| Spanish                   | English                | Notes                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Tú tienes mi número?      | Do you have my number?    | *Tú* (you, accented) vs. *mi* (my, unaccented).       |
| Tu casa es más grande que la mía. | Your house is bigger than mine. | *Tu* (your, no accent) — not the pronoun *tú*.    |
| Ese regalo es para mí.     | That gift is for me.      | *Mí* (me, accented) after a preposition.             |
| Mi hermano vive cerca.     | My brother lives nearby.  | *Mi* (my, no accent) before the noun.                 |
| ¿Y tú, qué piensas?        | And you, what do you think? | *Tú* stands alone as the emphasized subject.        |

## Words that break the default stress rule

Everyday words whose stress doesn't follow the standard pattern, so they
need a written accent to show where the emphasis really falls.

| Spanish                  | English                 | Notes                                            |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Necesito un café rápido.    | I need a quick coffee.     | *café* and *rápido* both break the default rule and need accents. |
| ¿Cuántos años tiene el árbol? | How old is the tree?      | *árbol* ends in a consonant but stresses the first syllable. |
| Él ya habló con el médico.  | He already spoke with the doctor. | *habló* and *médico* both carry accents that shift where the stress falls. |
| Tengo un examen el lunes.   | I have an exam on Monday.  | *examen* stresses the second syllable without needing an accent — contrast with *exámenes*, which does. |
| Vive en la ciudad de México. | He/she lives in Mexico City. | *México* keeps its accent on the first syllable.       |

## Question and exclamation words

Question words carry an accent whenever they're used to ask something,
even inside a sentence — a different rule from stress, but one that shows
up constantly in real speech.

| Spanish                    | English                | Notes                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| ¿Qué hora es?                | What time is it?          | *Qué* takes an accent in questions.                     |
| No sé dónde está mi teléfono. | I don't know where my phone is. | *dónde* keeps its accent even inside a statement, because it's still asking a question indirectly. |
| ¿Cómo te llamas?             | What's your name?         | *Cómo* takes an accent in questions.                    |
| ¡Qué rico está esto!         | This tastes so good!      | *Qué* also takes an accent in exclamations.             |
| ¿Cuándo llegas?              | When are you arriving?    | *Cuándo* takes an accent in questions.                  |
