# The Spanish Alphabet — Conversational Sentences

## Asking How Something Is Spelled

The question you reach for before someone spells a word, name, or address for you.

| Spanish                               | English                                            | Notes                                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Cómo se escribe tu nombre?           | How do you spell your name?                        | The default, all-purpose version of this question.             |
| ¿Cómo se escribe esa palabra?         | How is that word spelled?                          | Points at something already mentioned.                         |
| ¿Cómo se deletrea tu apellido?        | How do you spell your last name, letter by letter? | _Deletrear_ emphasizes spelling it out loud, letter by letter. |
| ¿Me puedes decir cómo se escribe eso? | Can you tell me how that's written?                | Softer, more polite phrasing for the same question.            |
| ¿Se escribe con hache o sin hache?    | Is it written with an H or without one?            | Common because H is silent and easy to miss.                   |

## Spelling Something Out Loud

What you say when you're the one doing the spelling.

| Spanish                                  | English                                  | Notes                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mi nombre se deletrea E-L-E-N-A.         | My name is spelled E-L-E-N-A.            | Direct letter-by-letter spelling.                                             |
| Se escribe hache-o-te-e-ele: hotel.      | It's written H-O-T-E-L: hotel.           | Naming letters, then the whole word.                                          |
| Voy a deletrear mi correo electrónico.   | I'm going to spell out my email address. | Common before reading an email aloud letter by letter.                        |
| Te lo deletreo: jota-a-eme-o-ene, jamón. | I'll spell it for you: J-A-M-O-N, jamón. | _Te lo deletreo_ — indirect + direct object pronouns stacked before the verb. |
| Es con mayúscula: la ele de Lima.        | It's capitalized: the L in Lima.         | How Spanish speakers clarify a capital letter using a familiar word.          |

## Clarifying Which Letter You Mean

Since several letters sound alike, Spanish speakers often name a familiar word to pin down exactly which one.

| Spanish                                        | English                                       | Notes                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Es con jota, no con ge.                        | It's with a J, not a G.                       | J and G can sound alike before _e/i_, so speakers often distinguish them this way. |
| Es con be grande, no con ve chica.             | It's with a "big B," not a "small V."         | Common way to distinguish B and V, which sound identical in Spanish.               |
| No es con ka, es con ce.                       | It's not with a K, it's with a C.             | K is rare in Spanish, so this clarification comes up with loanwords.               |
| Se escribe con doble ele, no con una sola ele. | It's written with a double L, not a single L. | Distinguishes _ll_ from _l_.                                                       |
| ¿Es con eñe o con ene?                         | Is it with an Ñ or with an N?                 | The most common letter mix-up for English speakers to double-check.                |

## Talking About Tricky Letters

Comments people make about the letters that don't behave the way English speakers expect.

| Spanish                                            | English                                              | Notes                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| La hache es muda, no se pronuncia.                 | The H is silent, it isn't pronounced.                | States the silent-H rule as a fact about the language.                     |
| La jota suena fuerte, casi como una "h" en inglés. | The J has a harsh sound, almost like an English "h." | Describes the sound rather than just naming the letter.                    |
| La eñe no existe en el alfabeto inglés.            | The Ñ doesn't exist in the English alphabet.         | Comes up naturally when explaining Spanish spelling to an English speaker. |
| La ka casi no se usa en español.                   | The K is almost never used in Spanish.               | K appears mostly in loanwords like _kilómetro_.                            |
| La doble ve casi no se usa en español.             | The W is almost never used in Spanish.               | Same pattern as K — mostly loanwords like _whisky_.                        |

## Everyday Spelling Requests

Practical, polite requests for someone to spell something for you — forms, emails, phone calls.

| Spanish                                             | English                                                 | Notes                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ¿Puedes deletrear tu correo electrónico?            | Can you spell out your email address?                   | Extremely common on phone calls and at counters.                        |
| Necesito que me deletrees el nombre de la calle.    | I need you to spell out the street name for me.         | Subjunctive after _necesito que_ — common in requests.                  |
| Por favor, deletrea tu apellido para el formulario. | Please spell your last name for the form.               | Typical instruction at a front desk or on a form.                       |
| ¿Me repites cómo se escribe, letra por letra?       | Can you repeat how it's spelled, letter by letter?      | Asks for a repeat when the first spelling wasn't caught.                |
| Gracias por deletrearlo, ya lo anoté.               | Thanks for spelling that out, I've written it down now. | _Deletrearlo_ — the infinitive with the direct object pronoun attached. |
