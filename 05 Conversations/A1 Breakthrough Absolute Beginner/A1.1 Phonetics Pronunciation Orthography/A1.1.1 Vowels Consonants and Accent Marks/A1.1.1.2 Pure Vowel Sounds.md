# Pure Vowel Sounds — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Everyday greetings built on pure *a* and *e*

Common opening exchanges — say every *a* and *e* clean, without gliding.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Buenas tardes. | Good afternoon. | Two pure *a* sounds back to back — keep each one separate. |
| ¿Cómo estás? | How are you? | The *e* in *estás* stays pure — no "ey" glide. |
| Estoy bien, gracias. | I'm fine, thanks. | *Gracias* has a pure *a*, even though it's unstressed. |
| Te quiero mucho. | I love you a lot. | The *e* in *te* is unstressed but stays full — no schwa. |
| Hasta luego. | See you later. | The *a* in *hasta* stays open, like "father," not "hat." |

## Statements built on pure *o*

Common everyday sentences where *o* has to stay a clean sound, not a glide.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Como pollo los domingos. | I eat chicken on Sundays. | Three pure *o* sounds — none of them glide into "ow." |
| No tengo dinero. | I don't have money. | The *o* in *no* is short and pure, not drawn out like English "no." |
| Vivo solo. | I live alone. | Both *o* sounds stay pure, even at the end of the word. |
| Es mi hermano. | He's my brother. | The final *o* in *hermano* doesn't glide, even though it's the last sound in the sentence. |

## Statements built on pure *i* and *u*

Sentences that lean on the tight, pure *i* and *u* sounds.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Sí, entiendo. | Yes, I understand. | *Sí* is a single pure *i*, like "ee," not "eye." |
| Es difícil. | It's difficult. | Both *i* sounds in *difícil* stay pure and tight. |
| Tú eres mi amigo. | You are my friend. | *Tú* is a pure *u*, like "oo" in "flu," not "you." |
| Un momento, por favor. | One moment, please. | The *u* in *un* stays pure even though the word is short and unstressed. |

## Everyday phrases where unstressed vowels must stay full

Longer, common words where English speakers tend to collapse an unstressed vowel into a schwa — Spanish never does this.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Necesito una computadora. | I need a computer. | Every vowel in *computadora* stays clear, even the unstressed ones. |
| La comunicación es importante. | Communication is important. | English "communication" schwas several vowels — Spanish keeps them all full. |
| Voy a la universidad. | I'm going to the university. | The unstressed *i*'s and *a*'s in *universidad* don't soften at all. |
| Trabajo en una oficina. | I work in an office. | Say *oficina*'s three vowels with equal clarity, not with the first one softened. |
