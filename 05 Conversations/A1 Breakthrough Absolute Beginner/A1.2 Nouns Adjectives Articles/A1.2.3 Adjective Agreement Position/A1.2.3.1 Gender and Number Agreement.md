# Gender and Number Agreement — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Describing something with a full four-form adjective

*-o*-ending adjectives like *rojo* and *bonito* shift for both gender and
number.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Me encanta tu carro rojo. | I love your red car. | Masc. sing. *rojo*. |
| Tiene una casa bonita. | She has a pretty house. | Fem. sing. *bonita*. |
| Compramos unos muebles baratos. | We bought some cheap furniture. | Masc. plural *baratos*. |
| Son unas fotos hermosas. | They're beautiful photos. | Fem. plural *hermosas*. |

## Describing something with a number-only adjective

Consonant- or *-e*-ending adjectives like *azul, feliz,* and *inteligente*
only shift for number.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Tiene los ojos azules. | She has blue eyes. | *azul* → *azules*, no gender change. |
| Es una persona muy feliz. | She's a very happy person. | *feliz* stays the same for gender. |
| Son estudiantes muy inteligentes. | They're very intelligent students. | *inteligente* → *inteligentes*. |
| Vive en un apartamento grande. | He lives in a big apartment. | *grande* stays the same for gender. |

## Checking or correcting agreement out loud

Agreement mistakes are common enough that native speakers often correct
them casually.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Se dice "la casa roja", no "la casa rojo". | It's "la casa roja," not "la casa rojo." | Corrective, teaching context. |
| ¿Es "azul" o "azula"? Es "azul" siempre. | Is it "azul" or "azula"? It's always "azul." | Reinforces that *azul* doesn't change. |
| Las paredes quedaron muy bien, ¿verdad? Sí, están lindas. | The walls turned out great, right? Yes, they look nice. | Plural feminine agreement in natural reply. |
