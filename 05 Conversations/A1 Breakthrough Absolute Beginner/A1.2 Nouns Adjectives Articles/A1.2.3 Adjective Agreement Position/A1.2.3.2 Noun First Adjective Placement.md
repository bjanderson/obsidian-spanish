# Noun-First Adjective Placement — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Describing an object with a single adjective

The everyday default — noun first, then the describing word right after.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Quiero la camisa azul. | I want the blue shirt. | Noun *camisa*, then *azul*. |
| Vive en una casa grande. | She lives in a big house. | Noun *casa*, then *grande*. |
| Leí un libro interesante. | I read an interesting book. | Noun *libro*, then *interesante*. |
| Compré unos zapatos negros. | I bought some black shoes. | Noun *zapatos*, then *negros*. |

## Describing a person's nationality or trait

Nationality and personality adjectives follow the same noun-first pattern.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Conocí a una chica mexicana. | I met a Mexican girl. | Noun *chica*, then *mexicana*. |
| Es un hombre muy amable. | He's a very kind man. | Noun *hombre*, then *amable*. |
| Trabajo con un equipo talentoso. | I work with a talented team. | Noun *equipo*, then *talentoso*. |

## Stacking two adjectives after the noun

Multiple descriptions often stack after the noun, joined with *y*.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Tienen una casa grande y bonita. | They have a big, pretty house. | Two adjectives stacked with *y*. |
| Es una persona inteligente y trabajadora. | She's an intelligent, hardworking person. | Two adjectives stacked with *y*. |
| Buscamos un apartamento pequeño pero cómodo. | We're looking for a small but comfortable apartment. | Two adjectives contrasted with *pero*. |
