# Invariable Adjectives — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Describing something orange, pink, or violet

These color words never change, no matter the noun's gender or number.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Me gusta tu camisa naranja. | I like your orange shirt. | *naranja* stays the same. |
| Compré unas flores rosa. | I bought some pink flowers. | *rosa* stays the same, even plural. |
| Tiene una bufanda violeta. | She has a violet scarf. | *violeta* stays the same. |
| Son unos zapatos naranja bien llamativos. | Those are some really eye-catching orange shoes. | *naranja* stays the same in the plural. |

## Describing something brown with café

*Café* is the common, safe choice for brown across neutral Latin American
Spanish, and it never changes either.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Los zapatos café son mis favoritos. | The brown shoes are my favorites. | *café* stays the same, even plural. |
| Tiene el pelo café oscuro. | She has dark brown hair. | *café* plus invariable *oscuro*. |
| Busco una mesa color café. | I'm looking for a brown table. | *color* construction — always safe. |

## Adding light or dark to a color

*Claro* and *oscuro* stay fixed after any color, regardless of the noun.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Prefiero el azul claro. | I prefer light blue. | *claro* stays invariable. |
| Las paredes son verde oscuro. | The walls are dark green. | *oscuro* stays invariable, even with a plural noun. |
| Quiero pintar la puerta verde claro. | I want to paint the door light green. | *claro* stays invariable. |
