# Common Gender Exceptions — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Talking about a problem or plan (-ma words)

*-ma* words like *problema, programa,* and *sistema* stay masculine despite
ending in *-a*, and they come up constantly in daily conversation.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Tenemos un problema. | We have a problem. | *el problema* — masculine despite *-a*. |
| ¿Cuál es el programa de hoy? | What's today's schedule? | *el programa* — masculine. |
| El sistema no funciona bien. | The system isn't working well. | *el sistema* — masculine. |
| Fue un buen día. | It was a good day. | *el día* — masculine. |

## Asking about someone's hand or a photo (-o words that are feminine)

*Mano, foto,* and *moto* stay feminine even though they end in *-o*.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Dame la mano. | Give me your hand. | *la mano* — feminine despite *-o*. |
| Me encanta esa foto. | I love that photo. | *la foto* — feminine. |
| ¿Es tu moto? | Is that your motorcycle? | *la moto* — feminine. |
| Se lastimó la mano izquierda. | She hurt her left hand. | *la mano izquierda* — feminine adjective agrees. |

## Describing water or something starting with stressed a-/ha-

*Agua* and similar words take *el* in the singular for pronunciation but
stay grammatically feminine.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| El agua está fría. | The water is cold. | *el agua*, but *fría* stays feminine. |
| Necesito un poco de agua fresca. | I need a little fresh water. | *agua fresca* — feminine adjective. |
| Las aguas del lago son tranquilas. | The lake's waters are calm. | Plural reverts to *las*. |
| Cuidado con el hacha, está muy afilada. | Careful with the axe, it's very sharp. | *el hacha*, feminine adjective *afilada*. |
