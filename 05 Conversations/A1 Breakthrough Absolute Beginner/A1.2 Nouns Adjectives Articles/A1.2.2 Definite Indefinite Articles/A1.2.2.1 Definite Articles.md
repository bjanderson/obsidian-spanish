# Definite Articles — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Pointing out a specific thing

The most basic use of *el/la/los/las* — identifying a particular object.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| El libro está en la mesa. | The book is on the table. | *el* (masc. sing.), *la* (fem. sing.). |
| Los niños están en el parque. | The kids are at the park. | *los* (masc. plural). |
| Las llaves están aquí. | The keys are here. | *las* (fem. plural). |
| ¿Viste la película nueva? | Did you see the new movie? | *la* (fem. sing.). |

## Talking about a general category

Definite articles show up with things you like or dislike, even when
English would drop "the."

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Me gusta la música en vivo. | I like live music. | General category, article required. |
| No me gusta el frío. | I don't like the cold. | General category, article required. |
| Odio las mentiras. | I hate lies. | General category, article required. |
| El café me encanta. | I love coffee. | Subject placed first, still takes article. |

## Saying what day something happens

Days of the week take the article for recurring or specific events, but
not right after *ser*.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Nos vemos el jueves. | See you on Thursday. | *el jueves* — specific day. |
| Trabajo los lunes y miércoles. | I work Mondays and Wednesdays. | *los* — recurring days. |
| Hoy es viernes. | Today is Friday. | No article after *ser*. |
| La reunión es el martes. | The meeting is on Tuesday. | *el martes* — specific event day. |
