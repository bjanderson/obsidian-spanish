# Basic Article Omission — Conversational Sentences

Natural sentences a Spanish speaker would actually say, using this lesson's point,
grouped by the pattern each sentence follows.

## Stating a profession without an article

The most common everyday case — introducing what you or someone else does
for work.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Soy maestra. | I'm a teacher. | No article — unmodified profession. |
| Mi hermano es abogado. | My brother is a lawyer. | No article. |
| Es enfermera en el hospital central. | She's a nurse at the central hospital. | No article. |
| ¿Eres estudiante? | Are you a student? | No article in the question. |

## Adding the article back once you describe the person

As soon as an adjective describes the profession, the article returns.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Es una maestra excelente. | She's an excellent teacher. | Article returns with *excelente*. |
| Es un abogado muy honesto. | He's a very honest lawyer. | Article returns with *honesto*. |
| Soy una enfermera muy dedicada. | I'm a very dedicated nurse. | Article returns with *dedicada*. |

## Stating nationality or religion without an article

The same no-article pattern applies right after *ser* for nationality and
religion.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Es colombiana. | She's Colombian. | No article. |
| Somos católicos. | We're Catholic. | No article. |
| Es peruano, ¿verdad? | He's Peruvian, right? | No article. |

## Dropping the article after sin or in a casual question

Common in everyday speech when the exact identity doesn't matter.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Salió sin paraguas. | He left without an umbrella. | No article after *sin*. |
| ¿Tienes carro? | Do you have a car? | Casual possession question. |
| Vino sin abrigo, con este frío. | He came without a coat, in this cold. | No article after *sin*. |
