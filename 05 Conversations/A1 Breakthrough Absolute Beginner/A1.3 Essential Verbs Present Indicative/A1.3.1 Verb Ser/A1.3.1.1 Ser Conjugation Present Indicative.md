# Ser Conjugation Present Indicative — Conversational Sentences

## Introducing Yourself with Soy

The *yo* form used to give your name, origin, or occupation.

| Spanish                  | English                | Notes                       |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Soy Ana.                   | I'm Ana.                 | name                          |
| Soy de Chile.               | I'm from Chile.          | origin                        |
| Soy ingeniero.              | I'm an engineer.         | occupation, no article        |
| Soy nuevo aquí.             | I'm new here.            | trait                         |

## Asking Someone Who They Are with Eres

The *tú* form in everyday questions between people who know each other.

| Spanish                       | English                     | Notes            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| ¿Eres de aquí?                    | Are you from here?             | origin question   |
| ¿Eres estudiante?                 | Are you a student?             | occupation question |
| Eres muy amable.                  | You're very kind.              | trait, statement  |
| ¿Tú eres el hermano de Marcos?    | Are you Marcos's brother?      | pronoun kept for clarity |

## Talking About Someone Else with Es

The *él/ella/usted* form for describing a third person or addressing someone formally.

| Spanish                   | English                    | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Mi amiga es de Bogotá.        | My friend is from Bogotá.     | *ella* implied by subject   |
| Es profesor de historia.      | He's a history teacher.       | occupation, no article      |
| Usted es muy generoso.        | You're very generous.         | formal *usted*              |
| ¿Quién es esa persona?        | Who is that person?           | question with *es*          |

## Talking About a Group with Somos or Son

The *nosotros* and *ellos/ustedes* forms for groups.

| Spanish                     | English                   | Notes            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Somos vecinos.                  | We're neighbors.             | *nosotros*          |
| Somos de la misma ciudad.       | We're from the same city.    | *nosotros*          |
| Son muy trabajadores.           | They're very hardworking.    | *ellos*             |
| ¿Ustedes son hermanos?          | Are you (all) siblings?      | *ustedes*, formal plural |

## Everyday Small Talk with Ser

Common short exchanges that rely on *ser* for identity or origin.

| Spanish                        | English                      | Notes                  |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| ¿De dónde eres?                     | Where are you from?             | very common opener         |
| — Soy de Estados Unidos.            | — I'm from the United States.   | reply                      |
| ¿Cómo es tu hermana?                | What's your sister like?        | asking for description     |
| Es alta y muy simpática.            | She's tall and very nice.       | reply with traits          |
