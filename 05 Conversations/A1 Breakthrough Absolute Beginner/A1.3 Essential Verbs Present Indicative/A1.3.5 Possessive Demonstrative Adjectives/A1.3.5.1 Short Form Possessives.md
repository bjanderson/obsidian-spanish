# Short Form Possessives — Conversational Sentences

## Talking About Your Own Things with Mi/Mis

Everyday statements about what belongs to you.

| Spanish                     | English                     | Notes             |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Mi celular está descargado.       | My phone is dead.                | *mi* + singular           |
| Mis amigos viven cerca.           | My friends live nearby.          | *mis* + plural            |
| Este es mi asiento.               | This is my seat.                 | *mi* + singular           |
| Perdí mis llaves otra vez.        | I lost my keys again.            | *mis* + plural            |

## Asking About Someone's Things with Tu/Tus

Casual questions between friends.

| Spanish                    | English                     | Notes            |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| ¿Es tu mochila?                   | Is this your backpack?           | *tu* + singular           |
| ¿Cómo están tus padres?           | How are your parents?            | *tus* + plural            |
| Tu español está mejorando mucho.  | Your Spanish is improving a lot. | *tu* + singular           |
| ¿Dónde dejaste tus cosas?         | Where did you leave your stuff?  | *tus* + plural            |

## Clarifying Ownership with Su/Sus

Using *su/sus* and clarifying with *de él/de ella/de ustedes* when needed.

| Spanish                          | English                          | Notes                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Su oficina está en el tercer piso.        | Her office is on the third floor.            | *su*, context makes it clear     |
| ¿Es su carro o el carro de ella?          | Is it his car or her car?                    | clarifying ambiguous *su*        |
| Sus hijos estudian en esa escuela.        | Their kids study at that school.             | *sus*, plural                    |
| Es la casa de ellos, no la de nosotros.   | It's their house, not ours.                  | clarifying with *de ellos*       |

## Talking About Shared Things with Nuestro/Nuestra

*Nuestro/nuestra* agreeing with the noun's gender.

| Spanish                        | English                        | Notes                    |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Nuestro apartamento es pequeño.        | Our apartment is small.                | *nuestro*, masculine             |
| Nuestra reunión es a las diez.         | Our meeting is at ten.                 | *nuestra*, feminine              |
| Nuestros planes cambiaron.             | Our plans changed.                     | *nuestros*, masculine plural     |
| Nuestras vacaciones empiezan mañana.   | Our vacation starts tomorrow.          | *nuestras*, feminine plural      |

## Possessives With Body Parts and Clothing

When Spanish skips the possessive and uses the article instead.

| Spanish                         | English                       | Notes                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Me duele la cabeza.                      | My head hurts.                            | article instead of possessive      |
| Ponte los zapatos.                       | Put on your shoes.                        | article instead of possessive      |
| Me lavo las manos antes de comer.        | I wash my hands before eating.            | article instead of possessive      |
| ¡Es mi abrigo, no tu abrigo!             | It's my coat, not your coat!              | possessive kept for emphasis       |
