# Long Form Possessive Pronouns — Conversational Sentences

## Answering "Whose Is This?" (Standing Alone After *Ser*)

The most common everyday use — someone asks whose something is, and you answer with
just the possessive, no noun repeated.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —¿De quién es esta bufanda? —Es mía. | "Whose scarf is this?" — "It's mine." | Feminine singular agreement with *bufanda*. |
| —¿Este paraguas es tuyo? —No, no es mío, es de mi hermana. | "Is this umbrella yours?" — "No, it's not mine, it's my sister's." | Shows the negative form and a clarifying *de + person*. |
| —¿Estos guantes son suyos, señora? —Sí, son míos, gracias. | "Are these gloves yours, ma'am?" — "Yes, they're mine, thank you." | Formal *usted* register — *suyos* asking, *míos* answering. |
| —¿La maleta negra es tuya o es de Carlos? —Es mía, la de Carlos es la azul. | "Is the black suitcase yours or Carlos's?" — "It's mine, Carlos's is the blue one." | Contrasts ownership between two people in one exchange. |
| —¿Son nuestras estas entradas o son de ellos? —Son nuestras, guárdalas. | "Are these tickets ours or theirs?" — "They're ours, keep them." | Plural feminine agreement with *entradas*. |

## Lending and Borrowing (Comparing "Mine" vs. "Yours")

A very natural context for long-form possessives — comparing your thing to someone
else's when one of you needs to borrow something.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Mi cargador se rompió, ¿me prestas el tuyo? | My charger broke, can you lend me yours? | *el tuyo* stands in for "tu cargador." |
| Nuestro carro está en el taller; ¿podemos usar el suyo? | Our car is in the shop; can we use yours (formal)? | Mixes short form *nuestro* with long form *el suyo*. |
| Mi computadora es más rápida que la tuya. | My computer is faster than yours. | *la tuya* replaces "tu computadora" in a comparison. |
| Olvidé mi paraguas en casa; ¿tienes uno extra, o uso el tuyo? | I forgot my umbrella at home; do you have an extra one, or should I use yours? | Natural everyday borrowing request. |
| Tus llaves están aquí, pero no encuentro las mías. | Your keys are here, but I can't find mine. | Plural feminine *las mías*, paired with short-form *tus*. |

## Introducing People and Things as "Of Mine" (After the Noun)

Placing the long form right after a noun, usually to single one thing out among
several — a common pattern when introducing people or mentioning possessions in
passing.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| Te presento a un amigo mío, se llama Diego. | Let me introduce you to a friend of mine, his name is Diego. | Implies Diego is one of several friends. |
| Una prima mía vive en Guadalajara. | A cousin of mine lives in Guadalajara. | Same "one of several" implication, feminine noun. |
| Ese comentario tuyo me hizo reír mucho. | That comment of yours made me laugh a lot. | Long form after the noun, singling out one specific comment. |
| Un colega nuestro nos recomendó este restaurante. | A colleague of ours recommended this restaurant to us. | Plural-owner long form, *nuestro*, still agreeing with singular *colega*. |
| Esa costumbre suya de llegar tarde ya la conocemos todos. | That habit of his/hers of arriving late, we all already know it. | Shows *suya* used descriptively, after the noun. |

## Clarifying Ambiguous *Suyo/Suya*

Since *suyo* can mean "his," "hers," "yours (formal)," or "theirs," speakers often
add *de él/de ella/de usted/de ellos* to make the meaning clear.

| Spanish | English | Notes |
| ------- | ------- | ----- |
| —¿Este abrigo es suyo? —¿De quién, de ella o de él? | "Is this coat theirs/his/hers?" — "Whose, hers or his?" | Shows a listener asking for clarification when *suyo* alone is ambiguous. |
| No es de él, es de ella. | It's not his, it's hers. | Directly resolving the ambiguity with *de él* vs. *de ella*. |
| Ese asiento es suyo, de usted, señor. | That seat is yours, sir. | Formal clarification adding *de usted* after *suyo*. |
| Los boletos son suyos, de ellos, no nuestros. | The tickets are theirs, not ours. | Contrasts *suyos (de ellos)* against *nuestros* in the same sentence. |
| —¿La idea fue suya? —Sí, fue idea de ella, no mía. | "Was the idea hers/theirs?" — "Yes, it was her idea, not mine." | Combines a *suya* question with a *de ella* + *mía* answer. |
