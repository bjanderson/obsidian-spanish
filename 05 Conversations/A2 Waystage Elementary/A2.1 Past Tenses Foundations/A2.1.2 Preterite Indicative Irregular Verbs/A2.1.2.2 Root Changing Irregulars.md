# Root Changing Irregulars — Conversational Sentences

## Talking About Having Something (Tener)

*Tener* in the preterite often means something was received, or an event happened to you.

| Spanish                                | English                              | Notes                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Tuve un problema con el carro.            | I had a problem with the car.              | *tener*, *yo*                |
| ¿Tuviste tiempo de leer el correo?        | Did you have time to read the email?       | *tener*, *tú*                |
| Ella tuvo un bebé el mes pasado.          | She had a baby last month.                 | *tener*, *ella*              |
| Tuvimos suerte con el clima ese día.      | We had luck with the weather that day.     | *tener*, *nosotros*          |

## Talking About Where You Were (Estar)

*Estar* in the preterite pins down a specific, finished stretch of presence somewhere.

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                  |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Estuve en el hospital toda la tarde.        | I was at the hospital all afternoon.       | *estar*, *yo*                |
| ¿Dónde estuviste anoche?                    | Where were you last night?                 | *estar*, *tú*                |
| Mi hermano estuvo enfermo la semana pasada. | My brother was sick last week.             | *estar*, *él*                |
| Estuvimos esperando por dos horas.          | We were waiting for two hours.             | *estar*, *nosotros*          |

## Talking About Ability or Managing to Do Something (Poder)

*Poder* in the preterite usually means someone actually managed (or failed) to do something.

| Spanish                                    | English                              | Notes                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Por fin pude terminar el proyecto.            | I finally managed to finish the project.   | *poder*, *yo*                 |
| ¿Pudiste dormir algo anoche?                  | Were you able to sleep at all last night?  | *poder*, *tú*                 |
| No pudimos encontrar estacionamiento.         | We couldn't find parking.                  | *poder*, *nosotros*, negative |
| Al final pudieron resolver el problema.       | In the end they managed to solve the problem. | *poder*, *ellos*           |

## Talking About Putting Things Somewhere (Poner)

*Poner* is common in everyday narration about arranging or placing objects.

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Puse las llaves en la mesa.                   | I put the keys on the table.               | *poner*, *yo*                |
| ¿Dónde pusiste mi cargador?                   | Where did you put my charger?              | *poner*, *tú*                |
| Ella se puso nerviosa antes del examen.       | She got nervous before the exam.           | *ponerse*, *ella*             |
| Pusimos música para animar la fiesta.         | We put on music to liven up the party.     | *poner*, *nosotros*           |

## Talking About Coming or Arriving (Venir)

*Venir* often shows up when describing visits or someone's arrival.

| Spanish                                   | English                              | Notes                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Vine directo del trabajo.                      | I came straight from work.                 | *venir*, *yo*                 |
| ¿Viniste caminando o en carro?                 | Did you come walking or by car?            | *venir*, *tú*                 |
| Mi tía vino a quedarse con nosotros.           | My aunt came to stay with us.              | *venir*, *ella*               |
| Vinieron sin avisar.                           | They came without warning.                 | *venir*, *ellos*              |
