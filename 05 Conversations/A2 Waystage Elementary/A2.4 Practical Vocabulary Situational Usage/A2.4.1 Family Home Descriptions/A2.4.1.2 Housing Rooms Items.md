# Housing Types, Rooms, and Domestic Items — Conversational Sentences

## Describing Where You Live

Common ways to describe your home when meeting someone new.

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Vivo en un apartamento en el centro.     | I live in an apartment downtown.          | *apartamento*             |
| Mi casa tiene dos pisos.                 | My house has two floors.                  | *piso* = floor/story here |
| Rento un estudio cerca del trabajo.      | I rent a studio near work.                | *estudio*                 |
| Compartimos un departamento con dos amigos. | We share an apartment with two friends. | *departamento*             |

## Giving a Room-by-Room House Tour

Sentences you'd use showing someone around your home.

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Esta es la sala, y aquí está la cocina.  | This is the living room, and here's the kitchen. | *estar*, location  |
| El baño está al final del pasillo.       | The bathroom is at the end of the hallway. | *estar*, location       |
| Arriba están las recámaras.              | The bedrooms are upstairs.                 | *estar*, plural           |
| El comedor da al patio.                  | The dining room looks out onto the patio.  | *dar a*                   |

## Asking About Someone's Home

Everyday questions when you're curious about someone's living situation.

| Spanish                              | English                            | Notes                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| ¿Cuántos cuartos tiene tu casa?          | How many rooms does your house have?      | *tener*, question         |
| ¿Tu apartamento tiene balcón?            | Does your apartment have a balcony?        | *tener*, question         |
| ¿Dónde está el interruptor de la luz?    | Where's the light switch?                  | *estar*, question         |
| ¿La cocina es grande o pequeña?          | Is the kitchen big or small?               | *ser*, description        |

## Talking About Furniture and Setting Up a Space

Sentences about buying, arranging, or missing furniture.

| Spanish                                  | English                              | Notes                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Necesitamos una mesa nueva para el comedor.   | We need a new table for the dining room.   | *necesitar*               |
| Puse el espejo cerca de la puerta.            | I put the mirror near the door.            | *poner*, past              |
| El sofá no cabe en la sala.                   | The couch doesn't fit in the living room.  | *caber*                   |
| Falta una lámpara en esta esquina.            | This corner is missing a lamp.             | *faltar*                  |

## Talking About Moving or Renovating

Common everyday scenarios about changing living spaces.

| Spanish                              | English                              | Notes                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Nos mudamos a una casa más grande.       | We're moving to a bigger house.             | *mudarse*                 |
| Van a pintar las paredes de la cocina.   | They're going to paint the kitchen walls.   | *ir a* + infinitive       |
| Cambiamos las cortinas de la sala.       | We changed the living room curtains.        | *cambiar*                 |
| El nuevo apartamento tiene más ventanas. | The new apartment has more windows.         | *tener*, comparison        |
