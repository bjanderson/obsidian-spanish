## English Translation

Ambassador Cecilia Duarte had, that day, three consecutive meetings with representatives of the same country, but in contexts so different that they demanded three completely different registers from her, almost as if she were speaking three different languages while never stopping being the same language.

The first meeting, at nine in the morning, was with the Minister of Foreign Affairs, in a formal reception room decorated with flags and official portraits. "Your excellency," Cecilia began, bowing her head slightly, "it is an honor for my country to continue this conversation we began at last spring's summit. We trust that, with goodwill on both sides, we can reach an agreement that benefits our peoples." Every word was calculated: not one extra phrase, not one gesture that could be read as a lack of respect for the hierarchy of the office.

At noon, she met with the ministry's technical team, a group of younger officials she had already worked with before. The tone shifted immediately. "Okay, let's get to the point," Cecilia said, taking off her formal jacket and sitting down more casually. "You and I both know the real problem isn't the tariff, it's the implementation timeline. What room do we actually have?" The team, comfortable with that directness, responded with the same honesty, and in under an hour they resolved details that would have taken days in the morning's formal language.

That evening, at a diplomatic reception with background music and glasses of wine, Cecilia ran into the same minister she had dealt with so formally hours earlier. This time, however, the conversation took a different tone. "You know, Minister? My daughter turns fifteen this weekend, and I still don't know what to get her," Cecilia remarked, smiling. The minister, relaxed by the setting and perhaps by the wine, laughed and shared a story about his own teenage children. In ten minutes of casual conversation, Cecilia gained something no formal meeting could have achieved: the personal trust needed for both delegations to give a little more than planned the next day.

Returning to her hotel that night, Cecilia reflected on the three versions of herself she had played that day: the solemn diplomat of the morning, the direct negotiator of noon, the warm, curious person of the evening. None of them was fake; all of them were her, adapting, as the profession demands, to the exact context each moment required.
