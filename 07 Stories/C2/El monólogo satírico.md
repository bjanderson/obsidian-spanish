## English Translation

Nicolás had spent weeks preparing his act for the school talent show, but he didn't plan to sing or dance like most of his classmates. His plan was to perform a comedy monologue, something no one else had dared to try, and the topic he had chosen — the school's absurd rules — seemed perfect, as long as he managed to say it without it sounding like a direct complaint.

"The secret is in the irony," his older sister explained, who had studied theater in college. "If you say exactly the opposite of what you think, with the right tone, the audience gets the joke without it sounding like an attack." Nicolás spent the following days polishing every line, looking for the perfect double meaning for every criticism he wanted to make.

On the night of the show, he walked onto the stage with his nerves twisting his stomach. "I want to thank our school," he began, with an exaggeratedly sincere smile, "for banning phones in class. Thanks to that rule, we now know exactly what time it is just by looking at the classroom clock, which, as everyone knows, always shows the correct time." The audience burst out laughing, immediately recognizing the broken clock that had stopped working years ago.

In the audience, the school principal, Mr. Peralta, listened with his arms crossed, not quite sure whether he should laugh or feel offended. When Nicolás went on to thank "the excellent cafeteria food, which prepares us so well for any future challenge, hunger included," the principal couldn't help an uncomfortable smile, while the students around him applauded enthusiastically.

At the end of the monologue, Nicolás closed with a line that sounded like a compliment but that everyone understood for what it really was: "And above all, thanks for always listening so attentively whenever we have a suggestion." The silence that followed lasted barely a second, until the principal, to everyone's surprise, stood up and started applauding, realizing that the joke, uncomfortable as it was, had a point.

After the show, Mr. Peralta looked for Nicolás backstage. "Your monologue made me reflect more than any formal complaint I've received this year," he admitted. "Next time you want to change something around here, come talk to me directly, no need to get up on a stage." Nicolás smiled, satisfied: with irony and humor, he had managed to say in five minutes what no student had ever dared to say in person.
