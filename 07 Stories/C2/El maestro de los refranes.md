## English Translation

Teacher Andrés Villalobos announced, as soon as class began, that that day they would set the books aside to devote themselves to something he considered as valuable as any grammar lesson: proverbs. "Every country, and even every region, has its own," he explained, while writing the word "PROVERBS" on the board in enormous letters, "and even though they share the same language, they rarely say things the same way." The students, intrigued, looked at each other, wondering what kind of assignment this would be.

"I want each of you to bring a proverb your family taught you," the teacher continued, "it doesn't matter where it's from, as long as you explain it well." The next morning, several students raised their hands, eager to share what they had learned at home.

Camila was the first to speak. "My grandmother, who's from Mexico, always says: 'Whoever leans against a good tree gets good shade.' It means it's worth surrounding yourself with people who can help you when you need it." The teacher nodded, pleased, and wrote the proverb in a column on the board dedicated to Mexico.

Then Mateo spoke, whose family was from Bolivia. "My grandfather says: 'God helps those who rise early, but he helps those who don't too, if they're lucky.'" The whole class laughed, and the teacher explained that, in Bolivia, it was common to add that humorous twist to the end of the traditional proverb, something not heard in other countries in the region. "That shows," he said, "that a proverb, however fixed it may seem, can always transform depending on who tells it."

To close the class, the teacher proposed a different game: double meanings. "What does one traffic light say to another?" he asked, with a mischievous smile. No one knew how to answer. "Don't look at me, I'm changing," he revealed, and the whole class burst out laughing, finally understanding the wordplay between changing color and changing clothes.

At the end of the day, while packing up their notebooks, the students kept repeating the traffic-light joke to each other. Teacher Villalobos, pleased with the lesson, wrote one last sentence on the board for them to copy before leaving: "Words, like proverbs, change outfits depending on the country that wears them."
