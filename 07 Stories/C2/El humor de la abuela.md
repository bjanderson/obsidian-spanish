## English Translation

At Grandmother Amparo's house, no family gathering ever ended without her cracking, at least once, one of those comments that made some laugh and others blush. That afternoon, with the whole family gathered to celebrate her eightieth birthday, no one was spared her sharp tongue.

When Uncle Ramiro arrived late, as usual, carrying a last-minute store-bought cake, Grandmother Amparo didn't miss the chance. "Would you look at that," she said, with a smile that didn't give away whether she was joking or not, "even cakes get here faster than you, and they have to be baked." The whole table burst into laughter, Ramiro included, who was already used to being his mother's favorite target.

Later, when her granddaughter Valentina proudly announced she had started studying philosophy, Grandmother Amparo raised an eyebrow. "Ay, sweetheart," she sighed, "in this family we already have plenty of people who talk a lot and say nothing. You didn't need to go to college for that." The family's laughter mixed with Valentina's mock protests — she knew her grandmother's sense of humor far too well to actually take offense.

Between courses, someone mentioned that the neighbor on the corner had sold his house for an absurdly high price. Grandmother Amparo, without missing a beat with her fork, remarked: "That man would sell the shadow off his own back to whoever would buy it, just like Tío Conejo from the old stories, who always found someone to trick." The younger members of the family, who had grown up hearing those stories from their grandmother, immediately caught the reference, while the little ones asked, confused, who this Tío Conejo was supposed to be.

At the end of the night, when it was time for her to blow out the candles, someone asked Grandmother Amparo what her secret was for turning eighty with her sense of humor still intact. She laughed, looking around at her whole family gathered around the table. "The secret," she answered, "is not taking even serious things too seriously. If you laugh at yourself first, no one else gets to laugh at you." And with that line, which sounded like ancient wisdom but had probably just been invented on the spot, Grandmother Amparo blew out the candles amid applause and one last round of laughter.
