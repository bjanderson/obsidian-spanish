## English Translation

The whole family — grandparents, parents, children — had spent the entire weekend emptying out the old Vargas house before the new owners arrived. They had found boxes of photographs, moth-eaten clothes, and furniture no one remembered ever using, but it was little Mateo who made the most important discovery, when he noticed that one of the bricks in the fireplace shifted at the slightest touch.

"There's something behind here!" he shouted, and within seconds the whole family had gathered around the fireplace. Behind the loose brick, wrapped in a yellowed silk handkerchief, they found a sealed envelope with wax on it, addressed to "my dear brother Fermín" and signed with a name no one recognized at first: Baldomero Vargas.

Grandfather Ernesto, hands trembling, opened the envelope with the care of someone handling something sacred. "Baldomero was my great-grandfather's brother," he murmured, "the one who left for America and was never heard from again." The whole family fell silent as Ernesto began to read aloud.

"Dear brother: by the time you have read this letter, I will already have left this town forever, and I do not know whether we shall ever meet again someday. Know that I did not leave for lack of love toward you or our parents, but because, the moment I discovered the truth about our grandfather's inheritance, I understood that my presence here would only bring more strife to a family that had already suffered enough. I prefer the silence of being forgotten to being the cause of more pain among my own. Should any descendant of mine come to read these words, let them know that Baldomero Vargas was no traitor, as was surely said of him, but a man who chose exile over discord. May God bless you all, and may this letter, should it ever be found, serve as forgiveness from one who could never ask for it in person."

The moment he finished reading the last word, Grandfather Ernesto set the letter down on the table and covered his face with his hands for a long moment. No one spoke. Finally, Grandmother Rosaura, her eyes wet, broke the silence. "All these years thinking Baldomero had abandoned us without explanation," she said, "and it turns out he left to protect us." Mateo, still not fully grasping the magnitude of the discovery, asked if they could keep the letter instead of leaving it in the house. Grandfather Ernesto smiled, tucking it carefully into his jacket pocket. "This letter," he said, "stays with the family. At last, after all these years, Baldomero has come home."
