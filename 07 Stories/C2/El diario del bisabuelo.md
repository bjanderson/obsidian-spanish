# El diario del bisabuelo

"¡[[abuelo-abuela|Abuela]], [[mirar|mira]] esto!" gritó, sacudiendo la caja para quitarle el polvo. Dentro [[haber|había]] un [[cuaderno]] forrado en cuero, con las esquinas gastadas y las [[página|páginas]] amarillentas. ## English Translation

Sofía was helping her grandmother clean the attic when her hand bumped into a dusty wooden box, hidden behind an old cabinet. "Grandma, look at this!" she shouted, shaking off the dust. Inside was a leather-bound notebook, its corners worn and its pages yellowed. On the cover, someone had written in faded ink: "Diary of Anselmo Reyes, 1948."

"That notebook belonged to your great-grandfather," her grandmother said, taking it carefully in her hands, as if afraid of breaking it. "I thought it had been lost years ago." Sofía sat down on the attic floor, and her grandmother, smiling, told her she could read it if she was careful. The girl opened the first page, and the handwriting, tight and elegant, carried her to another time.

*"Today, March 3rd, I have decided to set down in these pages the events of my life, in the hope that, someday, my descendants will find in them a faithful account of all I lived through. I was born into a humble home, but I never lacked bread or my parents' love, to whom I owe everything I am. I confess, however, that in my heart there has long nested a desire to set off toward distant lands, in search of a fortune this village would never offer me."*

Sofía looked up from the notebook, her eyes wide open. "Grandma, great-grandfather wanted to travel!" "Keep reading," her grandmother replied, "there's a part that always used to make me cry." The girl turned several pages, until she reached a date marked with a circle.

*"Today I must depart, and I do not know if I shall see these mountains I love so much again. My mother, her face bathed in tears, handed me this notebook so that I might record my travels in it, so that, even if the sea should part us, memories never would. I promise, then, to write every night, however brief the entry, so that, when I return, I may tell you all I have seen and felt."*

Sofía closed the notebook slowly, as if afraid the words might escape. "And did he come back?" she asked her grandmother. "He came back," she answered, her voice a little broken, "and he brought this notebook full of stories he never finished telling us." That night, before going to sleep, Sofía took a new notebook out of her backpack and, in clumsy but determined handwriting, wrote on the first page: "Diary of Sofía Reyes."
