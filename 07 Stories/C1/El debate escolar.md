## English Translation

Every year, the school organized a debate among the senior students on a current topic. This year, the topic was whether cell phones should be banned inside the classroom. Nicolás and his teammate, Fernanda, had decided to defend the position in favor of the ban, while the opposing team, led by Tomás, would argue that cell phones could be a useful tool if used responsibly.

During the week before the debate, Nicolás and Fernanda met every afternoon to organize their arguments. "First of all," Fernanda proposed, "we should mention the studies showing that cell phones distract students during class." Nicolás agreed, but suggested they also include an argument about the reduction of online bullying. "Also," he added, "let's not forget to mention that many schools that already banned cell phones reported a noticeable improvement in grades."

The day of the debate arrived, and the auditorium was full of students and teachers. Tomás opened the debate for the opposing side with a convincing argument: "Cell phones, used correctly, allow immediate access to information and make learning easier. Therefore, banning them entirely would mean ignoring their educational benefits." The audience murmured, visibly convinced by his reasoning.

Fernanda responded calmly. "It's true that cell phones offer certain benefits," she admitted, "however, the evidence suggests that, in practice, most students use them to get distracted rather than to learn. Consequently, a ban within the classroom would be more beneficial than harmful." The audience nodded, considering the argument carefully.

Nicolás took the floor to close his team's argument. "In conclusion," he said, looking directly at the judges, "it's not about denying the usefulness of technology, but about recognizing that, within the specific context of the classroom, the benefits of a clear policy far outweigh the risks of leaving cell phones without any restriction." The judges deliberated for several minutes while the audience waited in silence.

In the end, the judges declared Nicolás and Fernanda's team the winner, not because the other team had argued poorly, but because they had managed to present more solid and organized evidence. Tomás congratulated his fellow debaters with a sincere smile. "You won with good arguments," he admitted, "though I still think technology, used well, can be an ally." Nicolás smiled and replied that, ultimately, that was the purpose of the debate: to learn to defend an idea with solid arguments, no matter which side you were on.
