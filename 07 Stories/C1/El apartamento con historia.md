## English Translation

When Isabela found the apartment listing, it seemed like an incredible opportunity: an old building in the city center, whose rent was surprisingly low for the area. The owner, an older man named don Alfredo, explained to her that the apartment had belonged to his brother, a writer whose name Isabela didn't recognize right away, but which sparked her curiosity.

"My brother lived here for thirty years," don Alfredo told her while showing her the rooms, whose walls still held wooden bookshelves built to fit the space exactly. "He wrote novels he never got around to publishing, stories about which he kept a lot of secrets." Isabela, fascinated by the details, signed the lease that same afternoon without hesitating too much.

Over the first few weeks, Isabela noticed strange things in the apartment: drawers that appeared half-open, a smell of old ink that would occasionally rise from the study, a small room whose door was always locked. One night, determined to solve the mystery, she found the key hidden behind a painting whose frame looked like it had recently been moved.

Inside the room was a desk covered with manuscripts, each one with handwritten notes in the margins, and an unfinished letter addressed to a publishing house whose name Isabela recognized as one of the most important in the country. The letter explained that the author, after years of rejection, had decided to stop trying to get published, but that he held onto the hope that someone, someday, would find his stories and value them.

The next day, Isabela showed the discovery to don Alfredo, whose face changed completely when he saw the manuscripts. "I thought I'd lost them all," he admitted, his eyes wet. "My brother died convinced that his work didn't matter to anyone." Isabela offered something she hadn't planned to say: to help him look for a publisher willing to publish, even if posthumously, the stories he had fought so hard for in silence.

Months later, thanks to both of their efforts, a small independent publisher, whose catalog specialized in forgotten authors, agreed to publish a collection of the stories. At the book launch, don Alfredo, visibly moved, thanked Isabela for having rented an apartment whose history, without knowing it at first, ended up changing an entire family's life.
