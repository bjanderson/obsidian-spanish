## English Translation

Ever since their father had started having memory problems, the three Delgado siblings—Patricia, Óscar, and Verónica—knew that sooner or later they'd have to make a difficult decision about his care. The disagreement arose when Patricia proposed that their father move to a specialized care facility, while Óscar insisted he should stay at home, with the help of a hired caregiver.

The first conversation, over video call, ended badly. Óscar accused Patricia of wanting to "get rid of" the problem, and Patricia replied that Óscar didn't understand the seriousness of the situation because he lived in another city and didn't see their father every day. Verónica, who had stayed quiet up to that point, suggested they take some time before continuing to talk, to avoid saying something they'd regret afterward.

A week later, the three of them got together again, this time with a clear agenda Verónica had prepared. "I propose we start by listening to each person's concerns, without interrupting," she suggested. "Afterward, let's weigh the options calmly, considering both Dad's well-being and what's realistic for each of us." Patricia and Óscar accepted the format, though with some initial reluctance.

Patricia explained that her main concern was safety: their father had gotten lost twice in the neighborhood in the past month, and she feared that a caregiver, however well trained, couldn't offer the constant supervision that a specialized facility could guarantee. Óscar, for his part, admitted his resistance largely came from a feeling of guilt, since he couldn't be physically present to help more.

Hearing this, Verónica proposed a middle-ground alternative: hiring a full-time caregiver for a three-month trial period, with the condition of installing security cameras and setting up mandatory weekly visits from the three siblings, taking turns. If the situation got worse or the caregiver couldn't maintain the necessary safety, they'd then consider the option of the care facility. Óscar, relieved not to have to completely rule out his original idea, agreed immediately. Patricia, though she still preferred the facility, acknowledged the proposal was reasonable and was willing to give it a try.

Months later, with the caregiver already settled in and the routine working better than expected, the three siblings recognized that the process, though difficult, had forced them to communicate with an honesty they had rarely practiced before. "I think we learned to negotiate without anyone feeling like they lost," Verónica reflected during a phone call. Patricia and Óscar agreed: the compromise hadn't been a perfect solution for anyone, but it was one they could all live with in good conscience.
