## English Translation

Renata and Bruno had been best friends since elementary school, and they knew each other so well that they almost always understood each other's jokes without needing to explain them. That's why, when Renata showed up late again to the play rehearsal, Bruno remarked in front of the whole group: "What punctuality you have, they should hold you up as an example at school." Everyone laughed, including Bruno, who expected Renata to take it as a joke too.

However, Renata didn't smile. She stayed quiet for a moment and then said she had to leave early. Bruno, confused, thought maybe she was just tired, and didn't think much more about it. What he didn't know was that Renata had spent the whole week dealing with problems at home, and the comment, though said as a joke, had hurt her more than Bruno could imagine.

Over the following days, Renata avoided Bruno in the hallways and answered his messages with short replies. Bruno noticed the change in attitude, but didn't understand what he had done wrong. "Are you mad at me?" he finally asked, a little frustrated. "I don't know what you're talking about," she replied, in a tone that suggested exactly the opposite of what her words said.

Bruno caught the irony in her voice, but decided not to push it, thinking that silence would resolve things over time. Instead, the distance between them grew. A classmate from the theater group, Damián, noticed the tension and asked Bruno what had happened. Bruno told him about the comment about punctuality, still not understanding why it had affected his friend so much.

"Sometimes what someone says as a joke isn't received as a joke," Damián explained to him, "especially if the person is already going through something hard." Bruno realized, then, that he had never asked Renata how she was really doing. That same afternoon, he found her after rehearsal and apologized, not just for the comment, but for not having noticed that something was wrong.

Renata, surprised by Bruno's sincerity, finally told him what had been going on at home. "It wasn't the comment itself," she admitted, "it's that, that particular day, I felt like no one really noticed me." Bruno finally understood that jokes between friends only work when both people are in the same emotional place, and that the same comment could sound funny one day and hurt deeply the next.
