## English Translation

Doña Esperanza was seventy-five years old and, for months, had been keeping a half-written sheet of paper in her desk drawer. It was a letter addressed to her brother Gustavo, with whom she hadn't spoken in almost twenty years, ever since a dispute over their parents' inheritance that they had never fully managed to resolve.

Every so often, Esperanza would take out the sheet, reread what she had written so far, and put it away again unfinished. "Dear Gustavo," it began, in the careful handwriting of someone educated in a time when penmanship mattered. "I'm writing these lines to you after a long silence, hoping you're in good health, along with your family." After that formal greeting, the letter stayed blank for several paragraphs, as if the right words refused to come.

One afternoon, her granddaughter Marisol found her rereading the letter once again. "Grandma, why don't you just finish it already?" she asked, with the curiosity typical of her sixteen years. Esperanza sighed and explained that she didn't know how to put so many years of silence into words without it sounding either too cold or too desperate. "I want it to sound sincere," she said, "but I also don't want to beg for forgiveness that maybe I'm not even seeking."

Marisol suggested that, instead of thinking so much about getting the tone just right, she simply write what she truly felt. That night, for the first time, Esperanza wrote without stopping: she told him how much she missed their Sunday conversations, how sorry she was for letting pride get in the way for so long, and how much she wished her grandchildren could get to know their uncle before it was too late.

She ended the letter with a formula she had learned when she was young: "With nothing further for now, and hoping to hear from you soon, your sister bids you a warm farewell, still loving you despite the time and distance. Esperanza." She folded the sheet carefully, put it in an envelope, and wrote the address from memory, surprised she could still recall it after so many years.

The next day, with the envelope in her purse, Esperanza walked to the post office, though the whole way there she felt her heart pounding hard. In front of the mailbox, she hesitated for a long moment. Finally, taking a deep breath, she let the letter drop inside. She didn't know whether Gustavo would reply, or even whether he still lived at the same address. But, for the first time in twenty years, she felt she had done the right thing, no matter the outcome.
