## English Translation

When Mariana got her first full-time job, she felt proud to finally be able to save money every month. However, after six months, she noticed that her savings account was barely growing, despite earning a decent salary. She decided to ask her uncle Ernesto for advice, who worked as a financial advisor and always spoke enthusiastically about investing.

"The problem," Ernesto explained to her during the family dinner, "is that saving isn't the same as investing. If you leave your money sitting in a savings account, inflation reduces its real value over time. You need to make your money work for you." Mariana, a little confused, asked where she should start. "First," her uncle replied, "you have to create a budget. Without knowing how much you spend and on what, it's impossible to know how much you can invest."

Over the following weeks, Mariana logged every expense in an app and discovered, surprised, that she was spending a considerable amount on subscriptions she almost never used and on food delivery. By cutting those expenses, she managed to free up a portion of her salary that used to disappear without her noticing. "Now that you have a clear budget," Ernesto told her, "we can talk about how to diversify your savings."

Ernesto explained the basic concepts to her: the difference between stocks and bonds, the importance of not putting all your money into a single investment, and the value of thinking long-term instead of chasing quick gains. "A lot of people lose money," he warned, "because they panic when the market drops and sell right at the worst moment. Patience is just as important as strategy."

With her uncle's guidance, Mariana opened an investment account and set aside a small portion of her salary each month into a low-risk diversified fund. At first she found it hard not to check the value of her investment every day, but little by little she learned to trust the process. "It's not about getting rich quick," Ernesto would remind her, "it's about building solid financial habits that work for you throughout your whole life."

A year later, Mariana looked at her progress with satisfaction. Her investment account had grown modestly but steadily, and more importantly, she had developed a much more mindful relationship with her money. She no longer spent without thinking, and every financial decision felt more intentional. She had learned that building wealth didn't depend on a stroke of luck, but on the discipline of starting, even if it was with very little.
