## English Translation

Every afternoon, after school, Camilo would sit on the porch with his grandfather Rubén to wait for the heat to die down. His grandfather loved speaking in proverbs, and Camilo, though he didn't always understand the meaning at first, had learned to ask him to explain them. "Grandpa, today I wanted to copy my friend's homework instead of doing it myself," the boy confessed one day, a little embarrassed.

Rubén smiled and shook his head. "A road that costs nothing is worth little," he told him. "What's gained without effort is almost never truly appreciated. If you copy the homework, you finish the afternoon, but you learn nothing." Camilo stayed there thinking about those words while he played with a stone between his fingers.

The next day, the boy told his grandfather he had decided to save part of his weekly allowance instead of spending it all on candy. "That's it!" Rubén exclaimed, patting him on the back. "A bird in the hand is worth more than a hundred flying. It's better to take care of the little you already have than to risk it all for something that isn't even certain." Camilo smiled, proud of having made the right decision without anyone asking him to.

A few days later, Camilo woke up late and ran to the bus stop, only to watch it pull away without him. Frustrated, he sat on the bench to wait for the next one. When he got home that evening, he told his grandfather everything, complaining about his bad luck. "The early bird catches the worm," Rubén replied calmly. "Whoever gets up early usually gets better opportunities. Next time, set two alarms instead of one."

Camilo took the advice seriously, and over the following weeks he got up before his alarm went off. One day, thanks to arriving early at school, the teacher chose him to represent his class in a math competition. When he ran to tell his grandfather, Rubén nodded with pride. "You see? The early bird catches the worm. Today it caught you this competition." Camilo finally understood that his grandfather's proverbs weren't just old words, but lessons his family had kept for generations.

That night, before going to sleep, Camilo asked his grandfather to teach him a new proverb. Rubén thought for a moment and smiled. "Closed mouths gather no flies," he said finally. "Sometimes it's better to stay quiet than to say too much." The boy laughed and promised to remember it the next time he felt like telling a secret that wasn't his to tell.
