## English Translation

Rodrigo ran a small graphic design company that, six months earlier, had signed a contract with a restaurant chain to redesign its brand image. The agreement set out three payments: one at the start of the project, another at the midpoint, and the last one upon delivery of the final materials. Rodrigo had met every stage on time, but the client still hadn't made the final payment, even though almost two months had passed since delivery.

After several informal reminders that went unanswered, Rodrigo decided it was time to draft a formal communication. He consulted a lawyer, who suggested including specific references to the contract's clauses to establish that the claim had legal grounds. "It's not about threatening," the lawyer explained, "but about showing that you know your rights and are willing to enforce them."

Rodrigo carefully drafted the letter: "To Whom It May Concern, I am writing to you regarding the contract signed on January twelfth of this year, specifically regarding clause five, which stipulates the final payment within fifteen days following delivery of the materials. As of this date, said payment has not been made, despite the materials having been delivered as agreed."

"I request," the letter continued, "that you resolve this situation within a period of no more than ten business days from receipt of this communication. Should a satisfactory response not be received, I will find it necessary to consider pursuing the relevant legal actions to enforce my contractual rights." The lawyer reviewed the document and suggested softening the tone slightly, without taking away its firmness, to leave the door open for an amicable solution.

Five days later, Rodrigo received a response from the restaurant chain's financial director, who apologized for the delay and explained that an internal administrative error had caused the holdup. He attached proof of a transfer for the full amount owed, along with a formal apology for the inconvenience caused. Rodrigo, relieved, confirmed receipt of the payment and thanked him for the prompt resolution of the matter.

Weeks later, reflecting on what had happened, Rodrigo realized that writing that letter, though uncomfortable at first, had been essential. "I learned that being professional doesn't mean avoiding conflict," he told his lawyer during a later consultation, "but knowing how to communicate it with clarity, firmness, and respect, even when the situation is tense." From then on, he included clearer clauses about payment deadlines in all his future contracts.
