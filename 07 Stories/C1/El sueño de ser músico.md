## English Translation

Since he was twelve, Emiliano had dreamed of becoming a professional musician. Every afternoon, after coming home from school, he would shut himself in his room to practice guitar for hours, trying to perfect every chord. His parents, however, insisted that he focus on his studies, because they didn't trust that music could offer him a stable future. "It's not about talent," his father would tell him again and again, "it's about having something reliable to depend on."

Emiliano understood his parents' concern, but he couldn't stop thinking about music. He had secretly joined a local band that rehearsed on Saturdays in a friend's garage, and every time he fell more in love with the idea of devoting himself to writing songs. He had the support of his best friend, Julieta, who also played in the band and believed in his talent more than he did himself.

One Saturday, his parents decided to go pick him up early from school, and when they couldn't find him, they started to worry. They finally found out, thanks to a message from a neighbor, that Emiliano usually spent Saturday afternoons in Julieta's garage. When they got there, they found him in the middle of a rehearsal, singing in front of the small group with a passion they had never seen in him before.

That night, at home, there was a tense conversation. "We can't allow you to give up your studies to chase such an uncertain dream," his mother said, worried. Emiliano, his voice shaky but firm, replied that he had no intention of dropping out of school, that he just wanted them to trust him enough to let him try. "I know music doesn't always guarantee success," he admitted, "but I don't want to regret not having tried either."

His father, after a long silence, remembered that he too had once dreamed of being something different from what he ended up becoming, and that he had never dared to try because of fear. That confession changed the tone of the conversation. His parents finally agreed to let Emiliano continue with music as long as he kept up his grades, on the condition that he learn to balance both things.

Over time, Emiliano learned that pursuing a dream didn't mean giving up everything else, but rather finding a way for the two parts of his life to coexist. He kept practicing every afternoon, this time without hiding, and although the road to becoming a professional musician remained uncertain, he knew he no longer had to walk it alone.
