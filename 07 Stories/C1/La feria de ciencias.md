## English Translation

Sofía had spent three weeks preparing her project for the science fair, a volcano that spewed red foam when baking soda and vinegar were mixed. Having reviewed every step of the experiment several times, she was sure everything would go well. Her teammate, Iker, was in charge of the poster board with the explanations, while she finished painting the volcano gray and brown.

Once the painting was finished, they left the volcano drying overnight on the kitchen table. The next morning, Sofía ran downstairs to check on her work and discovered, to her horror, that the cat had knocked the volcano to the floor. One of the cardboard walls was broken, and the paint had run everywhere. Without losing her calm, though with her heart racing, she called Iker to tell him what had happened.

Having thought of several solutions, they decided to rebuild the wall with tape and repaint that part before leaving for school. Working fast and without talking too much, they managed to get it looking almost like new just in time for the bus. "I hope no one notices the difference," Iker said, eyeing the repaired wall with distrust.

Once at the fair, they set the volcano on their assigned table, between a project about the solar system and another about plants. With the setup finished, the two children waited nervously for the judges to arrive. When they finally came over, Sofía explained the chemical process in a steady voice, pointing to each part of the volcano, while Iker showed the poster board with the data and graphs.

Having listened to the whole explanation, one of the judges asked what they had learned from the accident with the cat, because he had noticed a small color difference on one of the walls. Surprised, the children looked at each other and decided to tell the whole truth. The judge smiled and said that kind of problem also happened in real laboratories, and that knowing how to solve them was just as important as the experiment itself.

Once the fair was over, Sofía and Iker received a special mention, not for the perfect volcano they had planned, but for honestly explaining what had gone wrong and how they had fixed it. Walking home, Sofía thought that next time, she'd leave the volcano somewhere the cat couldn't reach it.
