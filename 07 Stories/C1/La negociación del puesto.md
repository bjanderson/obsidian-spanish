## English Translation

After three interviews, Valeria finally received the offer she had been waiting for: a financial analyst position at a mid-sized company with a good reputation in the sector. The proposed salary, however, was a little lower than what she had researched as the average for that role in the industry. She decided it was worth trying to negotiate before accepting.

That night, Valeria carefully drafted an email, aware that the tone needed to be professional but firm, without sounding demanding or ungrateful. "Dear Ms. Ortiz," she wrote, "thank you so much for the offer for the financial analyst position. I'm excited about the possibility of joining the team and contributing to the company's growth. Before confirming my acceptance, I'd like to discuss the possibility of adjusting the proposed salary, taking into account my prior experience and the market average for similar positions."

Two days later, the recruiter's reply arrived. "Thank you for your message, Valeria. We greatly value your interest in the position and understand your point about the market. Although we can't match the exact figure you mention, we could offer you a five percent increase over the initial offer, plus a salary review at six months based on your performance." Valeria felt relief: it wasn't everything she had asked for, but it was a meaningful step.

Before replying, Valeria consulted her older brother, who worked in human resources, about how to continue the conversation without seeming insatiable. "You could accept the increase," he suggested, "but also ask for the salary review to be put in writing in the contract, instead of relying on a verbal promise." The advice seemed sensible to Valeria, so she decided to include it in her next email.

"Dear Ms. Ortiz," she wrote again, "I greatly appreciate the flexibility shown. I gladly accept the proposed increase. I would also appreciate it if the six-month salary review could be documented in writing in the contract, so that both parties have clarity on the agreed terms." The reply arrived the next day: the company accepted the condition without objections, and attached the final contract for her signature.

When Valeria finally accepted the position, she realized the negotiation had taught her something valuable: asking for what you deserve doesn't mean being difficult, but knowing how to communicate your own needs with clarity and respect. She signed the contract with the satisfaction of having stood up for her professional worth from day one.
