## English Translation

Ana woke up early, like every day. The sunlight came in through the window as she opened her eyes and looked outside. It was hot, but Ana liked the heat of her town. She got dressed quickly because she had to get to school early, where she'd been working for three years. She didn't know exactly what was going to happen that day, but she felt calm.

She lived with her friend Rosa in a small house, without luxuries, but full of life. Ana ate bread and drank coffee before leaving. "We need to walk fast," she said to Rosa, who was still asleep.

On the way to school, Ana ran into a man selling fruit near the central market. He was carrying a newspaper under his arm and told her there was an important trial in town: two families wanted the same land, and no one yet knew who was telling the truth. Ana paid the man for some fruit and continued on her way. The town's land produced corn and potatoes, which was why the land was worth so much. Ana listened closely and thought about the state of the problem; it seemed sad to her that this was happening between neighbors.

When Ana got to school, her students were already waiting for her. That day, Ana wanted to do a special activity: have her students write a little book or story about their lives. Class started at eight, and Ana led it calmly, because she wanted to teach them more than just grammar. She explained the steps patiently and let each child choose their own topic. One of the kids asked her how many pages his story needed to have; Ana told him there wasn't a fixed number. Another child forgot his pencil, and Ana gave him a new one. She showed them a map of the town to help them think, and asked them to repeat the new words out loud. For the activity, Ana used recycled paper instead of buying new notebooks. She also helped the kids paint the classroom walls in bright colors. The students worked through almost the whole morning, until lunchtime, and managed to finish the whole activity. Ana felt proud watching everyone's effort. That work kept her fully occupied, but it made her happy.

At noon, her friend Diego arrived with food to share. "Today it's our turn to have lunch together," he said, smiling. Diego worked at the town's health center and always brought good news about people's health. They talked about the trial, and Diego guessed the judge would decide it soon. "I think the two families' situation will get better once this is over," Diego said, and Ana smiled, because she was hoping for that too. Diego's food was going to be enough for everyone, even for any neighbors who happened to stop by.

In the afternoon, Ana decided to take a break. "Are you coming with us to the river?" Rosa asked. "Yes," Ana answered. She went with Rosa and Diego to the river, where they played and swam for an hour. Diego threw a ball into the water, and Rosa hit it with her hand, laughing. Ana ran after the ball and managed to keep her balance before it slipped out of her hands. No one won or lost, because no one was keeping score. At one point, Diego pulled on the rope of an old boat, just for fun. "I like this free time," Ana said, and the three of them laughed.

On her way back home, Ana saw that a window was broken; something had broken it that morning. She got out some tools and tried to fix it herself. She used a hammer to close up the frame properly, and after a few minutes she managed to get the glass to sit firmly again. Rosa helped her put away the tools and move a chair that was out of place. It was already late by the time they finished, and Rosa called her in for dinner. Before sitting down, Ana set the table while Rosa served the food.

Before Diego arrived with his guitar, Ana heard laughter outside and recognized his voice. That night, Diego came over again to sing some songs with his guitar and started playing while Rosa danced and Ana laughed. At one point, Diego set the guitar down and introduced her to a new song he had written for her. Diego had to leave early the next day, but for now he wanted to stay a little longer. Afterward, he gave her a small notebook. "It's so you can keep writing your stories," he told her. Ana took it with trembling hands and felt her eyes fill with happy tears.

When everyone had left, Ana stayed by herself and thought about her life. The world seemed very big to her, but her town, with its problems and its joys, was the place where she wanted to live. She imagined the future: maybe Diego and Rosa would get married someday; maybe the trial would bring justice and peace between the families; maybe her students would grow up to be great writers. Ana didn't know for sure, but she believed everything could turn out fine if people treated each other with respect.

Before going to sleep, she wrote a few lines in her notebook. She remembered how, years earlier, she'd met Rosa on the very day she arrived in town, and how over time she'd come to understand that a good friendship doesn't need grand words. She also remembered when Diego and Rosa fell in love, one afternoon when he showed up at the door with flowers; back then nobody understood why they smiled so much, but Ana accepted it as a natural part of life. She thought about the school, which years ago had started as a single classroom and, over time, had turned into a place where real community existed. The neighbors had helped build its walls with their own hands, determined to create something that would be part of their children's future. She turned off the light and fell asleep thinking about everyone who made up her small, beautiful world.
