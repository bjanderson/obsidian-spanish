## English Translation

Every year, the school organized a talent competition, and that year Mateo wanted to win with his guitar. He practiced every afternoon, after finishing his homework, until his fingers hurt. He wanted to show everyone how much he had improved since the year before.

His friend Lucía was also going to take part; she danced better than anyone in the whole school. Everyone said Lucía would win the competition, and that made Mateo feel afraid of losing. "I don't think she's practiced as much as I have," Mateo told himself, though he knew a little jealousy was the real reason.

The afternoon before the competition, Mateo was practicing in the schoolyard when he heard music near the auditorium. It was Lucía, practicing her dance one more time. She looked tired, but she kept dancing with all her energy. Mateo felt bad for having doubted her. "Maybe she really has worked as hard as I have," he thought.

On the day of the competition, Mateo got up on stage and started playing his favorite song. But halfway through the song, one of the guitar strings snapped. Mateo didn't know what to do, and the audience watched him in silence.

From the audience, Lucía got up and ran to the stage with her own guitar, which her brother had taught her to play. "Here, use mine," she said, smiling. Mateo looked at her, surprised. "Why are you helping me, if we're rivals?" he asked. Lucía answered: "If I were you, I'd want someone to help me too."

Mateo took the guitar and finished his song without any more problems. The audience clapped loudly, and Mateo felt grateful. When it was Lucía's turn, Mateo cheered for her from the front row, shouting her name. "I'm really glad you decided to help me," he told her afterward. "And I'm glad you came to watch me dance," she answered.

In the end, neither Mateo nor Lucía won first prize, but neither of them minded anymore. "If we had worked together from the start, we would have been better," Mateo said, and Lucía smiled. "Next year, let's do something together," Lucía said. The two of them hugged and laughed, happy to have learned that winning isn't always the most important thing.
