The day of the history exam arrived, and Valeria felt nervous, even though she had studied a lot all week. She walked into the classroom and sat down near her friend Diego, who looked calm.

During the exam, Valeria noticed that Diego had a small piece of paper hidden under his hand, with the answers written on it. She was shocked and didn't know what to do. "If I said something now, Diego would be in serious trouble. If I said nothing, I'd also be part of the lie," she thought.

After the exam, Valeria couldn't stop thinking about what she'd seen. She asked Diego if he had cheated, and he told her he was very stressed about his grade and needed to pass the class.

"I think you should tell the teacher yourself," Valeria said. Diego lowered his head and admitted he was very afraid of failing the year. "You're right," he finally said. "It was a bad decision, and I feel guilty."

The next day, Diego talked to the teacher and confessed what he had done. The teacher listened closely and told him it was very honest of him, though he would have to retake the exam.

Valeria felt relieved that she had talked to Diego before saying anything to anyone else. "If I had told the teacher without talking to him first, maybe I would have lost his friendship," she thought. Diego, for his part, learned it was better to tell the truth than to live with the guilt of a lie.
