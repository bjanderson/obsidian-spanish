## English Translation

Camila spent the summer at her grandmother Rosa's house, like every year. She loved that place, because she always discovered something new among the old corners of the house. One day, Rosa asked her to help clean the attic, which no one had entered in years. "There's a lot of dust up there," her grandmother told her, "but there are also a lot of memories."

Camila climbed the stairs carefully and opened the attic door. Light came in through a small window and lit up old boxes, old clothes, and a wooden trunk. Camila opened it carefully and found a bundle of letters tied with a red ribbon. The first letter was addressed to "Carmen," and the handwriting looked like a child's.

"Grandma! Who was Carmen?" Camila asked, coming downstairs with the letters in her hand. Rosa smiled when she saw them and sat down slowly. "Carmen was my best friend when I was your age," she explained. "We lived on the same street and did everything together." Rosa said that she and Carmen had written those letters the summer Carmen's family moved to another city, and that they had promised to write each other every week.

Camila asked her if they were still friends. Rosa told her that, over time, they stopped writing and lost touch. "I wish we had kept writing to each other," Rosa said, her voice a little sad. "Sometimes I wonder what became of her life." Camila read one of the letters aloud, in which Carmen told Rosa about a tree where the two girls had buried a small metal box as a promise that someday they would open it together again.

"The tree in the old garden!" Rosa exclaimed, surprised. "It's probably still there." Camila asked her if she wanted to go look for the box, and Rosa, her eyes bright, said yes. "I hope the box is still buried there," Camila said excitedly. The two of them left the house and walked to the neighbor's garden, which had been, many years ago, the yard of the house where Rosa grew up.

When they reached the tree, Rosa told Camila that she and Carmen had made a promise: that no matter how much time passed, they would always be friends. With her hands, Camila moved aside the dirt and the dry leaves near the roots, and after a little while she felt something hard. It was the metal box, rusty but still closed. Rosa opened it with trembling hands, and inside they found an old photo of the two girls and a note that read: "For when we're grown up: don't forget your best friend."

Rosa read the note and her eyes filled with tears. "I wish I could have kept that promise," she said quietly. Camila took her hand and told her they could still look for Carmen. "Maybe it's not too late," she said. Rosa looked at her and, for the first time in years, felt hope of finding her old friend.
