## English Translation

Mariana signed a rental contract for a small apartment downtown. The landlord, Mr. Ramírez, explained all the rules to her very formally.

A few weeks later, Mariana noticed there was no hot water in the apartment. She wrote a formal letter to the landlord, in which she explained the problem and asked him to repair it soon.

The letter was received by the landlord, but the repair wasn't made on time. Mariana waited two more weeks, but the hot water still wasn't working.

Mariana decided to talk to the landlord in person. "You have a legal obligation to repair the damage in the apartment," she told him firmly. "According to the contract you wrote yourself, problems must be resolved within ten days."

The landlord promised her that someone would come fix it that same week, but no one came.

When another week passed with no repair at all, Mariana wrote him one last letter, in which she told him that, if the problem wasn't resolved soon, she knew her rights as a tenant and would seek legal help.

The following week, a man arrived and fixed everything in a few hours. The landlord also gave her back part of that month's rent, as an apology.

Mariana learned that knowing her rights as a tenant had given her the strength to demand what was fair.
