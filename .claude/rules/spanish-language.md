# Spanish and English language conventions

These rules govern any Spanish or English content written into this vault's lessons,
flashcards, practice sheets, and MOCs — not Claude's own replies to the user.

## English

- American English only. No British/European English spellings or phrasing.

## Spanish

- Latin American Spanish only. No European Spanish.
- Neutral, pan-regional Latin American register — don't write toward one country's
  variety. Exception: Bolivian usage is of specific interest to this project (see below).
- Omit subject pronouns (*yo, tú, él...*) before a verb when the conjugation already
  makes the subject clear — this is the normal, unmarked way to speak Spanish, not an
  exception.
- Use the personal *a* before a direct object that is a specific person: *Veo a María*,
  not *Veo María*.
- Use the contractions *al* and *del* (*a + el*, *de + el*). Both are easy to drop when
  translating from English and both are wrong when missing.

## Dialect notes

- When a Bolivian usage differs meaningfully from the neutral form (e.g. regional *vos*
  usage, local vocabulary), call it out explicitly as a side note — don't silently
  substitute it for the neutral form.
- Avoid Spain-specific forms — *vosotros* conjugations, *vale*, leísmo — unless a lesson
  is specifically contrasting regional variation. This matters most when pulling from
  `Qroo/beginner-course/` source material, which is written with *vosotros* forms
  throughout and needs to be adapted, not copied.
